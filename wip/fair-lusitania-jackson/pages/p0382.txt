He was willing that we should see all the sights of the place, but <i>he</i> just then “cared for none of those things.”

Had I been alone, I should have visited most of the quaint old buildings of Barcellos—interesting if only for their historical associations. From the little I saw of the country-people they appeared to be active and industrious, and of very merry mood. I witnessed a strange scene or dance performed amongst a party of men and women, who had assembled in a field near the gardens of the <i>quinta</i>. A young woman stood in the midst of a circle of her companions, and after dancing for some time with much gesticulation, clapping of hands, and cries and calls, of which I understood not a word, she suddenly assumed an attitude of despair, and dropped to the ground. This, I fancy, represented despairing love; for a young man stepped towards her from the rest of the circle, and gave her his hand. With an expression of great delight she jumped up and danced with him round the ring, nodding and smirking to the lookers-on as they passed. This seemed to make him jealous; he let fall her hand, whispered in her ear, and both uttered cries so angry and wild that they were anything but pleasant to hear.

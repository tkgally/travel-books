I saw two or three old churches in the Manoelino style, one of them containing several ancient pictures, all of which are said to be by Gran Vasco; to whom every old picture in Portugal, possessing a grain of merit, is attributed. Pictures of any value are not numerous in this country. The Portuguese themselves have exhibited a talent for sculpture rather than painting, and they have several sculptors of eminent ability. “A dead Christ,” of exquisite design and workmanship, may now be seen at a studio in Lisbon. It has been bought, I am told, by Dom Fernando. I do not at this moment remember the artist’s name.

Setubal has a pretty theatre, named after the poet Bocage—the famous <i>Improvisador</i>, of whom the Setubalense are so proud, and to whom they have erected a neat, pretty monument in one of the <i>praças</i>. The <i>praças</i> of Setubal are very pleasant places; that of the Senhor de bon-fim is especially charming, having a garden in the centre, fine shady trees and sparkling fountains. Of course there is a circus for bull-fights; but I understand that it is of recent erection. The Portuguese seem to be much more addicted to this kind of amusement than formerly.

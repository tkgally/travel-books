[[cont]]Some one less fluent had written beneath it “<i>é verdade</i>”—’tis true. Our friend, remorselessly clearing away the next effusion to make room for his own, wrote beneath the above, “<i>Erreur! erreur! L’esprit n’existe pas ici; les prosateurs l’ont chassé.</i>”

This was <i>meant</i> to be keenly cutting and <i>spirituel.</i> I was so dull as to think it a <i>niaiserie</i> rather than a witticism; of course I did not say so, but assumed an expression of “dear me, how clever!” The Frenchman handed his pencil to the Spaniard. Thus invited, with true Spanish courtesy, he wrote in French—“<i>Partout, où se trouve un Français, là l’esprit existe.</i>” The Frenchman bowed, and, seeking perhaps another compliment, prayed the <i>senhora</i>—the sister of the Spaniard—to write a line also. She declined; “she feared to venture after the display of “<i>tant d’esprit.</i>” It came lastly to the Englishwoman’s turn. “Would she write the thought of her heart at that moment?” She had a strong objection to such defacement of walls and windows; but as they were already so thoroughly scrawled over that a word or two more or less could not in any sense do further damage, “if <i>monsieur</i> would be her amanuensis she would dictate two words.” Of course he could not refuse; indeed “he felt himself honoured. What should he write?”

“Immediately under <i>Sua Excellencia’s</i> line write <i>c’est possible.</i>” He smiled, and wrote the words in a large bold hand; then, with a very low bow, said, “<i>Maintenant, je vais signer pour madame.</i>”—“<i>Perfide Albion!</i>”

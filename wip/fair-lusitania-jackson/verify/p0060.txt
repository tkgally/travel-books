[[cont]]<i>vagabondage.</i> The climate is favourable for out-of-door life, and the Portuguese are a very charitable people. Much misery, doubtless, is endured by many of these beggars; but, on the other hand, many more pick up a very good living in the streets—better far than that of some of the poor toiling labourers. Once, passers-by used to be horrified by the exhibition of dreadful deformities and sickening sores. I have not yet seen enough of Lisbon to say that this blot on her beauty is removed; but I am told that it is.

Many blind people I have already observed, but there has always been a large number thus sadly afflicted—the gritty summer dust, and the glare of the sun, too often affecting the sight of persons who by their occupations are much exposed to their baneful influence. Those who need the protection of eye-preservers, of course do well to wear them; but is it not fashion or affectation, rather than necessity for any aid to sight, that makes the double eye-glass and its elegant chain so generally in use among the ladies? A Spanish gentleman gallantly says, “it is in tender pity to his sex that the Portuguese ladies thus dim the lustre of their brilliant dark eyes.”

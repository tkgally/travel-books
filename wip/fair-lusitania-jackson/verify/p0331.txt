I returned to Foz rather late on the evening of the <i>festa.</i> The day had been excessively hot, and after sunset a very sirocco-like wind—heating, irritating, anything but a refreshing breeze—had begun to blow. Instead of the usual clear, calm, blue sky with its myriads of glittering stars, there were large patches of smoky cloud flying about, and an occasional rush in the air raised the deep sand of the <i>Praia</i> most uncomfortably. But notwithstanding these indications of a probable change in the weather, the promenaders were there in full force.

The <i>Americanos,</i> which from five until ten in the morning come down from Oporto crowded with bathers, in the evening are filled to overflowing with people who live in or near the city, or between it and S. João da Foz, and who regularly take their after-dinner stroll on the <i>Praia,</i> where they are sure to meet all their friends and acquaintances, and sit chatting and smoking under the trees for hours. And it is not the <i>cavalheiros</i> only who congregate on this deep, dusty plain, it is also a favourite resort of their <i>Excellencias.</i> All the rank and fashion, beauty and commerce of Oporto and Foz are to be seen there, arrayed in the latest Paris fashions. It is a great place for flirtations and stolen meetings.

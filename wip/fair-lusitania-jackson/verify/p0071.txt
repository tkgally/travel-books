[[cont]]Rua do Ouro, and at last get on level ground, not far from the Praça do Commercio.

My friend de F. suggests that we descend on teh left, the Rua Nova do Carmo. Though we have but part of the throng yet, from the narrowness of the road and the pavement, and the greater height of the houses, it looks more crowded here than on the Chiado. There are in these streets several good shops of modern appearance, the rest are of the old-fashioned kind; mere stalls, or dark passages, according to their depth, and of the width only of the door that gives entrance to them. They have no window, except the few panes of glass in the door, which is closed only at night or in bad weather. Some of these little boxes or sheds remain just as they were when first made, a century ago; but in a few, the glove shops, for instance—for gloves are a national <i>spécialité</i>—an attempt has been made to improve and decorate the small space, by scooping out a bit from a passage or cupboard at the back; putting up a small half-circular counter, a glass, to give an appearance of depth, mahogany pigeon-holes for the glove boxes, and engaging a pretty <i>dame de comptoir</i> to preside. Room too is found for a chair or two, which serve for the ladies who purchase there in the morning, and for the gentlemen later in the day, when they lounge into these pretty little alcoves, oftener, perhaps, to pay compliments to the <i>senhora</i> as they smoke a <i>cigarette,</i> than to buy any gloves. When we reach the end of the street, a few steps bring us to the immense Praça del Rocio, or the square of Dom Pedro IV.; and here also we find level ground.

The <i>douche</i> baths establishment, for which some people come expressly to Cintra, is on the Mafra road. The Conde de Ferreira’s schools, which interest many persons, are close at hand, and the journey to Mafra itself is shortened by making Estephania your headquarters.

Cintra has several hotels, but, after Victor’s, Mrs. Lawrence’s is the one most patronized by English visitors. Mrs. L. is an elderly woman, and has lived in Portugal, if not in Cintra, nearly all her life, I am told. Led by one of the boy guides, we made the mistake of entering her house at the back by a sort of low-roofed kitchen, where an old dame, whom I supposed to be the cook, sat shelling peas; but she lifted up her head, and peering through her spectacles, said, “Well, marm! and what may be <i>your</i> pleasure?”

“<i>Queremos almoçar</i>,” said my hungry companion, somewhat impatiently; while I replied by inquiring—“Are you Mrs. Lawrence?”

“Yes, to be sure, marm; and ain’t you English? for the young gentleman speaks Portuguese.”

“We want breakfast directly,” he said, in good English.

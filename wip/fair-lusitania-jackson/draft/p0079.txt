“Then I regret to have raised a doubt of it in your <i>Excellencia’s</i> mind. But remember, I do not assert that it <i>is</i>, I only believe it to be English—most probably it is English.”

“Then I assure your <i>Excellencia</i> that it is <i>verdadeira Portuguez</i>,” replied the owner of the crystal treasure, triumphantly, as she restored it to its place; “for it was made at Marinha Grande.”

“Is it possible?” exclaimed the <i>connoisseur</i>. “I thought that manufactory produced only articles of a useful kind—of a high quality, of course.”

“Yes, and sometimes precious trifles such as that you have so much admired.”

“Where is Marinha Grande? I will go there to-morrow,” he said.

“It is in the district of Leiria. The railroad will not carry you very near it; neither will you find there a stock of such things on hand. A pair of vases was produced for the Paris Exhibition; one, by some mischance, was broken; it was too late to replace it, and the remaining one, that which you have seen, was sold here for five hundred <i>milreis</i>.”

“About a hundred pounds, I think; far less than its value,” responded the <i>connoisseur</i>. “I shall not leave Portugal without paying a visit to the <i>Real fabrica</i> at Marinha Grande.”

Whether he is gone or going, I know not; but speaking of the <i>dépôt</i> brought the circumstance to my mind.

# Chapter XX.

[[subtitle]] Americanos—Serra do Pilar—Valle d’Amores, etc.

I <sc>have</sc> now been a week at S. João da Foz; running about pretty nearly the whole day, in spite of the heat, which though modified here by the breezes that blow from the Atlantic is yet as great as can well be borne in the middle of the day. In Oporto it is much more than that; it is the heat of a furnace. But so lively, so amusing, is that charming, quaint old city, that I spend all my mornings there; going up and down in the <i>Americanos,</i> which are exceedingly convenient—travelling the two miles between Oporto and this suburb in about a third of the time that a hired carriage takes for the journey. The charge is six <i>vintems</i> (sevenpence-halfpenny). There is plenty of space and air, no sun or dust, and all the world uses them. They began running only last year, I am told, and are a great success. Some of these <i>Americanos</i> go as far as the Rua dos Inglezes, in the lower part of the city; those that take their passengers <i>acima,</i> stop at the summit of the hill, near the Misericordia.

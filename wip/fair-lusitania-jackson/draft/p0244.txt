As we approach the lovely valley of the Varzea, stretching far away before us are olive-grounds and vineyards, from whose rich fruit the Burgundy of Portugal is produced; <i>quintas,</i> embosomed in their orange groves and gardens; Collares’ far-famed orchards, the peach, the plum, the pear, the almond, apricot, and cherry; and issuing from the lake, or Tanque de Varzea, and winding through apple grounds and banks of fragrant verdure, the bright little Rio das Maçaãs, or apples’ river, glides onward to the sea.

Near the town of Collares is a celebrated <i>quinta</i>, known as the <i>Quinta do Dias</i>. Few persons who visit Collares fail to go through its lovely gardens and grounds—remarkable even where all around is loveliness—or to ascend its lofty <i>Belvédère;</i> whence there is a prospect so extensive, beautiful, and varied, that once seen can hardly be forgotten. Wildly grand, enchanting, romantic, it must remain engraven on the memory, “a joy for ever.”

Some of our party had the folly to add to the mass of nonsense scribbled over every part of the <i>Belvédère</i> where a word or letter could be squeezed in. Examining these effusions, the Frenchman’s eye lighted on the following:—

<verse>
“<i>Do espirito de poesia</i>
<i>é a morada encantadora.</i>”
</verse>

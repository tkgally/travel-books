# Chapter XXVII.

[[subtitle]] Fires—Funeral Mass—State Carriages, Etc.

<i>Lisbon.</i>—My companions were bound for Badajoz, but were uncertain about their movements after arriving there. However, as I had a wish to see that city, I decided on accompanying them thither, taking my chance of what should follow—whether of being dragged further into Spain, or being driven out of it. It was arranged that we should sleep at Abrantes, where they expected to find letters; but, though no letters were forthcoming, their old friend of Barcellos—Castella, as I have called him—made his appearance there.

This unexpected <i>rencontre</i>, as it seemed to be, entirely changed the plans of the party, and instead of going on to Badajoz, they arranged to return to the <i>Entroncamento</i> and thence to proceed to Santarem, I of course, continuing my journey to Lisbon. This new arrangement was very unsatisfactory as far as I was concerned; for having gone thus far I should have liked to see something more of Abrantes than its fortifications and castle at a distance—even that view being partly intercepted by a hill.

At last we came to a mysterious passage, dimly lighted by the smoky flame of a small oil lamp. “<i>O Hotel de suas Excellencias</i>”—Their Excellencias’ Hotel—was blithely announced by mine host. “<i>Aqui?</i>”—here—we exclaimed, in a chorus. “<i>Sim, Excellencias, aqui</i>,” he answered, with a low bow, and taking the lead. His victims followed, toiling up I know not how many steps; but so many that to weary, way-worn travellers it seemed like the pilgrimage to the <i>piedade</i>, but without the resting-places. At the top of the ascent two grimy grooms of the chamber welcomed us with many bows and smiles, and our host ushered us into a large room, whose whole furniture, apparently, consisted of a folding screen and a small jug and basin on a chair. But we were deceived. Our host stepping forward a little, drew aside the screen, and behold there was a bed. What more could be desired? Yet Senhor T. complained, and grumbled in a way that would have done credit to any Englishman. The <i>senhora</i>, more resigned because more weary, shook her head, sighed, put the basin and jug on the floor, drew forth her <i>tabaqueira</i>, and meekly sat down.

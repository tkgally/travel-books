# Chapter XVIII.

[[subtitle]] By Rail to Oporto.

<i>S. João da Foz, Porto.</i>—I expected to be jaunting about Coimbra to-day instead of sitting down rather weary to pen an epistle to you from S. João da Foz. De F. had urged me to travel by night; the heat and the smokers, he said, would be intolerable by day. And that although many passengers might at this season be leaving Lisbon in the evening, and there were no carriages exclusively for ladies, yet, by slipping a <i>milreis</i> or so into the guard’s hand, he would secure me from intrusion by the label “<i>reservado.</i>” I did not follow his advice, for I dislike travelling in darkness. It is pleasant to get a glimpse of the country you pass through. As for the smoking—well, if the tobacco is good, in moderation it is very endurable, especially if their <i>Excellencias</i> take the trouble to spit out of window. “The more, the merrier,” applied to the Portuguese, is a very true saying; therefore, the more amusing would it be to travel with as many passengers as could be comfortably taken in, without squeezing.

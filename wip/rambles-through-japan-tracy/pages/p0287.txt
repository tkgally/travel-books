The labour was nearly done. Already steam was hissing through the valves of our engines, as if the mighty force was impatient to be at work. The anchors were weighed. One by one the barges of the coaling fleet pushed away from our vessel. Fires were lighted under tea-kettles; some refreshment was to be had by the little workers even before they landed. A bell sounds; a tremor hardly perceptible shakes the ship; we are off for other lands. Of the people whom I had learned to appreciate so well, this, then, was to be my last glimpse—some little Nagasaki coaling-girls, blackened with dust, hungry and tired from a long day’s work, yet singing and laughing as they floated at sunset toward their homes. Happier and healthier, even in such poverty, I am sure they are, under these skies, than thousands of their sisters who toil in the dark slums of London and New York, earning by their utmost endeavour barely enough to keep together body and soul.

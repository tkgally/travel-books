[[cont]]boatman pushed his skiff to shore as directed. In a few minutes I had transferred my belongings to a karuma, and paid off the gondolier. He was profuse in thanks; indeed, I fancy I paid him a trifle extra for not “doing it.” Poor old heathen! In his honest, simple heart he never dreamed, I dare say, of the dark suspicion with which a passenger once, for a “bad quarter of an hour,” regarded him!

Drawn by a single coolie along a solitary country path by a riverside, at eleven o’clock at night, was but little more inspiring, and I was not sorry, after nearly an hour’s ride, to draw up before an inn, even though it was closed and fastened up for the night. After much knocking and shouting, the landlord was awakened, and admitted me, more graciously than a traveller arriving at that hour could expect to be received, and I was soon safe and sound in a pleasant room. Safe enough I had been all the while. One laughs at the fears of midnight when morning comes. These honest heathen never had a thought of doing me an injury. Happily, they never guessed, I hope, the wrong done them in the imagination of a timorous traveller.

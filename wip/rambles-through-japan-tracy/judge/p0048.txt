It seemed as if most of the dishes were luxuries to which he was unaccustomed; for even the fragments he gathered up. How curious that articles of food which are an abomination to one human being are so relished by another! Yet everything was good and clean; it was simply strange to me.

Dinner finished, Matoi inquired—by going through the pantomime of closing his eyes and snoring—whether I wished to retire? There was no use standing about in a room without furniture, and I indicated assent. The maid-servant then brought into the chamber three or four thick cotton mattresses, and made a bed on the floor, placing a dainty wooden head-rest at one end; then she stretched over it all a mosquito-net with its corners secured to the sides of the room; and leaving behind her a paper lantern, in which a tiny wick floats upon a basin of oil, she made her final prostration, and left me to my note-book and “what dreams may come.”

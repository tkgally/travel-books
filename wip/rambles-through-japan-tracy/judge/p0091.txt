I had no idea what he should charge, but presumed he was asking for more than the usual price; so, as a matter of principle, I told him that was too much—“Takai! Takai!” At this he expostulated forcibly; and I ended by engaging him, as I intended to do all the time.

<i>August 8th.</i>—Up early and packed, ready to start long before the rest of the house was stirring. Breakfasted on beef-tea (Liebig’s) with rice, and boiled eggs. At seven the luggage was duly packed into the karuma, and I called for my account. It was such an outrageous overcharge that I somewhat fiercely protested, showing him at the same time my account at Omiya on this road, where Waku and myself were charged far less than what he presumed to charge me alone. He studied the account for a while; concluded, I hope, that one foreigner was not absolutely inexperienced in Japan travelling, and cut down his charge to about half, which I paid.[[fnref 1]]

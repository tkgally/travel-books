Sometimes he departs without making any provision for his offspring, and leaving their mother to poverty. Still, as the relation, in Japanese eyes, is a sort of wedlock, her reputation is in no way injured, nor her chances diminished of making another marriage.

Everywhere in and about the European quarter I came upon children of the mixed race, some of them exceedingly good-looking. About one I was told a little history that perhaps points a moral, unforeseen by impetuous youth. Some years ago a young man came to Japan to seek his fortune, and in a short time had followed the example of his friends and compatriots in setting up his household gods. Two children were born to him—“the handsomest children in Yokohama,” said his friend, who told me the story.

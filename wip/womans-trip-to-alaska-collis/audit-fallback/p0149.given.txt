Yet I could not help thinking of the desolation to which our departure condemned those who had been so kind to us; nor of those poor souls whose darkness may never be dispelled by the enlightenment of education and civilization; and it occurred to me that if I owned the steamship line I would build a little hotel there, that the pafsengers might have an opportunity of occupying a week in excursions to points of interest; which it is impossible to reach in a large ship, and then life at Sitka would not be so intolerable; and that it I was the government of the United States, I would put the Indians under such discipline that their quarters should be subject to inspection, and their children compelled to go to school. What two great boons these would be to Sitka, and how easy to accomplish both. But the scenery is so beautifully grand that I must keep my moralizing for some future time.

A five-hours’ break at Chicago afforded us an opportunity to freshen up and get a good dinner at the Richelieu, a capital hotel, nicely situate on Lake Michigan, where, during my meal, a couple of my <i>nouveaux-riches</i> countrymen, who in England would be called “cads,” unintentionally afforded me a great deal of fun. One of them who wore a dress-suit, a diamond shirt-stud, and a watch-chain of most attractive proportions, insisted that he must have a <i>garçon</i> who spoke French, and this article being supplied, he commenced discussing the <i>menu</i> in the very worst French I ever heard, and in so loud a voice that he impressed those who did not know better, that he was some remarkable personage; when, however, he selected the vintage and brand of his <i>rouge vin</i> as he called it, I am afraid my outbreak of merriment was observed; it certainly was by the waiter, who felt at once relieved of the high strain of dignified reserve to which he had nerved himself, and fairly guffawed.

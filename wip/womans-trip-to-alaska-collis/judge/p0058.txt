There were then but three buildings of any pretensions in the place—a pretty Episcopalian church, a young ladies’ seminary, and a three-story brick store. The only object of interest was the Old Tacoma saw-mill, about two miles distant, and the little town surrounding it, excepting when the clouds lifted now and then to give a view of Mount Tacoma, which process of lifting, by-the-by, never took place while I was there at that time, so that I began to believe its beautiful lines and its snowy hood were all a myth.

Seven years have come and gone, and seven times five thousand people have come and stayed here since then; not only stayed here, but they have prospered and grown rich, and their wealth is now invested in banks, manufactories, storehouses, handsome shops, one charming hotel and many others of less pretensions, a university, two or three colleges, a dozen school-houses and as many churches, a beautiful theatre, and every thing that goes to make up urban life.

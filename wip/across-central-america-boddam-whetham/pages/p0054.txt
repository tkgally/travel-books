Another painful feature is the fact that these gewgaws, these artificial flowers, these mock pearls and cheap decorations have to be watched all day for fear of theft. All the pickpockets from the city choose this occasion for meandering through the crowds, and when they have secured all the available pocket-handkerchiefs and other trifles, for no one thinks of attending the fête with anything of value about him, they turn their attention to the offerings to the dead. These gentry must have taken a hint from Baron Hügel’s advice to botanists, “Put everything into your pocket.” At sunset everything is removed, and carefully packed away to do duty the following year.

Sunset in Guatemala is the signal for closing business, and one might almost think for going to bed, so quiet and empty do the [[corr steets|streets]] become; there is little inducement indeed to remain out of doors after dark, as the city is only lighted by a few oil lamps, and with the exception of a band which plays occasionally in the street in front of the President’s house, there are but few amusements to attract either strangers or residents. Fortunately for me I had the entrée to the hospitable houses of our Minister Resident, and of our Consul, and thus evenings which would otherwise have been dreadfully dull and tiresome, were delightfully pleasant and amusing.

At the forts flies the Union Jack, with the Company’s monogram upon it: amongst the officers there is an <i>esprit de corps</i> and a loyalty to their calling that is almost patriotic. To them rivals, like the smugglers of old, are ‘free traders,’ and the survival of the old-world phrase is eloquent of the very tale of the Company’s history.

And yet the stress of modern commercial life is making great, and in a sense devastating, changes in the old order. The system of appointing a new Commissioner every five years disturbed the continuity of the Company’s policy as little as the changes in the Viceroyalty of India now disconnects that Government, so long as that policy was determined by an annual conference of the chief officers. Those were days when great discretion was necessarily left to each officer in the discharge of his duties. Fraud, peculation, and dishonesty were unknown things in the Company’s history, and it must be obvious to any one that to secure trustworthy men, and trust them absolutely, would have remained the best security for a continued immunity.

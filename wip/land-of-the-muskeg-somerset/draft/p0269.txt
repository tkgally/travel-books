Père Morice was, of course, a Frenchman, but his English was irreproachable. It is something of a surprise to find a <i>savant</i> and a man of learning working amongst the Indians in a lonely Northern mission. But, judging by his congregation, it was evident that his talents were not thrown away. The Carrier Indians are immeasurably superior to their relations the Beavers. They build log-houses, and many speak English, and read books and a monthly review in the native tongue, printed in the syllabary which their priest has invented for them. This is one of the many extraordinary achievements of this prince of missionaries, who not only is his own editor, compositor, and printer, but has invented a most ingenious syllabary, which is easily learnt—so that Indians who have no idea what writing is, have been known to learn to read and write this language with perfect correctness after two or three days' instruction.

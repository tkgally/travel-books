# The Great Educators.

<i>A Series of Volumes by Eminent Writers, presenting in their entirety “A Biographical History of Education.”</i>

<i>The Times.</i>—“A Series of Monographs on ‘The Great Educators’ should prove of service to all who concern themselves with the history, theory, and practice of education.”

<i>The Speaker.</i>—“There is a promising sound about the title of Mr. Heinemann's new series, ‘The Great Educators.’ It should help to allay the hunger and thirst for knowledge and culture of the vast multitude of young men and maidens which our educational system turns out yearly, provided at least with an appetite for instruction.”

Each subject will form a complete volume, crown 8vo, 5<i>s.</i>

[[center]] <i>Now ready.</i>

ARISTOTLE, and the Ancient Educational Ideals. By T<sc>homas</sc> D<sc>avidson</sc>, M.A., LL.D.

<i>The Times.</i>—“A very readable sketch of a very interesting subject.”

LOYOLA, and the Educational System of the Jesuits. By Rev. T<sc>homas</sc> H<sc>ughes</sc>, S.J.

<i>Saturday Review.</i>—“Full of valuable information.... If a schoolmaster would learn how the education of the young can be carried on so as to confer real dignity on those engaged in it, we recommend him to read Mr. Hughes' book.”

ALCUIN, and the Rise of the Christian Schools. By Professor A<sc>ndrew</sc> F. W<sc>est</sc>, Ph.D.

<i>The Times.</i>—“A valuable contribution, based upon original and independent study, to our knowledge of an obscure but important period in the history of European learning and education.”

FROEBEL, and Education by Self-Activity. By H. C<sc>ourthope</sc> B<sc>owen</sc>, M.A.

<i>The Scotsman.</i>—“After a brief sketch of Froebel's career, Mr. Bowen deals exhaustively with his system of education.”

ABELARD, and the Origin and Early History of Universities. By J<sc>ules</sc> G<sc>abriel</sc> C<sc>ompayré</sc>, Professor in the Faculty of Toulouse.

<i>The Manchester Courier.</i>—“The account of the general spirit and influence of the early universities are subjects scarcely less interesting than Abelard's own career, and are all capably treated by the author, who has throughout dealt with an important subject in a brilliant and able manner.”

HERBART AND THE HERBARTIANS. By Prof. <sc>de</sc> G<sc>armo</sc>.

<i>The Saturday Review.</i>—“Remarkably clear, and will certainly be of the greatest service to the English student of the history of education.”

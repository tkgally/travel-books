When Ramsey joined us upon the mountain he had told us of the Chinook Indians who had camped near our outfit. It seemed that they had remained in the same place waiting for our return. Some small exchange of goods had taken place, so that we found that our breakfast, bear-meat, by the way, had been bought from them for a shirt. They had kept our party in meat for some days, always getting something in exchange; they had also been very anxious to sell furs, wishing to obtain tobacco, soap, and tea, but had been told that the big Oukimows were up the mountain, and that, as these things belonged to them, no bartering could be done until their return.

Many years ago, the Beaver Indian chief, Pouce Coupée, settled here with about five hundred followers, who constituted themselves a separate tribe. But a fever broke out amongst them, and when their chief returned from his hunt one winter he found their lodges empty and their bodies lying round the ashes of the camp-fires, half-eaten by the wolves and cayotes. Of the flourishing colony not one

We found that our camping-ground was not a good one, as the water was tepid, and so nasty that the tea was almost undrinkable. However, we had some cocoa (pig’s blood, the Indians call it), and so managed very well.

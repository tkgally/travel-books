Ginkakuji, or the Silver Temple, lies in a totally different direction from any of the other “sights,” but nothing could be more restful, after several days of sightseeing, than this hour’s ride mostly through the “truly rural” districts, past tea fields and farm-houses. We were finally led by an “embryo priest” with the face of a cherub to the house where Yoshimasa, eighth of the Ashikaga shoguns, with the assistance of Murato Shinkio, the priest, and Soami, the painter, evolved the Tea Ceremony or <i>Cha no yu</i>; then to the incense room, to admire the art treasures of kakemonos and print-books and lastly through the delightful garden. When offered tea we took no chances, mindful of our disgrace of the other day.

Fortunate indeed are those who reach Kyoto armed with an introduction to the charming principal of The Doshisha Girls’ School. Hers is the magical key that opens all doors and solves all problems. Marvellous are the excursions she plans and endless are the joys and surprises she arranges for these favored ones.

The old Torii sometimes on dry land, again with its feet in the water, is different from any other in Japan, the temple is charming and full of unusual interests, historical and otherwize. The sacred crows, the tame deer that eat out of your hand, the white horse which belongs to the goddess of the island and which you are allowed to feed, for a consideration, but not to touch, the innumerable peach, plum and cherry trees and the fascinating shops and booths where toys and other trifles are so cheap that it seems extravagant to leave anything behind, where all bewilderingly alluring.

One could be busy every moment for a week doing the various things supplied for the amusement of the traveler and pilgrim. One day could be delightfully spent climbing, or being carried a chair swung between two poles, to the top of the mountain; another in accompanying a pricst in a sam pan to the haunt of the sacred pigeons with food for these beautiful birds.

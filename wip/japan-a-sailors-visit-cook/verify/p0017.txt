Not being allowed to return home, he married a Japanese woman by whom he left one son and one daughter. There are some people now living at Yokohama who claim to be his descendants. The tomb for himself and wife was chosen by Adams and commands a splendid view of the navy yard, bay and surrounding country.

His burial expenses were borne by the people by whom he was adopted, and even at this late day on the 15th of each June the people of Tokio meet and celebrate the day in his honor.

Then the thoughts quietly advance apace until in July, 1853, we see our own Perry with his four war vessels off the coast of Yedo, and his return the following year, his fleet increased, and he ready to negotiate a treaty of friendship, which was successfully accomplished, and thus this far distant Eastern land united with the great American nation of the West, and commercial and friendly relations established which no power has yet had the temerity to attempt to dissolve.

At last, after patient waiting, one night during a heavy gale, the land of promise was sighted.

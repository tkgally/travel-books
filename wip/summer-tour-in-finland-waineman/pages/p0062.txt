That imperial journey, close upon a hundred years ago, in a country which for the greater part did not even possess roads, and which was so sparsely populated that for days the imperial party saw no human habitation, was an act of chivalrous self-denial that alone would have made Alexander I. beloved by his Finnish people. The memory of that noble Emperor is still kept green in the hearts of the people. Finns are slow to forget—either a kindness or a wrong.

Yet time can heal the deepest wounds. From the sunlit verandas of the Klippa, listening to the music of a Neopolitan orchestra, which played favourite old Italian airs with much dash and <i>bravura</i>, it was easy to believe that the lightheartedness of the South existed there, rather than the melancholy of the North. Everyone around looked bright and happy. A young girl close by laughed merrily, and her laugh seemed the embodiment of youth and happiness.

I looked away from her, across the water towards the beautiful trees of Brunspark—a park that had, in the middle of the last century, been transformed from an uninhabited rock to a charming summer resort for the inhabitants of Helsingfors. Numerous villas and swimming establishments were erected there, and a fine casino was opened in 1848. From that date to the early seventies, Brunspark was the height of fashion, but since then its glory has declined. The old casino still exists, but is used nowadays as a summer restaurant and open-air theatre, where operettas and variety entertainments are given, which are mostly patronized by Russian naval officers. There are always several cruisers and torpedo boats at anchor in the Helsingfors harbour during the open-water season. The town has recently so encroached on the boundaries of the park that it is almost difficult nowadays to discern the dividing line.

However, even the excitement of seeing a real live “lady member” could not prevent my astonishment at the easy rules that now held sway in a country where two years before the passage of these well-guarded portals was hedged about by scores of formalities. Now, apparently, the doors of Finland stood wide open for any one who desired to enter. Wonderful to relate, not even my passport was asked for, and during my whole trip in Finland it never left the corner of my pocket-book where it had been deposited on my departure from England.

I can honestly say that the most timid lady traveller could with the fullest confidence essay a tour in Finland should the present millennium continue. A tour round the Isle of Wight would not be fraught with less peril. Yet I believe the greater part of the globe-trotting population of the world is deterred, not so much by the distance of Finland, as by the thought that they may there be pursued by secret police, or come in for the tail-end of a bomb intended for some one else!—both things to be avoided. They most certainly formed no part of my summer experience in the year of grace 1907.

Helsingfors has quite the air of a Capital, with a big “C.” There is something imposing about it, something go-ahead and determined, that makes one understand the calm with which it withstood the recent political disturbances. The storm has passed and has apparently left no mark behind, unless it is the assurance that should another storm arise it would be faced as coolly.

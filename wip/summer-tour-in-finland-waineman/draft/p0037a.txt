The only historic episode of Helsingfors was the opening of a special Diet there on January 29, 1616, by the soldier-king Gustaf Adolf II. of Sweden, for the purpose of raising a new regiment. His Finnish subjects were so won with his chivalrous bearing and kingly manner, that a regiment was quickly formed from all ranks of society, who volunteered to follow the victorious Swedish banner. This was the famous regiment that was afterwards known as the “Iron Soldiers of Finland,” and became the most renowned of his army. Finnish people can well boast of the fighting capacities and endurance of her sons. They have fought right well throughout the centuries.

Gallant soldiers of Finland! Perhaps when the north winds touch the grass of forgotten battlefields, they will bring you a breath from the forests and surging rivers of the land wherein your cradle stood! Only those who know Finland, and who have read the annals of her soldiers, can understand, even in a slight degree, those bitter days in the fading hours of the nineteenth century, when for the last time the roll-call of a Finnish regiment was sounded. Even the Finnish Life Guards were not spared, and to-day in the museums strangers can see specimens of the uniforms and swords of the disbanded regiments.

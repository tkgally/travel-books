The treasury of the cathedral is not shown to the general public, except by permission of the chapter. It contains a very valuable collection of old silver, as well as a few objects of historical interest.

I wished I had had the time to linger over them, but I could only carry away a faint recollection of its treasures. For one moment I held the slender gold wedding-ring of Karin Månsdotter in the palm of my hand. The Queen’s fingers must have been very small, it was so tiny. Amongst the numerous antique silver ornaments I noticed a large silver baptismal bowl, with the date 1644 engraven on it; also a pair of gorgeous silver candelabra, 1654. A set of four two-branched silver candlesticks in the form of snakes were most uncommon, as well as an exquisite centre candelabra of massive silver, depicting an angel holding in her uplifted arms two outstanding branches for candles. At the foot of this was a gold plate, on which was inscribed, “Presented by Brigitta Horn, 1654.” But it is supposed to be of much older date than the year of its presentation.

These are only a few examples of this unique collection of old silver, which would take a whole chapter to describe. The magnificent candelabra are still used on the high altar on festival days of the Church.

[[cont]]Red River to St. Boniface, and took the 8 a.m. train on the Pembina Branch of the Pacific Railway. The Rev. Dr. Young accompanied us as far as Emerson, where he is stationed at present. At Dominion City (not much of a “City” yet—only three or four houses), we met the Rev. C. E. Blakeley, who is stationed at this point, and who is labouring diligently in his appointed field. The people are preparing to build a church, which is much needed. At Emerson we changed cars, and had some considerable delay passing baggage, &c. The town is some distance from the station, and we could not spare time to visit it; but Dr. Young reports it as a busy, prosperous place, and likely to be a point of considerable importance in the future.

On board the train I met Mr. Cubbit, of Bacton Abbey, Norfolk, England, one of the English Farm Delegates who, at the request of the Dominion Government, had visited this country with a view to ascertaining its advantages as a field for immigration. Mr. Cubbit proved a most intelligent and agreeable fellow-traveller, and his views in regard to immigration were worthy of attention.

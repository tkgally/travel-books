# XVII.

[[subtitle]] Birtle to Portage la Prairie.

LEFT Birtle on Monday morning, Sept. 10th, at 8 a. m. The storm has abated, but the weather is cold. The heavy rains have made the roads very “greasy,” and travelling is slow. The storm and exposure have been too much for “Bichon,” and he is quite sick to-day. We are passing through a fine prairie country,—rather low, perhaps, and with numerous ponds; but a considerable amount of good, rich soil. Mr. Marcus Smith, who explored this section of country in 1879, from Shell River to Birdtail Creek, speaks as follows:—“North of the Assinaboine the country rises gradually and imperceptibly to the eye up to the crown of the Riding Mountain, 2,000 feet above the level of the sea. The southern portion of this district is chiefly prairie; the soil good, but light in some places, and in others largely mixed with boulders.

Next day the weather was pleasant, and as a fair wind was blowing down the river, the waggon-sheet was transformed into a sail, which greatly aided our progress, and relieved us from the fatigue of rowing. During the day a small flock of wild geese were seen in a bend of the river, and after several unsuccessful attempts I managed to bag one. About 3 p.m. we reached Victoria, and were kindly received by the Rev. J. A. McLachlen and his estimable wife. The garden attached to the Mission house showed something of the capabilities of the soil. Potatoes, peas, beans, carrots, cabbages, tomatoes, &c., were thriving apace, while beds of pansies, and other favourites, showed Mrs. McLachlen’s care, and gave a delightful home-look to the surroundings.

This Mission, too, is a creation of the McDougalls. When George McDougall first visited the country in 1862, he found Mr. Woolsey at Smoking Lake, some 30 miles north from the river, where he had just erected a little cabin, and proposed to locate a Mission. Bro. McDougall was convinced that a spot on the bank of the river was a much better location, and resolved to begin a mission there, though strongly dissuaded by others on the ground that the place selected was right in the war-path of the Blackfeet.

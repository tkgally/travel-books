When at a point about midway between Forts Carlton and Pitt they had camped for the night. In the morning the half-breed went after the horses, and Mr. Skinner proceeded to arrange his tent, &c., on the buckboard. No one saw what followed, but when the messenger returned with the horses, he found him lifeless by the side of the buckboard, shot through the heart. The half-breed thoroughly terrified, instantly mounted one of the horses, and, without touching the lifeless body, fled back to Fort Carlton and gave the alarm. A party of men were at once dispatched to bring in the body; but in the meantime an English gentleman, the Hon. Mr. Percy, came by, returning with several others from a hunting expedition to the Far West. He took careful note of all the surroundings, and his evidence at the inquest subsequently held at Fort Carlton made clear the way in which the accident had occurred.

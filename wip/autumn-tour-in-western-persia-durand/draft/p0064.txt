Kashan was formerly a great town, famous for its tile work and other manufactures, but it is now chiefly kharábát, like Kum, and one was saddened by riding through miles of crumbling walls and houses and gardens. I could not help thinking of a pamphlet I had been reading about the terrible overcrowding in England, called, I think, <i>The Bitter Cry of Outcast London</i>. Every family in Kashan could have ground enough for a palace, and as much blue sky and open air as man could wish for.

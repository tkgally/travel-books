# Chapter VIII

W<sc>hen</sc> we had eaten our lunch, Mr. Taylor took us to see the bridge he was building, and explained to us how we were to get over the river.

It was not a very tempting prospect. The point chosen for the bridge is no doubt a good one, a bend in the river where the rocks on each side come comparatively close together, perhaps within sixty yards. There are some remains here of a former bridge of masonry, built in ancient days when the country was comparatively civilized. All over the Bakhtiari country such remains are to be found, bridges and forts and stone causeways, but it is always the same story, “Kadím ast, khaile kadím, hala kharáb shud”—“It is old, very old, now it is ruined.” The river roars over the boulders a hundred feet below.

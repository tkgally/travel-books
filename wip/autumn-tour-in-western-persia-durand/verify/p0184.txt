The tomb of Esther and Mordecai is at Hamadan, the ancient Ecbatana. Probably the Persian Court migrated in their day, as it does now, from “kishlak” to “yelak,” from winter quarters to summer quarters. Low-lying Shushan would have been the winter quarters, as it is now for some of the Lur tribes, and Ecbatana, at an altitude of 6,000 feet, would, though distant, have been pleasanter than Shushan for the summer. I am told that it was in fact the old summer capital.

From Daniel’s tomb we climbed up a winding pathway into the fort which the French excavators have built on the very mound of the palace of Ahasuerus. It is a triangular fort, with thick brick walls, in places thirty or forty feet high, and a tower in one angle, and a flagstaff.

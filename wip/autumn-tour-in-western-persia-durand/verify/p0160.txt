We stayed at Ahwaz two or three days, as my husband had work with Colonel Meade and the other officials, and with the merchants, this being the main port for our trade in Western Persia.

It is a curious place. There is a natural dam of rocks across the river, which is here perhaps half a mile broad; and in the rocks are flour mills, the water rushing through narrow channels in the rocks and turning mill wheels. The mills are, I believe, very old.

The village lies on the left bank of the river, an old village and a new village. The latter has grown up around the landing-place of Messrs. Lynch Bros.’ steamers. There is a good house where the Arab Shekh lives, and Lynch Bros. have built themselves another, and the bazaar is increasing fast.

The smoke of the wood fires in these village rooms was the most trying thing about them. Sometimes it made one’s eyes smart so as to be almost unbearable. The villagers do not seem to mind it at all, but they often have bad eyes, which may be due to the smoke.

Off our room was a kahdán, or granary, where the household kept firewood, and chopped straw for the animals. They strolled through our quarters to get what they wanted.

The room itself had walls of smooth mud with many recesses, and a fireplace at the end. In the recesses were ranged bottles of various shapes, some ordinary European bottles, some Persian flasks with round bodies and thin necks. On the walls were stuck as decorations bright coloured labels, English and Russian, such as the round gilt labels from sugar loaves, the wrappers of Russian candle packets, and small pictures of girls from match boxes.

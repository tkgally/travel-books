[[cont]] by name Julfa, and of the Araxes, the boundary between Russia and Persia.

Two swarthy individuals, clothed in long boots, wide dark-blue knickerbockers and jackets of the same colour lined with fur, and wearing gaudy neckerchiefs bound tightly round their shaven heads and hanging down behind their necks and ears, armed too with huge knives and a brace of pistols a-piece, assisted me from my carriage. They turned out to be Gholam Abdul Rezah Beg and Lazar, a Nestorian servant, sent down by her Majesty's Consul-General at Tabreez to convoy me thither. Lazar spoke a few words of English, and forthwith handed me a letter from the Consul congratulating me on my safe arrival at the frontier and expressing the hope that, as the weather was very severe and there was much snow, the brigands and wild tribes who frequent this part of the country and find impunity for their crimes by continually transferring their residence from one side of the Araxes to the other, would prefer keeping themselves warm at home to molesting my passage. A polite hint to me to keep my powder dry.

The post-house of Julfa is the best on the road from Tiflis. A good substantial building, evidently meant to impress the Persians with a favourable idea of Russian power and civilization. A few surrounding huts, built of sun-dried bricks, the only other habitations in the place, bring out the dignity of its appearance. I found it well warmed too, and calculated on having at last a comfortable night.

The fruit-gardens are still very extensive, and celebrated for their peaches and apricots; which, if I mistake not, were introduced from Persia to Europe. January was not, however, the month to appreciate their beauties and products, nor indeed to enjoy any out-door sight-seeing, for from the altitude of its position the winters are very severe and long. Luckily, beyond the novelty which a European must find in his first visit to a Persian town, there is not much to be seen. A fine mosque, called the Blue Mosque, from the colour of the varnished tiles in which it is cased, built by Abbas the Great early in the seventeenth century, but much injured by neglect and the earthquakes[[fnref *]] with which Tabreez has from time immemorial been continually visited; the ark or residence of the heir-apparent, a large frowning building situated in a commanding position near the centre of the town; the Great Maidan, a species of open square, in its front; and the spacious and well-filled bazaars: that is about all.

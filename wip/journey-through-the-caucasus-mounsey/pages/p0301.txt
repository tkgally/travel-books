What more can they want?” No sooner was his back turned than the royal ferrashes fell upon the suppliants and beat them within an inch of their lives. Some of them then sought asylum in the Mission stables, and we learnt from them that the settlement referred to by the King was a grant of 7,000<i>l.</i>, to which sum the original offer of 16,000<i>l.</i> had been reduced as soon as the government were relieved from the fear of further interference on our part. The King had ordered the 7,000<i>l.</i> to be paid in specie, and no doubt thought his orders had been executed. His ministers, however, could not make up their minds to what they considered a waste of good coin, and made the following arrangements with regard to it. First of all they decided that, in consideration of the protection afforded them, the Jews should make the King, <i>i.e.</i> his ministers, a present of 1,600<i>l.</i> They then proposed to relieve them of debts due to Mussulmans (most probably fictitious) to the extent of 1,800<i>l.</i>, and to restore clothes and property recovered by the Commissioner, that were really worth about 400<i>l.</i>, but were valued by the Persians at just double that amount.

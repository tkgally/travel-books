From this magnificent church I went to the famous Capuchin convent, erected under the auspices of the grand-master Verdala, in the year 1584. The lower part of the edifice is occupied by the church of the convent, and a spacious court, where we found a monk sweeping, who seemed unwilling that we should pass without giving him something, to pay, I think he said, for the lights in the convent. The upper storey is traversed by several narrow corridors lined with the cells of the monks, of which there are about sixty. The walls of the passages are covered with pictures representing several miracles performed by the saint of this Order. Over each cell is a small Latin inscription taken from the Holy Scriptures; and over the large entrances are prayers in Italian, intended for the use of pious Romanists, among which I noticed the following:—

<blockquote>
“O Maria che entraste nel mondo senza macchia; impetratemi la grazia di uscirne senza colpa.”[[fnref *]]
</blockquote>

[[fn *]] O Mary, who entered into the world without spot; grant me the grace to leave it without fault.

# Chapter V.

O<sc>n</sc> Tuesday, June 26th, at a quarter-past nine a.m., I left London from Waterloo Station in the train for Southampton, arriving at the latter place at a quarter-past twelve. Immediately on my arrival I had my baggage taken to the steamship <i>Nyanza</i>, and had each article properly marked and labelled and deposited on board. As she was not to leave till the next day, I made my way, as best I could, through a crowd of urgent hotel agents—each of whom seemed determined that I should go with him; thrusting into my hands cards in all languages—to Radley's Hotel, which, for respectability, accommodation, and civility of servants, is, I think, unsurpassed, perhaps unequalled, in Southampton. I was most politely shown to my room, which I found very comfortable. But as soon as I had taken my seat I was seized with violent headache and severe pain in the eyes—a complaint that affected me very much while in London. A dreadful feeling of despondency then came over me. The idea of falling seriously ill in this strange place haunted me like a gloomy spectre.

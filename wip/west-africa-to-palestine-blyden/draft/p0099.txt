Mr. Palgrave tells us that among the Wahabites, Mohammedan purists in Arabia, such is the heinousness of the sin of smoking, in the view of the religious chiefs, that any man, however elevated his position, is punished with a severe beating with rods if found guilty of it. The king’s brother, the heir apparent to the throne, was detected in the use of tobacco, and was publicly hoisted and beaten at his own palace gate for the offence. The Minister of Finance, who had also indulged in the practice, was beaten so severely that he died the next day. I wished a thousand times during that railway ride that the Wahabites had extended their vigorous laws and practices on the subject of tobacco into Egypt before my arrival there.

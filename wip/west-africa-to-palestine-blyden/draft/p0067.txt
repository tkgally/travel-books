[[cont]]But, alas! all this glory of vegetation, in a few short months, shall have passed away; nature will be stripped of her gorgeous beauty and appear naked and deformed; her winning and enchanting smile will be chased away by the lowering brow and stern aspect of storms and tempests. Earth has no spot of unchanging and permanent bliss. In heaven alone is there fulness of joy, uninterrupted and constant—

<verse>
‘<i>There</i> everlasting spring abides,
And never-withering flowers.’”
</verse>

The next morning, Monday 25th, I returned by the seven o’clock train to the Victoria Station, London; spent the whole day in making preparations for my departure to the East; went to the office of the Peninsular and Oriental Company, in Leadenhall-street, and purchased a ticket for a passage in their steamship <i>Nyanza</i>, to sail from Southampton on the 17th for Alexandria.

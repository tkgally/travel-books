At Bendo I had the pleasure of an interview with two of the brothers Tucker, native chieftains belonging to a powerful ruling family in that section of the country. They spoke English almost perfectly, having little of the native accent. I heard the elder of the two give a very intelligent exposition to some of the European traders, of a certain law which they had just enacted for the regulation of trade in their portion of the country, and of which the foreign traders were disposed to complain. I think the law forbade foreign traders from going for purposes of trade beyond a certain point up the river—a kind of Port of Entry law.

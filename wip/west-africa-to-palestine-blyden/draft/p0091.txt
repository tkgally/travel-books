There are separate chapels for each of the languages of the Order of St. John of Jerusalem. In the chapel of the Spanish knights are the mausoleums of four grand-masters—Martin de Redin, Raphael Cottoner, Perillos e Roccafeuil, and Nicholas Cottoner.

In the chapel of the knights of Bavaria there is a recess enclosed by a brass balustrade, said to be a substitute for one of solid gold, which, the guide said, was taken away by General Bonaparte in 1798. The rarities found in the public treasury and in the churches of the Order, together with their standards and trophies, were all carried away by the spoilers, but never reached the country for which they were destined: part of them perished in the <i>Orient</i>, which was blown up in the battle of Aboukir, and the rest were captured by the English in the frigate <i>Sensible.</i> From this chapel a staircase leads down to an underground apartment, in which are sarcophagi of twelve of the grand-masters, with marble covers, and full-length images of the deceased.

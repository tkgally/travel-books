In the centre of the hall stands the large porphyry coffer in which the embalmed bodies of the kings were deposited—evidently too large to pass through the narrow passages by which we entered. How was it brought to this place? The Arabs said it was put here while the pyramid was building. “While the pyramid was building!” thought I: “that takes us back to the days of Noah—anterior to Abraham.” What a wonderful sight!

Sir J. S. Wilkinson, one of the most competent authorities on all Egyptian questions, fixes the date of the construction of the pyramids at <sc>b.c.</sc> 2400. Job refers to them in chap. iii., 13, 14. “Now,” says he, “should I have lain still and been quiet, I should have slept; then had I been at rest, with kings and counsellors of the earth, which built desolate places for themselves.” “Desolate places,” in the Arabic translation of Smith and Van Dyck, is rendered “<i>Pyramids</i>” (<i>ahram</i>). The Coptic version is said to give “monuments.” It is clear that Job saw or knew of the pyramids. Perhaps Abraham during his sojourn in Egypt, Jacob, Joseph, and his brethren, Moses and Aaron, stood and wondered at these structures.

I was very anxious to see the interior of this great octagonal structure, which, with its immense dome, is always so prominent a figure in every picture of Jerusalem. It is called Kubbet-es-Sakhrah—the Dome of the Rock—because it is built over a very large rock regarded as sacred by Jews, Christians, and Mohammedans. On entering I was surprised to see nothing but a large bare limestone rock immediately under the dome, surrounded by a railing so as not to be touched by visitors. This mosque was built, not as is generally supposed, for the common purposes of Moslem worship, but to enclose this extraordinary rock—and nothing more. On this rock, the Jews say, Abraham sacrificed the ram instead of Isaac. (Gen. xxii., 13.) It is the stone which Jacob used for his pillow, and on which the angel stood when about to destroy the city. (2 Sam. xxiv., 16.) The ark is buried here.

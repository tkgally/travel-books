# Chapter XV.

[[image: decorative initial Z with a small figure climbing it]]

ŹABIE was, I think, as regarded scenery, the best that that country could do. The village was seven quadrate miles in extent—Austrian miles—positively almost a small country. It lay for the most part in a rich valley, through which a river came serpentining, washing the edges of as many hayfields as possible. The houses were, some of them, in elevated positions; others seemed to have rolled down like stones to the river’s edge. The great hills, seeming to hold hands like children at play, stood in a circle to look on at what Źabie was doing. They verged from pine-black to the green of poloninas, and further to the greys and blues of the far mountains, the mountains where I was soon going—Czerna-góra.

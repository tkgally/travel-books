A lump of bread and some of the friable Krimsen cheese was in my sack; also, some tea, maize-meal, and potatoes. My temper was even, and the sun at his best. The weather and the world were with us, and little by little as we approached them, the big mountains lifted corners of their delicate veils and allowed their rough old features to show through.

To be alone with a great deal of scenery is intoxicating; I had to sing when the solitudes grew quite assured. I was sorry, because the little snakes and lizards were asleep, sweltering on the grey rock faces, and I daresay it annoyed them; but I had to.

Then it came that I must get off my horse, and feel the ground myself at first-hand. As I walked, there was a springy resistance to my feet—was it that the earth heaved itself beneath my sandals, and impelled me forwards?

Dear Cracow! Never, never again it seems to me, shall I come so near to the deathless hidden sentiment of Poland as in those first moments.

It would be no use to tell her to take heart, that there may be brighter days coming, and so forth: Lemberg may feel so, Lemberg that has the feelings of any other big new town, the strength and the determination; but Cracow’s day was in the long ago, as a gay capital, a brilliant university town full of princes, of daring, of culture, of <i>esprit.</i> She has outlived her day, and can only mourn over what has been and the times that she has seen; she may be always proud of her character, of the brave blood that has made scarlet her streets, but she can never be happy remodelled as an Austrian garrison town, and in the new Poland—the Poland whose foundation stones are laid in the hearts of her people, and that may yet be built some day—in that new Poland, there will be no place for aristocratic, high-bred Cracow.

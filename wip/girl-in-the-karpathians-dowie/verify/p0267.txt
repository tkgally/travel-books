Certainly it was a wonderful little Kettle, but—what was there for an artist? Lonely, unknown, far from the madding crowd truly; but what was there to catch hold of? Well, if I do not know that one could paint great pictures there, I am very sure that one could dream great dreams.

The lack of water weighed and preyed upon me very much. I suppose the snow was “all right,” but it was not so refreshing, however much you ate of it, as a single cup—I had a silver quaich with me—of pure water would have been, and we could not be certain of bringing up near a spring at evening.

As to sleeping out o’ nights, I can only plead that this is to me no hardship, so long as it does not rain too much: it does not require colossal fortitude, or courage, or any of these big words. By the way, what may courage be? and what fortitude? A set of circumstances presents itself, and you, in the middle of those circumstances, which are partly of your own bringing about, take the one simple sensible course open to you.

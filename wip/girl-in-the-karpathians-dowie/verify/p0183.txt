Once again we raked the whortleberry hill, then heroic and resigned, made our way over a grass meadow, up and up the stone path at considerably slower time than we had come down it.

The painter met me with a severe brow and savage inquiry in his voice. The watch had to be found—if he stayed a week on that confounded mountain he would find it—that was the sum of what he put into the next hot ten minutes.

Nothing could persuade him otherwise. Let me remain with the baggage—he would search with Paraska, who would show him where I had been. “Make us late? Very likely. No doubt we should not reach Źabie; but we should arrive exactly where we should arrive!” In vain I explained the utter hopelessness of the whole thing, threatened to continue my journey with the man and horses, having offered a reward—to the empty air—of ten or twenty gulden to whomsoever found the dear turnip.

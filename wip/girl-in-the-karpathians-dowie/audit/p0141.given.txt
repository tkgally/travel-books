As I was passing in I felt a hand at my girdle, on my watch chain or knife-handle; I had just time to note that if was the Jewish lad’s, when a smothered word behind me, and the ringing sound of a “clout” on the side of his head, told me that the painter had protested against this impertinence. I explained in vain that the fellow had acted from the reasonless curiosity of his nation, and had not meant to steal. To the painter it was a “Frechheit,” and he expressed deep satisfaction at having given him “what for.” Then we went in.

The circus was a good one—the horses well fed, the children sharp, clever, and uncowed. But Mikuliczyn as assembled was more interesting by far. The jewish womanhood was marvellous in summer-hats and thin pale-coloured dresses. A great many people I had never seen before were present.

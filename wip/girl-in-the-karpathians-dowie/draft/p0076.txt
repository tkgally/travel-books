I learnt more Polish than chess in watching them, for though the postmaster conducted his daily business in German, he conducted chess in Polish—in a great deal of Polish. He would repeat one expression about twelve times in a low chuckling voice, but quite distinctly, while thinking out and making a move. During the long length of a minute it would be “Poczekaj” in a self-satisfied, humming tone; and when I had learnt this phrase never to forget it, the painter would fall upon him, and he would emit a little sad, high cry, like a frog’s note, as he watched his favourite piece, the “Läufer” (Bishop) whipped off the board. His large blue nails glittered like round flat shields on his brown hand, as it hovered revengefully above the painter’s Rook, and he would fall to crooning “Tak jest” nine or ten times, when he compelled his opponent to move his Queen.

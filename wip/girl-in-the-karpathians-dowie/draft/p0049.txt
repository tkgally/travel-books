Then some one hung on to the off-side, and I stepped into the saddle; for mounting in the ordinary way was out of the question with a thing so near the ground.

The Jew proceeded to make a difficulty about the price, although this had been fixed at four shillings before the saddling; my late driver, however, came out and swore at him in an explanatory way, and after the handsome peasant had got his kreutzers and his last kiss from my hand, we started forward over the great rambling pine-bridges that crossed the river.

A supreme power of balance kept me in the saddle, and I began to “find” <i>Doppelmäuser’s</i> trot at last. When whipped he kicked savagely and got his little head a-twisting, and rubbed the Pelham on his ankle, and in the road, and had a world of ways,—for all of which I could have grown to love him in time.

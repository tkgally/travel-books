You are brought face to face with things which civilisation saves its women the need of looking at, and consequently the need of thinking about. I don’t mean only chicken-killing; but the discipline of such experience, especially for excitable persons, is excellent, and teaches a repose and a calm philosophy in the face of distressing occurrences which would do much to hasten the extinction of the fussy and mouse-screeching woman, a type of which the world is at last a little weary.

The servant-girl was called Ulanno, but Olena is, I am told, the correct spelling of the name. She was a farm-hand as well, and deserves a few words all to herself. She was the roughest-voiced, roughest-laughed, roughest-mannered person I ever knew; when in the morning I looked from my window and shouted “Daj Wody,” “Bring water,” she nodded her head vigorously under her dirty yellow and red kerchief, and I saw her bare red-brown legs springing up the wood steps to the house, and her two tomato-coloured aprons, one in front, one behind, narrow, skimpy, and tight, disappearing round the door corner.

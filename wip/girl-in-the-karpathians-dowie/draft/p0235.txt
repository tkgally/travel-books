But Maryjka was only a delightful accessory, not the central figure in the room; that was her old grandmother, Feodor’s mother, sitting at the hand-loom, weaving in three colours, white, black, and grey, with a big shining shuttle, one of the barred blankets, whose fellows, loose to begin with, but thickened upon such a water-mill as I had seen, hung from the rafters beside the spiky ropes of the maize-cobs, plaited together with their own leaves. Upon the bed-bench in the darker corner by the stove sat Dmytro, the grandfather, working the old blackened shuttle-filling machine with a stick and a piece of string. What a fine figure his was, a figure made fine by a fine character, a character built up by a sound, sane, and simple life.

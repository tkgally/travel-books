In the next was a dilapidated wardrobe with books, and two beds both with the big rough blankets, patterned in coloured diamonds, upon them—not attractive: I walked drearily through to the third.

This the last had three windows, which looked upon the garden, and I concluded at once that I would house myself there, throwing down what baggage I had on my shoulder in a castorless arm-chair of the “this suite greatly reduced” order. A settee against the wall, covered in faded rosed-over tapestry, upon which the moths were busy, and an <i>oval, rosewood what-not!</i> The character of an inventory, which the last few paragraphs have assumed, culminates here: an <i>oval, rosewood what-not</i>—I repeat the phrase, because I love this suggestive terminology. Let none confess that he does not recognise a table under the title of “what-not,” for that would be to say he has never gone through the catalogue of a furnishing warehouse; and there is much matter worthy of remembrance in such.

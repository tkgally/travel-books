The pathetic whine in her voice when she addressed me was the only alternative to the <i>allgemeine Schimpferei</i> (Universal Scolding), which long years of dealing with the peasantry had taught her. She had her moments of amusement too, when shrill screams of vulgar laughter announced that she was playing with Paulinchen, the four-year-old infant of Mathilde and the man who went to sleep drunk once in the snow, and never awoke to repeat this piece of carelessness. I used to be very sorry for Paulinchen, though she was being early hardened, and her wonderful courage enhanced every day, for the Proszę Pani had a huge muscle, and pulled the poor child about unmercifully.

Mein Herr, the head of the family, was a big, fair, soft man, who by fits and starts interested himself indolently in fishing. At one time he was never without a cast of flies round his green-black hat and a rod leaning against the house corner, within two minutes’ walk of which you could always find him.

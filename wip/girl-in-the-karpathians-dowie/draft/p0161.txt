# Chapter XII.

[[image: a young peasant standing in a sheepskin waistcoat and boots, one hand on his chest, beside a rock]]

D<sc>uring</sc> the day I tried to write and read in my room, but the way in which the peasants kept wandering in and sitting down, and having to be offered cigarettes, made study or amusement alike impossible. They had no idea of knocking at a door, or waiting for an invitation to be seated, and as I was not paying for my room, I did not see any way to keep them out.

On the whole, too, they were very <i>naif</i> and funny. Wasyl I soon saw to be the village beauty <i>par excellence.</i> He was a slim, bent, lanky young man of peculiarly idle build, dressed in top boots, preternaturally unsullied linens, and a short sheepskin laid round his shoulders, his arms being rarely through the sleeve-holes.

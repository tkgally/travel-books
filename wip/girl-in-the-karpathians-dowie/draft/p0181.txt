We found the whortleberry bed and the tree that I had sat beside. I searched the place where I had tripped and fallen on my right side, and the place where I had tripped and fallen on my left side; the place where I had tripped and fallen on my back, as well as the place where I had tripped and fallen on my front, along with other places where I had fallen variously, for the hill was steep, and whortleberry bushes slippy to the feet.

Everywhere my <i>mäderl</i> searched also—nowhere could I find my watch. Somewhat dashed, we went down the further hill to the spot where my horse had fallen with me. Upon the rocks we could see the scars made by the horses’ shoes. The watch had been very insecurely fastened, and I could readily see why it had gone loose; very easily it might have slipped up from my belt and gone amissing when my tiny white mare scrambled to keep her four tiny white feet.

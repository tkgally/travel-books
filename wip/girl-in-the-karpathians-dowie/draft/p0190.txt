The chatter of these persons, so little of which was comprehensible, irritated me; and I told the painter I must have a rest simply to get rid of them, and sat down beside the little white mare, who was rocking upon her feet like a fainting lady.

The peasants’ voices and their persons were absorbed by the growing night ahead of us, then, quite silent and commentless, we did another steady hour. At last, “It’s no good!” cried the painter. “You must put up somewhere! There is an hour and a half more, and these beasts can’t manage it. Besides, you would arrive so late, you’d find no room!”

“But where am I to put up?” said I.

“Anywhere!” answered he, lighting and unconcernedly puffing at his thirty-second cigarette. We stopped by a wooden bridge to consider.

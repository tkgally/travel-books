It is better to imitate Nebuchadnezzar—if you must imitate any one, and some people certainly must—and go out to grass for six weeks at least by yourself. Give your whims a loose rein, follow the promptings of that queer live soul in you which always retains its affinity to simpleness and green-growing things, and be prepared to be thought very odd when you come back.

You will have acquired a calm smile, an ability to suffer fools gladly, which will stand you in good stead. For, though with slight comment, loneliness is permitted to a man, it seems the opportunity for immense chaff to a woman. A public resents fiercely the conclusion that a woman, a fairly light-hearted young woman more especially, is happy alone and from choice. A preference of Nature to human nature, of green trees to people, and of her own reflections to their witless comments, is an oddity, a whimsical eccentricity which may be smiled upon, but which requires solid demonstration and justification before it be accepted and believed in.

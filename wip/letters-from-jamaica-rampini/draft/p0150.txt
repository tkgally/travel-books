Manchester has always been the head-quarters of the Moravian Mission in Jamaica, and to the praiseworthy efforts of the clergy of that denomination is to be attributed much of the improved social position of the peasantry in the parish.

One of our first visits was to the Moravian settlement of Fairfield.

We left Mandeville about twelve o’clock. The morning had been dull and cloudy, with little or no breeze. But towards mid-day a gentle gale sprang up, and shortly afterwards the sun shone forth in unclouded brilliancy. The road for the first few miles was rough and stony. Recent rains had worn it away into deep ruts and spring-breaking holes, and our carriage-wheels were often impeded by the luxuriant growth of grass and “bush” which told of the infrequency of traffic over the solitary highway. But every tree and rock was clothed with verdure. Ipomæas dangled from the boughs of the orange trees. Golden, white, and purple creepers—a mazy thicket of colour—bordered the road like a hedge. The night-blowing cereus, with closed, tassel-like flowers, dragged its fleshy stems over every broken wall and limestone boulder. By degrees the landscape opened, and through the breaks in the bush we caught glimpses of the far-off sea, misty and hazy in the distance.

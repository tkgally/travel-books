After having vainly attempted to elude the ceremony of taking the oath by kissing his thumb instead of the book, or by not touching the Bible with his lips—in either of which cases all that he may say thereafter is not accounted perjury by the negro code of morality—he is at length successfully sworn, and a dialogue very much like the following ensues:—

<i>Clerk of Petty Sessions</i> (to witness)—Now, sir, where do you live?

<i>Witness</i>—Sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i> (more loudly)—Where do you live, sir?

<i>W.</i>—Live at Friendship district.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What parish?

<i>W.</i>—Cumberland.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Do you know the prisoner?

<i>W.</i>—No, sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What! don’t know the prisoner? Did you never see him before?

<i>W.</i>—Oh yes, sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Then why did you tell me you did not know him? Now be careful! Do you remember the 10th July of this year?

<i>W.</i>—Can’t rightly say the day, sa! Last week, Monday—

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Stop, sir! You must try to remember the date.

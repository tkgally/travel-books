<i>C. P. S.</i> (to witness)—Answer the question. Were you in a state of intoxication?

<i>W.</i>—No; sa! Never did sich a thing!

<i>P.</i> (to witness)—Did you have any evidence that I did use that low word to you?

<i>W.</i> (calling to some one in court)—Hi! Susan Powell! Mrs. Powell!

<i>P.</i>—Who and who was in present?

<i>Bystander</i>—I deny the question!

<i>Constable</i>—Shut your mouth!

<i>First Magistrate</i> (to prisoner)—Do you wish to ask the witness any more questions?

<i>W.</i> (appealing to Magistrate)—My respectable worship—

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Hold your tongue, sir! (Addressing prisoner)—Do you hear what the Magistrate asks you—“Do you wish to put any further questions to the witness?”

<i>P.</i> (sulkily)—Don’t bother asking him any more questions, for him won’t speak de truth.

<i>First Magistrate</i> (to witness)—You can stand down.

Witness is retiring, when the prisoner calls to him, George Edwards, did I—

<i>First Magistrate</i>—Call the next witness.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—That’s the case, sir!

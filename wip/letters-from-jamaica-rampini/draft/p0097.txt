Her apron is embroidered with coloured threads and decorated with texts of Scripture and moral precepts. Here are a few examples. On one we read—

<verse>
Purge me with hyssop
For I will be clean.
</verse>

[[right]] M. T.

On another, below a cocoa-nut tree in purple worsted—

<verse>
Open to thee is Paradise:
Go in and take thy place.
</verse>

A third bears—

<verse>
I know that my Redeemer lives,
With Joy the sweet assurance.
</verse>

A fourth—

<verse>
I am the Rose of Sharon
And the Lily of the Valy.
</verse>

But perhaps the most absurd of any we ever saw is the following, which tells its own story:—

<verse>
Once the world was all to me,
But now it turns its back on me,
O you freckle-hearted young man!
I lay my eye at Jesus’ feet
Till I find my secret love.
</verse>

[[right]] A. P. 1870.

Bringing up the rear is a troop of pot-bellied children—the girls with their clothes tied up high above the knee to give them ease in walking, the boys with fowls under their arms, or carrying wooden trays full of fruit and vegetables.

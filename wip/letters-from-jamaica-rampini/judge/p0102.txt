<i>W.</i> (with much hesitation)—It was tbe 15th July.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What do you say, sir? Do you remember the 10th July?

<i>W.</i> (meekly)—Yes, sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What year?

<i>W.</i> (evidently much depressed)—1899, sa!

Laughter in Court.

<i>Two Constables</i> (at once)—Si-lence!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What, sir!

Witness looks hopelessly puzzled, and does not answer.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Was it this year?

<i>W.</i> (much relieved)—Yes, sa! This year, sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Then why did you not say so at first? Go on, sir!

These preliminaries disposed of, the witness proceeds tolerably enough.

<i>W.</i>—Last week, Monday, I was coming from my ground. Jus’ as I come to Mr. Abraham’s grog-shop—

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Is that at the cross roads?

<i>W.</i>—Yes, sa! Jus’ as I come to Mr. Abraham’s grog-shop, I meet up with Henry Thomas, the prisoner. I had a crocus (Osnaburg) bag with bread-kind on my back and my machette in my hand. Says I to him, “Good evening, breda” (brother). He tole me, “Good evening.” So I says to him, “Good evening.”

<i>Prisoner</i> (interrupting)—What does I says to you?

<i>W.</i>—Hi!

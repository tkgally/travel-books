Over and above the lovely motive—which shows the imperial figure bending forward, divesting herself of her sandal in token that in Athens she is henceforth to abide—there is a sympathy with womanhood as the modern world is recognizing it which is commonly conspicuous by its absence from ancient art. Passion in divorce as it existed from tenderness was rightly seen to be a subject unmeet for ideal treatment; and the stalwart maids and matrons, born to yield warriors to the State, were rather awe-inspiring than pleasing. But into this little Nike (little by reason, not of its manner, but its scale) there has stolen a softness, a truly feminine element, suggestive of a new power; and, seen in contrast with all around, one cannot but wonder how it came there.

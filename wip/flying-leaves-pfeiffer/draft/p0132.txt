The immediate foreground is little interesting to the eye. The earth, of a pale ashy hue, is covered with short tussucks of grass, the colour of which is a sickly yellow, with plentiful weeds interspersed. Seen thus in passing, the bosom of mother earth does not in this region appear to overflow with those daintily devised floral forms which move us to passionate love and sympathy in some countries of which the main beauty is less incontestible. Of course, I have not seen it in spring, which is nature’s most mirthful season, or in summer, which is its most consolatory; but it is difficult to fancy that this pallid soil laughs out as our own does in buttercups and primroses, and I can nowhere on the hills detect traces of the heather with which the Scotch moors are at this time jubilant. That clean, sweet, peaty soil of the Highlands which bears it, I have more than once gathered up in my hands and inhaled with a sort of rapture. But my conscience smites me as I write, or, rather, as I try to write and cannot, for the beauty which makes appeal on either side.

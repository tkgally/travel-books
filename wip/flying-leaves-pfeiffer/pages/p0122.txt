While E—— and his brother are thus engaged, the children at school, and the house-mother busy in her multifarious tasks, I am generally to be found in the parlour, feeling to myself just as useless a person as the “queen” who, “in the pantry, was eating bread and honey.” The occupation of Mrs. E—— as contrasted with that of Mrs. John is something pitiful. Mrs. John having washed and dressed, breakfasted and despatched the children, has been making bread, and is now churning butter. She will soon be cooking the dinner, and will afterwards be dressed for company either at home or abroad. I have never heard her utter a hasty word, or seen so much as a cloud on her face, always self-forgetful and occupied with others, and seemingly pleased with the good things which fall unearned into their laps. But I know that she is often very weary, and sometimes anxious lest strength should fail for all that is required of her before Marie is old enough to lighten her duties. When I say that all the work that I have here found to do is the making of clothes for Adèle’s doll, some degree of self-contempt on my part will be understood. To judge from what I am told (which I do not believe), there is not a stitch wanting, a tape or button to be renewed, for anything in human shape about the house, excepting only this puppet that arrived with us.

The next island was St. Lucia, distant only forty-five miles, and therefore we passed it in the night. We landed the mails at its chief town—Castries—but had left it a long time before daybreak. From St. Lucia, one hundred and eight miles took us to Barbadoes, but being rather late in starting from Castries we did not arrive at Bridgetown till 5 p.m. Barbadoes, or “Little England,” as the “Bims” proudly call it, is a low, ugly island of limestone-looking formation, entirely planted and cultivated throughout with sugar canes. The houses and barracks are remarkably well built, the roads excellent, and the whole island highly civilised; but nothing picturesque or interesting is to be seen. The “Bims” are noted for being the most conceited people in the world, and pride themselves on being the most loyal, but one hardly knows in what their loyalty consists. They have a peculiar twang when speaking, which makes them conspicuous and at once known all over the West Indies. There were a great many ships in the harbour. We went on shore at once, and landed near the barracks, and were met by Major B., who took us to his house to see his wife, who had come out from England with us.

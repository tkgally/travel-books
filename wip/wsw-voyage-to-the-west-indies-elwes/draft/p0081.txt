The Rio Cobre still retains its Spanish name; but I was much puzzled to see that, on the milestones by the side of the road, the distance was measured from “Santiago de la Vega.” The driver could not explain it, and knew of no place of that name; but I found that it was the original name of Spanish Town, and that it was still used in all official documents, or those sort of things. The English are fond of altering foreign names, and usually do not improve them; but there are so many Santiagos in Spain, Chili, Mexico, and Cuba, that there may be some excuse for changing this.

Soon after leaving the Bog Walk, we came to the large village of Linstead, where we were to breakfast and to rest the horses. The “Tavern” was clean, but had not much in it besides furniture; however, we sent out and got bread, eggs, coffee, and milk, and so did well. Leaving Linstead, we drove on towards the mountains, along the sides of one of which, Mount Diavolo, the road to the Moneague ascends. We passed along a very pretty road, and in one place the bamboos were most magnificent.

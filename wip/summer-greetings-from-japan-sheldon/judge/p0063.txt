We went through the big Engineering Building, including Architecture; then through the Department of Geology with its museum; afterwards the Chemistry Building, where the Professor performed an experiment with <i>radium</i> for me. We saw the physical laboratory, the professor of which told me he had been a pupil of Mr. Chaplin's. They make most of their physical apparatus, in a <i>shop</i> which I saw. It was especially novel to see a building set apart for teaching how to make weapons, armor, cannon, etc. <i>In that</i> they are surely without a rival. There is one whole new building going up, just for naval architecture. Most interesting of all was the Seismological Building, where they do nothing but study earthquakes. Prof. Mitsukuri showed me the instrument by which they record them, and <i>then</i> let me see the record of the San Francisco earthquake, all accurately written out by these instruments, as the vibrations had crossed the Pacific to Tokyo, and also a <i>second</i> record as they had passed across America, Europe and Asia, and so reached Japan, in the reverse direction. They have Japan all mapped out in earthquake records, as we have America in records of geology. Prof. M. says if they have another big earthquake here while he is at work in his laboratory he is quite sure it will be the end of the laboratory and <i>him, too.</i> They cannot get earthquake insurance in Japan, I understand.

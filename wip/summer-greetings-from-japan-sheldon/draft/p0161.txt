There are four self-supporting churches there. Dr. DeForest showed me a Japanese bath-tub, the first I had seen. So far the missionaries have impressed me favorably. It was raining again yesterday. When I offered the young man who had been interpreter fifty cents he declined to receive it. He said he was connected with the hotel; so I told him to give it to Dr. DeForest for the famine fund. I had a whole coupé and boy to myself yesterday to Nikko. This time there was no dinner. I had miscalculated. Finally I tried to get some boiled eggs. A man brought me some, and I took them in triumph, thinking I was safe till night—but it turned out they were raw. However, the boy warmed them in some hot water, and I was glad to stay my hunger with them. (I'm traveling now with flea-powder.) The bed last night beat anything for humps I have ever experienced. Well, I will stop now, and take a stroll in the rain. This is the beautful and famous Nikko. In five days I shall be on the journey starting homeward. This letter may get there after I do.

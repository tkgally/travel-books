Thus was the tedium of my second trans-Pacific voyage beguiled. Everything favoured a pleasant passage—a few interesting fellow-passengers (not <i>always</i> to be met with on an ocean voyage), a ship of unsurpassed comfort, and, above all, most propitious weather and an unruffled sea. The only circumstance that detracted from the entire enjoyment of the passage, was that, as we approached our destination, we found ourselves enveloped by the smoke of a burning forest, which is so apt to impede the navigation in the Gulf of Georgia, and which hangs about in that region for days after the fire has burnt itself out. Our arrival was delayed by it about twenty-four hours, and the rumour reached Vancouver that we had gone on the rocks. No risk, however, was run—the <i>Empress</i> too cautiously felt her way to come to grief, till a breeze suddenly arose and in a few moments cleared off the smoke, and Mount Baker in all his magnificence stood forth to our view. We were welcomed by practically the whole town of Vancouver, which takes a special pride in its great White Liners.

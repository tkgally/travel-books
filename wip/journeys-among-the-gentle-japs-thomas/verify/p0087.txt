After closing the shutter again, and turning on the electric light, I found that the clearance had been fairly complete, and the one or two that still remained gave me little trouble. The mosquito is almost unknown at Tonosawa, but in its stead there is another little pest during July and August, a fly called <i>bûyô</i>, the sting of which is said to be very painful. But I was fortunate in only knowing him by repute.

The Japs, like all Orientals, are early risers, and just as I felt that refreshing sleep, for some hours wooed in vain, was at length come on, I heard the shutters being thrown back, and the cheery “<i>Ohayo!</i>” of O Ewa San or her sister (I could not make out which) saluting me. There was nothing to be done but to be content with such sleep as I had had, and to jump into my clothes and begin the day’s work.

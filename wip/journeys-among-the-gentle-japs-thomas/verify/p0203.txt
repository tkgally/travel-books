I was quartered in the spacious house of the Rev. Mr. Bryan, who, like many missionaries, was spending a hard-earned holiday during the hot season among the hills of Arima. Owing to his absence I was doomed to pass the time at Hiroshima without coming in contact with a single European or American. I walked much about its intricate streets, seeing all that was thus possible of native life unaffected by European influence, but a fellow-countryman was nowhere to be seen. Still, my wants were never better ministered to than they were by Mr. Bryan’s native caretaker and his wife, though the former knew but little English and the latter not a word.

From Hiroshima I made an excursion to the sacred island of Miyajima. The first part of the journey was by <i>ricksha</i> along an excellent road about ten miles to Ajina. My <i>ricksha</i>-man wore the usual hat, which has been compared to a washing-basin, a tight-fitting drawers of dark-blue cloth, with his name and number in grand characters on his back.

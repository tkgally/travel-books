# Chapter III.

[[subtitle]] Yokohama.

[[subtitle]] Landing in Japan—Custom House examination—The <i>Jinrickisha</i>—Coolies—Some scions of the nobility—Fares—The Queen’s English in Japan—Old Japan at Yokohama—Hotels—The Bluff Population—Clubs—Public hall—An American “star”—Churches—Seamen’s mission room—Currency—Mysterious notes—Railways—Carriages—The natives as fellow-passengers—Commissariat—Fares—Luggage—Officials—“Treaty ports”—Passport regulations—A recent change.

W<sc>e</sc> are on Japanese soil. The examination at the Custom House is a mere formality. The attempt to smuggle opium alone can cause any real trouble. Even Japanese officials, the pink of politeness and courtesy as they are, and ever with a smile of welcome for the foreigner who lands on the shores from which he was so long excluded, can scowl at the sight of the contraband drug. Landing in the Far East is not the ordeal it is in the East, notably at Jaffa.

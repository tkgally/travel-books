[[cont]]<i>Kanranjô</i>, are shown, on payment of a small fee, the weapons and the armour, with other relics, of the Rônins. There are also to be seen statuettes of them carved in wood, with coloured faces and lacquered dresses. Each tomb, especially that of Oishi Kuranosuké, the leader of the band, has its tribute of water and incense laid on it by admiring pilgrims. Visiting cards also abound. When I made my own pilgrimage to the spot, I went on foot, and made my way as best I could through interminable streets with the help of a not very elaborate map. The consequence was that I had to make many inquiries, and to follow very freely the advice to “ask a p’liceman;” but though the name of the temple did not always give a clue to the place I was seeking, I found that <i>Shi-jû-shichi</i> (Forty-seven Rônins) was quickly understood.

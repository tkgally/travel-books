Miyajima is one of the <i>San-kei</i>, or “three chief sights” of Japan in native, if not in foreign estimation, and has from ancient times been regarded as sacred. It supports a population of about three thousand, who are mainly fishermen and image-carvers. Agriculture is unknown in the island. There are several charming valleys, with the usual tea-houses commanding the most lovely views. A few deer are also found, which, as at Nara, feed out of the hands of passers-by. What gives Miyajima its sanctity is its famous Shintô temple, the <i>torii</i> of which, standing some distance out in the sea, is a favourite subject of Japanese art. The temple itself is partly built over the sea on piles.

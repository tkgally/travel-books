They reside either in the building or in licensed boarding-houses. They wear a special uniform, which is a cross between an English academical costume and a military dress. The discipline is semi-military, and is probably stricter than that of any university in Europe or America. Every student must provide two sureties to be responsible for his good behavionr while he is in <i>statu pupillari.</i> He has to be indoors early; he must neither drink intoxicating liquors nor smoke in his room. Whether it is the cause or the effect of the close surveillance to which he is subjected, he is said to be rather rude in his manner, especially towards foreigners, differing very much in that respect from other classes of the community. When, a few years ago, it was found necessary to impose such rigid discipline, a mutiny broke out among the students, over a hundred of whom were expelled. The University expenses, as given in the calendar, range from a maximum of 12 <i>yen</i> to a minimum of 7½ <i>yen</i> a month (about £1 10<i>s.</i> to about £1).

According to the same authority, there are 8,000 Shintô temples in Japan, attached to which there are about 20,000 priests and attendants. A few years ago there was granted annually for the support of those shrines a sum of £58,000. Formerly a Department of Public Worship dealt with such matters. They are now under the charge of the Home Secretary.

The priests are said to be, as a body, men of considerable culture and ability. Though I came in contact with many at the temples, I was never fortunate enough to meet with one who knew English. I have heard them highly spoken of as preachers, and certainly, if one may judge from translations of Japanese sermons given by Mitford, they have a quaint and telling way of putting things.

There was some disappointment among the voyagers, as we were steaming out of the inlet of Vancouver and threading the Narrows, at missing a sight of the <i>Beaver</i>, or rather of her bones. The <i>Beaver</i> was the first steamer that churned Pacific waters, having been brought round the Horn in 1836 for the service of the Hudson Bay Company, and, after being incapacitated, was used as a tug-boat when, in 1889, it became a wreck off Vancouver. But the remains had lately disappeared, and so the interesting sight of the earliest and latest steamships in the Pacific in juxtaposition is no longer possible.

From Vancouver to Victoria, a distance of eighty-two miles, is a land-locked passage of great beauty. The course is down the Straits of Georgia. The noble peak of Mount Baker, nearly fourteen thousand feet in height, and covered with perpetual snow, impresses us in the distance.

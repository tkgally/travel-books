# Chapter XVII

T<sc>he</sc> Tawareks are a Berber race. This is evident from the fact that their language, in its various dialects, is nearly related to the <i>patois</i> spoken by the Kabyles, Riffs, and other Berber tribes, and that they call themselves Imoshagh—the ‘noble people’—the name being merely another form of Amazigh, Mazigh, Masix, and Maxys, as it is variously pronounced by other Berber races.

As a whole these people are divided into five main tribes, or rather confederations of tribes—the Askar, living in the deserts about Ghat and Ghadames; the Kelowi, dwelling round Aïr; the Hoggar, in the mountains of that name and in the centre of the Sahara; the Awelimmiden, living in the desert to the north and east of Timbuktu; and the Arrerf Ahnet, a recently formed section split off from the Hoggar tribe and domiciled in the Adrar Ahnet region to the west of them.

Formerly the Hoggar and Askar tribes were both ruled over by one <i>amanokal</i>, or sultan, who belonged to the Imanan family. This family was a very influential one, being not only of noble descent among the Tawareks, but also <i>shereefs</i> or descendants of the prophet Mohammed, and so members of the religious aristocracy of the country as well.

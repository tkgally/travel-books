The Tougourt market, like that of Biskra, seemed to be the <i>rendezvous</i> of all the <i>marabouts</i> in the place.

One cantankerous-looking old negro saint had somehow managed to get hold of a length of gold fringe, apparently of European origin, which, to the envy of his rivals in sanctity, he had sewn all round the edge of his <i>burnous</i>, thus giving a very smart effect to his otherwise ragged attire.

But the <i>marabout</i> which most attracted my attention was a holy man who got a living by eating scorpions for the delectation of the people who visited the market.

At the moment of our arrival he was standing with his face turned up to the sun, and two of these loathsome creatures crawling over his forehead and cheeks. He allowed them to creep for some time about his face. Then suddenly, with a frantic yell, he snatched them off, flung one of them down, causing a small stampede in the crowd around him, tore the tail and claws off the other, put the body in his mouth, and crunched it up with apparent gusto. He then picked up the other one, and treated it in the same way. It was a most disgusting spectacle.

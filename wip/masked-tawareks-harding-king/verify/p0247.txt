I declined to sleep in that bed. The <i>zawia</i> was apparently badly in need of a laundry. I covered it over instead with a rug, and had my own blankets spread on the top of it, and then lay down to recover from my supper and to dream the most hideous nightmares in which the Tawareks and the Shaambah, alternately armed with that jingling musical-box, chased me about the waterless wastes of the Sahara.

At daybreak we went to pay our respects to the <i>marabout.</i> We found him just leaving the mosque. He was a most kindly-looking old gentleman, with evidently a considerable quantity of negro blood in his veins. He walked by the side of his son, leaning on a walking-stick covered with baize of the holy Mohammedan green.

Aïssa stepped forward and raised the edge of his <i>burnous</i> reverently to his lips. The <i>marabout,</i> in return, muttered a blessing. He then turned to me and, after we had kissed hands, assured me politely that everyone was welcome at the <i>zawia,</i> but that he was particularly pleased to have been the host of a European. He then asked me several questions as to how I had been treated while his guest. To all of which I was able to reply that I had had everything that I had required. Then, as Aïssa twitched my sleeve as a sign that it was time to go, with many thanks for his hospitality, I took my leave.

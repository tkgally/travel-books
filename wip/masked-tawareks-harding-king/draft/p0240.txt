We entered into a small court shaded by two palm-trees, and passed through a door in one of its walls up a wide and well-built staircase into a room which Aïssa whispered to me was the ‘Salon.’ Here our guide left us.

The room was disappointing. It was a bare white-washed apartment with a vaulted ceiling, and was furnished throughout with tawdry European furniture.

It contained no less than four timepieces. A huge grandfather’s clock, gaudily painted and gilt, ticked solemnly in one corner, with the hands pointing at half-past ten. A cuckoo clock hung on the wall beside it, giving the time as five minutes to seven. The other clocks had stopped. A gilt and marble console table, with a musical-box placed upon it, was fastened against the wall. Photographs of various French officials were scattered about the room, and some artificial flowers in a glass case stood on the floor in one corner. But the place of honour was given to a large green umbrella which hung in a red flannel case on the wall over the mantelpiece.

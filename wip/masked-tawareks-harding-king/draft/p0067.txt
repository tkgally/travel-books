The curious pillars of sand raised by the whirlwinds so common in the desert, and known even in England as ‘sand-devils,’ are attributed to the playfulness of a passing <i>jinn</i>.

A story is current among the natives of Algeria of an Arab who, while travelling in the desert, made some slighting remark to his companion about a ‘sand-devil,’ which chanced to be passing near. The ‘devil’ immediately wheeled round, rushed swirling and roaring towards him, caught him as he turned to fly, knocked him down, and tearing the <i>burnous</i> from his back, whirled it up into the sky until it was lost to sight. The ‘devil’ swept shrieking triumphantly on, leaving the Arab to rise sadly to his feet, the poorer by a new <i>burnous</i>, and with a greatly increased respect for the unseen <i>jinns</i> of the desert.

The road between Tougourt and Wargla is a mere track, and, especially after a high wind, when the footprints of previous caravans have been obliterated by the drifting sand, cannot be distinguished from the desert which surrounds it. In order to guide travellers on their way little mounds of earth and branches torn from the desert scrub are placed at frequent intervals on either side of the track.

Until one has become well accustomed to these caravan routes they are very difficult to follow. More than once, when walking ahead of my caravan, I followed the footprints of some grazing camels, which I took to belong to a caravan proceeding to Wargla, and wandered off the road into the desert around. This always threw my guide into a state of the greatest excitement. He would come rushing after me, frantically waving his <i>burnous</i> to attract my attention, and calling out to me in an agonised voice to come back or I should get lost and die of thirst in the desert.

Towards nightfall we were overtaken by our fellow-traveller of the morning, who came spurring after us at a spanking pace on his mule.

Soon after joining us he produced again his Koran, and spent nearly an hour in chanting it as he rode on his mule, being now and then joined in his devotions by my Arabs.

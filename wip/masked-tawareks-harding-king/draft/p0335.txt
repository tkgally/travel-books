In arranging beforehand the details of a <i>mia’ad</i> the greatest precautions are taken by each side to ensure that neither party shall have any advantage in the event of the discussion ending, as not unfrequently happens, in hostilities. The exact number of armed men which each side is to be allowed to bring to the debate is fixed, and then after much discussion the spot upon which it is to be held is decided upon.

The first day of the meeting is entirely taken up in consuming an enormous meal and in an exchange of the most fulsome compliments between the two sides.

On the second day the actual palaver commences. The debaters squat on the ground in parallel rows, with their swords beside them and their spears stuck upright in the ground behind. In their rear are seated those of their party who are to take no part in the verbal discussion, and who are only present to see fair play for their side.

Each black-masked orator gives his opinion on the subject of discussion with the utmost solemnity and deliberation. He is heard in dead silence, broken only by an occasional muttered invocation to Allah to keep off the devil and his evil counsels, responded to by a murmured ‘Amen’ from the remainder of the crowd.

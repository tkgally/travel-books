All around the castle was spread out a broad expanse of rice fields and waving grain, while a few miles to the east lay the ocean, whose distant roar could easily be heard, and whose waves had so long broken upon the shores of a land locked in mystery. The thatched-roof houses and huts of Shidzuoka (then called <i>Futsiu</i>, or Chief Capital,) were scattered in great numbers about the castle; and all the inhabitants rendered complete submission and homage to their feudal lord, the First Tycoon. This castle is now in ruins, and fire and earthquake have left little there save the walls and moats and crumbled towers shaded by patriarchal pines. The mail-clad warriors are gone who once walked these now deserted parapets; the archers no longer shoot their arrows from the fir branches of the trees; the spearmen and swordsmen who guarded the gates have gone to their graves long ago, leaving the copper-covered gates unbarred, the drawbridges tottering and decayed, and the wild foxes to roam among the ruins and vine-covered rocks of the once mighty fortress.

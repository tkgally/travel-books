However, they had a right to keep as cool as possible, for never did mortals work harder; and notwithstanding the difficulties of the current, and the shallowness of the stream, they tugged at their poles with a vigor and perseverance we have never seen equalled.

We continued up stream until nothing was in view but low meadows of long rank grass skirting the river bank. Our men toiled on, pushing their poles with as much vigor as at the outset, until coming to a place where the bank was low and level they suddenly ran the boat close to the shore and jumped off; and while we were wondering what it meant to see all the nimble legs disappear at once, we felt ourselves impelled by a new form of motion.

We went on top of the little cabin to take a view of the situation, and found the men about forty yards ahead of us, tugging away at a long rope attached to a short mast near the centre of the boat. This rope could be lengthened or shortened by a crank turned by the steersman, who with one hand guided the boat well out into the stream, and with the other accommodated the rope to the distance from the shore.

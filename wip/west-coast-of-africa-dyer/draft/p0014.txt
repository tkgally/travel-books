## Chapter VI.

Anchoring at Appolonia.—Surf boat capsized—the Doctor nearly drowned.—Grand Dance outside our quarters by Torchlight.—Imperfect sanitary arrangements.—Visit from an Ashanti Commander and Taniki the King’s Nephew.—Palaver with King Amakee.—Negociations for peace between Blay and Amakee.—Etiquette at the Ashanti Court.—Return to Axim and make amicable arrangements with Amakee. 108

## Chapter VII.

On board the <i>Druid</i> at Cape Coast Castle.—Butterfly hunting.—Description of the Towns on the Gold Coast.—Curious incident.—Cruising between Jellah Coffee and Kabenda.—Visit the British Factories at Ambrizette.—Barter with Spirits.—Dearness of Provisions at St. Paul’s de Loando. Penal Colony.—Shooting Koodoos and surprising Baboons.—Enjoying the hospitality of the Portuguese.—Convict Settlements at Benguela.—Statement of a Convict.—Mode of Punishing.—Treaty with King Antonio. 136

## Chapter VIII.

Fever on board.—Picturesque scenery at Kabenda.—Fetish men.—The Chief of Kabenda and his minister.—Buying wives.—Robbery of 50 Ivory Tusks.—Naval Court Martial.—Chase after a Portuguese Trader.—Absence of constituted authority on the South Coast.—Arrival of Commodore Commerell.—Visit M’Boma, the Great Slave Market.—Punishment of Natives for stealing.—Depart from the South-Coast with the Commodore.—Causes of war between the Bonny and Opobo men.—Palaver with King George on board the <i>Torch</i>.—Human Sacrifices.—Return of the Commodore to Bonny in the <i>Torch</i>.—Fire at Bonny.—Attempted Suicide.—Negociations for peace by the several Tribes.—We Convoy the <i>Supply</i> to Cadiz.—Anchor at Anno Bom. 160

## Chapter IX.

Description of Anno Bom.—Cupidity of King David Carter.—Start for Ascension.—The “Rollers” at Ascension.—Sailing to Dâker and thence to Gibraltar.—Conclusions. 170

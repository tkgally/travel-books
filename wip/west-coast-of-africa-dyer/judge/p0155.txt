This was kept in a room, the sides of which were boarded, and had a door fastened with a good strong padlock. It smelt very musty as we entered, for there was no window, or any means of ventilating it. In the corner were piles of cotton cloth, round the sides dozens of empty bottles, powder barrels and gin cases: several pictures hung against the walls, one of them an engraving of the “Black Brunswicker,” well framed; and several guns, flints, brass rods, and coloured bottles and beads were arranged about the room. The old man was very proud of his possessions, and carried the key of his treasure room suspended by an iron chain, of no slight dimensions, round his neck.

At the native burial ground are to be seen graves ornamented with wooden crosses, having on them an imitation of our letters: the graves are also covered with pieces of glass and crockery, sometimes broken and sometimes not. I noticed this also near Antonio's town and elswhere. The graves are also sometimes railed round. In the centre of the town of Kabenda is a wooden cross painted black, on which is printed the Lord's prayer in Portuguese. There are no missionaries in the place and have not been for many years, but there was a house called a school house where children were taught something, I was told, but I don't know what.

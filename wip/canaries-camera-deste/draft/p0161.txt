It is strange that people so poor, who wear no kind of ornament themselves, should indulge in such decorative and expensive collars for their goats. The workmanship is rough, but the effect is very showy and attractive.

Tomás, our donkey boy, has been employed lately in carrying invalids about in hammocks, and has not been with us on our expeditions; it is astonishing to see this slender young fellow and another man march off with a weight upon their shoulders such as a strong Englishman finds unendurable even for a moment, and walking, moreover, at such a pace, that anyone accompanying them on foot has to break into a run now and then to keep up.

The hammocks—which are often very pretty with their frilled chintz awnings and gay cushions—are slung from a massive pole, and hang so near the ground that people who are new to them find the swaying motion rather unpleasant, and are in terror of being bumped upon the rough stones that strew the paths over which they are being borne.

In Tomás's absence he has entrusted us to the care of Antonio, a nut-brown urchin of about ten, with eyes like sloes and a hat like an old apple pudding.

On arriving here we found there was a block in the canal, thirty steamers besides ours waiting to pass through. It may be a day or two before the <i>Ceylon</i> will arrive, so for the present we are high and <i>very</i> dry here in the sand. There are many nice people in the hotel waiting for their ships to go off to new and distant homes in the East, so we are all, in one sense, in the same boat; and our misfortune in being obliged to wait in so dreary a place as this has tended to link us together in ties nearer than those of mere acquaintances.

Opposite the hotel, as far as the eye can reach, there is naught but sand and water to gaze upon, the latter looking crisp and fresh, for a stiff breeze is whitening the tops of the blue waves, and giving a most enjoyable freshness to the air.

The courtyard of the hotel is quite a little oasis in this blinding desert of sand, and the eye gratefully lingers upon the green-leaved palms and other tropical shrubs with which it is filled.

After dinner we are treated to stories illustrative of the ferocity of the mosquitoes, from which it appears that they will rip up the beds and pull them all over the room in their rage, if they are unable to get at one through the curtains.

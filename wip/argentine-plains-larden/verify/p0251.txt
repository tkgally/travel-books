Indeed I, in my ignorance, several times mistook weeds for crops. But the thistle plague is really a serious matter, especially (in my brother’s opinion) as regards the cardo castillo; for the thistles often abound in and spoil the alfalfa pastures, while other weeds as a rule do not invade these. The locusts, by the way, appeared to eat the thistles first of all; but I fear they did not anticipate and hinder the seeding to a sufficient extent to diminish the plague for the next year (1910).

In this connection I may say that where the alfalfa had been eaten down close, so that the ground was nearly exposed, a native trefoil, which before (I imagine) was kept down by the tall native grasses, had spread wonderfully. In one paddock where the alfalfa had failed and the herbage was excessively short, this trefoil carpeted the ground. This is <i>not</i> a weed; it forms very good pasture.

## <i>Water.</i>

When in the Andes I came across no water that was not saline. Even the glacier streams left some white crust on stones; and any springs that I saw issuing from the mountain-side were very salt and also deposited much lime. [See p. <pref>207</pref>.]

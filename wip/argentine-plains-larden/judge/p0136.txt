The smoke from the branding suggested pain, but the animals showed no signs of it. Indeed, I do not think that it would “answer” to make a real burn, a sore, as the flies would then give trouble.

## <i>Counting the Stock.</i>

The counting of the stock was interesting. My brother and the capataz, each provided with a sort of rosary of beads, went with attendant peons to one paddock after another to count the cattle, some 500 or 600 at a time, while the sheep capataz with his men counted the sheep in the pens.

The cattle were rounded up, and then the peons made them pass in a thin line between the two counters. The beads served to record the tens or hundreds. A significant detail was that when I photographed the process I had to stand so as to be concealed by the horses of the break. The cattle are used to horsemen, but the sight of a man on foot would have made the animals unmanageable. So also native cattle may horn, and other cattle will mob, a man on foot. I had some uncomfortable experiences of this.

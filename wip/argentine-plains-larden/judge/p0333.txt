## Provisions and Luggage.

Strong wooden cases, such that two slung one on each side of a mule together weigh about 80 kilos when full, are good for stores; and it is well to have one long enough to contain the ice-axes. These cases should be sent up to Puente del Inca in advance by goods-train from Buenos Aires with stores of Nestlé’s milk, jams, &c. Of course yerba (with the corresponding maté gourds and bombillas), coffee, sugar, and macaroni can be sent up also if the traveller can learn about how much is wanted; but these can be bought at Puente del Inca. Cooking vessels, enamelled iron plates and cups and such-like, must also be sent up. Dr. Helbling had strong aluminium vessels for cooking purposes, beside his lighter etna intended for carrying up higher where mules could not go. There is a “store” at Puente del Inca, and it would be well to arrange beforehand about prices. The main things for which we sent back the men were bread, biscuit, coffee, sugar, macaroni, and sheep; these last killed and cleaned, but with the skins on. Up where we went, meat in the shade would keep good for an indefinite time.

Save for the penitentes, which, in the clumsier form in which they occurred on this glacier, might almost be mistaken for séracs—(if one did not wonder what séracs were doing on a glacier of such gentle slope!)—the whole scene appeared to me quite familiar; I could almost have believed that I was on some “Concordia Platz.” And I could hardly believe that I was hours from the camp, the camp two long days or more to Puente del Inca, and Puente del Inca separated by enormous distances of land and sea from Switzerland; nor that, the same evening, I should be eating sheep’s ribs with my fingers at a camp fire, and later see Orion standing on his head. Indeed, after twenty-seven years of routine teaching, and even routine boating and bicycling, I had, all through this Andes expedition, a great difficulty in realising that I was myself. To be actually camping out in the Andes—it seemed incredible!

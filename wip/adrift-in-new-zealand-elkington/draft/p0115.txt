Each bath has a different name, but the only ones I can recollect are the Blue Bath and the Postman’s Bath. The latter I remember well, for it was outside that building on a bright moonlight night that I had my first lesson in the “Washington Post.” It is a strange place in which to learn dancing, but we four were on our usual after-dinner ramble, and the “Washington Post” was just becoming fashionable. My friend had got it into his head, and, instead of talking, he did nothing but whistle it. So we set to work and danced it, and he was soon cured and out of breath, as the rough slabs outside that bath-house are not conducive to easy movements.

Ten miles out from Rotorua is Wairoa—the little township that was half buried by the Tarawera eruption in 1886, when the pink and white terraces were also destroyed; these, however, are re-forming, though they will probably take a century or so before they are anything like they were prior to Tarawera’s eccentricity.

Their idea is that a house is sacred, or tapu, until the last bit of timber has been laid and the last piece of carving finished. When all this is done a ceremony takes place which removes the tapu. So that when the tohunga heard of the chief’s disregard for the tapu, he went to him and warned him that calamity would fall upon him for his act unless he at once stopped the building. But the chief only smiled at him and ordered the carvers to go on with their work. The tohunga became filled with righteous anger and alarm, and said that seven people should die if the work were not stopped at once.

The carvers went on with their work, and a few days later the chief’s wife died. He loved her dearly, and was so cut up that he hearkened to the voice of the tohunga and had the work stopped but as soon as his grief had worn off, and he had taken a new wife, he set the men to work again; and, strange to say, directly they began his second wife died.

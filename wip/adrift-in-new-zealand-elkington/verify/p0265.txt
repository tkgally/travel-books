I remember well his glee and my discomfort, when the first one who called had on his card the name of a well-known dramatic company which had recently been touring the colony. We were all sitting in our room when the landlady brought the pasteboard up. She gave it to me, and I handed it to Bones, and I know my face went a shade paler, but there was little sympathy from him.

“By gum!” he cried, “what an ass you’ll look! Fancy an actor coming to learn stage-craft from you! Never mind,” he added, “you’ve got to face it,” and he made some silly allusion to a biblical remark about a plough; and then he turned to the landlady and told her to tell Mr. Blank to wait in her sitting-room.

As soon as she had departed, he dived into a portmanteau of his and pulled out a blue jacket, which the sun had turned green, and told me to put it on; then he ruffled my hair, and handed me my pipe.

“Actors,” he said, “always wear faded blue coats. Now go,” he added, “and bring his guinea back when you return, or by——”

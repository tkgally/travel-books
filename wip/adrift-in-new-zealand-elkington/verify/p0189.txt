Suddenly, through the trees, far on a hilltop, I saw a mob of cattle tearing wildly about. Men were rushing here and there rounding them up, and oh! the joy of it, I saw a swagman coming helter-skelter towards me. I was so weak that I just sank on to the ground.

“Hallo, mate, what’s up? Have you had a run for it too? Damned if I didn’t think the whole blessed mob would be on the top of me ’fore I could get here. You look sick,” he added; “can I do anything for you?” he spoke roughly but tenderly, as he swung off his swag and knelt down beside me.

As soon as I could I told him my plight, and in a moment he was as busy as he could be, boiling the inevitable billy, and in less than a couple of hours he had me in the station I had been hunting for, and round and round which, I afterwards heard, I had been travelling. The squatter’s wife, when she heard my story, sent me to bed with a big basin of bread and milk, and I slept and slept and slept.

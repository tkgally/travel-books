At eight o’clock my fellow-worker arrived, and, after a critical survey of me, he asked if I were the new man, and on my explaining that I was, he had the impertinence to inquire if I knew anything about carpentering. I was piqued, and said “No,” very sharply.

To my astonishment he said, “Neither do I,” and swung open the gate and walked into the empty house. From that moment we were fast friends, and between us we papered, white-washed, and thoroughly repaired those two houses, which, as I have already hinted, are still standing and still occupied.

When I was a boy my father once told me that “nobody was too old to learn.” I don’t say that he invented the phrase, but I thought it was fine at the time, and, like all young impressions, it clung to me, and I have been surprised oftentimes since by the truth of my father’s remark. After I had finished my house-repairing job I applied his maxim, and decided never to refuse to take on anything.

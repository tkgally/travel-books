“I always thought gum was a liquid,” I said, as I handed back the precious thing.

“So it is in its infancy. See there,” he added, stooping forward and pointing to an enormous tree which was in front of the tent; “that is a kauri-tree, and that white resin on the branches is the gum. This piece of amber-like stuff was just the same as that a few centuries ago.”

“Tell me some more, quick,” I said, as he stopped and sank back on his fern couch.

“It’s too hot,” he replied; “ask Bob.”

The Manchester man looked up; he had been puffing silently at a short clay pipe.

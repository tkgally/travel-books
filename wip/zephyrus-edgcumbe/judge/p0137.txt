The revolutionary movement of 1886, however, started under much more favourable conditions. The revolutionists were able to place no less than 1,500 well-armed soldiers at once in the field, who had been drilled for months. They possessed, moreover, a battery of artillery. Is it any wonder, then, that the people of the Banda Oriental, three-fourths of whom favoured the revolutionists, regarded the coming revolution as already an assured success?

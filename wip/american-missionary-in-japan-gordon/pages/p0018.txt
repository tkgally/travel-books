It seems more like miracle than history to tell that, in Kyōto, where Yokoi Héishiro was in 1868 assassinated for entertaining Christian convictions, there now stand Neesima’s Dōshisha University and many Christian churches; while in Tōkyō, the son of Yokoi preaches the gospel, both as pastor and editor. To find in the first Imperial Diet of constitutional Japan no fewer than fifteen pronounced Christian men, is but to tell, in another way, what God hath wrought through the American missionaries.

For these men have, apart from their holy calling, done a mighty work in the making of New Japan. Not less than Matthew Perry and Townsend Harris, have they incarnated the United States as the Great Pacific Power.

One of the most impressive of these theatre meetings occurred in Kyōto in 1881. It was impressive because of the place, an historic theatre in the old capital of Japan, the centre and stronghold of Buddhism; because of its size, nearly three thousand hearers, including several hundred priests, being present; because it was the first public challenge made by the Christians of central Japan to their fellow-countrymen, and especially to the Buddhists; because of the length of the meeting, the eighteen addresses occupying almost all the time from one to ten

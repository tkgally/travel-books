This desire becoming known to our missionary societies, there was a considerable influx of physicians in 1872 and the immediately succeeding years. These new men everywhere received a warm welcome, one in striking contrast to that accorded to their predecessors.

Dr. Berry (A. B. C. F. M.) was met on his arrival by an invitation from the physicians of Kyōto to locate there, and they offered him the use of a house free. The Kyōto governor and vice-governor received him with deference and attention; but as they could not give him freedom to teach Christianity publicly in that inland city, he declined to go. He became connected with a hospital in Kōbe (a treaty port), where he not only successfully treated many patients, but had a number of physicians under his instruction. Visits were made to surrounding towns, and many sick were treated in connection with the native physicians.

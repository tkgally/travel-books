The Japanese alphabet has no “l,” and so it comes about that, in speaking English, “l” and “r” are even more of a perplexity to them than “p” and “q” to an American child. A class of custom-house officials whom Mrs. Gordon, during our residence at Osaka, had taken through “Wilson’s Third Reader,” which is largely composed of Scripture stories, deserted her at once when Bibles were put into their hands. Years afterward we received a letter from one of them, at that time an interpreter in the foreign office of the Osaka Fu, asking wherein the essence of Christianity lies. “Does it consist simply of obedience to the Gordon Rule?” Unfortunately we could not claim that “the Gordon Rule” is a perfect standard. One who had become a Christian announced with a profound bow, “I am the Protestant Church.”

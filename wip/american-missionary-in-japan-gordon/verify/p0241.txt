As scarcely one of the witnesses had ever heard of the science of Comparative Religion, their testimony is all the more valuable, because of its unstudied spontaneity. It is explicit upon such points as these:—

<i>Christianity brings a sense of sin which the other religions named above do not produce.</i> There often has been an intellectual recognition of the fact of sin and its consequences, but a powerful impression of it as a personal burden is not common. One old lady testified that she had hardly thought of herself as a sinner, until she began to hear this “new way.” Then her sins stood up before her in strong condemnation. A young lady, a teacher in the public schools, referring to her parents’ unwillingness that she should become a Christian, said that they consented only when told that she could not otherwise get rid of her newly-felt burden of sin.

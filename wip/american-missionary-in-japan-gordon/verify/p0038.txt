He is joyful because of his favorable surroundings, the glorious message which he bears, and the immediate success which is given him; hopeful because of his faith in the power of the gospel and the possibilities of the people, the evidences of whose quickened and vigorous life he sees on every hand.

As has already been intimated, besides the “detested exile from his native land,”—to use the expressive words of a Yokohama editor,—which one who spends his life in a foreign land necessarily undergoes, and the pain of family separation, which words cannot exaggerate, the missionary has no hardships or privations which call for the consecration usually associated with his work.

# Chapter XXI.

[[subtitle]] The Present Outlook.

N<sc>o</sc> account of mission work in Japan would be complete or candid, which did not recognize the conservative and anti-foreign reaction which has been noticeable during the past few years.

The coming of such a reaction has long been regarded as inevitable, and in many respects desirable. For a quarter of a century and more, the government and the people had been looking with wonder and admiration upon foreign lands. Our chapter on New Japan has shown us how many things they found in our civilization which they could wisely appropriate. A regular foreign fever set in. Everything domestic came to be looked upon as inferior; everything from abroad was accepted as superior, and adopted with little inquiry as to its merits. Government offices and schools had buildings in foreign style. Men of wealth began to follow the same fashion in building their residences.

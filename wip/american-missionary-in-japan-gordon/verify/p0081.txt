# Chapter V.

[[subtitle]] The “Kumamoto Band.”

I<sc>n</sc> the previous chapter I have spoken of the fact that the teaching of English was, in the early days, a key to the hearts of young Japanese which was constantly used by the missionaries. Other Christian teachers, not under missionary appointment, used the same key with great faithfulness and a good measure of success. This was especially true of Captain L. L. Janes, of Kumamoto, in the south; and of President Clark, of the Massachusetts Agricultural College, who was for a time at the head of the Japanese Agricultural College at Sapporo, in the extreme north. As an illustration of the good work done by these teachers under more or less favorable circumstances, I give below a translation (made by a Japanese) of an account of the work of the gentleman first named, prepared by one of his pupils, afterward the Rev. P. Kanamori:—

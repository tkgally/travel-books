“Ima shibashi kuni wa ochi-kochi hedatsurumo

The meaning, but not the aroma, of this, may be gathered from these rude lines:—

<verse>
Though now awhile we dwell in lands apart,
Soon in heaven’s fields heart shall gladden heart.
</verse>

Then there is a larger <i>sōbetsukai</i> at the college, arranged for and managed by the students. Members of different classes dwell upon the many good deeds and incomparable virtues of the departing, in fervid oration, in stilted Chinese <i>shi</i> (verse), and in smooth flowing Japanese <i>uta</i> (poems) of the style given above. There are songs, general and special, in English and Japanese, and the whole is crowned with a <i>sembetsu</i> (parting present) of a beautiful vase.

A feast <i>à la Japanaise</i>, given by the graduating class to the faculty of which he is a member, at a restaurant by the riverside, is next in order.

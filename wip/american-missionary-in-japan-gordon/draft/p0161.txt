And by what power is this young man, without wealth and reputation, enabled to house, feed, clothe, educate, and train in various industries, these more than two hundred orphans now under his charge?” And to these questions their unchristian friends have no reply.

When the terrible earthquakes occurred in October, 1891, Mr. Ishii had an agent promptly on the ground, and forty-one orphans were taken to the asylum. But he did not stop with this. He inaugurated a campaign for funds in aid of the sufferers. The orphans went everywhere in the city and vicinity, and pupils of the two Christian schools were also enlisted. The contributions began with sixteen cents from an orphan, “all that she had.” One orphan entered the largest house of ill-repute in the city, and was quickly given one dollar by one of the inmates. So much zeal was shown that eleven hundred dollars and seventeen hundred articles of clothing were in this way secured.

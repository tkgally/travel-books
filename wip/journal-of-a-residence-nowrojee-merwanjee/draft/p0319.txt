[[cont]] Watts, Esq., by his will dated 22d of August, 1579, founded this charity for six poor travellers, who, not being rogues or proctors, may receive gratis, for one night, lodging, entertainment, and fourpence each.” How rogues and proctors came to be thus named together is not exactly known. But the common report is, that having once been taken suddenly ill, and having employed a proctor or lawyer to make his will, upon his recovery he looked at the will made in his illness, and found that the proctor had left all the money to himself; and to perpetuate the knowledge of this act of villany, he thus left his bequest. There are also some very nice almshouses on the new road, Rochester, called St. Catherine’s Hospital, where several elderly females, who have known better times, have a small house, with sufficient weekly income to keep them. These are very good things. These are such things as do honour to Englishmen. And we are told that there is scarcely a large town of ancient origin but what has some of these benevolent institutions.

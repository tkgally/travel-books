We cannot for one moment imagine that our impressions, or description of what we saw and felt, will either instruct or amuse English people; but we do think many of our own countrymen, both Parsees and Hindoos, will be amused at hearing of what we saw in England; we may as well state here that we have confined ourselves strictly to truth; and if we have put a wrong construction upon anything that we have described, it has been for want of knowing better. We hope, in our little journal, any remarks we may make, may not be of that nature to give pain to any one. We have endeavoured to avoid all personal remarks; and when we speak of any sect, we mean our observations to apply generally, and not to individuals. We have, in our long stay in England, had much to make us attached to it. We have received friendly kindness from many, and have formed some friendships that on our side will cease but with our lives; we have received courtesy from a still greater number, and we shall ever think of England with sentiments of esteem and admiration.

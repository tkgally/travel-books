In a raised chair, at the opposite end to the Speaker, is seated Sir William Gossett, the serjeant-at-arms, who is to preserve order below the bar, and to take into custody any member, or other person, upon the command of the Speaker, who may do anything contrary to the usages of Parliament; when he wishes to withdraw for a short time, John Clement, Esq., deputy serjeant-at arms, takes his seat. When we entered the house Mr. Gisborne was addressing the house from the ministerial side, and there were not then perhaps one hundred members in the house, and then Mr. Cholmondeley answered from the opposition; Mr. M. J. O’Connell replied to him, and Mr. Tennent opposed the second reading, and Mr. W. Roche spoke in favour of it. This was all what soldiers would call light skirmishing; and now what they call the big guns began to go off. Mr. Shaw, the Recorder of Dublin, from the opposition benches, in a very lengthy but good speech, spoke against the Bill, and this called up the great Irish agitator, Mr. Daniel O’Connell.

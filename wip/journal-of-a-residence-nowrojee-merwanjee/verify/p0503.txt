As many of our friends were kind enough to permit us to copy any thing we liked from their books, we here indiscriminately annex a few, which will convey to our countrymen, an idea of what other things, besides the kind and feeling expressions of friendship, are written in Albums.

## M<sc>y</sc> N<sc>ative</sc> I<sc>sle</sc>.

<verse>
“Oh, tell me not of fairer lands,
Beneath a brighter sky,
Of streams that roll o’er golden sands,
And flowers that never die.

My native Isle! My native Isle!
Though bleak and bare thou be,
And scant and cold the Summer’s smile,
Thou’rt all the world to me.

The flowers that on thy mountain’s brow,
When Wintry winds assail,
Securely sleep beneath the snow,
Their cold and kindly veil.

Transplanted to a richer soil,
Where genial breezes play,
In sickly gloom we droop awhile,
Then wither and decay.”
</verse>

<verse>
“Women have whims and small requests,
That agitate their tender breasts,
Tho’ we as trifles eye them;
But when their feelings they divulge,
With looks they ask us to indulge,
Can any man deny them?”
</verse>

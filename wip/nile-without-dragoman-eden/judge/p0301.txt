Below Chobac is Bedreshayn, where antiquaries and men in health stop as the nearest point to Memphis. Between these two villages there is a lovely view for all. A reach, broad and long, runs down to Toorah, with its quarries and forests of masts. Above and behind Toorah stand some large government buildings, which show with great effect from the commanding position they have taken at the extremity riverwards of the Toorah Massarah hills. On the east lies the desert. On the west, a sloping green bank, broken here and there by large woods of palms. Behind these again, visible between or over them, stand the great Pyramids and the less imposing ones of Sakarah, Memphis, and the Colossus. At the foot of the reach, Cairo suddenly comes into view. The capital of Egypt is seated like a bird on a hill, the whole of which it covers with outspread wings. The face of the mount now presented to us was a sheer abrupt descent. The buildings nestle to the very verge of the precipice; below, no foot could rest, and there is nothing to detract from the boldness with which the city has, as it were, taken its perch. High above all stretches upwards the citadel, with the dome and minarets of its magnificent mosque.

I was glad enough when a couple of strong arms lifted me bodily out, minus one boot; and, after thanking my stalwart friend, <i>lay cautiously down</i> upon the hard part of the beach, and shoved my arm down after the missing article, which, singular to say, I drew out perfectly empty. It had collapsed as soon as my foot left it; and all that I had to do was to pull it quietly on and walk more carefully in future.

As soon as I had stamped my foot well down into the softened leather, and scraped off a pound or more of the adhering patent bootjack-mixture, I took a long stick and shoved it down the half-filled hole from which I had rescued my boot, to see how far I <i>might</i> have sunk had not a “friend in need” been at hand. It had gone down only about two feet when I felt a rock or some other hard substance; and we subsequently found that these “patches” seldom extended to a greater depth, so that, though considerably inconvenient, they were not at all dangerous.

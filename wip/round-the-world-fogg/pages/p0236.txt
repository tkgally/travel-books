After dipping the flowers, which seemed to me like yellow marigolds, into the fountain the priests threw them through the door way to the people, who scrambled after them most frantically, believing that they possessed some marvelous charm. A fat, oily-looking Brahmin waddled to the door and threw round my neck a string of flowers, at the same time holding out his han[[supplied d]] for <i>bucksheesh.</i> The crowd behind scowled at my good luck, and my guide drew me away, for no one could tell what the zeal these deluded fanatics would lead them t[[supplied o]] do to an unbeliever. Ten years ago my life would not have been worth a minute’s purchase in such a place. I bestowed my strin[[supplied g]] of marigolds upon some one who could appreciate their value, and we elbowed our way into the comparatively fresh air of the street, having seen enough of Hindoo idolatry.

On our way back to the “Victoria,” we passed hundreds of shops devoted to the manufacture of brass and copper idols of Vishnu, Shiva and Buddha. In other shops where no manufacturing was going on I saw large quantities of hideous little idols made of iron and covered with gilt lacquer, which my guide assured me were made in Birmingham. Verily, not India alone, but Christian England also, is somewhat given up to I-dollar-try.

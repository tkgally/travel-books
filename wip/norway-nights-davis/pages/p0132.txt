This was the beginning of many disasters; several conflagrations and the pestilence of the “Black Death” nearly annihilated the unhappy town until, about a hundred years ago, it struggled into life again, and now holds its own right valiantly, encouraged also by the railways to Christiania and to Stockholm.

We were a jubilant party at Trondhjem, proud of our punctuality and our strict adherence to our chart of travel; happy that no personal accident or failure of any kind had barred our way. As the fiord steamers ply only on certain days, which are frequently changed, the delay of a single day might have entailed the loss of our rooms on the <i>Capella.</i> But here we were, within twenty-four hours’ sail of that Arctic Ocean which we had so often traversed in fancy, and, turning away from the generous but unsufficing Past, we lifted eager eyes towards that anomalous meridian which marks the fusion of day with immediate day again.

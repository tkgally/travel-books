The Russian language is very pleasant to the ear and pictorial to the eye; but its grammar, which has no article, bristles with declinations, inflections, and inversions which no foreigner but Mezzofanti could conquer and only Mark Twain conld justly describe. Breakfast was served in our sitting-room—<i>tchaĭ slamonum</i> and strawberries, of course; and our brief vocabulary, here as everywhere, proved an essential acquisition, for the servants, with one exception, spoke nothing but Russ; our chambermaid also was a native and knew only four French words, but made up the deficit with smiles.

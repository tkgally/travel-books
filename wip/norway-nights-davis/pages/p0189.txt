Half an hour’s drive took us to Trollhatten Falls, which are considered even finer than those at Schaffhausen, and on our way we stopped to see the remarkable locks of the Götha Canal, a great triumph of engineering skill when they were cut, but probably surpassed by the science of our day. It was early in the fifteenth century that the problem was first presented of uniting the east and west coasts through the numerous intervening lakes and rivers, and thus making an island of the southern part of Sweden. The cataracts and rocks proved too formidable for the engineers of that period.

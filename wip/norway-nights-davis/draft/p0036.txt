Some years ago Norway was an El Dorado of freedom for sportsmen, but several Englishmen have purchased or leased large estates of the best salmon-grounds (or waters), and the government now exacts a tax on public lands of two hundred to three hundred krone (or ten to fifteen pounds sterling) for the season. Salmon-fishing is not now to be obtained; but other fishing is always at hand, and reindeer may be hunted if one will take a guide to their mountain haunts and accept the hardships involved in the pursuit of creatures so shy and keen-scented. Sleeping on straw in a leaky hut or under a snow-covered rock, with only milk and oat-cakes to live upon, certainly demands the noble incentive of “something to kill.”

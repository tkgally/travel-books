In the foreground, majestic in form and attitude, stood St. Mark upon his pedestal, with outstretched hand pointing to the mural recess far above the tessellated pavement, and revealed by a solitary taper inside, where a departed pope sleeps until his successor claims his place. At every one of these rites, for centuries past, the inexorable hand of the marble apostle relegates to rest the marble pontiff.

No such startling realism can be offered by the flatness of paint and sheen of gilding in the Greek Church.

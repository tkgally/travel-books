The great white auks which inhabit some of the far northerly islands are models of a social propriety and philanthropy that is limited to Aukland. The females seem to be fewer in number than the males; and as their conjugal relations are strictly observed, the bachelor-bird is forced to bide his time until death comes to the rescue and gives him an opportunity to unite himself with his brother’s widow, which, not being a citizen of England, he can do with impunity.

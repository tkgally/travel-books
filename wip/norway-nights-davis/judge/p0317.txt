The Ikonostas, which rises to the vaulted ceiling, is incomparably rich in precious stones around the aureoles of sainted hierarchs; in close proximity is the silver-gilt tomb of St. Sergius, glittering with lamps and a canopy supported by four columns all of the same metal: around it knelt a group of pilgrims with long white beards and some noble faces illumined with faith and fervor; desolate beggars in brown rags with yellow lights, their legs bound in rags strapped on like a classic cothurnus; moujiks in dull reds and blues, prostrating, signing the cross, kissing the sacred tomb; while above them scintillated the prismatic hues of rubies, sapphires, and diamonds, irradiating here and there an uplifted head or a suppliant hand—a most typical picture which nothing in Western Europe can repeat.

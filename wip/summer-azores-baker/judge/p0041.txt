Mr. Irving speaks of them in the Alhambra, and says, “Some are still to be seen among the Moorish ruins, which have been there upwards of eight centuries.” When the Spaniards invaded the Netherlands, the tiles went with them, and their cleanliness made them acceptable to the Dutch. In old colonial days, our forefathers brought them to New England, where we know them as Dutch tiles; but they are Dutch only by adoption. They are still manufactured in the Spanish peninsula. Those in use in the Western Islands, are mostly made in Oporto.

The houses are built in continuous blocks close up to the sidewalk, the lower floor being on a level with it. Either because living in the lower stories would be disagreeable from this circumstance, or on account of the dampness, they are given up to shops, or used only as a sort of inner court-yard from which entrance to the living-rooms is made.

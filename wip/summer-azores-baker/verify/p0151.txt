The <i>carro</i> is a pleasant though not a rapid mode of conveyance. We rode for some miles along the shore between high walls and under trellises covered with great masses of bougainvillea in full bloom. We passed the Consumptives’ Home, a free hospital founded by the Queen of Portugal, whose daughter died here of that terrible disease. Madeira, with a dry, bracing air, and a mean temperature of 65°, is now considered preferable to the South of Europe for incipient pulmonary disease. We alighted at the Portuguese cemetery. The grave-stones contained tintypes of the deceased. All along the walls were bougainvilleas with trunks as large as a child’s body.

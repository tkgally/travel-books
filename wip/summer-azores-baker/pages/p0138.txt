The governor’s residence, a long, yellow building, with arched windows, stands on the quay, from which a fine avenue of sycamores leads up into the town.

Slowly steaming in, we pass an American brig anchored, with a yellow flag flying from her peak. It bodes no good to us.

The bay is full of gayly painted boats, green, yellow, blue, black, orange, with a streak of white or orange at the top, and the keel rising as high as a man’s head above the gunwale at both prow and stern. One bearing the Portuguese flag, and covered with an awning, draws solemnly up to the steamer. It is the “healthy boat,” as our English-speaking Portuguese steward calls it, and contains our consignee and the “healthy doctor” in uniform. Our captain descends the steps. Hats are lifted. “Where do you hail from?”—“New Bedford.”—“How many passengers? How many in steerage? Any sickness? Any deaths?

A dozen times a day I find him smiling at my bedside with a cup of unsavory pottage, and sternly bid him begone. “Ver’ well,” he says, as he goes: “you no eat, you die.” And his argument is irresistible.

Lying on one’s back and looking up at the sky through the twelve hours of these long July days is tedious enough. Dead calm: water glassy—not a ripple; the heat intense; the glare from the sails almost intolerable.

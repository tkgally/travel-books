St. James's Park is situated to the south of St. James's Palace, which became the Royal residence after the destruction of Whitehall Palace. The park was once a marshy meadow, transformed by Henry VIII. into a deer park and riding path. Now it is one of the best parks in London—the broad avenue of handsome trees, the winding expanse of water, enlivened by the water-fowl, the suspension-bridge, and fine view of the handsome and stately buildings round it, make the place very attractive.

After visiting these, we saw Queen Anne's Mansion, near Queen Anne's Gate, and I was astonished to see gigantic buildings of thirteen stories, which Dr. Hopkins and I twice counted over; and in this large building, on the fourth floor (flat), stays the ex-Governor of Bombay, Sir Philip Wodehouse.

After visiting this locality, we went over to the extensive shop or premises of the Army and Navy Stores, in Victoria Street, where you can get anything from a pin to an anchor, and, in fact, all the articles that are to be had on the face of the earth. Hundreds of men, women, and children were assembled there to make purchases. This alone was ample proof of the extensive nature of their trade. The members only are allowed to make purchases.

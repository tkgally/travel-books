# Chapter XXII

[[subtitle]] Mount Ungwala Again

[[subtitle]] Suspicious attitude of the natives—Lost among the rocks—An anxious moment—Speeding the parting guest—Descent into the plains—The Ajuba river—Obliged to abandon stores—Hardships tell on the men—The Akobo again—A difficult crossing—More elephants—Our old camp—Timidity of our friend Olimi—Face to face with an immense elephant—A terrible storm—An opportune messenger from Mc Millan—Relief—Difficult crossing of the Gelo—Through a thick forest—Discovery of a village—Arrival at Komaton—Back on the Baro—Rapidity of our march—A magnificent tusker—Hadji Ali's narrow escape—The caravan paid off.

O<sc>n</sc> the morning of June 16, we broke up camp early and pointed due north on our return journey. We had quite expected that some of the natives would have paid us a visit at daybreak as usual, but not a single one appeared, nor was there a living soul visible in any direction. We had hoped to get some one to guide us out of these fastnesses and though of course our general direction was obvious enough, it was not so easy to find the best path out of these rocky defiles.

Naturally we missed the best way and instead of taking the path that would have led us directly down to the lower ground, and which I believe to have been a tolerably easy one, we got entrapped into a series of bypaths along the side of the mountain, passing at times through large tracts of corn just ripening; but there was nobody to be seen.

While we were looking in ever increasing admiration from north to south, from east to west, over this vast circle of snow, the Sta. Caterina guide kept rooting incessantly round the foundations of the stone man, at the imminent risk of bringing him down on our heads, and at last, from some hole or corner of the structure, he triumphantly produced the inevitable bottle. Of course we turned out its contents and inspected them, and of course, among other Alpine names, we found the card of that indefatigable and ubiquitous climber who, to judge by the constant recurrence of his name in all mountaineering books, can have left few, if any, neglected summits among the Alps on whom he has not paid a morning call; be they high or low, easy or difficult of access, he is on visiting terms with them all.

And now our time is up; one more glance round, one more lingering look, and we leave our exalted resting-place, probably never to return, and struggle on our downward way, through the general overthrow, till we reach the glacier, where all is plain sailing, and partly on snow, partly on ice, we descend to the table land below.

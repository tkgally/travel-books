We had accordingly told Amrhein to go at once to the <i>bureau</i> and look for a guide who could be well recommended. The <i>bureau</i> turned out to be a figure of speech; at least its locality could never be found, but there really was a <i>guide-chef</i>, and after supper Amrhein entered the <i>salon</i> with a face of ill-disguised amusement, to announce that this authority was coming in person to speak to us. With this preface he drew aside, and a queer little figure stepped from behind him on the scene. He had no coat on, and was dressed in the usual rather scanty costume of the country, which gives one the idea, at first sight, that the individual is still wearing, as a full-grown man, the clothes which he got as a small boy, and to which no provident mother had ever made any addition as his length increased; his hair was cut very short behind and left long in front, and he held in his hand a pipe, which I must do him the justice to say he did not smoke while with us. He was altogether a most ambiguous-looking personage; he might have been a goatherd, or he might have been a beggarman, and he was not unlike an Irish pig-driver. He came forward, however, smiling and bowing, and we understood that we were in the presence of the <i>guide-chef</i> of Heiligenblut.

Amongst men who live an outdoor life—farmers, coolies, and labourers—this deformity is not so noticeable; because their legs are given more chance of growing. In stature the men are seldom much over 5 feet 4 inches—about the height of the average European women—and the women in comparison mere dolls.

The costume worn by the shizoku is extremely plain and neat, and varies only from the kimonos of the women in the differences of colour and cut. The chief article of dress is the kimono, which for a man is made of stripcd grey silk, and for a woman of any colour that may take her fancy. It is fastened from left to right, and held in place by a girdle, the obi. Below this is an under-dress also the shape of a kimono, but shorter, and usually of white silk, showing an edge about half-an-inch broad at the neck and breast. Over all, when they walk out, comes kind of Inverness cape, called the haori, made mostly of black or gray silk, and on which the crest of the wearer is worked in white silk; this is fastened loosely in front with white silk cords tied in a sailor’s knot.

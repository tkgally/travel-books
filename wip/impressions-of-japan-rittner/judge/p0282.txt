The answer the Japanese make is by pointing to the cryptomerias which surround the temples like so many sentinels. These trees are the guardian angels of their temples. The winds cannot penetrate through the thickness of their foliage, and for that reason they are preserved. Being so lightly constructed they can also better withstand a severe earthquake, because, offering less resistance, they can shake more easily and with less risk of destruction than if they were built of solid masonry. Whether these answers are correct is matter of conjecture. It is, however, a fact that they do withstand wind, rain, and earthquakes far better than the ordinary Japanese house which is more exposed.

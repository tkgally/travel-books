This road-sweeper superseded the <i>eta</i>, a man who was considered too vile to be reckoned worthy even of notice in the census. The <i>eta</i> were grave-diggers, scavengers, loiterers, the scum of the earth, hardly deserving the name of man; loathed by their fellow-creatures as beneath contempt, touchers of dead bodies, and persons who had no rights at all. The official street-sweeper of to-day—though still the lowest of the low—is treated as a living creature, and is honoured by being included as a “man” in the census. We should consider him rather a boon in a city like London; but then, opinions differ. His vocation is to wander round the towns collecting the paper that is lying about, emptying the dust-boxes which are placed outside of the houses every morning, and generally cleaning the streets. He is undertaker and grave-digger in one; but now he may by hard work aspire to something higher, and has an equal chance with the rest of mankind of rising to a position in the social world of Japan.

# Chapter IV

[[subtitle]] Bathing, Private and Public

I<sc>f</sc> you reach a tea-house after a long day’s walk the inhabitants seem to realise that their guest must be tired. At any rate, whether you are or not, certain formulæ must be gone through unless you are sufficiently fluent in the language to resist the pressure which is brought to bear on you.

The landlord and his family seem to be here, there, and everywhere at once—they run about, shout to each other, and whilst the boy is removing your dusty boots, the others are preparing the room for the honoured stranger. It is almost considered an insult to them to remove your own boots. Shoes are at once forthcoming and placed on your feet, or, if one prefers <i>tabi</i>—a kind of mitten for the feet, reaching only as far as the ankle—instead of felt slippers, they are there for the mere asking.

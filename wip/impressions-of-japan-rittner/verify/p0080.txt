Kioto is the chief town for the manufacture of works of art, priceless bronzes, wood carvings so magnificently coloured as to resemble the works of the finest clay workers. The modern carvings, however, cannot, though still beautiful to our eyes, be compared with some of the carvings of former centuries on the temples at Nikkō. It is hard to believe that carvings such as one sees there can be the work of human hands. At one temple the carving is so deep that birds are depicted life size, one peacock, I remember, at the temple of Yakushi standing out from the background with his tail forming the most perfect fan, each feather exactly carved, the neck craning forward as if ready to pick up food; the depth of the carving from the beak of the bird to the background was 8½ inches. Other smaller birds, standing on a ledge or perched on a branch, surrounded the centre piece, and all most beautifully coloured.

On the ninth day of the ninth month another holiday comes round, also based on some Chinese legend, when a liquor is drunk made from a labiate plant resembling the flower of an antirrhinum, and prayers are offered up to the god of that particular flower.

In October one of the Shichi Fukujin—the seven gods of luck—has his day, because he is the only god who remains in his own temple instead of wandering off to hold a consultation with his brother gods at Izumo. His name is Ebisu, and he is generally depicted as fully dressed (an exception to what is usual amongst the gods), and with a rod, to the end of which a <i>koi</i> or carp is attached. This benign person also wears a moustache and goatee, and on account of his loneliness is particularly honoured in this month.

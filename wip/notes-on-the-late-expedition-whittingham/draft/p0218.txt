The return to China of the Powhattan gave the notification of the shipwreck to the English and French consuls at Shanghai early in March, and it was directly made known to the British commander-in-chief. About three months subsequent to the shipwreck, it was rumoured that the Powhattan carried Russian despatches and a copy of their Japanese treaty to Shanghai, and that these were forwarded by an eminent American firm by the first overland mail. This may be unfounded, and a “pendant” to the story that the Powhattan met a French whaleship, Le Napoleon, lying in a bay near the Russian frigate, and informed the latter of the opportunity of making their escape from Japan; and it is believed that there was an attempt made to capture her, the Admiral leading the boats in their cruise: the whaleship's master had a hint given him just in time to get to sea before the boats could enter the bay: whether this hint was given by the Japanese, or whether the American captain, in order to balance the information afforded on the other side, and thus preserve a new species of neutrality, sent the notice, seems doubtful.

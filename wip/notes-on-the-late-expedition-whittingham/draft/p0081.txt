There was some mystery about the prices of things, for a question on this point from our own interpreter would be answered by the seller in so many hundred copper cash, of which about fifteen hundred are equivalent to a dollar; whereas the chief interpreter, to whom, or to the officials, our money was always taken, demanded only dollars and integral multiples of dollars. By degrees the shopkeepers received the money themselves, when they could do so without official supervision, and their stock was sold much cheaper.

The whole of the foreign coin must sooner or later pass into the hands of the government, as it is useless to the people, who cannot trade directly and freely, and who are punished severely if the police discover that they possess foreign coins. We suspected that the government profited largely by the exchange forced on the people; and subsequent information of the great extent to which the depreciation of the national currency is carried, from the excessive adulteration of their money, made us aware of the reasons for the great jealousy of the government.

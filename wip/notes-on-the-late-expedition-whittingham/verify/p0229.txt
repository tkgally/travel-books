The north-east winds carried us down till within forty or fifty miles of Van Diemen’s Straits, the last two days of the passage having been retarded by strong currents to the northward: when close to the straits the mountains of Kiusu and its smaller sister-isle, shut off the north-east wind, which had been as constant as a monsoon hitherto almost the whole distance from Aian, and the navigation of the straits became most tedious, as we had no steamer to tow us and in a few hours bring us to the open sea, to do which six days were spent.

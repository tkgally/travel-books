The broiling day was coming to a close as we passed the cordon of boats moored across the mouth of the inner harbour, and met the fresh sea-breeze not yet dying away.

The French and British admirals arrived at Nagasaki in their fine frigates, towards the end of September, and were soon followed by the Pique and La Sibylle (French). The sight of so many noble vessels gathered together was grateful to the feelings, despite of the continued indignities offered by the Japanese, and the failure of so many hopes once entertained. It began to be whispered, whether truly or falsely, that urgent requests to be sent against the enemy’s stronghold had been made by several of the captains; and at last it was rumoured that an expedition to the Amùr had been determined on.

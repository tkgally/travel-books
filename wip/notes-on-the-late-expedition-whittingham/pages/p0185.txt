June and July were frittered away, and now August and September, the remains of the summer, were to be passed in performing that which could have been done years ago as easily as under present circumstances.

To return to our visitor, the harbour-master, who diplomatically endeavoured to elicit the fruits of our campaign; and though he failed himself, his adjutants, scampering in their lame fashion about the vessels, probably acquired all the information needed by the Japanese government. He made the old difficulties about furnishing supplies of fresh meat, and the old promises of sending fish and fruits and vegetables, regretting that the number of vessels which had visited Hakodadi had greatly diminished the amount of their supplies.

But our time is short for even a passing glance at a few only of its wonderful processes. Already the waning day admonishes us that we must return to Hilo. I remember that ride home as the time of my first realization of the perfect adaptation of these Hawaiian horses to the land they traverse. It is mistaken and unnecessary kindness to let them mope over these slippery ways. Experience has made their feet as sure as a goat’s; and, like the goat, they can scamper over the rocks better than they can walk. It is only a question of how much the rider can endure of this lively agitation. Challenged by the daring speed of my nearest companion, the spirited and fearless Miss R——, I set out on my return side by side with her, in true Hawaiian negligence of neck and head. We got back in time for a late dinner, so delighted with the novelty of the day’s sights that we forgot to be weary with the day’s travel.

I had heard and read so much of the skill of the natives in swimming and their perfect domestication in the sea, that I very much wished to see some exhibition of their aquatic accomplishments.

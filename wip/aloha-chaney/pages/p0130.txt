Make any thing in nature a subject of real study, and you shall find indifference turn to enthusiasm and ennui disappear. There is so much freedom and such endless variety, even in the household which goes by the same name. The members of the asplenium family, for example, differ at first sight even more than they agree. What a contrast between the <i>Asplenium fragile,</i> brought from the cool cleft of the mountain, where the waters flow unceasingly and rains fall every day, and the <i>nidus,</i> nesting in the old ohia trees of the Hilo woods, and spreading its strong, thick leaves on all sides like an Indian’s feather head-dress, the under surface of each fertile leaf embossed with ashy lines of fern fruitage! Or who that studies these, and reconciles them in his mind with the same family, is prepared for the sudden freak, as it seems, in the <i>Asplenium gemmiferum,</i> where little ferns, the very image of their mother, start out from the leaves of the parent fern, and grow there, with root and stem, until their weight bends their supporting stalk to the earth, when they come of age and start on a career for themselves!

The first cost of the works at Onomea was one hundred thousand dollars, and the annual cost of running them is thirty thousand. No wonder sugar-makers on the Islands must have either reciprocity with America or annexation, with its freedom from consuming duties.

Onomea is on a headland, six hundred feet above the sea. Two ravines converge at its feet, and the bright waters meet there, if not as calmly as in the sweet vale of Avoca, still with a rapture of salutation which is more in sympathy with this impassioned land. A miniature village nestles under its trees, and on the height above it rise the roofs and chimneys of the sugar-mill, the regularity and stiffness of the one made almost picturesque by the rambling and varied character of the other. We make the last ascent, pass the mill and its attendant houses, and dismount before the garden paling which, in true Yankee fashion, runs along in front of the dwelling-house, and defends a patch of flowers from brute invasion.

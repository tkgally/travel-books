And now our prince is coming on board. The king, his brother, comes with him. A retinue of fifteen or twenty men accompany his Highness. The band strikes up Kamĕhamĕha’s March. The bustle of departure, the babble of farewell messages, the last words and final commissions, the hissing of steam, the hauling of ropes, the calls and responses of officers and men, all the seeming confusion which ensues when a hundred minds, at one and the same moment, seek to do a hundred different things, goes on with the music and seems to spoil its harmonies.

But there is something more than harmony of sound in real music; and though this be marred, the other influence prevails. Every faculty feels the impetus of the soul-stirring band, whether the ear is consciously listening or not. As we move into the harbor, its surface ruffled with waves, but sustained by a still deep below, we seem to owe as much to the band on shore as to the crew on deck. The music sustains the toil. A flutter of white kerchiefs on the pier, taken up, when the wharf has disappeared, by the breakers along-shore, and continued out at sea by waves that seem always signalling their sorrowful farewells; and so we go upon our voyage.

How long Old Jacko would have spun his lava yarns, if weariness and sleep had not put in their claims upon us, I know not. He ceased. What is that heaving, rumbling, half-moaning, half-exulting sound which, distantly or near (who can calculate distances by sound in this thin, still, upper atmosphere?), invades my ear? Is it possible that Kilauea’s roaring can be heard thus far, in spite of Mauna Loa’s intervening bulk, or do I hear the waves breaking on the rocks of Kealakakua? Romantic reader, would that I could indulge your fancy; but the truth must be told,—our kanakas were asleep, as we should be, and this was the sound of their snoring.

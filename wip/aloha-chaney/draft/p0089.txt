Every room in it had once been occupied by the children of their large family; but now all had grown up and flown away, and they were left alone. Happily for them as for the people they had so long loved and served, two of their sons were settled in Hilo, in homes of their own. Every day the voices of their grandchildren, at a soothing distance from their own door, could be heard laughing or crying, according to the fortunes of their sport. The great mango trees, with their thick, dark foliage, hid their son’s house from view; but they could not shut out the household cheer which came over, not only in voices but in active little persons, who trooped through the gateway in search of rose-apples or after water-lemons, or with a sure instinct which led them to grandma’s pantry to taste the crisp fruit which only ripens in her oven. Every forenoon the older children meet in their grandmother’s room, and she teaches them. But these matters belong to a later acquaintance with this house.

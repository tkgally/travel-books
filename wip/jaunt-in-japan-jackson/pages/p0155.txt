Having arrived at the race course, His Majesty viewed the sport from the top of the grand stand where he remained, well above the level of his subjects, until four o’clock in the afternoon when he returned by another route, through similarly decorated streets, to the Railway Station.

The visits of the Mikado to the essentially foreign town of Yokohama are very rare and far between, and the curiously balanced native mind attributed all the display made by the residents to an attempt on their part to curry favour with the Ruler of the Country, in hopes that he may deal with them gently when once the new treaties shall have come into force.

These new treaties are forming the subject of much anxious speculation in the minds of the foreign residents in Japan, and Europeans justly view with some anxiety an innovation which will place them at the mercy of Japanese justice, without the right of appeal to their own Consuls. It is a curious sign of the times that special accommodation for foreigners is now being added to all the jails.

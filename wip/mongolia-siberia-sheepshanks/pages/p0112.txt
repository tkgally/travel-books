[[cont]]It is said that they live longer than the men. They marry very young and are often betrothed as soon as born. As in all the uncivilized tribes that I have made acquaintance with, they are careful to prevent marriages among near blood relations, and may not marry within seven generations of the common ancestor. The dowry given with a bride is usually paid in live stock. In one instance that I heard of the dowry given by a wealthy father consisted of 900 sheep, 150 cattle, 300 horses, and 100 camels.[[fnref 1]] They are, I believe, usually faithful in the married state; but sometimes a wife will leave her husband and marry another. The dowry is in that case returned to her father. In such a case the male children remain with their father, and the females with the mother. The bridegroom frequently does not see his bride until the nuptials. At the time of the wedding the groom goes to the house of the bride's father to offer sacrifices and pay his respects. He remains there during the night, and in the morning takes his bride veiled to his hut. His parents and invited guests go out to meet them with samshu and boiled mutton. In his hut the Lamas recite prayers and sprinkle them with holy water. Then the veiled bride is taken to the house of her husband's parents. An offering is made to the domestic fire, which is regarded as sacred, by casting in butter. The bride pays her devotions to the fire upon the hearth and to the Lama, who is sitting down.

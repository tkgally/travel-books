They will understand also, what seems otherwise remarkable, how it was that those great kings were so ready to yield all honour to the God of Israel. If they believed in one great god of the heavens, it was easy for them to identify him with Jehovah. On the other hand, it will be observed how careful the writers and prophets of that period were to set forth Jehovah as being the <i>Creator</i> of the heavens and their host. There has been much the same notion among the Chinese under the word “Tien,” concerning which, and the question whether or not it is a proper translation of “God,” there have been so many disputes among the Christian missionaries in China. It is probable that there, too, there has been for many centuries little more than the idea of an active vivifying principle. In the old Mongol philosophy, as in that of their neighbours the Chinese, and indeed in a hundred mythologies, there was the idea of “Heaven” and “Earth” as representing the active and passive, the paternal and maternal, principles in nature.

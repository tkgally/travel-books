# Chapter VI

[[subtitle]] Lake Baikal to the Ural Mountains

S<sc>peaking</sc> of the projects for union, or, as I pointed out, for such a nearer approximation of the Orthodox and the Anglican Churches as would permit the members of the one to communicate with the other, I found Bishop Benjamin very stiff on the question of the “filioque.” How could there be any union with a difference in the Creed? On my venturing to submit that the nature of the eternal procession of the Holy Spirit is a sublime and inscrutable mystery not clearly revealed to us, and that surely, therefore, the indeterminate doctrine should not be a stumbling-block in the way of fraternal union, he would only reply that the matter must be yet further treated of. The Orthodox Church must desire, for she always prays for, the unity of Christendom. He knew but very little about the Church of England, inquired how many sacraments we own, and whether we possess the Apostolic succession.

And though separated by vast tracts of country, and holding no communication with each other, they have varied in no important respects from their primitive beliefs; they have certainly made no progress whatever towards a better and worthier faith, but rather, whatever change there may have been seems to have been by way of retrogression. And thus, while elsewhere wondrous changes have been going on in the world of thought, these simple tribes have been holding to the rude notions handed down from their forefathers for tens of centuries.

These præ-Aryan tribes appear for the most part to have a belief, which in the past ages was more distinct, but is now very vague and unreal, in a supreme god, or a chief among gods. But some of them, as the Kóch, Bodo, and Dhimál tribes of the north-east, “have no word in their languages for God, for soul, for the future life, nor for sin, piety, prayer nor repentance.”[[fnref 1]] Indeed, it should always be borne in mind that there is in demonolatry no moral element whatever. They have also a crowd of secondary divinities, such as would accord with the needs of their peculiar circumstances, <i>e.g.</i> the genii of the various rivers.

[[cont]]Britain—Mr. Gladstone and Mr. Disraeli. At 10 <sc>p.m.</sc> went to Mr. Pender's to see the telegraphic communication between England and India and America, which was lately completed. The Prince of Wales, the Duke of Cambridge, and many ladies and gentlemen were present. I was introduced to Lady Mayo, Lady Northcote, and some ladies and gentlemen. I was struck at seeing that the Prince of Wales received the answer to his telegram from the Viceroy of India in five minutes. The whole of Mr. Pender's house was very nicely decorated and set up, especially the drawing-room and court-yard.

24<i>th</i>.—Went to the Paddington Station at 3 <sc>p.m.</sc> The train in which we sat arrived at Windsor at 4.20 <sc>p.m.</sc> From the station we drove in the Queen's carriage to Windsor Castle. After going through several fine rooms we went to the lawn where Tippoo Sahib's and other very good tents were pitched, and two or three bands were playing. It was raining till 5 <sc>p.m.</sc>, but afterwards it was clear and the sun was shining beautifully. I was presented to the Queen by the Lord Chamberlain on the lawn. She made a graceful bow to me and asked me kindly whether this was my first visit to England. She appeared to be in good health, and to be a kind-hearted lady. There were many ladies and gentlemen.

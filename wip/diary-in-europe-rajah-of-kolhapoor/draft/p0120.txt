In the evening, with Captain West, a King’s general, and an aide-de-camp, drove in the King’s carriage to see the opera. The English Envoy joined us there. The opera-house is very pretty, and the King’s box and the whole house are nicely furnished. We took some champagne in the refreshment-room, and returned to the hotel at 10 <sc>p.m.</sc> I could not understand the play as it was in French.

The Belgians are noted for farming, and they appear to be quiet and frugal.

<i>5th.</i>—Went to the station at 9 <sc>a.m.</sc> and left for Cologne at 10 <sc>a.m.</sc> Arrived at Cologne at about 5 <sc>p.m.</sc> Drove immediately to the “Hotel de Belle Vue.” It is a good hotel and commands a very good view of the Rhine which flows beside it. The arrangements in the hotel are said to be good and perfect.

<i>6th.</i>—In the morning, took a drive through the principal parts of the city. Saw a great many French prisoners in the streets. Then we went to the cathedral which is a beautiful large carved building. It has very pretty stained glass windows. It is about six hundred years old. There is a silver shrine in it. Then we drove to the museum which contains some beautiful pictures and busts. Cologne is a fortified and curious-looking town. It is full of the French prisoners at present.

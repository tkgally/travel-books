12<i>th.</i>—Called on Lady R——, who was not at home. Then to the India Office, where we saw Mr. Kaye, Sir Bartle Frere, and his brother. Then went to the House of Commons, where we heard the debate on the Irish Land Bill. It was very interesting, important, and historical. Then, with the Marquis of Lorne, eldest son of the Duke of Argyll, went to the House of Lords. This building is prettier than that of the House of Commons. There was no interesting debate in the House. We were standing near the throne. The members of the Government sit on the right hand of the Lord Chancellor, who sits in the middle and puts on a wig. The Opposition party sit on the left of the Chancellor. The House was not full when I was there. We went back to the House of Commons, and heard Mr. Gladstone, and Mr. Disraeli and some others. Afterwards the House was closed for some time, and we returned to our house.

13<i>th.</i>—Went to call on Mr. Disraeli, who could not see me, and then drove to the French Gallery. Some of the pictures were very fine. Called on various people, who were all out.

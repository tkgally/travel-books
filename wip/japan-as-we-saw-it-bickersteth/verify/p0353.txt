It was three miles out of the city, but a Chinese gentleman having put his carriage at our disposal, we had nearly arrived at our destination when the axletree broke; the carriage began to collapse, and we had to jump out at a moment’s notice and walk the rest of the way. After tea at Government House, General Trotter drove us some three miles further in order that we might see the Penang Botanical Gardens, which are even finer than those at Singapore. He then took us safely back to the harbour before the <i>Peshawur</i> sailed again at 6 <sc>p.m.</sc>

In the course of the evening it was found that a poor Chinaman had got on board by mistake, thinking it was the right steamer for Canton, and having discovered his whereabouts, was nearly wild with fright and distress. We had already gone some distance from Penang, but the captain sent him back by the pilot-boat, and the passengers crowded to the bulwarks to see him lowered into it. He looked just like a fat black satin pincushion as he was swung over the side, and we all hoped he would be in time for his own steamer, which was not sailing until 10 <sc>p.m.</sc>

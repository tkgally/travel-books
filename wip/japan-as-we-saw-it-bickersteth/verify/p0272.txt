Reports, however, of further and yet more alarming earthquakes were current in the city. On Friday one of them said that at midday there would be a terrible shock. Midday came, and only a very slight one occurred. The report then promptly changed to “Government had telegraphed to put it off until midnight!” On Saturday morning a post-card arrived from Mrs. Chappell, the missionary’s wife at Gifu, who had greeted us ten days before at the station. When the earthquake began her husband was away on his work in the country, and she was alone in the house with her servants. She was wakened by the paper screen collapsing on one side, and the wall crashing in on the other. In terror she rushed out on the verandah, and was pulled through part of its wall by her servants, escaping in her night-dress into the garden. We heard some of these details afterwards; but her post-card was written soon after the shock. It said she was still in the road, where she had been living all day, terrified and alone, and that she wanted to know whether somebody from Osaka would not come to help her.

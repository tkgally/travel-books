# Chapter V.

[[subtitle]] Further Jottings from our Tokyo Journals.

<i>Oct.</i> 9.—The following morning the two Bishops and Mrs. Bickersteth visited the principal cemetery of Tokyo. My father notes in his diary: “We went to the great cemetery this morning. It was most suggestive of recent progress to find the portion of ground, perhaps half an acre, where the Christians were buried, and also to see many graves surmounted with a cross scattered now and then among the heathen monuments. It is only during the last few years they have allowed Christians to be buried in Tokyo. Mr. Williams, C.M.S. missionary here, had to take his infant to be buried at Yokohama, but now there is no difficulty. The cemetery is beautifully kept. The most costly stones are rough-hewn, with only a smooth tablet for the graven incription.”

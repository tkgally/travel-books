S<sc>pecial</sc> O<sc>bjects of</sc> S. P<sc>aul’s</sc> G<sc>uild</sc>.—1. To offer intercession that God may call clergy and others to His work in Japan, and to enable them to carry it on to His glory. 2. To collect alms for the University Mission of S. R<sc>oll of the</sc> G<sc>uild</sc>.—The Guild of S. Paul is now divided into sixty-six Branches, and has over 2000 Members.

<i>Bankers</i>:—Messrs. Sanders & Co., The Exeter Bank, Exeter.

## N<sc>ote</sc> C.—The Bishop’s Missions at Tokyo.

### S. A<sc>ndrew’s</sc> U<sc>niversity</sc> M<sc>ission</sc>.

Rev. L. B. C<sc>holmondeley</sc>, 1887, Oriel College, Oxford.

Rev. C. G. G<sc>ardner</sc>, 1887, S. Stephen’s House, Oxford.

Rev. A. F. K<sc>ing</sc>, 1889, Keble College, Oxford.

Rev. H<sc>erbert</sc> M<sc>oore</sc>, 1890, Keble College, Oxford.

Rev. F. L. R<sc>yde</sc>, 1891, S. John’s College, Oxford.

<i>The following work is now carried on by this Mission</i>:—

<i>The Divinity School</i>, under the Wardenship of the Rev. H. Armine King, assisted by the Mission. It numbers thirteen Members, who receive careful training in theology, and from time to time go out into the country districts to prove their powers of teaching.

<i>The Night School and Club</i>, conducted by the Rev. L. B. Cholmondeley for clerks in Government or merchant offices. This is attended by thirty to thirty-eight students, and some of its members have been baptized.

<i>Four Mission Districts of Tokyo</i>, named Kyobashi, Ushigome, Mita, and Akasaka. Each, except the last established, Akasaka, has a small Church and native congregation, and in each full parish life is maintained, supplemented by direct evangelistic work

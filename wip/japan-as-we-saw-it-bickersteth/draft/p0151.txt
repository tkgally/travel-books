For three hours the line skirted Mount Fuji; and being one of the most beautiful in Japan, we had constant glimpses of woodland, and waterfalls, and of the “royal mountain,” as my father called it, from every point of view.

It was also a real interest to watch our Japanese fellow-travellers. They seemed most comfortable in a railway carriage; and a lady, who evidently found the effort to balance herself on the high foreign seat rather tiring, soon solved the difficulty by tucking up her feet, and sitting on her heels as usual, though of course at a greater elevation. We heard some quaint stories while in Japan of the first beginning of railways. For instance, one man waited all day at the station hoping the fares would diminish by the evening; and numbers of passengers, by mere force of custom, took off their wooden clogs before entering the train, as if it had been a house, but were greatly discomfited to find themselves shoeless at the other end, having expected the clogs would somehow or other follow their owners!

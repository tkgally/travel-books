When we arrived at the High School, a large building on the outskirts of Osaka, we found our hosts, Mr. and Mrs. Price (the Principal and his wife), and their guests thinking and talking of little else but the events of the morning. They had rushed out of doors, but neither they nor any of the boys had sustained any injury. After luncheon we went all over the schoolhouse, and heard the boys, about fifty in number, translate into English, and work out a problem in Euclid. We also visited their dining-room and dormitories, and on returning to the large schoolroom, my father made a speech to the assembled school, to which one of the boys returned a very grateful answer in English. The school, though only lately opened, is doing a very good missionary work, and since we were at Osaka some of the pupils have been baptized. The Principal said it was rather difficult to secure as many high-class boys as he would like on account of the powerful attraction which draws so many of them to the great schools at Tokyo.

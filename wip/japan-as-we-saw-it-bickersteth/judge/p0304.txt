That evening we all went down to the Mission Church, and my father preached to a large and attentive congregation of the converts. The Mission had at first made extremely rapid progress, and the people had built their own church, partly by help from outside, but mostly by their own exertions. Women had given the proceeds of their knitting, and a farmer a share in the profits of his poultry-yard, and a hotel-keeper a percentage on his till. But the inhabitants of the island of Kiushiu, of which Kumamoto is one of the largest cities, are a peculiarly proud, independent race. Anybody who has studied recent Japanese politics will know that, from the days of the Satsuma rebellion in 1877 to the latest election riots, the inhabitants of Kiushiu have been noted for a strong conservatism that has resented the enlightened policy of the present Government, and has clung, with an almost dogged devotion, to the ways of old Japan.

They included crucifixes, holy pictures, and rosaries, an oil painting of the ambassador in prayer before a crucifix, and another of him dressed in his Italian costume. All had evidently been most jealously guarded during the persecution.

In the same cases, and of even greater interest, were “the ‘<i>fumisita,</i>’ or trampling boards, oblong blocks of metal, with figures, in high relief, of Christ before Pilate, the Descent from the Cross, the Madonna and Child, etc., on which persons suspected of Christianity were obliged to trample in times of persecution, in order to testify their abjuration of the despised sect.”[[fnref *]]

Those cases were indeed a powerful witness to the faith and devotion of the Japanese under severe trial, a witness all the more important, because the attention of many is now caught by the bright attractiveness of the national character, and they rashly decide that there cannot be depth where there is so much of outward show.

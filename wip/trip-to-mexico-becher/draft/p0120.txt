“The most conspicuous ornament was a chain of gold and silver hearts alternate, suspended round his neck, emblematical of the sacrifice in which he most delighted. A more unequivocal evidence of this was afforded by three human hearts smoking, and almost palpitating, as if recently torn from the victims, and now lying on the altar before him.”

Of the temple or chapel it was in, and of another, he says:

“The walls were stained with human gore. ‘The stench was more intolerable,’ exclaims Diaz, ‘than that of the slaughter-houses in Castile!’”

I cannot leave these two horrid stones without mention of the pyramid of skulls of the victims found in this <i>teocali,</i> counted by one of the soldiers at one hundred and thirty-six thousand! “Belief,” says Prescott, “might well be staggered, did not the old world present a worthy counterpart in the pyramid of Golgothas, which commemorated the triumphs of Tamerlane.”

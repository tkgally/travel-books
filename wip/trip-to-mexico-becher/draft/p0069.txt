Boca del Monte is 8,326 feet above the sea, much higher than the valley of Mexico, so that we have to descend on our way there. We have already seen infinitely more than repays us for the long journey we have taken—even the last three hours were more than enough to do that. Were we to turn back now, we should be thankful that we came: shall we be more so as we go on?

The country is now tame, uninteresting, but for three hours or more we keep Orizaba in sight; and soon on the left Malintzi, another snow-capped mountain, named by the Indians after Cortez and his Indian mistress, who so much aided his conquest (they called both Malintzi), appears. It is a great, big, beautiful mountain, but not so imposing as its three volcanic neighbours, only because it is not so tall as they are, by some two or three thousand feet, and they are its close neighbours.

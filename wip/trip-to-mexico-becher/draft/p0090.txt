The men do, and they appear in the ordinary morning dress suit with the black silk hat worn by English gentlemen; but nine-tenths of those we see are of the industrial classes, and their costumes are new to us, and always more and more taking and picturesque the more we see of them. I must not forget the street cries, or rather the many who cry them: there is the water-carrier, perhaps the strangest in get up and appearance of them all; he has two large globular red porous earthern jars suspended from his shoulders, so large, the wonder is how he can carry them; these he fills at the aqueduct and takes from house to house, slaking the thirst of those who stop him on his way. There is the woman with a brazier of hot charcoal and other apparatus all ready, who wants you to wait while she sits on the curb-stone and cooks you some fish—and there is the fish, sea fish from the gulf, 263 miles off, all ready too, to be cooked—and I see she has a bit simmering in a griddle which has a <i>very</i> savoury flavour to the nostrils—wouldn’t you like some?

And now, having brought you here, my dear A——, I can add we have had them all, and even some of that leisure which comes always in little retired places like Jalapa; but we have not been idle. We have been all round and about the town, and even ten miles out of it, to the Village of Coatepec, which is nothing of itself, but is beautifully situated in an undulating, fertile country. We went to it by a tram-road, worked by mules, up and down steeper hills and round sharper curves than ever tram-road traversed before. The mules were poor, half-starved—a sad contrast to those which brought us from Vera Cruz on the English Railway Company’s line, which were well cared for under the eye of their superintendent, and saved from the ill-treatment these poor brutes suffer. And to-day three of our party dined with the superintendent, meeting Mr. T—— and his beautiful bride, to whom we had been introduced in Mexico. He is a Director of the Railway Company, and is here, on its business, on his way to England.

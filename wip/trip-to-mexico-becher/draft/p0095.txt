The main streets are generally crowded, and the mules, donkeys, horses, carriages, carts, vehicles of every description, with their drivers and riders, are continual sights to which our eyes open wide. The caballero, as he prances by on his mettled horse always stops us, that we may see more of him and what he wears and carries about him. If his steed were a Rozinante, the rowels of his spurs might almost be made to meet in its middle.

We like to go into the patio of our hotel when the diligences arrive from Toluca and elsewhere. Six mules, and such a jingling and rattling, as they come in! The passengers seem mostly of the caballero class, and are more given to travelling in this fashion than other people.

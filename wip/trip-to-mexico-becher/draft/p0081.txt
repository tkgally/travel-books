In an hour from leaving the pyramids we are close to the waters of Lake Tezcuco; we run round its northern margin, pass the heights of Guadalupe, and soon enter the station. Night has fallen, and we are in the City of Mexico. Leaving our trunks to be looked after by Van W., we seek and find shelter and rest, in what was the palace of the first Emperor, now the Hotel Iturbide: we have large handsome rooms, with brick floors, but nicely carpeted, allotted to us; there is a French restaurant downstairs, where we are to be fed, and the charges there, and for lodging and attendance, all put together, are to come to something short of two dollars and a half, or half a guinea a-day each—more than a dollar and a half a-day less than the like charges at New York and Southern States hotels.

We send back to the station for our trunks, but <i>not sending the keys,</i> have to put up with the contents of our bags till the morning. Of course we are very much impressed with being where we are,

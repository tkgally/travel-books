The Indians use the jaws for knifes and prepare their skeletons with them. More mountains and palms, and now we are in front of Ladario.

Here the Brazilian Government has recently constructed an arsenal where a thousand men are employed. Some are English and one of our passengers, Mr. Gaston Lavigné is one of the overseers, having just arrived from Europe, after working in the French and English dockyards. From here to Curumbá is 4 miles. Some batteries command the river, being armed with Krupp cannon. Thickly wooded hills, much infested with tigers, follow the road to Curumbá, a place formerly used as a trading station with Chiquïtos Indians, but now a thriving port.

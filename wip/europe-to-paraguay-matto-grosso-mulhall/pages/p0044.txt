The General and Madame Urquiza receive us most kindly. The cultivation of flowers and fruit is the General’s occupation since he gave up politics. The flower garden is of great extent and contains the rarest plants from every quarter of the [[corr globé|globe]]. There are two aviaries containing a number of canaries, cardinals, finches and some strange birds. There is a row of marble vases on the terrace. From this point we have a good view of the gardens and lake; the three principal gardeners are Frenchmen. The General who is very proud of his plants next led us to his winter-garden: in a large marble basin he has a place for growing water-cresses; in another is an artificial fish-pond.

The palace is built in the modern Italian style. We remained two days under this hospitable roof.

We little thought on our departure from San José that in less than a fortnight, it would be the scene of one of the bloodiest deeds recorded even in South America. A band of revolutionists rushed into the palace and murdered the poor old General in his library. Madame Urquiza and family were obliged to take refuge in Buenos Aires, but she has since recovered her property and established a model farm and agricultural colony at San José.

Having taken my meridian altitude, and carried my aneroid a sufficient distance up the western slope to prove the rise of the ground, I set off towards the Rishi ridge, telling Najm where I was going, and giving orders that the camp should be pitched two hours’ march from our last night’s halting-place. As I did not think it necessary to carry on the step-system any longer for my aneroid observations, I took Cash with me. Half an hour’s walk brought me into the most western portion of the double range of Rishi. Here I noticed that the strata had the same dip as those in the main range, a fact which pointed to the conclusion that this promontory had not been affected by the catastrophe which formed the Arabah. The rock of which this range is formed is a crystalline and very brittle limestone, remarkably rich in fossils, and which, I judge, from its nature must have undergone the action of heat.

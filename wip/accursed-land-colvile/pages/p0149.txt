We commenced operations on the beach at seven o’clock on the morning of December 1st, by reading the three aneroids at high-water mark, and noting their differences. Leaving No. 3 in charge of Cash, who had orders to follow in an hour’s time, I mounted my dromedary with Nos. 1 and 2, and started off at a sharp trot for the western side of the valley. After a fifty-minutes’ ride I dismounted, laid out the aneroids, let them rest for five minutes, and read them, Cash at the same moment reading his on the beach. After an hour’s wait Cash arrived, very glad of a rest after fifty minutes’ bumping. The aneroids were compared again, and off I started to read the aneroids again, after an hour’s interval, at the second station. In this way we worked the whole way up the valley; and if any approach to accuracy is to be obtained with aneroids, I ought to have obtained it.

At a distance of eighteen miles from Akabah i.e. five miles to the north of Mekreh Hadîd, the bed of the valley has fallen again to 137 feet above the sea-level, and as no large water-courses here open into the Arabah to the east or west, no <i>talus</i> are formed, and its surface is almost level. This part of the valley during the rainy season is the receptacle of an enormous quantity of water, receiving as it does not only that flowing from the northern slopes of Mekreh Hadîd but the whole drainage of the valley for the next thirty miles. The water thus collected having no outlet can only be dispersed by evaporation, and the result is a deep deposit of silt strongly impregnated with salt.

An hour’s walk brought me to the furthest point which I had reached on Saturday, and in another quarter of an hour I was able to see for myself the downward slope which Peacocke had reported. Slopes are extremely difficult to judge in this valley, the refraction from the hot stones throwing up the distance, and making the spot on which one stands almost always appear to be in a hollow. This peculiarity made it almost impossible to say with any certainty what was the formation of the upper part of the valley. At my feet, to the northward, was an undoubted depression; and on the far side of this depression the ground appeared to rise considerably on the east, and slightly on the west side of the valley.

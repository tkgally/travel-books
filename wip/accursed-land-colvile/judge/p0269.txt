This had been going on for about a quarter of an hour when Najm asked me if I would like to see the governor. “Certainly,” I said, “if he will do me the honour of calling on me.” Whereupon Najm dived into the kitchen, and brought forth a little grey-haired man, in black cloth trousers, with a braid stripe, frock-coat, and tarbush, and presented him to me as the governor. His Excellency had, it seemed, been waiting for me for the last two hours, and had whiled away the time in conversation with Petrōs, before the kitchen-fire. He was a pleasant, intelligent man, with rather an energetic face, and I pitied him sincerely in being buried in such an out-of-the-way place among such a set of lazy thieves. He had been much troubled in his mind about me, he said. He had received orders from Cairo to pay particular attention to three Englishmen who were travelling in the Arabah, and the order had been brought by the same messenger who carried the letter.

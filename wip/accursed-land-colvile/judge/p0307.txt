One would naturally have imagined that the farther one went from civilization the more natural and simple-minded one would have found the inhabitants of the country to be. I found the case to be exactly the reverse. The Tawarah Bedwin whom I took from Suez were, if rather insignificant in appearance and slow in their movements, a pleasant, well-conducted set of fellows. My present companion, also living on the outskirts of civilization, came up, except in stature, very nearly to one’s ideal of the free son of the desert.

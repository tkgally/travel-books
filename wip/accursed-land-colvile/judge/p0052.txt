As far as the eye can reach to the north and south is a perfectly flat plain of clay covered with stones; not a blade of grass, a mimosa or tamarisk bush, not one single scrap of colour for miles and miles; nothing but one perpetual dirty drab. To the east, in the middle distance, a long, low range of sand-hills, and beyond the sand a straight line of limestone cliffs. To the west, some four miles off, a narrow strip of leaden sea, and beyond that the dark mountains of the African coast. May be, as time went on, my eyes got more accustomed to the dreariness of desert scenery, and had I come back to this place on my homeward journey I might have taken a brighter view of it. As it is, it will always leave on my mind the impression of being the most dreary scene I ever beheld.

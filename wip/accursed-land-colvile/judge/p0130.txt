During the course of ages these <i>débris</i> heaps have increased in size, till many of them stretch nearly across the valley, making the course of its narrow bed extremely tortuous. Now it is evident that if two large ravines happened to open into the Arabah exactly opposite to each other, their <i>talus</i> would meet and a barrier be formed right across the valley. At a point thirteen miles from the head of the gulf this actually occurs; the Wady el Amrah disgorging its refuse from the limestone ranges of the west exactly opposite the Wady el Methedi, from the mouth of which rolls out the granite <i>débris</i> of the eastern range. In the centre of the valley the two <i>talus</i> meet, and the first watershed of the Arabah is formed, 252 feet above the sea-level. This ridge is called by the natives Mekreh Hadîd.

I have said that the western range had a granite base overlaid with sandstone, limestone, and chalk.

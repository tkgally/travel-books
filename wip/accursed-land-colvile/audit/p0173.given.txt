For some time they kept to their posts, but gradually, in the excitement of the general hubbub round them, they slackened their hold, and one by one dropped off to join one of the numerous cursing-matches which were going on. Choosing a moment when everyone was engaged, I gave my dromedary a kick behind the shoulder, and started off at a good swinging trot, closely followed by Peacocke and Cash. As I had expected, no one followed for some time; it was certainty that when they found out that we bad gone they would stop for a quarter of an hour to argue about whose fault it was.

Our beasts had been travelling all night, and were not very good ones at their best, so after half an hour’s trot I pulled up to a walk, till presently I heard in my rear a faint cry of “Khawajah! khawajah, bishwaysh!” (gentleman, gentleman; slowly) and turning round saw an unfortunate Bedwin, utterly out of breath, struggling after us.

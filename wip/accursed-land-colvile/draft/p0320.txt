## Return Journey.

| Number. | Date. | Name of place. | Distance from Akabah. Intermediate. | Distance from Akabah. Total. | Remarks. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 1883. Dec. | | Miles. | Miles. | |
| 1 | 24 | Wady el Haserat | 6½ | 6½ | Deep rocky gorge. Very barren. Good road. Might easily be made passable for wheeled conveyances. |
| 2 | 24 | El Kantara | 4½ | 11 | A substantial stono bridge, 30 feet wide. |
| 3 | 25 | Wady Abumazarik | 18 | 29 | A flat-bottomed valley, 5 miles wide (stony). |
| 4 | 26 | Wady Manjar | 14 | 33 | Water collects in considerable quantities after rain. |
| 5 | 26 | Jabal Rishar | 11 | 44 | Remarkable hill, with contorted strata. |
| 6 | 27 | Bir Abasseyeh | 11½ | 55½ | Large stone reservoir. Water impregnated with nitre. |
| 7 | 27 | Wady el Aryt | 8 | 63½ | Water probably by digging. Succulent plants. |
| 8 | 28 | Nakh’l | 27½ | 91 | Stone fort. Capable of holding 600 or 700 men. Plentiful water (good). Two date palms. |
| 9 | 30 | Wady Abukanadu | 16 | 107 | Broad stony plain. Very barren. |
| 10 | 31 | Wady Haritan | 19 | 126 | Road from last station across barren stony plain. |
| 11 | Jan. 1 | Wady el Haj | 7 | 133 | Narrow winding gorge. Good road. |
| 12 | 3 | Suez | 38½ | 171½ | Road from mouth of Wady el Haj, through sand hills. Canal crossed by a wooden swing bridge. Inconvenient at low tide. |

No forage for horses is to be obtained on this route. The route by Nakh’l is practicable for wheeled conveyances throughout its length.

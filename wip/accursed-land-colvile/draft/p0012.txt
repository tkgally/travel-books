It will be noticed that I have made some concession to those critics who objected to my phonetic spelling in “A Ride in Petticoats and Slippers;” I have even gone so far as to meet half-way the gentleman who objected to my spelling of “<i>Wizeer</i>,” by representing the Arabic “yā” by <i>î</i> instead of <i>ee</i>. He would have liked me to write Vizir, but even to please him I cannot represent “waw” by a <i>v</i>, and write about Vadys and Bedvins. By the way, I wonder if the person who is responsible for our pronunciation of Oriental names hailed from Whitechapel; such words as <i>vizir</i> for <i>wizir</i>, <i>caravan</i> for <i>karawan</i>, and <i>’Ind</i> for <i>Hind</i> rather suggest it. I must also refuse to follow in the footsteps of the unfortunate Frenchman, who, having no <i>w</i> in his alphabet, is obliged to write Ouady and Bedouin.

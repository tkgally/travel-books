The sun had fairly risen, next morning, before we were under weigh; so I thought, after the cook’s slackness the day before, that it was about time to give my followers a general stirring-up. Najm listened to my remarks in respectful silence, and when I had done produced his watch showing 6.30 by Suez time.

“If it comes to watches,” I said, “here is one which shows 4.20, which is the time now in London.” This I thought rather a good practical way of showing the effect of longitude on time.

“But <i>my</i> watch very good, sar, given me by Mr. Smith from England—you know him, sar?”—was all the answer I got. I did not think it worth while pushing the lesson in astronomy farther, so simply settled to start at 6 instead of 6.30, by Mr. Smith’s watch.

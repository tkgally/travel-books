They were a very nice, pleasant set of fellows, these Houiât, always taking whatever one chose to give them without grumbling, never asking for presents, and going where they were told and stopping when they were told, without wanting to argue the matter. Their sheikh, Louefeh, was a capital man, as wild-looking a young fellow as one could wish to see, and a man I would a good deal sooner ride beside as a friend, than meet as an enemy. He had none of the cringing, begging ways of Hammid Jad and his tribe, but was always most respectful and obliging, and kept his men in capital order.

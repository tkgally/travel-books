# Chapter XIII.

[[subtitle]] The story of Imri the devil of Abumazarik—A narcotic—The watershed—Bir Abasseyeh—A good mount—An offer of help—A valuable life.

U<sc>nder</sc> that heap of stones lay all that was mortal of one Imri, Imri el Sheitân, or Imri the devil of Abumazarik, as he was called by his acquaintance—a man who had at one time been the terror of the whole neighbourhood—a man to whom nothing was sacred, not even the laws of hospitality which the most reckless men and the most godless marauders were, as a rule, afraid to break. To accept a night’s shelter and a meal, and then to ride away before daybreak with two or three of his host’s camels, seemed as natural and proper a proceeding to Imri, as stealing a Christian’s purse to any one else.

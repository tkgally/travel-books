At the Ras el Barrah, a narrow pass which divides the valley of that name from the Wady el Barak, we reached the height of 3000 feet. The view from this place is rather fine. On either side of the pass are two strangely shaped conical hills, surmounted by castle-like projections of rock. Looking between them one sees at one’s feet the sandy amphitheatre of the Wady el Barrah opening out on the south between the somewhat similar cones of rock at Ain el Barrah. Through the second pass is a pretty peep of the low, undulating country to the north of Sinai, lit up at the time I saw it by a streak of pink from the setting sun, contrasting with the black, frowning rocks of the Ras el Barrah, and the misty purple of the Sinai range which towered above it.

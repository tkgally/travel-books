I had intended to make a section of the valley in the neighbourhood of Ain Defeyeh on my way down, but the news of the governor’s arrival had rather hastened my movements, and I had thought it better to run straight down to the sea first. If I had been able to get the sun the day I arrived, I should have had the next day free for this job, but as it was I thought it more important to get the latitude, so stayed in camp myself while Peacocke went out to run the chain of levels. As it turned out, I should have done far better to have gone out myself; for the sun was clouded at noon, and my life was made a burden to me by the persecution of the people who wanted presents. I must say in justice to Hammid Effendi, however, that, having received the pound which I gave him for his trouble with the barometer, he never bothered me again.

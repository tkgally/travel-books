The country to the south-east of Wady Ghurundel is a very remarkable one. If it is not in its original condition, it must have reverted to the state of this earth when it was “without form and void.” Such a tumbled mass of cream-coloured irregularity never drove a map-maker to despair. In vain I tried to take bearings on one misshapen mound after another, but all the mounds were misshapen, and all alike in their utter formlessness and want of individuality; and their name was legion. There is a plan, familiar to photographers, of getting a picture of the moon as seen through a powerful glass, which consists in stirring up a Seidlitz powder (Eno’s fruit salt also answers admirably) in a soup-plate, with a little water and some plaster of Paris, and photographing the result. The photograph thus produced would also pass well for a bird’s-eye view of the country east of Wady Ghurundel.

[[cont]]a strange place it was for two rival parties of Englishmen to meet in! A hundred miles or more from one of their kind, forced, literally, to jostle each other arm to arm in this narrow gorge, and determined, on no account, to have anything to do with each other. I have since had the pleasure of making Major Kitchener’s acquaintance, and we have had many a laugh over the absurdity of the situation, which was further enhanced by the fact that he and Peacocke had been old friends at Woolwich. In the Wady el Arabah we were deadly foes, and I do not mind owning that I did my humble best to put as many spokes as possible in his wheel. He has never made a similar admission to me, but I do not fancy that he went very much out of his way to assist “the civil engineers,” as we were called by our rivals.

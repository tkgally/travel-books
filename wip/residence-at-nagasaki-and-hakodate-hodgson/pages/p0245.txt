At one o’clock on the day appointed, an interpreter came to say that the Governor had approved of our visit, and that Mrs. Kōwātchī was ready to receive us. Of course we went in all state, my wife in one norimon, the little girl in a second norimon with her maid, and all our party of Chinese and Japanese in their gayest dresses.

The great gates were open as on an official visit, all the minor servants, at the vestibule, were on their knees, and numerous officers with two swords in a second room. There were several children of the Governor waiting about, neatly dressed in crape of all colours, and evidently in their costumes of ceremony.

As it is not the custom for high ladies to move out of their own rooms to receive visitors, although they accompany them on going away to the outer door, we were ushered, in silence and pomp, into their presence. The Ani-san, or mistress of the house, with her mother and children, came to the door and shook hands with us, saying, “ŏhīō,” “how do you do?” and making their very lowest and very gracious bows.

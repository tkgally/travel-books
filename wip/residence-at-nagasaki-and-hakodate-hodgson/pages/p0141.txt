We went over the temple, and met some Government officers, who wanted to lodge us all in one or two rooms; but this could not be accepted, so they gave us, at last, nearly all we thought absolutely necessary. The whole building was in such a state of confusion, that a few days must elapse before I can describe it to you. That same evening we walked over the cemetery, and were surrounded by a not very clean crowd of people, half dressed, who clamorously demanded “bouton cashee,”[[fnref *]] which we at last found out meant a gilded button: they were delighted to receive the very smallest present, even a pin, and were mad with joy. I was still so little satisfied with this people, from yesterday’s excursion and reception in the town, that I had soon enough of them, for it is not pleasant to be followed by a crowd in rags; so I soon left them without seeing much of the country, and returned on board.

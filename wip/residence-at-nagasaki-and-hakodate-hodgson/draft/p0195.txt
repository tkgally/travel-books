# Chapter VII.

[[subtitle]] Temporal Government of the Japanese.

F<sc>rom</sc> the date of our commercial intercourse with Japan, I consider the want of success we have generally met with may be attributed considerably, though not entirely, to our own faults as foreigners.

A trade, and a lucrative one, was opened and established at Nagasaki before the installation of any British Consul. One article, sapan-wood, was sold by a friend of mine <i>for</i> 1<i>s. a lb.</i> for dyeing. There never was a complete understanding between the chiefs of the treaty nations. Each had its own views and interests; no unanimity or sympathetic action existed. One nation had cotton, another manufactures, and a third wines to protect; while Holland was not at first, whatever she may now be, our unprejudiced or our most disinterested adviser. Holland had stakes of interest and of old date, and could not have at first the same interests as ourselves.

Thus, with my maid, I had a party of two gentlemen and four womankind, including a child. I had all my Japanese servants with a red belt, bearing Her Majesty’s arms, around them; and trusting to that symbol of peace, I went unarmed, my only weapon being my court sword, which of course I had a right to carry, although of no use for attack or defence.

I was not quite sure of the result or the success of my expectation with so many petticoats in train; but Church and State have always supported Great Britain, and so they will, please God, in Japan.

Thanks to the commissariat, I had an unlimited supply of horses; and as I thought they were doing their duty quite as efficiently, when carrying two ladies, a maid-servant, and a little child, as cropping grass at Her Majesty’s expense and doing nothing else, I took them.

Had Her Majesty seen her subjects, and how kindly they were received by their new friends, she would not have scolded me, if even one horse had come back sore-footed, which, happily, did not occur.

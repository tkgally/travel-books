As we came down again to the sea we found the beach very rocky, so we mounted a second hill with dangerous ravines, about 1200 feet deep, covered with ferns, where horse and cattle browsed pleasantly.

Thence we saw Cape Yezan and lost sight of dear Hakodate. The descent was very precipitous; everybody had to dismount; but we found at every few hundred yards a nice bench with a fine sea view, whereon to rest; proving that the Japanese, when tired, rejoice also in the beautiful.

There was nothing very remarkable betwixt this and Sirouey, where we arrived about noon, and found a comfortable breakfast in a fisherman’s hut. Fortunately we had taken Mr. Feltoe’s preserved meats with us, with some live fowls and vegetables; for though on the sea-shore we could obtain no fish of any kind, but any amount of sea-weed and rice, which our ladies did not vastly approve of, and pronounced inferior to Véry’s <i>cuisine</i>, and yet they are not very difficult to please.

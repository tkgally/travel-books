Who had exchanged the most rice and wheat for the pure gold of Nāmbŭ, Meno, and Kāgŏsĭmă? Whose mulberry trees nursed the best silkworms? Whose plantations afforded the greenest tea? By these signs a neighbour judged his neighbour. Wealth in kind purchased the gold of Matsmai and the silver of Sado Island; wealth in kind nursed and fed either the thousand or ten thousand warriors ready to draw the bow or wield the sword in defence of their lord and master; and in proportion to the number of his adherents and followers a noble was esteemed and courted.

Thus, without money there were followers; and when a Tȳcŏsāmă summoned his faithful Daimios, that man would enjoy the greatest favour and honour, who could bring with him the greatest army of retainers without impoverishing his principality by a too heavy drain on its labour.

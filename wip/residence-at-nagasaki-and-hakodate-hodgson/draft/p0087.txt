The “Ainos” are, I understand, the despised aborigines of Japan! Their number does not to-day exceed 80,000; but, if one were to take the head of a Socrates, or other learned Greek, into one’s hands, and give him a long black beard, with long black hair, not scrupulously neat or very clean, and then clothe him in nature’s garb, simplicity, you would have some idea of an “Aino” man. They are strong and muscular; but they are despised as Jews are by the Arabs; yet, in my own poor opinion, they are as fine a race as most demi-savages.

The women have a profusion of black flowing hair, combed “long, long ago;” their appearance is not the most cleanly, but they seemed so humble that we pitied them. Barring this long hair, with its untidiness, they (the ladies) have a tattoed upper and under lip, beautifully blue: it might please the poet, not the man. Long hair uncombed for ages, and blue tattoed lips, are not the most inviting or most pleasing,—so I thought at least.

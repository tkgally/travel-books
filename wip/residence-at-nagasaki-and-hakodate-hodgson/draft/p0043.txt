On his arrival at Nagasaki Mr. Alcock “found fifteen square-rigged vessels in the harbour.”[[fnref *]] For two hundred years previously two Dutch ships and two Chinese junks had been the only annual visitors allowed to trade with Japan; but Mr. Alcock continues[[fnref †]]: “fifteen thousand tons of shipping appear to have found profitable employment here within the last six months.” The British traded here, having already taken advantage of the favoured-nation clause of Admiral Stirling’s Treaty of 1855: the Americans and Dutch also had their merchantmen in the port. Japan had hitherto been a self-supporting country, but here was a sudden drain on her resources! here a colossal demand upon the internal produce of a state scarcely opened to trade! Fifteen thousand tons of shipping already supplied, and the harbour still swarming with greedy vultures, waiting to bear off for ever the exuberance of her poverty or wealth!

As the Tēră (temple) is always open, worshippers come in and out as they please, taking off their shoes at the vestibule, and either standing or sitting down within the precincts of the house. Their adorations are frequent; more like those of Mussulmen than anything else, and generally three in number, the forehead each time touching the mats, which are scrupulously clean.

I never saw a “quête” publicly made, it I except the congregation throwing in a mass on their mats their cash; but there are so many “troncs” all about the Tēră, of most capacious bulk, that the faithful have little difficulty in finding a receptacle for an offering, generally a few “sapecs,” or cash.

The beggars are always at the door, seated rows, on mats, waiting for the bounty of the charitable; but if anyone passes by these almost legally recognised mendicants without dropping his sapec on the mat, he or she is immediately made aware of the illiberality by some not over-gracious epithet.

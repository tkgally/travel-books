## Description of the Japanese Coins on the Preceding Pages.

No. 1. <sc>Gold Cobang</sc>; worth on our arrival in 1859 about 6<i>s.</i> 4<i>d.</i>; in 1860 worth about 1<i>l.</i> 1<i>s.</i>

2. <sc>Gold Piece of Two Itzaboues</sc>; rings added for a brelogue, value 3<i>s.</i> 4<i>d.</i>

3. <sc>New Silver Itsaboue</sc> of Mexican dollar silver, made expressly for foreigners; two to one dollar; weight for weight.

4. <sc>The Original Itzaboue</sc>; three to a dollar in 1859; in 1860 two only were sometimes offered.

5. A <sc>very Old Silver Ichou</sc>; worth 5<i>d.</i>

6. A <sc>Nichou</sc>, half silver and half gold; worth about 10<i>d.</i>

7. <sc>The New Nichou</sc>; with more gold in it, and smaller.

8. A <sc>handsome Piece of Copper Money</sc>; fifty-two for a dollar in 1859, and forty-six in 1860. The square hole in the centre is to enable the owner to carry many of them more easily, a piece of rope, bamboo, or other flexible matter, being passed through the hole; and thus all copper coins are carried, and exposed for sale at the exchange.

9, 10, and 11, are other copper coins; and one can easily discover the precautionary notice for preventing exportation of copper coin; it is cheap, and was nearly the only coin the Japanese people knew. The exporter made 200 or 300 per cent. profit.

# XII.

[[subtitle]] <sc>Expedition to the Bromo.—Native Life as Seen from the Railway.—Pasoeroean.—Paserpan.—Satu Per Sen.—Roadside Incidents and Reflections.—Poespoh.—Mine Host.—A Moonlight Scene.—Road making on the Hills.—Scenery and Cultivation.—Birds and Flowers.—Nine Lives wanted, but where are the Eight?—Tosari.—Impoliteness of Nature.</sc>

AN expedition to the Bromo is required of all who would say that they have visited Eastern Java, and being anxious to fulfil all such requirements, I made up my mind to achieve the Bromo at all hazards. The expedition is over, and I am still alive to tell the tale, though how much longer I shall survive the effects of the journey is a matter of doubt, if not to the doctor's perceptions, at least to my sensations, for I have come down from the Bromo afflicted with a most distressing cold, and write my experiences with an array of medicine bottles in front of me. My catching cold in the crater of a volcano shows how utterly and disgustingly inconsistent with herself Nature is sometimes. Let no one undertake the Bromo expedition who is not prepared to face a considerable amount of fatigue and discomfort, and on the other hand let no one be deterred from it by the fear of catching cold, for my experience, if not unique, is not common. The first discomfort is having to get up before 5 a.m. to catch the early morning train to Pasoeroean.

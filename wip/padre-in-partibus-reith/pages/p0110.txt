It surely cannot be the intention of a civilised nation like the Dutch to destroy all manliness in their subject races, and yet that must be the effect of a regulation so humiliating.[[fnref *]]

After an hour and a half or two hours of toiling up hill, the ponies suddenly stopped, and looking out I saw that we had arrived at a hotel. The landlord hastened to meet me, his countenance was rubicund and his general appearance such as one rarely sees out of a French comic paper. An experimental conversation at the beginning showed that the medium of communication between us must be French, but I was relieved to find in the hotel a Dutch gentleman with his wife, who both knew English, the former speaking it extremely well. He puzzled me at first by the drift of his questions, which he began to ask the moment I arrived.

“Are you indisposed?” he asked. I am sure I did not look indisposed. “No,” I answered, “I am as strong as a horse, and I am bound for the Bromo.”

“Oh well,” he said, “a stay here is very good before going to the crater.” Meanwhile I had paid off the dog-cart, told the landlord I would spend the night there, and made some necessary changes of raiment. As we sat down to tiffin, the questioning was resumed.

[[fn *]] I am informed by a Dutch gentleman that it is incorrect to say that the Dutch <i>insist</i> on this practice. I gladly withdraw the word. My informant says that the natives have always done it, being accustomed to greet their own chiefs and rulers in this manner. It remains to be explained how every white man is regarded by the natives as taking rank with chiefs and rulers, and the history of European relations with Java and the adjacent islands during last century and the early part of this, will be found to contain the explanation.

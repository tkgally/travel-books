# XIX.

[[subtitle]] <sc>Advantage of Seeing the Capital last.—Batavia.—The Business Quarter.—Weltevreden and the Koningsplein.—The Schutterij.—Waterloo Plein and the Lion of Belgium.—Historical Fictions.—The Ethnological Museum.—The River.—The Fort.—Vehicles.—Clubs and Bands.—Reputation for Unhealthiness.—The Achinese War.—Overwhelming Attractions of young Englishmen.—Solacements in Exile.</sc>

IT is an advantage to go from the confines to the centre of a great country, instead of going from the centre outward, for travelling about at the extremities of the capital's influence, where nevertheless its influence is strong, one is prepared to visit the capital with a more intelligent interest than if he had begun his journey there, even though he had primed himself with the information of guide books and histories. A persuasion of this kind follows the traveller as he moves slowly inward from the circumference, through deepening life and activities, toward the centre; and by the time the capital is reached the wanderer has gathered, perhaps unconsciously, a mass of information both important and trivial that enables him at once to look out for what is most worth seeing and to see it with more understanding. It is in this way that I have seen Netherlands India. Beginning with occasional picnics to the Karimon and other small islands, and with two flying visits to Deli and Langkat in Sumatra, my route has been from the outskirts of civilisation to the centre of fashion and Government, from Celebes <i>via</i> Sumbawa, Lombok, Bali, and provincial Java to Batavia.

# XVI.

[[subtitle]] Railway Orthography.—A Rough Road.—Cephalentomology.—Kali-Bening.—Siva and Buddha.—Parambanan.—The Temple of Siva.—Amateur Art-Criticism.—The Temple of Nandi.—The Temple of Vishnu.—Meditations on the Devil.—Lingering Paganism.—Jargon.—Jokjakarta Again.—The Aloon-aloon.—The Waterkastel.—An Asiatic Characteristic.

THROUGH the stupidity of a hotel-boy I missed the train from Jokjakarta to Brambanan, and in Java, when you miss a train; you have to wait till the next day for another. Brambanan is the railway spelling of Parambanan; to save space and printer’s ink railway authorities take liberties with orthography everywhere; one great English Company, for example, spells “the grey metropolis of the North” <i>Edinbro,</i> and another <i>Edinboro’</i>, probably supposing that the full spelling of the name was Edinborough. Having missed the train, and not wishing to lose a day, I hired the carriage that had taken me to Boro Boedoer, and did the journey by road. It is a distance of twelve Java <i>pals</i> (nearly twelve miles) from Jokja, and can be done in little more than an hour and a half. But the traveller is severely shaken who goes to Parambanan by road, for instead of bowling swiftly along a smooth and well-kept highway, he bumps along a broken and irregular track, the ponies taking flying leaps from ridge to ridge, and playing a lively game of cup-and-ball with the carriage and its occupant.

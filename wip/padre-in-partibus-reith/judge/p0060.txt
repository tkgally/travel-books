Darkness as of night spread over the surrounding sea and islands for hundreds of miles, and the sea was covered with a scum of pumice and volcanic dust to the depth of several feet. In short, reading the various narratives of the Temboro eruption is like reading a detailed prediction of the Krakatau eruption, names only being changed. This fact will enable the gentlemen of the press to write full and accurate reports of the next eruption in the Archipelago, before it happens. Temboro is silent in the meantime. No sign of activity was to be seen issuing from its wide-mouthed crater. It is now one of the safety-valves of the earth, and the next cataclysm, when it occurs, will not be in the neighbourhood of Sumbawa.

By noon on the following day (March 11th) we had sighted the small islands and reefs off the west coast of Celebes, and like a grey mist in the distance, the lofty head of Bantaeng. Night had fallen before Macassar was reached, so we dropped anchor in the roadstead, going alongside the wharf at daylight next morning. Before the return of the <i>Glatik</i> with news of our safety, the merchants of Macassar had settled in their own minds that we were at the bottom of the sea or, as a mild alternative, burned to death, the <i>Teresa</i> having some cases of petroleum on board. Re-insurance could not be effected even at fifty per cent. premium, and I think there must have been a shade of disappointment in the relief of the Macassar people when they learned that the <i>Teresa</i>, ten days overdue, had met with nothing worse than the breaking of a shaft. It does not seem to have occurred to anyone that the missing steamer might be lying at Ampenan.

# VI.

[[subtitle]] Batoer.—Temboro and Krakatau.—Macassar.—Supposed Fate of the “Teresa.”—Amenities of Weather.—The Town from the Hotel.—Former Glories of the Bugis.—Native Philology.—Amateur Ethnology.—Amusements in Macassar.—Three Days’ Rigorous Imprisonment.

W<sc>e</sc> left Ampenan on the afternoon of March 9th, with a good steel rope between the <i>McAlister</i> and the <i>Teresa</i>. The weather at first was not propitious for towing,—rain, wind and a choppy sea, but ere long it quieted down, a favourable wind set in, and all anxiety about the strain on the tow-rope set at rest. When the rain cleared off the crater of Batoer in Bali was seen to the west of us, a brilliant glow alternating with flashes of light, like the appearance of a smelting furnace when the door stands open. Next morning another famous volcano was in sight, Temboro on the island of Sumbawa. On April 11th and 12th, 1815, Temboro burst into eruption with frightful violence, destroying nearly the whole population of the island, and scattering ashes and dust for incredible distances. Lady Raffles in her memoir of her husband, preserves a number of independent accounts of this eruption, and it is interesting to note how they correspond, almost in every detail, to the oral and written narratives of the Krakatau eruption of thirteen years ago.

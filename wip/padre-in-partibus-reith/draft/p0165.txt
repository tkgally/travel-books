“Do you speak English, sir?” I begin.

“O-yes!”

“I am very glad you do, for it is several days since I either heard or used my native tongue.”

“O-yes!”

“It is very beautiful country in this neighbourhood.”

“O-yes!”

“Do you live here, or are you like myself a visitor?”

“O-yes!”

“I don’t quite understand. I meant to ask if you stayed here, if you are a resident, you know, or a traveller?”

“O-yes!”

I take it for granted he is a traveller after this, and then ask:

“Are you going East or West?”

“O-yes!”

Conversation thus leading me into a <i>cul-de-sac</i>, I desist from it, and relapse into moody silence. Sometimes I try Malay, but in the first place the dialect spoken here differs in many ways from ours, and in the second, I have an impression that the Dutchmen take even less pains to learn Malay than the English do.[[fnref *]] French is a better medium of communication.

I visited none of the craters accessible from Garoet, for I went there to rest after the laborious journeying of the previous fortnight. The favourite excursion is to the crater of Papandajang, because it is the least trying; but one can see the crater without climbing the mountain, for it is a great hole blown out of the side of the hill, from the middle of which issues a long jet of steam.

Now for the real Bangkok as I saw it.

Traces of the recent trouble between France and Siam were visible at the bar, where a line of hulks sunk by the Siamese in 1893 leaves only a very narrow passage for ships. It will be remembered that two venturesome French gunboats the <i>Comète</i> and the <i>Inconstant</i> came through this narrow channel in the wake of the <i>Jean Baptiste Say</i>, which was promptly sent to the bottom by the Siamese. They then forced their way past the Paknam forts, which the visitor to Bangkok eyes from the steamer-deck with considerable interest. In my imaginative picture of Bangkok, I had placed these on the wrong side of the river. They stand out white on a green promontory, in a commanding situation, and one wonders how the two small French gunboats could have run the gauntlet of their heavy guns, if there had not been treachery at work. The non-French Europeans at Bangkok do not hide their suspicions that there <i>was</i> treachery at work, and with this fact in mind one does not know whether to regard the forcing of the Menam as an act of brilliant daring or as a stage-trick played for the astonishment of the European gallery. I cannot repeat all the wild theories on this point which were poured into my ear as solemn assurances, and I do not believe that the true history of the incident will be told in our time. The Muse of History is of doubtful parentage, and the theory that the Father of Lies was one of her progenitors is gaining ground in my mind. Her mother was probably Patriotism, or her sister Prejudice.

# District of Hakone.

YOKOHAMA to Miyanoshita is an exceedingly pleasant trip, and is made inside of five hours. By taking the train at eight o’clock in the morning, Kodzu (thirty-one miles, fare ninety-three sen) is reached at about half-past ten. From Kodzu station, if the atmosphere be clear, the upper half of Fuji is distinctly seen.

The tram car starting from Kodzu first traverses the length of the town; then through a fine avenue of pines shortly enters the main street of Odawara <i>(O-dah-wah-rah)</i>, three miles in length; thence through the Hayakawa <i>(Hah-yah-kah-wah)</i> valley follows the course of the river of the same name to Yumoto (eight miles, fare fifty sen). Beer, cakes, etc., may be obtained at several tea-houses in Yumoto; for although there is quite an extensive inn at the farther end of the bridge, the proprietor does not care for foreign custom.

[[image: Covered bridge and inn buildings at Yumoto nestled against wooded hills]]

At Yumoto, jinrikishas are taken for Miyanoshita (two men necessary; fare in good weather twenty-five sen, double fare if stormy).

Crossing the bridge and turning to the right, the road is a continuous ascent for nearly five miles.

From the picturesque village of Tonasawa <i>(Toh-nah-zah-wah)</i>, less than half a mile from Yumoto, is seen a white Russian chapel across the gorge on the side of the opposite hill. From here on the road presents a variety of mountain and valley scenery seldom surpassed. Winding along the side of the mountain, twisting like a snake around corners, every turn brings into view a different picture. The roar of the river at the bottom of the gorge on the right is never absent, while on the left numerous waterfalls attract the eye.

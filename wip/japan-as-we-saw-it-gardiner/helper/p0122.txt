## English and Japanese.

### Pronunciation.

Letters of the English alphabet, when used to form Japanese words, are pronounced as follows:—

| A | like | a | in | father, |
| E | “ | e | “ | obey, |
| I | “ | i | “ | machine, |
| O | “ | o | “ | bowl, |
| U | “ | oo | “ | moor, |
| AI | “ | i | “ | isle, |
| AU | “ | ow | “ | how, |
| SH | “ | sh | “ | shall, |
| HI | “ | she | “ | shear, |
| CH | “ | ch | “ | chance. |

Consonants are pronounced as in English.

<i>G</i>, used elsewhere in a word than at its commencement, usually is sounded like <i>ng</i> in gong. In the province of Kyushu, however, it is pronounced like <i>g</i> in good.

Where a word ends in <i>su</i>, the <i>u</i> is silent, making the rendering of the word’s final nearly like the sound of <i>ss</i>; for example, Tokitsu is pronounced <i>Toh-kee-tss</i>, with a dwell upon the last syllable, as in the termination of the English word <i>admits</i>.

Where a syllable ends with <i>n</i>, the preceding vowel is pronounced as it would be in English.

There is no difference in the Japanese language between singular and plural.

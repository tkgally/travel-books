Ojigoku <i>(Oh-gee-goh-koo)</i>, elevation thirty-five hundred feet, known as the “big hell,” or place of boiling mud, is a pleasant walk of about five miles; or if disinclined for ten miles’ pedestrianism, a mountain chair carried on the shoulders of four coolies may be utilized.

A favorite day’s excursion is to Hakone, six miles in chairs, $1.40 each. This mode of conveyance lacks the comfort of the jinrikisha, yet is not disagreeable so long as the coolies walk; but when they strike a “dog-trot,” the passenger having due respect for his spinal column prefers to get down and walk. The path is an ascent, but quite shaded for the first mile and a half, to Ashinoyu <i>(Ah-shee-noh-yu)</i>, where there is a semi-foreign inn, with sulphur baths. From here onward, passing the stone monuments dedicated to the Soga brothers, and a little farther the image of Jizo <i>(Jee-zoh)</i> carved in relief on the rock forming the mountain side, but few trees are seen.

“Then would your Government have any objection to my going to Baku, and travelling across the Caspian?”

The official smiled at my perseverance.

“You may go to Baku as much as you like. Baku is a commercial port, we cannot prevent you from going there.”

“Thank you,” I said, “I am very much obliged to you,” and I was, for I wanted an excuse to visit Baku and cross the Caspian.

I next turned my steps to the Persian Consul-General, the famous Mirza Reeza Khan, who some time ago was commonly reported to have been engaged to be married to Miss Nikita, a well-known American prima-donna, who had given a series of concerts at Tiflis.

This wily Oriental diplomatist, who has since been promoted to St. Petersburg, received me with fitting dignity. He was magnificently installed in a sort of brummagem palace. I told him I was an English tourist, and that I wished to have my passport viséd for travelling in Persia.

Mirza Reeza Khan looked at me dubiously, and an incredulous smile lighted his countenance.

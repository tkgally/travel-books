Yet the Armenians are a convivial race, they are fond of the pleasures of the table, and will, when establishing a “feast,” spend an incredibly long time at the festive board; but on these occasions they will sit side by side with their wives and families, and behave in a decorous and thoroughly civilised manner.

Young Armenians, who had been educated in Paris, and had tasted the delights of the wild life of the <i>Quartier Latin,</i> confidentially confessed to me their disenchantment on returning to their native land, where they found the conventional and moral codes much more stringent than in the gay capital of pleasure. As soon as a young Armenian arrives at the age of discretion he is expected to marry, and old bachelors are regarded with an unfavourable, not to say a suspicious eye. The young women are most carefully brought up and watched over. I never heard of, or saw, an Armenian woman of the unfortunate class. Constantinople is full of Greek and German women of doubtful character; but there are no Armenians, so far as I could ascertain, in the ranks of this class of women either there or at Tiflis.

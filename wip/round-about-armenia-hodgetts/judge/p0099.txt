In Russia the lives of the Armenians are at least safe. They are permitted to amass fortunes, to teach, to follow the learned professions. They may enter the service of the State, they may hold commissions in the army, they may practise at the bar. Russia is a great country with large trade interests—there is room for everybody. If the Armenians are threatened with extinction as a race, it is better that they should be absorbed by a civilised state like Russia, than be exterminated by Turkey. Possibilities are open to them as Russian citizens which must for ever be denied them as subjects of the Sultan. They therefore pretend they would like to see Turkish Armenia annexed by Russia.

With this party the question of religion does not carry much weight. It is tainted with the liberal tendencies of a certain school of Russian thinkers not altogether inimical to Nihilism, and it secretly hopes that the present condition of Russia will not last. It looks forward to a great Slavonic republic, of which Armenia shall form part, a sort of confederation of Eastern European races, which will be allowed individual autonomy and scope to develop separate national characteristics. In Poland and on the Balkans we shall find similar dreamers. Stambuloff himself was once a believer in this view.

They have little in common with the narrow spirit of a “zoological patriotism,” as Professor Solovieff designated it, which has animated the ruling powers of late. They cannot understand the fanatic Slavophilism of the new school of Russian patriots, and think that education and commercial enterprise are better calculated to help on the progress of a great country with vast natural resources than the shibboleths of mystic dreamers. For this reason Odessa is regarded as a disintegrating force in the Russian empire, as unpatriotic, as disaffected, and hence the Government is doing all in its power to deflect from Odessa the trade which used formerly to flow to it.

This policy has only been partially successful. Odessa still is, and will probably always remain, the commercial capital of the south of Russia, notwithstanding the fact that Kieff is the headquarters of the military district, and Novorossisk, as we have seen, the depot of the grain trade.

The enterprising and wily Greek and Jewish merchants of Odessa are likely to make a stubborn fight for their commercial existence; they are resourceful, self-reliant, and wealthy, and moreover educated. Nevertheless the town of Odessa is feeling the consequences of the distrust of the Government, and complaining of bad trade. The good old times have passed, it is said, and all that people can do is to live.

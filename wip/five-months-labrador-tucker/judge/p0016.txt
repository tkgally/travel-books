# Chapter VI.

White inhabitants of Labrador; their character and pursuits—Source of industry.—Animals and birds of the peninsula.—Eggs on Mecatina isles.—Disputes of the English and Yankee fishermen.—Manner of taking seals; and season of the fishery.—Mode of constructing habitations.—Character and occupation of females.—Improvidence and ignorance.—Scarcity and value of the Esquimaux dogs. <pref>106</pref>

# Chapter VII.

Origin of Labrador.—Account of the natives.—Northern Indians.—Their manners and customs; their doctors or conjurers; neglect of the sick and the dead; singular traditions.—Opinion of the Greenlanders and customs of the Esquimaux respecting their condition; general prevalence of superstitious belief and universal ignorance. <pref>125</pref>

# Chapter VIII.

Preparation for return to Yankee land.—Gale at sea.—Arrival at Bonne Bay.—St. John's islands; natural curiosity known as “Jacob's Well.”—Departure.—Storm.—Arrival at Cow Bay in Cape Breton island.—Sydney.—Description of the important resources of coal mining; its present condition.—Departure from the bay.—Arrival at the home of the Author.—Conclusion. <pref>159</pref>

They are in general a timorous people, and strike their breasts, in token of peace, when they approach strangers. Their dwellings in winter resemble caves, or holes, dug in the earth; and though generally comprising but one apartment, it is not unfrequently occupied by several relatives, with their wives and children. In summer, they frequently shift their abodes; and then live under tents, made of skins stretched upon poles stuck in the earth, and drawn at top into a conical shape. They keep a great number of dogs, which guard their habitations, and draw their sledges. Occasionally their dogs are used for food, and their skins converted into clothing. But the greatest luxury of an Esquimaux is seal blubber, which he devours with avidity, and to excess.

But the natives of the interior; and the shore inhabitants of whom we have been speaking, are called upon neither to pay taxes nor tithes; and so long as they refrain from public violence and interference with the wcalthier and aristocratic classes—so long as they are content to be peaceable hewers of wood and drawers of water to the “upper ranks society”—and submit to the exactions of the traders—they are let alone by the authorities both in church and state! It is a rare thing to see a civil magistrate leaving his stately dwelling in St. Johns, or placentia, to go among these rude people, to protect the innocent, or punish the guilty. It might soil the ermine of these dignitaries to come in contact with these poor fishermen, and skin-clad hunters! They are consequently left to settle their own disputes in their own way, without interference or oversight on the part of the magistracy.

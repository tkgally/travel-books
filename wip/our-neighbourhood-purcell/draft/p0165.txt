She’s pleasant to converse with, too. “Is she fond of cats?” “Oh! yes, and the master delights in them too. He keeps quite a number. <i>Neko</i>, did the gentleman say? No, no, this is not a <i>nedzumi</i> or rat killer, he’s a <i>heko</i>—a <i>hebi</i>, or snake killer, and that tortoise-shell fellow who has just come through the hole in the paper door is a <i>tôko</i>, or <i>tori</i> (bird) killer. <i>Otoko</i> did the gentleman say? Ah! I see he’s joking, and I am such a poor hand at finding out a joke. Is it not strange the power that cats are possessed of? I have heard the master say that in noble families when a member dies and lies in state, a <i>wakizashi</i> or short sword is placed beside his hand to enable him, if brought to life again by a cat sitting upon his body, to kill his tormentor. With us poor folks it does not matter, though, as we are hurried into our coffins at once. Extraordinary, indeed, Sir! Then again,—but I hear the master calling for me and I must go. Good bye, Sir. Won’t you come again?”

[[cont]] the ‘ten thousand pleasures,’ when friends, gathered round the fire on a moonlight night or snowy morning, tell tales with hearts to which care is a stranger. And when one’s loneliness and melancholy are dispelled by the advent of a friend, how one’s bosom expands under its genial influence! Who refuses a gift of fruit and wine from ‘behind the curtain’? Who is insensible to the comforts of a small snug room in winter time when the snow is on the ground and the wind howling without, the kettle bubbling on the fire, and by your side an intimate friend? Under such circumstances is it not right pleasant to have a fill of <i>saké</i>, without which your enjoyment is not complete? Remember that the proverb says, ‘drink and sing while you may, for one inch before you reigns black night.’”

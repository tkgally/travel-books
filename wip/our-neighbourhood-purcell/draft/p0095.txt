“Many many years ago,” he commenced, “before the tide of western civilization had set in upon our shores, and the people of Japan had not yet abandoned the customs of their ancestors; when men still wore the costume which for centuries had been recognized as their national dress, and shaved their head and beards as becomes decent and respectable people,”—the little barber here heaved a sigh and said “aye! aye!”—whilst yet the <i>Yamato damashi</i>,” the spirit of old Japan, was abroad, and retainers of a chieftain still loved and reverenced their lord, and the lord was a father to his dependants,—in such a good old time, I say, a certain Daimio renowned for his wisdom and benevolence, assembling his household together, recounted to them the following story.

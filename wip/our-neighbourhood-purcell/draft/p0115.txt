Make way for a new worshipper when you have said your prayer and wakened up the deity by a tinkle on the bell. He may be asleep, or perchance he is hunting, you know; and be sure you don’t forget the money-box. “But I’m leaving the neighbourhood, and shall enlist under another god in a day or two.” “The greater reason why you should act liberally by the present one. Don’t you know that he hands over his clients when they change their quarters, and would be able to say a good word or two for you. Who has kept you in health for the year past? Who has saved your house from fire and storm and the fearful lightning? Out upon you for an ingrate; an’ you cheat his godship, I would not wear your <i>obi</i> for a Daimio’s revenue.” And so the money flows in, and the rubicund priest’s eyes twinkle till he seems to be winking to himself, as he rubs his fat hands together and thinks of the eels and <i>saké</i> in the box. And indeed a wise man will not offend a priest. He has much in his power with a superstitious people. He has been known to tumble down the headstones in a graveyard, when improperly, or rather insufficiently garnished with the votive coin by a degenerate posterity.

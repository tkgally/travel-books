A fresh mound having by the side thereof a tall lath (inscribed with Chinese and Sanskrit characters), and a tea-cup on the top, betokens that death has lately been at work amongst the Imai. These laths or <i>tobâ</i> are furnished by the priest, and a fresh one is supplied every seven days, until the forty-ninth when eight <i>tobâ</i> may be seen surrounding the grave. The tea-cup contains water, as being the purest offering which can be made, and is daily re-filled. When the number of <i>tobâ</i> is completed, this part of the c〓remonial is finished and the next step is to erect the headstone which must be placed by the hundredth day. The <i>tobâ</i> however are not taken away when this is done, but remain until removed by decay or laid prostrate by the wind. Persons of means place a small monumental tablet in their temple and another on their <i>butszdan</i>, or family altar-shelf, at home, and on the 1st, 3rd, 7th, 13th, 17th and 33rd anniversaries of the festival of <i>bon</i>, (12th day of 7th month) lights are burned before them. Each corpse interred is placed cross-legged in the coffin, and is dressed in walking costume with sandals on feet and stick in the hand, and is supplied with six cash to pay the toll at the six cross-roads which he will meet before he can pass the river which bounds the confines of Hades.

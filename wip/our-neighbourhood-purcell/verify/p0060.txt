The <i>Améya</i> is really a genius of a higher order. He combines painting and modelling together. He carries about with him his studio and appliances, and is prepared to execute any order, be it never so difficult. He’ll stick you a bit of his tenacious barley gluten on a bamboo joint, and, puff-f-f-f—it’s a white glistening balloon—pinch it in at the middle, fashion off the mouth, draw out a bit for a cord, wind it quickly twice round, and back again, tie it into a bow knot, and you have as well-shaped a gourd in a few moments as nature ever took months to produce. “Please, Sir! I want a couple of rats nibbling a bag of barley.” Ah! My chubby little master, that’ll surely puzzle him you think. Not a bit of it. He does not even stop to consider how it is to be set about, but takes in a twinkling out of drawer No. 2, a lump of his plastic material of just the proper size.

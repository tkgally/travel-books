It is not without reason therefore that the doctor is wont to aver, that, for the size of it, he does not believe there’s a nicer little establishment of the kind in Yedo.

A word must also be said in favour of the tea shop, so neat and orderly, with its rows of glazed earthenware receptacles for teas of many varieties. Upon a back shelf, in full view of the passer-by, may be seen an assortment of teapots, with here and there a metal caddy, tastefully arranged to catch the eye. On certain days, too, when tea-sorting or other mystery of the trade is being pursued, the teashop is a very posy, with its crowd of little maids, each dressed in her holiday best and wearing a resplendent <i>obi</i>, all working with a will, and, if one may judge from, their hearty laughter, enjoying the fun amazingly. Notwithstanding such examples to the contrary, however, it must be confessed that “Our Neighbourhood” has a dilapidated, not to say, in many places, a tumble-down appearance. The main street has a cloistered look about it, from the projecting verandahs on each side thereof. It is always damp, even in the summer time; fungi sprout luxuriantly, and it is here that the moss lies thickest on the roofs.

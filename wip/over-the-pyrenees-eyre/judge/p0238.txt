Cruelty seems an inherent vice of the Spanish character. Miramon, during the Mexican revolution, flogged a woman to death, for not betraying her husband, who had hidden himself to escape the conscription. Under the torture, she was seized by the pangs of childbirth, and the demon—true son of Satan—ordered the executioners to lash on till she dropped down dead. One can hardly help feeling fiendish oneself, when one thinks of such horrors, and wishing him to suffer at least as cruel a torture himself. I never could bear to reflect that an English vessel gave this wretch shelter, when he had to fly from an oppressed and incensed country. And their people ask what good the French troops do in Mexico? Why, they help to humanise the Spaniards.

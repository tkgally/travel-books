At the street leading towards the post, my kind Spanish <i>donna</i> left me, but not before I had thanked her very warmly. In returning, I was exposed, of course, to the same insolence, and I went back to the inn, determined to leave Granada that night. There was but one place vacant in the <i>coupé</i>, which cost double a seat in any other part of the diligence, but I took it, and shaking the dust from off my feet at the inhospitable city, thankfully left it that very evening. My two travelling companions were, to my deep regret, both young men. The farther one would have been tolerably well-behaved if left to himself; the other was a brute. He was a man of Herculean mould and immense height, with a bull-neck, a huge head covered with thick, crisp, negro-like hair, and coarse, negro-like, bloated, sensual features; I hardly ever saw any man whose aspect was so loathsome and revolting. I instinctively shrank from him.

I reached Avignon by six o’clock, and am disappointed in it. The view of the Rhone, the papal castle towering above, the distant blue mountains behind, and the woody heights of Avignon Villeneuve opposite, are very beautiful; but the streets are narrow and dirty, and the whole town has a squalid appearance. It needs a visit from the Emperor, who would certainly order demolitions to begin at once. The new Palais de Justice is, however, a fine building; so is the theatre close by.

I do not admire the Château des Papes, except as seen from the bridge. I stayed four days at Avignon, at the Hôtel du Luxembourg. It was tolerably clean for a French provincial hotel, the cooking good, the <i>vin ordinaire</i> excellent, and the attendance civil. The master of the house died suddenly the day before I arrived, and was buried the morning after I came. ‘A onze heures il a déjeuné comme un bœuf, et à quatre heures il était mort,’ said the <i>garçon</i> to me. ‘D’apoplexie?’ ‘Oh non, il a bien souffert.

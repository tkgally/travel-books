‘Yes,’ thought I; ‘and there be rivers in Wales, look you, and there be rivers in Macedon, and there be salmon in both.’ ‘The French flowers,’ I answered, ‘are all well known and described by others; those of Andorre are not. I am going on to Spain to visit the Spanish Pyrenees, and wish to go by Andorre. I am told it is necessary to have a recommendation from the <i>viguier</i>, or otherwise I should have to sleep in the street. I hope you will not refuse me one.’

‘And where,’ said he, ‘are your papers? How do I know who you are, or what you want? And then, if anything is wrong, M. St. André will be to blame!’ I handed him my passport and the letter from the Chef du Cabinet, which proved I was an author by profession. He tossed the first indignantly aside. ‘C’est en Anglais,’ said he,—‘mais visé par el Consuelo d’Espagna.’ Qu’est-ce que ça fait? et la lettre, elle n’est bonne à rien. Vous n’avez pas de papiers, et vous me demandez des recommandations en Andorre? Vous auriez dû m’apporter des lettres de M. le Préfet de Foix.’

‘M. le Préfet told me to come here.’ But I might as well have talked to a stone wall.

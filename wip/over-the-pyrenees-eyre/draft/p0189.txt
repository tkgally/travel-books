A little further on, my side and back gave me such exquisite pain, I said I could ride no further. The sack of straw was stuffed so as to form a sharp cone, and it must be hollowed out to enable me to sit. An elderly man who came by saw what I needed, and tried to persuade Joan to alter it to a hollow, but he would not do it, and I was obliged to dismount and walk.

Our way lay along a rough stony road, the bed of a mountain torrent, and then up the spur of a mountain range. In all that road Joan never once stayed for me. I often stumbled or hit my foot against a sharp stone, but I had no time to stop to rub the bruise. Joan and the mules were often out of sight even, and but for Keeper, who ran backwards and forwards between them and me, I should have lost their track.

Then, the Andorrans drew a circle round the mountain-side where he lay hid; he wanted food, and he tried to break through one night, and fell down a precipice and broke his leg.”

‘“Then they took him, of course?”

‘“No; they said, lying there three days and nights—he was there three days and nights; because, you see, he had tried to go down the other side of the mountain, which was not guarded, to get food at night, and so fell where no one was within call, until at last some shepherds heard his dreadful groans, and they went to the bottom of the ravine and found him nearly dead, and carried him to the village. And the Andorrans said God had punished him enough by the breaking of his leg, and those three days and nights of pain, so they let him go free. All the punishment inflicted on him was banishment from Andorre.”

‘“Is not murder, then, punished by death?”

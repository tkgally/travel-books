Once, the diligence had to stay here two days and nights—and you see there is no inn—because the river was impassable.’ ‘<i>Cosa d’Espagna</i>,’ as Mr. Ford says, thought I to myself.

It was dark when we stopped to sup at the last town in Spain—Figueras, I think, it was called. We had an excellent supper—soup, bouilli, roast meat, soles, fowl, the large crawfish-like lobster, partridges, murdered by being dressed with tomatoes, vegetables, apples, pears, greengages, peaches, melon, and grapes; it was the last meal, and the best meal, I tasted in Spain. The hostess stood by and carved, and pressed us all to eat, and we required no pressing, for the fare was good and we were ravenous; probably, like myself, all had had nothing since morning. The charge was the very moderate sum of three francs. Again we started; by-and-by we reached Perthus. ‘We are in France,’ said the Frenchman, by my side. ‘Thank God I am in France once more!’ responded I heartily.

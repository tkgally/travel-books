‘I thought that in France, and here, the law guarded against parental injustice. It ought, for parents and relatives are often frightfully unjust and capricious; and even where injustice does not cause terrible crime, it always causes alienation between the members of a family.’

‘This is the French law. A man can dispose of the fourth part of his estate or goods. If he chooses he can give one-fourth of all he has to an entire stranger. If he gives it to a favourite child, that child has, first of all, the fourth part of all the father has; afterwards, as one of his children, he or she comes in for a share of all that is left; if there are six children, for a sixth part; if two, for half the remaining property.’

‘That seems to me very unjust.’

‘It is the law.’

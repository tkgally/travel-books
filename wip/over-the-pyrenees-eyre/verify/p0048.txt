# Chapter II.
[[subtitle]] The Eastern Pyrenees.

T<sc>he</sc> route was not pretty at first. As the train whirled on, the country grew more interesting. I noticed many, to me, new and beautiful plants on the banks of the railway cutting. I should think that a botanist would find much to reward him at Cette. The fields hereabouts were often hedged by tamarisk, whose slender graceful branches and pale-pink spike of flowers waved with the slightest breeze. It was a beautiful day, a blue Italian sky above, and the sea below almost as blue. Here I saw large dikes or dams on the other side the railway, in which the sea-water is left to dry up.

# Chapter IX.

[[subtitle]] Barcelona to Madrid.

I <sc>had</sc> been advised to avoid Madrid, on account of the intense heat which pervades one side of a street, while a cold wind from the mountains sweeps down the other, so that a wayfarer passes as it were, at the angle of a street corner, from Syria into the chilling atmosphere of an English March: which is apt to bring on fever, especially with strangers. The Spanish proverb itself acknowledges the disadvantages of the climate—

<verse>
El aire de Madrid es tan sotil,
Que mata á un hombre, y no apaga á un candil.[[fnref *]]
</verse>

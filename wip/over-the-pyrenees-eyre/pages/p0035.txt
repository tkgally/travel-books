Before long, came the conductor—‘Monsieur, cette voiture est réservée aux dames.’ ‘Monsieur, je le sais, mais c’est que je suis si pressé.’ ‘Mais, monsieur, vous ne pouvez pas y rester; il faut que vous descendiez,’ and unwillingly the man obeyed. If there had been a young and pretty girl of the party I should not have wondered. As to his being ‘pressé,’ the train did not go on for some minutes after he left our carriage. One of my companions observed it was well there was a carriage for women. She had lately travelled in a mixed conveyance, and half a dozen drunken sailors got in, and made the journey very unpleasant. It is only in a country like France that a carriage would be reserved for women travelling alone by third class; and yet the young poor girl requires more protection than the rich one, for she is not so fenced in by the respect always paid to apparent wealth, and consequent respectability.

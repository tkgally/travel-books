There were no worms at that season; they had all passed into the pupa state, or become moths. He showed me two which had just emerged from the cocoon; ugly, grey, short, thick-bodied moths, with two small whitish wings, which they flapped as if to help them in the difficult process of crawling over the paper on which they were, and quite unable to fly; they seemed to me the very dodos of the entomological world. Certainly they must have been created for the express purpose of benefiting man, for I can scarcely think they can have any animal enjoyment of their short existence, which lasts but a few hours. Then my new friend gave me some interesting details about the man whose energy and perseverance had given so rich a gift to his native country. M. Berlandier was born at Barbantane in the Arrondissement des Bouches du Rhône, near Avignon, in 1826.

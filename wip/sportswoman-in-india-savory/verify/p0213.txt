[[cont]]<i>shikari</i> Lalla proved to be a plausible old villain. Kashmiris are not show-specimens among mankind: lazy, cunning, liars ; if they constitute, as people say, remnants of part of the lost Ten Tribes of Israel, who would be an Israelite? They are certainly Jewish-looking—dark, with hooked noses.

We were inundated with competitors for the two posts; but the cook we chose, and Lalla, had both of them better <i>chits</i> than the rest. Natives have the most extraordinary faith in <i>chits</i>—that is, written testimonials as to character from old masters. Dismiss a man, cut his pay, punish him as you like, but—<i>give him a chit.</i> Though he cannot, probably, read a word of what you have written, he looks upon it as a talisman. A native handed us a <i>chit</i> from a former master, a Captain-sahib, with immense pride; it ran thus: “This man surpasses all other Kashmiris for rascality and cunning.”

By law of the State, no European can buy land and build himself a house in Kashmir, consequently travellers all use tents or house-boats, or rent a log hut in a place like Gulmerg. The admission of British visitors used to be limited, and permission was granted to military officers by the Government of India. In like manner more than eight hundred years ago, as we learn from Arabic histories, the passes used to be watched, and few outsiders admitted.

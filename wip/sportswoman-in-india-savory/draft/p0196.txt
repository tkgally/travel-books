We sat round the camp afterwards, well wrapped up, talking of our adventures to-day and to-morrow’s plans. One of the best parts of travelling consists in all that it gives one afterwards to look back upon.

<verse>
So with the friends whom death hath spared,
When life’s career is done,
We’ll talk of the dangers we have shared
And the trophies we have won.
</verse>

Talking about carelessness and slackness over shooting, almost the same thing which had happened to S. happened to M. while after tahr. She was toiling along, lazily allowing the <i>shikari</i> to carry her rifle, when suddenly an ibex appeared on the sky-line only about eighty yards from her. He stood perfectly motionless and had a good look. <i>Tableau!</i> As he nipped round and was off like a flash, M. dashed at her rifle and had two shots at him. The moral is obvious; but, <i>cui bono?</i> As long as human nature lasts the same thing will happen.

I have often wondered how one would define a real sportswoman, and I think any definition should include an appreciation of the free camp life—such as ours. It might run thus: “a fair shot, considering others, and never doing an unsportsmanlike action, preferring quality to quantity in a bag, a keen observer of all animals, and a real lover of nature.”

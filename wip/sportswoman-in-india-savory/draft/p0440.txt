The huts were innocent of windows or of proper doors, and the only way in or out was by a square hole, not quite a yard high, cut in the front of each hut and closed from the inside by a solid, sliding block of wood. It was a hands-and-knees business to get in.

The pale faces, blue eyes, and black, waving hair rather suggested Greeks; then again the hooked noses and retreating foreheads looked more like a degraded Jewish type. We could not get much out of them, though they took small coin with avidity. One hut, looking empty, I boldly ventured inside, crawling gingerly through the doorway. Standing up and seeing as best one could by the feeble light, there seemed to be nothing at all except a raised platform or sleeping-place, covered with buffalo skins and a coarse mat or two; it looked filthily dirty. There was a fireplace with a stone slab upon which to cook, but no chimney; the reed and rattan roof arched over our heads. I looked round for pots or pans of any sort, and other interesting relics, but nothing of the kind was forthcoming.

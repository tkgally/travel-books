I remember Sala Bux once forgot to pack S.’s mackintosh. He gave it, therefore, to a coolie to carry. At the end of the march he brought it to us, and it was necessary to hang it up at once, spread out in the open, when several crows promptly appeared, and perched, pecking, upon it, as they do upon sheep at home.

It was a silent and thickly wooded jungle which closed around us at our backs at Netanissa; the river was deep, flowing without a sound; and the country, appearing to be little inhabited, should be exceedingly likely for bear.

The Himalayan black bear is essentially a forest-loving animal, and seldom ascends above twelve thousand feet; he does not, like the red bear, delight in digging for roots on the grassy slopes immediately below the snow-line. He is a larger and heavier animal than the Indian bear, and occasionally, certainly, he proves a formidable antagonist.

[[cont]]sitting outside at breakfast by 5 o’clock, the grass covered with dew, and grey dawn just stealing over the country. There was an air of suppressed excitement at that hour when starting was so near, and the morning before us might contain so much of the unexpected. Preparations were being made all round, and one of the most important items in the shoot, the beating of the jungle, had to be arranged.

The headman of the district had been interviewed beforehand, and arrangements made between him and Lalla for natives to beat for us. We had about a hundred and fifty this time; they were all made to sit down outside our camp, squatting in a great circle. The noise which those natives made it would be hard to describe—such a jabbering, vociferating, clamouring, shouting. Lalla and the servants patrolled the circle inside and tried to keep order with switches.

Each of the coolies was given a scrap of initialed paper, which they had to show at the end of the day before they were paid; otherwise many defaulters, who have not borne the burden and brunt of the beats, will creep in with the rest, towards evening, and try to get equal pay. As far as possible, we chose out only strong and younger men, for old ones are worse than useless. But Lalla, as usual, allowed a greybeard or two to creep in.

[[cont]]it was a land of pastures and woods below, of snow, rocks, and ice above. The hillsides were covered with blue, yellow, and purple blossoms, tossing their heads in the breeze; higher still, above the tree-zone, glittering glaciers were wedged between the barren crags, and the long, gaunt ribs of the mountains sustained fields of snow.

Our tents were soon up, at the edge of this mountain-locked <i>merg</i>; our unpacking quickly done. Having probably found our set of teacups in my long boots, a cake of soap hidden in a coat-sleeve, and my collars wrapped round a cream cheese which we got in Gulmerg (such were the vagaries of Sala Bux), we had guns often to look to, perhaps something to sketch, always diaries to be written up, while we lounged in the shade—those lazy afternoons. . . . Home letters had to be written—“Home,” which plays such a large part out in India, and is the final goal of every Englishman’s hopes and plans. Even we idle globe-trotters could not forget, and could realise a little that it might be possible to write—

<verse>
Oh the toil that knows no breaking!
Oh the <i>Heimweh</i>, ceaseless aching!
Oh the black dividing sea and alien plain!
</verse>

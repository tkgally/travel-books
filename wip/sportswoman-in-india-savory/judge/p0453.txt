Visits from natives are rather a trial. Well-to-do tradesmen would call upon Commissioners. The interviews I was present at were rather in this style:

<i>Native:</i> “Salaam! Great Chief.”

<i>Englishman:</i> “Salaam.”

<i>Native:</i> “Your Excellency is my father and mother.”

<i>Englishman:</i> “I am obliged to you.”

<i>Native:</i> “Your Excellency, I have come to behold your face.”

<i>Englishman:</i> “Thank you. Have you anything more to say to me?”

<i>Native:</i> “Nothing, Great Chief.”

<i>Englishman:</i> “Neither have I anything to say. So good-morning. Enough for to-day.”

<i>Native:</i> “Enough. Good-morning, your Excellency. Salaam! Great Chief.”

India is a land of the <i>contretemps</i>; few sojourners out there have met with none. Now the following happened to a young couple I met, in Government Service.

They had been sent upon an expedition to find <i>dacoits</i>, who had been making disturbances; were camping out, and had sent on all extra luggage to a small military station which they expected to reach next day. Through the night a lamp burnt in the tent; their clothes lay on chairs. On getting up the next morning, where in the name of fortune were those same clothes?

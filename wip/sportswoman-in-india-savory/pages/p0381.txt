INDIA is, in the minds of a certain class of people, connected indissolubly with snakes; but as a matter of fact, the ordinary Englishwoman, out there for a year or so, is quite likely never to meet with one in the whole of her visit. To most people that would be no disappointment. “Those lithe and elegant beings,” as Rymer Jones calls them, are not appreciated in one’s bath-room and in inconveniently dark corners.

A snake, if one considers it, is really a wonderful animal. Without arms or legs, it is able to catch its prey. It can swallow an animal twice the size of its own head.

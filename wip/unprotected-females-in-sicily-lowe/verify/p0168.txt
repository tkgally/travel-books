“We must descend,” says Angiolo.

“At once? only let me stop to breathe, and change my shoes and stockings.” Mamma had done this while I was on the crater, and it is such a comfortable plan no one must neglect to follow it; the nice dry woollen stockings and easy shoes rest one inexpressibly, and make one long to run down. The guides were in tremendous spirits; could hardly believe the whole thing real; half feared to triumph; and fervently thanked Santa Lucia for having so handsomely acknowledged the feast held in her honour the day before, and wound up by saying, “<i>Ah, Signora, bisogna che questa cosa sia stampata</i>” (“this must be printed”). Never, never, had they dreamt of taking ladies through such snow; of the very few men who try it hardly one in a winter reaches the forge; only a “giorno di Santa Lucia” could have worked such a miracle, and they ended, I rather suspect, by taking us for very near relations of her holiness; perhaps one for herself.

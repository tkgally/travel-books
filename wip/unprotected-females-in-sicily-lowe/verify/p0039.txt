The town lays beneath in all the eastern squareness of marked light and shade, and flattened roofs; here and there a palm springs forth and vies with the towers in grace . . . we gaze on, till sounding gongs, striking a hundred hours at once, seem calling the Mussulmen to prayer, and we women must descend before their turbaned heads crowd the court and stairs.

Drive back through Palermo, <i>Cocchiere!</i> It is now afternoon, and the streets are full of rags, tatters, priests, and equipages: the paper fans are in busy agitation over the sweets, the fruit stalls are festooned with pomegranates and figs of Barbary; the little boy in the basket on that man’s back, can pop up and pull off the passers’ hats, and pop down again without being seen by his victims in the crowd; carriages and carts fill up the narrow streets, the shops are transacting business

This gentleman just mentioned was manager of the estates of the monastery, and resided within its precincts. He had been introduced by our Cosenza friends in a letter of which the warmth of expression showed their kindness of heart; we had sent it him late in the afternoon, and he replied in the following words:—

<blockquote>
“R<sc>espettabile</sc> M<sc>iladi</sc>,
“As the hour of Sanctuary is past, I cannot come to pay reverence to you this evening; to-morrow I shall be ready to execute your honourable commands.
“Kissing your hand, and wishing you a happy night, I sign myself your meekest servant,
[[right]] “L——.
Addressed, “<i>Alla distinta Miladi Emilia.</i>”
</blockquote>

The next morning he appeared, followed by a boy, as we were sitting down to the usual Liliputian cups of coffee, in a room where the almost sole article of furniture was an enormous fan, waved by a little monk over a brazier. He knelt down to kiss my mother’s hand; then telling the boy to come forward, poured several pounds of cake and sugar-plums into my lap! Nor was this all; the table became suddenly spread with chocolate, milk, and rosoglio; we never had so sweet a breakfast!

Shudderingly we shuffled on in the downward direction, over lava rocks far apart, into creeping brushwood, down slippery crevices, into dust. Oh, stinging agony, now the feat was over, and we had thought to mount and ride in triumph.

We must not faint—fatigue and cold will never let us breathe again—but yet for <i>insensibility!</i> to benumb our feelings, rest our minds one moment; when “Santa Lucia!” screamed they; “a light was in the wood.”

“But is it banditti?” trembled we.

No! their hail is answered in a young muleteer’s voice; their joy bursts out in loud halloos.

A fire, a bed of straw—<i>security</i>—hour of peace, thy balm was Gilead’s.

[[cont]]Notwithstanding our training in Norway,[[fnref *]] we found it pretty hard work getting through the interior by the only two existing post roads, one of which goes west to Trapani, the other east to Messina <i>viá</i> Catania, a distance of two hundred and thirty miles, and recommend all, unless fascinated by what we encountered, to get a government carriage and order for horses, and post over the ground as fast as possible. Those who do not wish to go southward to Girgenti can see a fine specimen of Grecian temple at Segeste, by taking places in the Trapani courier, or driving to Calatafini, a day’s journey within three miles of the ruin, which, with its twelve Doric columns, stands majestically crumbling. A thirty-six miles further journey along bridle paths, past Campo-Bello, the ancient quarries whence the classic pillars were hewn before removing, will bring to Selinuntium, whose prostrate remains earthquakes laid level with the grass. But those who have Agrigentum in prospect need sacrifice no time in the temple of a minor deity; <i>there</i> are the high altars of the ancient isle.

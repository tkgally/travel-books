While all these charms stretched out to the right, to the left were displayed the wonders of Italian “Villeggiatura villas;” each hill crowned with them, from the stately porticoed palace to the little summer-house all vine and trellis; a thousand fancies expended in their forms and colours: here a crimson fortress, with many turrets, is guarded by mailed warriors in glossy paint; by them stands a temple, whose tinted arches shelter all the Muses; lapis lazuli and malachite form the walls of another; at this window sits a cat ogling a canary; at that, a monkey chases a parrot—to have a notion of Italian fresco you <i>must</i> follow this road—and how bright it all looked in the sun!

The first day’s journey brought us to Sestri, passing through Rapallo, beneath whose stone columns sat the female population at their rich silk-lace work. Next day commenced the passage of the “Apennini,” or Little Apennines, where rocks, grass, goats, shepherds, and stretches of distant peaks, were the subjects; till a long descending wind brought Liguria’s capital, La Spezzia, in view—its gulph set as turquoise in silver rim of round white-headed olives.

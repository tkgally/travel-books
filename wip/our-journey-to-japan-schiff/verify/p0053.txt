Reaching there in due time, we find a company of about thirty guests, principally high dignitaries, among whom we meet the Marquis Saionji, the Prime Minister, and most of the guests who were at Mr. Wilson’s the evening before. I am asked to take Mrs. Chinda (wife of the Vice-Minister of Foreign Affairs) to dinner and find myself seated between her and Mrs. Sakatani, a sweet-looking Japanese lady, the wife of the Finance Minister, who is in native dress. Both my lady neighbors speak some English and I get along very nicely. Mother is seated to the right of the host, with Mr. Wilson on her other side. The dinner is entirely in foreign style, and at its close the Finance Minister rises and in a Japanese address, which is afterwards repeated in English by his secretary, welcomes us as follows:

<i>Your Excellencies, Ladies and Gentlemen:</i>

<i>It is a source of great pleasure to me that you have favored me with your company at the dinner given in honor of Mr. and Mrs. Schiff and their party, the most distinguished guests we have ever had from the United States of America.</i>

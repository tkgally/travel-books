In the afternoon Mr. Takahashi calls to take me to Count Inouyé, who was away when we were in Tokio before, and to meet whom specially interests me, the Count having had charge of the Empire’s financial plans during the war. Count Inouyé, who is a man of about seventy years, one of the so-called Elder Statesmen, receives me most cordially, highly commends what my firm has done for his country, and drifting into general conversation, we exchange views upon the economic and financial situation for fully an hour and a half, after which I leave with the promise that I will take luncheon with him before we depart. The evening is quietly spent at the hotel, as we have announced that we shall accept no further invitations.

<i>Sunday, May 13th.</i>—Two days having been fine, it is in the regular order that a rainy day shall follow, and it pours with a vengeance.

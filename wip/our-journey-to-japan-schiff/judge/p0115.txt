We have now again received a new demonstration of this. However, Mr. Wilson, the American Chargé d’Affaires, said to me at the Mitsui dinner to-night that many people might be for years in Japan without getting such opportunities as we had enjoyed in less than the month of our presence in the country.

<i>Friday, April 20th.</i>—This is the day of the Imperial cherry-blossom party, and it is therefore doubly gratifying that sunshine again greets us. The forenoon hours are occupied by various minor matters. We leave the hotel shortly before noon to take luncheon with Mr. and Mrs. Wilson at the American Legation, where we find a number of American naval and military officers, Admiral Train and General Brooke, with their ladies, among the number. A pleasant hour and a half is spent at the luncheon table, and all leave toward two o’clock for the detached Palace Park, where the Imperial cherry-blossom party is to be held.

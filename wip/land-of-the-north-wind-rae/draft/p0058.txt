Jones fell off my horse into a bog, and made his hands and feet muddy. No one knows how it happened. The luckless fellow had been seen by the Doctor very shortly before. He seemed well and cheerful, and was humming some Lapp music. Whether the horse stumbled, or the girth broke, or whether Jones fell off independently, has not transpired, and probably now never will transpire. He has never referred to it himself. We pulled him together, and reseated him on the horse: and the Doctor and myself went rambling over the rocky moorland among lovely mosses—staghorn and reindeer moss, and club moss—and white and red lichens: and dwarf plants covered with eatable berries, whether bilberries, whinberries, or cranberries, I don’t know. I had forgotten to mention that our lunch was consumed on the banks of Lake Helgi, renamed Lake Mémie after one of the most agreeable of our fellow-passengers.

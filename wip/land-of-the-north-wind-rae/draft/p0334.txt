Pimi has eaten a pig to-day—Mr. Sharvin said to me. It was true: the little droll, careering about the place, had attacked, defeated, and eaten a young pig, and he presented a replete and stuffy appearance when he was brought in to join the rest of the luggage. No further proof was needed that Pimi was a wolf of parts, and it only remained to take precautions for the security of the Doctor’s life on the voyage.

We left this most hospitable of houses with many regrets and many kind farewells, and glided away down the Mezén river, on our way to the Kûloi. Behind us was the familiar coast of Kânin, stretching away into the shoreless sea, which faded and faded, and finally sank out of our sight, probably for ever.

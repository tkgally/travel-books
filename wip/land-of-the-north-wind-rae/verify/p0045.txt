At length we saw a fine stately frigate approaching, as we steamed up a narrow fiord; and as we passed and dipped our ensign, we saw the King upon the bridge raising his cap. We removed ours, and the Norwegians gave three short hoarse cheers. A small gunboat followed in the frigate’s wake, and with this unpretending escort Oscar the Second, King of Sweden and Norway, went down the coast to his coronation.

The voyage was drawing to a close, we had had dinner and were sitting on deck, when Tromsö came in sight. There was bustle and confusion on board of the Hakon Jarl, and when the anchor went down the decks were alive with human beings. We had planned an expedition to the Lapp encampment a few miles away, and Mrs. Wild’s courier, the reliable Ring—a bronzed stalwart Norwegian—a police detective when at home in Christiania—was sent on shore for a guide and boats, and as we waited for his return, passengers and visitors swarmed over the side of the steamer.

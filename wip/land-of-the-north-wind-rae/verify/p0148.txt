Of the two, the Russians, who for half a century repressed the native tongue, encourage these, rather than the Swedish champions; they are re-introducing it into the excellent and abundant schools too.

The Russians seem to treat this province fairly and considerately, and not ungenerously, as far as we could learn. The governors of sub-provinces are Russians and the fortress garrisons are Russian, but the State officials are Finlanders. These latter are blamed in many cases by their countrymen for their over obsequience to Russian authority, in their anxiety for promotion and patronage. Before many years—most likely two or three—are passed, there will be a railway made with Finnish resources, running from Viborg all the way to Uleåborg. The Finlanders have a public debt of about a quarter of a million, held, we understood, in Germany and Denmark; and as a gentleman told us with pride, they have more than half a million sterling in the State bank to redeem it with. He believes that if the country continues to prosper as at present, in a few years they will have ten millions in their bank.

At the second station, searching for a man to furnish our horses, I looked in through a window, and found my face close to the head of a man who lay with his hands folded on his breast. Then I saw that a pale horse had passed through the station before us, whose rider’s name was death.

At length we reached Kaskôva, made a portentous supper, of which our hampers provided all but the milk and samovar, and fell into a heavy sleep. We walked down to the river’s bank in the morning, our baggage drawn bcside us on a sledge. On the landing barge were two or three young Russians fishing: the boatman embarked everything, and we pushed out on to the Dvina. It took three quarters of an hour of hard pulling to bring us across: when landing amid a crowd of peasants waiting for the ferry, and a large herd of cattle and horses, we dashed off in a tcheléga drawn by three willing spirited horses.

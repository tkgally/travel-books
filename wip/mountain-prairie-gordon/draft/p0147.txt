Besides, the reserves could not equitably be of uniform size, for some parts of the Province being well suited for farming admitted of larger reserves of arable and of grazing lands than others; while, at the same time those tribes that lived chiefly by fishing did not require large reserves of land, and could be more appropriately assisted or compensated by the whites in other ways, such as by instruction in trades or by the supply of increased facilities for traffic.

Through their intercourse with the whites, especially in the southern parts of the Province, the Indians have already very materially advanced. When labour was scarce, in the early days of gold-mining, many of them were employed by the miners, and many also by farmers and others who soon followed on the track of the miners. They enjoyed almost equal rights with the white settlers; they were, for the most part, industrious and trustworthy; and so they became boatmen, porters, herders, and in a number of cases independent farmers and stock-raisers.

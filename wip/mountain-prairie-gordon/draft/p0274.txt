On Saturday morning the boats were loaded, and the carts started on their return trip, while I had the advantage of a buck-board which had been sent out with Mrs. Young, whom we met on her way to Slave Lake. The road from the Landing to Edmonton, which is an excellent waggon-road, ninety-six miles in length, was made by the Company to avoid the necessity of freighting goods for the north by the old and difficult trail which passed by way of Fort Assineboine to Lesser Slave Lake. After leaving the river it leads very quickly to the plateau which is here about 350 feet above the level of the Athabasca. The country for several miles south of the Landing is broken into ridges, the soil being at first rather poor, but it gradually becomes undulating prairie.

[[cont]]reach Fort McLeod. Having rested near Iroquois Creek on the 13th, we did not reach Fort McLeod until Monday the 14th,—seventy miles in seven days.

Fort McLeod is beautifully situated at the lower end of McLeod Lake, whose waters are emptied by the Pack River into the Peace. There is abundance of excellent pasture on the plateau around it, and it boasts a small garden that seems capable of raising anything that can withstand occasional summer frosts. Indeed there is sufficient good land in this immediate neighbourhood for a large farm, if the climate were only suitable.

Some have supposed that wherever an abundance of the service-berry is to be found it indicates a climate fit for the growth of grain, but this seems to be as great a mistake as to imagine that the presence of the humming-bird argues an equable and genial climate; for the humming-bird may be seen around the banks of Babine Lake, and as far north as the Stickine, while the service-berry grows in abundance near Fort McLeod; yet Babine, Stickine, and McLeod are all unfit localities for the growth of grain.

The snow-fall here is heavier than at Fort St. James, averaging about five feet, and gardening is about three weeks later. The lake usually freezes about the middle of November, and opens about the middle of May.

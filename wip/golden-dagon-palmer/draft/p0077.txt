# Chapter VIII.

[[subtitle]] Elephant-Back—“Old Injin-Rubber”—The Boa—The Caves—Guadma—The Bats.

S<sc>hortly</sc> after our arrival at Moulmein, an excursion to “The Caves,” some twelve miles to the north of the town, was planned by several English officers and resident merchants, and an invitation extended to our gun-room mess. Accordingly, garrees were bespoken, boats engaged to await us at the ferry, and elephants on the other side, to roll us to our destination; kitmudgars and bearers were sent on before with hampers, teeming with tongues, anchovies, sardines, chutney, eggs, and curry, together with the table furniture, and all the machinery of a pic-nic. And so, with the cheroots, and the “brandy-pawnee,” and the soda-water, and the beer, we set out, after an early breakfast of fruit and coffee, in our low, square garrees, drawn by the same brown, bob-maned, opinionated ponies, each with his proper gora-wallah—

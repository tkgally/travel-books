These were subsequently joined on shore by an American missionary, Mr. K——, a remarkable person, to whose influence, and the measures it procured, are unquestionably to be attributed the events of that and the following days. The Governor, to the surprise of all who were not prepared for Burmese treachery, arrogance, or caprice—for he had lately manifested the sweetest of moods—refused to receive the deputation, on the pretext of a customary <i>siesta</i>, and kept the officers waiting at his gate like so many beggars; his guards assuring them that it was as much as their heads were worth to disturb him, though the illustrious <i>Woon</i> of India himself (the Governor-General) should call “house! house!” The plea was plausible because consistent with the custom of all Indian people, but not the less crafty and insolent on that account, the Governor being well advised of the importance and friendly purpose of the deputation, and choosing to make capital of their humiliation.

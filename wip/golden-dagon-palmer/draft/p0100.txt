Such was the state of affairs when, leaving Moulmein, we approached the mouth of the Rangoon river on the morning of January 8th, 1852. Great was our astonishment to find the Commodore lying at the mouth of the river, with the King’s ship, a veritable prize. This “King’s ship,” or “yellow ship,” as we were accustomed to style her indifferently, was a large frigate, built for the Burmese on European stocks. She was new, having only her lower-masts in, and, though cut for heavy batteries, was unarmed and unmanned, being provided only with an awkward guard of terrified boats-people, under the command of one “Commodore” Abdoolah, a clever, amusing savage, half Burmese, half Malay, who, on the strength of having been a pirate once, had got himself appointed to the chief command of the navy of Ava—that is, the King’s ship.

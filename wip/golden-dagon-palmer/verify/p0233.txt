By this disposition, had we been compelled to pass within range, he could have sunk every steamer; with his musketry alone he could have swept our decks. The current runs powerfully around the point, and we should have been an hour or more under the full weight of his fire, without the possibility of returning a shot.

But we gave him the go-by. Right over against Akoktoung, as Bandoola’s bluff was called, was the village of Youngtzay, lying in the hollow of the river’s elbow; and at the upper end of Youngtzay a narrow arm, quite out of Bandoola’s range, was separated from the main stream by two small islands, to join it again two miles above. Two months before, this creek was entirely dry, and of course it had never entered the sagacious pate of the Little Hero, “the son of his father,” that vessels of our apparent draught would adventure the passage. By this we dodged him.

Drumming, gonging, tooting, singing, jumping, howling, laughing, scolding, waving flags and flourishing dhars, they tugged and swayed and tugged, then fell and were dragged awhile, then rose again and tugged, then rested and panted, and then tugged again. And all this time recruits from other villages were straggling in to swell the ranks of this side or of that, and to make the strain and struggle wilder.

At last some of the ropes on one side gave way with a loud report. A thousand screaming people at once went down on their backs, rolling over each other, spluttering and wriggling to disengnge themselves; and the other side, with a sky-splitting shout of victory, ran off with Poonghee, dragging, on their feet or their backs or their bellies, the few of the discomfited party who still held on by the ropes that were left.

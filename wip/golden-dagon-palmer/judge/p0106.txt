[[cont]]Lambert as a quarrelsome and overbearing person, who sent “a deputation of drunken officers and a low American missionary to make a noise at his gate.” Furthermore, he threatened that, if the Commodore presumed to send any of our vessels down the river again without his permission, his stockades should fire on them. The Commodore’s reply to this challenge appeared in the form of an order to three of the steamers to proceed down the stream and past the stockades, one after the other, and at <i>provoking</i> intervals.

The order was executed, but without success; we played off and on with them for half an hour without drawing a shot, and so left in disgust. We proceeded to the mouth of the river to see the merchantmen safely out, and then returned to the stockade more saucily than before, but, finding no work for us there, went back to the Commodore: another message from that “gassy” Governor, that if an attempt should be made to tow out the King’s ship, the war should commence on the morrow with an attack from him.

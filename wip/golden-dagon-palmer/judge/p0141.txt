The eastern of these surpasses, in its arabesque cornices, triple roof, spiral columns, and airy spire, the dreams of the boy Solomon—a golden throne, a temple all of gold.

Near the pagoda, under a sacred canopy, still golden, of its own, from gilded beams hewn from the proudest teaks in Pegu, hangs, within two feet of the ground, the Great Dagon bell. Straining my arm from the shoulder under its vibrating rim, I could touch only with my finger-tips its inner edge; yet never did my Lady’s silver toilet-bell utter mellower music. Pali inscriptions and hieroglyphics chase its surface from shank to rim; and a dozen funny demons of indescribable absurdity guard the portal of its lodge.

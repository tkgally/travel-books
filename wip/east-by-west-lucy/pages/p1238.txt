All the food was placed on the table at once, and with it a wooden box of considerable size. Regarded as a specimen of modern Japanese decoration, the box was perfectly hideous, being picked out with white flowers and bright green leaves. It opened in a series of trays, after the fashion of a lacquered box. On each tray was a supply of food, fish, jelly, vegetables, seaweed, and sweetmeats. On leaving, each Minister found one of these boxes in his carriage—a delicate and hospitable attention with which the Mikados have been wont through a thousand years to speed the parting guest. With the plain wooden box containing the meats was presented a beautiful little porcelain cup, from which the guest was supposed to have drunk his saké. The wooden box, with its green leaves, its white flowers, and its uninviting cold meats, was a thing to be got rid of as quickly as possible; but this little cup, with the Royal chrysanthemums in gold shining upon it, would fittingly remain as a souvenir of the interesting occasion.

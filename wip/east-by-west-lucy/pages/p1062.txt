# Chapter IV.

[[subtitle]] Life and Death in the Far West.

T<sc>he</sc> boarding of a railway train at Coolidge, an attempted robbery, and an accomplished murder, have awakened the West out of a pleasing dream of security. These attacks upon railway trains are by no means of frequent occurrence, though when they happen they are talked of so much and for so long a time that they grow to be familiar. To stop and rob a train, is an exploit that obviously demands united force, well-ordered plans, and desperate courage. It is the Waterloo of the rowdy’s campaign, which works its level way through the year by petty larceny, horse-stealing, gaming, and an occasional shooting. In 1874 the Union Pacific Road was the scene of the first of these outrages, when the train bound East was boarded by seven men, who got clear off with £2000.

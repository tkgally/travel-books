A Chinese coffin has an unaccustomed look, which relieves the boatloads from much of their ghastliness. They look like trunks of trees, hollowed, squared, and with the ends stopped up. They are not shaped with the stiff formality of the Western coffin, and are to my mind infinitely preferable. Generally the deaths are viewed with stolid indifference by the Chinese. There is one more bunk empty, one mouth the less to feed, and the purser and doctor have another handful of dollars. But when there is a family and one is taken, the commotion is considerable.

There was on board our ship an old dried-up Chinese lady from Demerara, said to be eighty years of age. She was hastening home to dwell for ever with kith and kin, but could not hold out, and died on the tenth day. One night she predicted her death on the following day, had herself dressed in grave-clothes, and lay quietly awaiting the tryst she had made, and which Death for his part faithfully kept.

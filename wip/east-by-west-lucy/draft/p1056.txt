One other thing the two eager young towns have in common is flies. In St. Louis it is said that “Kansas City has more flies than any town in the world.” But Kansas City is not to be outdone in this unwonted burst of generosity, and magnanimously insists upon the pre-eminence of the more southern city. As a sojourner in both, I should say the matter was not worth quarrelling about. A fly more or less is of no consequence where they are counted by tens of thousands. In neither city have I sat down to meat with less than five hundred guests at a single table, two righteously paying their bills and 498 not only settling down without saying “by your leave,” but insisting upon being the first to taste every dish that comes to the table.

The only means by which a fair share can be secured for the paying guests is for the waiter to stand and fan the dishes spread out on the table. This is rough on a plate of mutton chops or a cut of roast meat not very hot when it came to the table. But there are compensations even for this drawback.

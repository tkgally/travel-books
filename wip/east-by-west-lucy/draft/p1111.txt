A considerable majority of the congregation were women, plain-looking, hard-working, care-worn creatures, evidently glad of the little excitement brought into their dull lives by this festival. Next to the women, perhaps running them pretty close in the matter of numbers, were the children. There was no mistaking their presence. Long before the organ sounded or the choir rose to sing the babies began, squall answering to squall throughout the vast edifice. Occasionally one choked with howling, and after being vainly beaten on the back and shaken up, was carried out. But two or three were nothing in such a multitude, bawling and squealing, and the crowing went on without distinguishable decrease in volume.

The proceedings were opened by prayer offered by a rugged-looking elder, who stood by the rostrum with horny hands rigidly uplifted. President Taylor occupied a seat in the back row of benches in the gallery immediately behind the rostrum. Beside him sat his two counsellors.

Many of the women add to their natural charms by blacking their teeth. This is the sign of the married state and has a particularly hideous effect. I am told it is now going out of fashion. The younger girls when dressed for the day touch the front of their under lip with a brush dipped in vermilion. Our jinrikisha men made their way through the throng without running over any children, a feat accomplished only by dint of incessant shouting. We walked up the hill and finally came out on the racecourse, on the way obtaining a bird’s-eye view of Yokohama.

Coming back one of the jinrikisha men politely invited us to visit a “garden shop.” Not desiring to buy anything, we were reluctant to enter, but yielded to pressure, and were received by the nursery gardener with profound courtesy, not abated by one jot when we left without a chrysanthemum pot or a flowering shrub under each arm.

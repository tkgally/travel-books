The five retainers of the deposed prince wore white stockings, with the big toe in a place all to itself for convenience of tying the straw sandal. The people walking about the streets with paper umbrellas, and paper or straw cloaks, wore wooden pattens, standing fully three inches off the ground. To Western ideas it would have seemed better if there had been less clog and more trouser. But it was very wet, and there was no use in spoiling any clothing that might possibly be dispensed with. The jinrikisha men wore nothing on their feet but straw sandals, with which they gaily splashed through the mud, the water running down their bare legs in never-ceasing streams.

The next morning Yokohama underwent a glorious transformation. The clouds had rained themselves out, and the sun, like the Mikado breaking the bonds in which he had long been held by the Shoguns, had a complete restoration. We rose early, got into jinrikishas, and gaily bowled along for a trip round the Bluff.

The difficulty here and with kindred entertainments is that the piano has been tuned at least two notes low—a fresh evidence, it is agreed, of the economical policy of the management. Last Sunday it had been arranged to include in the hymns the one commencing “Eternal Father, strong to save”—a hymn which, sung in thousands of English churches on quiet Sundays, finds an echo in many a lonely ship making its way across the pathless ocean. Just before the service I came upon the captain, evidently in a mood of deep dejection, despairingly wrestling with a difficult problem.

“What's the matter, captain?” I asked. “Have the engineers come upon another layer of slate?”

“No,” said he, loyally resenting reference to the sore subject of the coal; “I'm thinking of the piano. We must pitch ‘Eternal Father’ two bars higher.”

This done the man with the clogs beat a rattling accompaniment, at which signal two hooded figures entered and carried off the now embarrassing agglomeration of corpses.

The next scene is an exceedingly pretty one, though as nobody is murdered it falls a little flat. The villain is discovered standing in front of a Japanese house, drawing water from a well, such a well as may be seen in any street in Japan. He washes his ensanguined sword and finally his feet. Whilst thus engaged a servant maid comes out and discovers him, again going through a pantomime of terror which, though, perhaps, a little overdone for English taste, was really very funny. Ito explains that the Two-Sworded Man, having escaped from his enemies (a considerable proportion of whom must by this time be dead) has come on a visit to his uncle and aunt.

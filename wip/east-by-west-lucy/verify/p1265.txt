Coming back from an excursion to the Chin-zen-ji, the lady of our party went to take a bath. A quarter of an hour later she was discovered, partly dressed, lying insensible across the threshold of the bath-house. These baths are heated with charcoal, and in the great majority, which are built in the passages of the houses, there is always sufficient ventilation to carry away the poisonous fumes. At Nikko, the bath having the rare accommodation of a door, the fumes are retained within the chamber. The lady, having taken her bath, was dressing, when she was suddenly overpowered. She had just strength to struggle towards the door, against which she fell. Fortunately the door opened outwards, and she got her head in the passage. Had the door opened from the inside there could have been only one result from an accident which in all probability would not have been discovered for half an hour.

They stayed on board partly because they had nowhere to go, and partly because they expected that their arrival would be duly notified, and that some one would come down and lead them to a place where they were to stay. Nobody coming, and hunger gnawing at them, Inouyé volunteered to go ashore and buy some food. They had three dollars left, which they had secreted beyond the ken of the rapacious bo’sun. Not knowing the value of such coin in England, it was deemed desirable that the emissary should take with him all the money. He accordingly pocketed the three dollars, and went forth in search of something to eat. He would surely come upon a place where rice was sold ready boiled, or little bowls of soup were dispensed, or, peradventure, a little fish, with trimmings of seaweed, might be purchased. Wandering about, with his weather eye open for such contingencies, young Inouyé at length came to a baker’s shop.

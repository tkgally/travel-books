Being Saturday, an ever-changing procession of grave elders, matronly women, young men, and maidens were walking round and round the tree, chanting a low strain. Every time they passed a particular point in its circumference, they threw on it with their hand water taken from the Ganges and carried in their <i>lotas.</i> Some varied the performance by throwing marigolds or grains of rice. It seemed a particularly dull game of follow-my-leader; but it is a serious religious function, and good Hindoos would not see Saturday’s sun go down till they had walked a hundred and eight times round the peepul tree, laved its trunk with holy water, or cast upon it some offering of food or flower.

A goat had discovered the richness of the land, and, climbing up the masonry, browsed upon the flowers; whilst the pigeons, coming down in swarms, pecked up the rice, nobody saying them nay.

As we left the city, Ayoub pointed to a portion of the wall, near the gate.

“That is the place,” he said, “where St. Paul is believed to have been let down, when he escaped from Damascus. It is not certain, but it is generally believed.”

Half a mile beyond, in an open space, was a caravan of nearly a hundred camels. These had crossed the desert from Bagdad, laden with goods for Damascus. Caravan camels, being desert-bred, are afraid of the town sights and noises; so this space without the walls is set apart for them while their freight is carried into the city.

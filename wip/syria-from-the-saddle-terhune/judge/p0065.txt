# Chapter VI.

[[subtitle]] House of Rich Damascene Jew—Damascus Massacre.

[[image: Decorated initial letter F set beside a sketch of a house front with shuttered windows, an arched door and a tree on a paved street]]

FROM the outside it looked like a dozen other houses in the narrow, winding street; the front having a dingy, ill-kept air. We knocked at the low door, and were admitted by a gorgeously liveried porter, who ushered us into a small, dark corridor. Opening on this corridor were several little rooms, with the usual sleeping-mat on the floor. These rooms were used as sleeping-places for servants and for such messengers from a distance as were obliged to stay in town all night.

It was doubtless in some such lodging that the Hittite captain lay, when, recalled from the field, he foolishly “slept at the door of the king’s house with all the servants of his lord, and went not down to his house;” an act of fidelity that cost him his life.

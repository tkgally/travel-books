# Chapter XXIX.

[[subtitle]] Dead Sea Fruit—Modern Jericho—The Dead Sea—The Jordan.

[[image: Decorated initial letter I beside a drawing of two Arab men crouching among bushes, one holding a long gun]]

IN the Plain of Jericho we found even richer foliage than in the Meadow of the Three Thousand. Thorn bushes and shrubs of all sorts were growing fresh after the rain, and acacia blossoms filled the air with fragrance. Herds of camels and sheep wandered picturesquely about, cropping grass, or rested under the shade of the low trees.

To the north lay Gilead, famous of old for its balm, and other well-known sites dotted the plain.

When Antony, having left Cleopatra for Octavia, wished to return to his old love, he deeded to her and to the Egyptian crown, by way of peace-offering, this Plain of Jericho, then one of the richest valleys in the world.

We crossed the dry bed of a broad river under the arches of a modern stone aqueduct, and rode to the top of a little hill on the other side, where stood our camp.

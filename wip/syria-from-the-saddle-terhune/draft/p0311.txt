The three had come to see me off. This pleased me greatly, since, all backsheesh having been paid, their only motive for coming could have been to wish me <i>bon voyage</i>.

The mists had cleared by the time our train entered the Valley of Elah. Here it was that the Philistines encamped against the army of Saul; and the dried brook in the center is the same, beyond doubt, from which David gathered the five white stones for his combat with Goliath.

“The Israelite army,” said Jamal, pointing to the right, where the valley broadened into a plain, “probably camped about there. David would have crossed the hills over there to the left to join them. Bethlehem lies behind those hills. We are coming into the country of the Philistines, now. Their army probably came up this very gorge where the train is going. The fight between David and Goliath was somewhere along the brook.”

As we traveled on, the valley grew wider, and on a hill to the far right we saw a village and the ruins of an old city.

“Those are the ruins of Zorah,” said David. “The field in the valley to this side of the city is believed to be the spot where Manoah’s wife saw the Angel who prophesied Samson’s birth. Samson must often have walked over this country that we see now.”

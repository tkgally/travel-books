Along this nearly effaced road are the Pictured Rocks, slabs smoothed on boulders and cliffs, and decorated with bas-reliefs of the various invaders who passed that way.

The slab nearest the main road represents Esarhaddon the Assyrian who came down from the Euphrates to crush a revolt against Nineveh, and, returning conqueror, caused a likeness of himself, in royal vestments, to be engraved on the rock. Beneath it is a hieroglyphic list of his achievements. Beside this, and of earlier date, is an effigy of Rameses II., who wished to perpetuate in stone his victory over the Hittites. Esarhaddon doubtless caused his own tablet to be carved next to that of Rameses, as an ironical reminder that the great power of Egypt had, ere then, succumbed to Nineveh.

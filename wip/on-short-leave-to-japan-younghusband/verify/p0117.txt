‘At that moment the <i>kaishaku</i>, who, still crouching by his side, had been keenly watching his every movement, sprang to his feet, poised his sword for a second in the air; there was a flash, a heavy, ugly thud, a crashing fall; with one blow the head was severed from the body. . . .

‘The <i>kaishaku</i> made a low bow, wiped his sword with a piece of paper which he had ready for the purpose, and retired from the raised floor.’[[fnref 1]]

A more impressive and appalling ceremony it is difficult to imagine: at midnight, in the half-gloomy interior of an old temple, the hardy soldier fearlessly meeting his awful doom.

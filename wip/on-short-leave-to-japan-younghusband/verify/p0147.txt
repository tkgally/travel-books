A little further on in this courtyard is the solid grey granite trough of holy water in which the faithful, on payment of a small fee, may purify themselves before proceeding. We noticed that, though many threw a copper to the attendant, comparatively few accepted the proffered ladle of water. Close to the trough is another fine building, in which is to be found a revolving bookcase containing the Buddhist scriptures, as at Asakusa; but the vulgar are not here allowed to turn it.

In the next courtyard above are to be seen, on payment of a small fee, in a corridor, many relics of the Shogun: his litter, dresses, several swords, helmets, and very good pictures of hawks—hawking being his favourite pastime. At the other extremity of this court is a sacred dancing-shed, in which is seated a lady—well, no longer young—clad in a scarlet skirt, and with a white cap on her head somewhat like that of a Norman peasant.

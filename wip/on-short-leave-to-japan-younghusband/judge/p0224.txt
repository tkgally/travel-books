# Chapter XV
[[subtitle]] Osaka to the End

A <sc>short</sc> journey of ninety minutes by train from Kyoto brings one to Osaka, celebrated for its castle, the number of troops stationed there, and the water scene at night. Our visit was a purely professional one, connected with the troops, and the climate was far too sultry to allow of our taking any but a jaundiced view of the place. 91° Fahrenheit at 8 <sc>p.m.</sc>, without punkahs, or other visible means of support, reminds one of the most turgid nights in June in Upper India. Moreover, there are probably more mosquitoes and sand-flies in Osaka than in the rest of the Japanese Empire put together. Professionally our visit was eminently a success; socially, except from the point of view of the mosquito and his little friend, it was a failure.

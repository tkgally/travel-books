A book composed entirely of personal experiences and adventures may become nauseous; one dealing extensively with history is open to the charge of being unoriginal or guide-bookish, even though the facts may have been gathered from sources far removed from guide-books, while one devoted largely to architectural description is considered dry. All these subjects as well as the natural scenery, antiquities, and customs of the people have a bearing on the interest of a Spanish tour, and a writer must cull from all if he would present an intelligible picture of a portion of what may be seen in Spain to-day.

Finally, this book makes no pretension to being an exhaustive treatise on the subjects touched upon.

F. B. W.

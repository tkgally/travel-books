“Can you not get us a room somewhere in the town?”

“Yes,” he replied vaguely, his eyes riveted on the cyclometer of the woman’s machine, “I think so.” Then with a look of delight at the bright thought that occurred to him, “That measures the distance does it not?”

We nodded and asked again, “Will you get us a room?”

“Oh yes, I will see about it soon. How many kilometres did you say an hour?”

“Fifteen to eighteen as the road is,” we answered, inwardly enraged, “but your honour will get us a room soon, we are tired.”

“Yes, yes.” And then he added, his estimate of our powers being evidently influenced by the enormous stories circulated among the people since the introduction of the bicycle, “Eighteen kilometres is nothing; we have a man in Tarancon who rides fifty an hour.”

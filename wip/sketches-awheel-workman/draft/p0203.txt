[[cont]] One side of the hill above the town is honeycombed with caves which are used by the poor as dwellings free of rent and taxes. These caves run in tiers with paths between, and before each is a garden, in which grow the prickly pear, fig, vines, maize, and vegetables. The combination of rock and foliage gives the whole hillside that singular appearance of petrous fertility seen only in southern lands and particularly in the presence of cactus growth. The people seemed quite as comfortably situated as many who lived in houses, and in general appearance this almshouse hill of Alcala was far more attractive than the Gypsy quarter of Granada. Doubtless these caves have the same advantage over ordinary houses as those of Granada, of being warmer in winter and cooler in summer.

At the <i>fonda</i> the chambermaid brought us some beautiful carnation pinks, and the <i>mozo</i> placed roses on our plates at dinner, which was served in a <i>comedor</i> opening on a marble-floored <i>patio</i> with singing fountain.

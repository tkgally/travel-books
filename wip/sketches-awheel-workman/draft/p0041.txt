Fourth course, roast mutton, with asparagus and salad. The mutton is often replaced by <i>volaille</i> or other roast. Dessert, biscuits, macaroons, delicious roasted almonds, and cheese. Sometimes also a delicate marmalade is added. The wine, served in large carafes, is the heavy red Spanish wine, the quality of which is better in the south than in the north.

The company at dinner was typically Spanish, all <i>caballeros</i>, all travellers, and all very jolly. They greeted us with courtly bows and “Buenas noches ustedes.” Nearly all wore their hats and several their <i>capas</i>, or cloaks. One couple especially interested us, a “grande,” or lord, with his servant. The master, a dark, handsome man, was a Velasquez, in his black <i>capa</i> lined with ruby plush, which fell with the grace of a Moorish burnous from his shoulders, as he ate his <i>manzo con garbanzos</i>.

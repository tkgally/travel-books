The Zaragozans seem wanting in appreciation of the care that is due to their art inheritances. On visiting the Casa Zaporta with its beautiful sixteenth century <i>patio</i>, the lower story is found occupied by a waggon factory and the upper parts given over to other business enterprises, and to poor families who run up and down the once palatial stairway and use its carved pillars and ornamented reliefs for purposes of their own, entirely oblivious to any damage resulting from such use.

The same indifference as to decorative value and the iconoclastic spirit more or less prevalent in Spain caused them to tear down the leaning tower, also built in the sixteenth century in Moorish style, which while lacking the fineness of real Moorish work was, if one may form an opinion from photographs, interesting on account of its form and of its leaning ten feet

Charming fountains with projecting tile roofs covered with violets, buttercups, and grasses abound, which serve as pleasing offsets to the refuse heaps. The Jewish quarter is less interesting than that of Tlemcen in Algeria, although the narrow streets spanned by arches are curious and Oriental. The <i>boutiques</i> of the gunsmiths were duly inspected as were those of the painted wood workers. In several of these last we saw men ornamenting large wooden bride boxes, which Salem said are still used for conveying the bride to her new home.

Salem seemed very much at home in Tetuan and walked with a grand air, stopping every few steps to greet Moorish friends with hand-shakes and often with a kiss. Towards eleven o’clock he took us into one of the prominent streets, where there were several <i>boutiques</i> provided with seats not apparently used for business purposes.

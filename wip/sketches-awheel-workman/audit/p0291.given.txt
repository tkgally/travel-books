Chapter XVII.

The University Town of the Tormes—The Professor Zamora—Mediæval Capital of Castile—Street Scenes during the Feria at Burgos—Cathedral—Tombs at Miraflores—Last of the Cid.

TOWARDS noon we came in sight of the decayed university town of Salamanca; in which Ford says “The commonest editions of the classics are hardly to be bad.” With its buff cathedral and colleges rising above rich green meadows that slope gently to the blue river, for the Tormes is really blue, the city forms one of the finest color pictures in Spain. The Tormes, its broad sweep of water spanned by a grand bridge of tweuty-six arches, partly Roman partly of the time of Charles V., is like the Duero at Zamora the strong feature of all the views from and towards Salamanca.

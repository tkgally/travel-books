The pilot has come on board. And how strange it seems that such a small rope should go out, the little rope with the leaden weight, the “blue pigeon,” to measure the depth of the water so that the stately dame who has carried us securely on her bosom, still holding her head majestically aloft, may enter the harbour in safety.

“Eat an apple when approaching Cape Town,” one passenger had advised. “The dull grey city under the shadow of the overhanging mountain will give you melancholia, but you can’t eat and cry at the same time.”

But only links with the land left behind can make one sad on entering so lovely a bay. Table Mountain does

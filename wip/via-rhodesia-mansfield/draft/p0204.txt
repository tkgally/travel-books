One gets quite accustomed to lightning in Africa, it is one of its chief characteristics, and I think attractions; it comes sometimes with an organ accompaniment of thunder, when the light flashes and the earth seems to split beneath one’s feet, while one expects to see the heavens open and reveal all their hidden wonders, but more often the lightning is dancing or gliding across the sky without sound to herald its approach, with movements more beautiful perhaps because of the silence; we must look and watch, our ears are resting, the music of motion is enough.

It is night, and suddenly you see, behind a bank of dark clouds, a shining silver snake running along on its belly, trying to wriggle out, or as a silver rocket the light leaps upward and cuts the clouds with a shiver and a spring, and the sky is like a cracked looking-glass.

Sometimes the lightning comes in spreading sheets, and illuminates the sky behind the grey clouds and turns them to curtains of rose-pink beauty, or the night is cloudless, and you have an uninterrupted view of the whole of the horizon, and then the wonder of travelling flashes of lightning will astonish and hold you spellbound. From east to west, from north to south, no matter which direction the living light has chosen to travel, it will cross the entire archway, rivalling the travelling stars in speed and beauty, for it is a long, luminous serpent, a gigantic comet, with a quivering, moving tail.

Lightning, as well as stars, will appear in or change to various hues and colours.

At my next stopping-place, about eighteen miles further on, there was a so-called Rest house, a hut kept for passing travellers, but I object to stuffy huts kept by natives, and so I refused to camp there, although my boys in advance had already lighted a fire and seemed to conclude that there I should certainly stay.

I, however, left them all chatting and arguing, and walked off up a hill. They had to follow, and pitch my tent where I directed, grumbling all the time, for they had heard that there were many lions in the neighbourhood, and very busy they were for several hours making barricades of trees, and the fires that night seemed like a positive wall of flame dividing our camp from the rest of the world.

Of the journey down the Shire and Zambesi I can write little, for I was ill most of the time, so tired that I just wanted to sleep for ever and ever. I remember only that it was a comfortable boat and that the captain was fair and kind, and that for companions I had a Government nurse—and a missionary.

At Chinde, I remember, there seemed nothing but sand. I remember nothing else save a harmonium and hymns at the boarding-house, which disturbed me when I wanted to sleep, and a cheery English Consul, who invited me to the Consulate and took me out in his boat to the tug. And I am afraid I was not a bit pleasant to anybody, for I was oh! so tired, and nothing seemed so desirable as sleep.

We therefore went to the Chinese farm that was nearest to the border, and obtained information from the farmer of the way to the savage villages. After leaving him, we soon passed the cleared land, and at last got into the bed of a watercourse leading up the ravine between the hills. We scrambled on for some distance, the wood getting thicker as we advanced, but could find neither natives nor huts, so returned disappointed. When near the farm again we met our friend the farmer, who had called out all his army, consisting of nine men, armed with matchlocks, spears, long knives, and shields, and was marching to our assistance, as he had thought, from our long absence, that we had got into trouble. Although we felt grateful to him for his anxiety to rescue us if necessary, it was difficult to keep from laughing at the appearance of the motley array.

At this time the people of Japan were very unsettled in consequence of the great changes that had taken place in the country, and there was a great feeling in favour of war among nearly all classes.

To stir up the people still more, the fête days of the Japanese Empress Jingu-kozu, who had partly conquered the Corea about <sc>a.d.</sc> 300, were held with great solemnity; processions took place during the day and illuminations at night. She was canonized for these exploits and for being the mother of Hachimang, who completed the conquest and had been created their God of War, and was still worshipped as such. The priests of both the Buddhist and Sintoist temples united in doing honour to the occasion.

One evening I was asked to a Japanese dinner to meet an official of high rank that I was acquainted with. The dinner was given in a very nice native house; the guests were served with dishes on little tables about a foot high and supplied with bowls for rice, fish, and the various delicacies.

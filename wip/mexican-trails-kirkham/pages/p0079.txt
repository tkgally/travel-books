The oft-repeated lines are to them like cradle songs. From childhood they have gazed upon that barnstorming group, the stage street, the well-beloved beach with its painted waves.

Don Juan is discovered in the boudoir of an estimable lady not his wife. He is evidently away from home and taking a much-needed rest from the omniscient feminine eye, enjoying himself accordingly. The boudoir, which looks chilly and forbidding, suggests the parlour of a Mexican hotel. But Don Juan, superior to mere externals, fairly radiates good nature. The scene, intended to be slightly <i>risqué</i>, does not offend so long as you do not understand what is said. Entering into the fun, the audience is with Don Juan heart and soul. He cracks a few of the good old jokes, of the broad and ample proportions characteristic of Spanish humour. The people are delighted.

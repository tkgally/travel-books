All that afternoon Colima was more or less in view as we gradually descended into one of the most beautiful of Mexican valleys. It was nine o’clock when Los Reyes was discovered, and the tired beasts dragged their weary legs up the long street, which seemed an endless lane in a jungle of bananas. Man and beast put up for the night at the <i>meson</i>, which is a sort of caravansary. There were tea and bread for supper, and corn-stalks for the beasts. The “tea” was made of orange leaves. Everywhere peons were lying on the floor, wrapped in their zerapes; but I, being a rich traveller, had a room. I kept on my clothes to make sure of them, and hung the revolver over the bed-post.

Los Reyes was left before daybreak without a regret. The following night was spent at Zamora, a very fair town, where fresh horses were secured.

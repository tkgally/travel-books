Never a monk’s garb is to be seen, nor are the priests much in evidence. The problem which sorely tries France to-day was solved in Mexico with the aid of bayonets fifty years ago. At that time my hotel was the infirmary of the great Franciscan monastery; the stable adjoining—a rare old building—was the refectory; while the Calle Independencia, on which they front, was cut through the monastery garden when the monastery was suppressed and the monks arrested for treason in September of that year.

On the east side of the Plaza stands the National Palace, a vast rather than imposing structure, devoted to government offices. The President has his residence elsewhere and may occasionally be seen abroad, surrounded by his body-guard. He looks what he is—the Man of Iron, the most forceful character in Mexico.

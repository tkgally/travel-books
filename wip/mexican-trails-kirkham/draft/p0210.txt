From Tejalpa, the trail crosses a low range of hills, and, descending the other side, it is as if you had come upon one of the South Sea Islands, for the entire slope is covered with vast numbers of palms, while on the Tejalpa side not one is to be seen. This palm, known by the Indians as the <i>tetilla</i>, is used by them for many purposes.

At eleven o’clock I rode into Yuatepec, beguiled on the way by a blue grosbeak, which led me over the fields like a will-o’-the-wisp. Yuatepec was asleep. The guard slumbered in front of the <i>carcel;</i> the people dozed in the market, the dogs in the road. The small stream that flows through the town also slumbered. A more somnolent place I have never seen; its lethargy might have lasted a hundred years.

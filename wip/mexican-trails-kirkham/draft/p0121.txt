It was in the first half of the eighteenth century that he came to New Spain, at a time when the land was essentially for the Spaniard, and for no one else, and the alien was not welcomed to share in its opportunities. But this man with a head on his shoulders—such is the rumour—voyaged to Spain, and, securing certain concessions, returned prepared to do business. At Tasco, a little hill town, remote from railroads to this day, he literally took out millions. One of his millions he put into the church of Tasco, to conciliate the Church, it may be, which was a power in that day and could not well abide seeing silver of New Spain diverted from the ecclesiastical coffers. Another million went into the garden in Cuernavaca, a concession not to the Church this time, but to the luxurious fancy and the pride of Don José himself.

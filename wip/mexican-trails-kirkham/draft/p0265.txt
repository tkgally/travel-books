In Oaxaca the talk is of mines, as in Cuautla of symptoms. Pass any group on the street and you hear “shaft,” “tunnel,” “crosscut,” “veins.” Day and night this goes on without cessation. Enthusiasts outside your window awaken you in the small hours discussing “concentrates” and “carbonates.” Here are no politics—the world is mining. Your café rings with the vernacular of the camp. It is a new conquest of Mexico, but the same old thirst for gold.

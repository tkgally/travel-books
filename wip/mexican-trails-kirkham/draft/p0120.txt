The garden is old, but one thinks of one’s youth. May not the perfume of jasmine or of a rose suddenly bring up the vision of a woman? ’Tis magic-play thus to conjure a face out of the all-obliterated past, and with it a train of associations and memories, like long-forgotten airs. That is a spell which surely resides in the garden. One might perchance read Omar in such a place—to another—and the thoughts, mellowed by the rare old vintage and bouquet, flow rhythmically and with that metre which is like music.

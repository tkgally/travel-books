“Ah, bey, cey, tshey, dey, ey, effey!”—shouted an urchin who looked like a little woolly black bear.

“Enne, enn-yey, O, pey, koo!”—roared another, whereupon the two grasped each other by the hair.

The larger boy who had been patting the bluing on the floor, now approached and rubbed his blue hands vigorously over the faces of the combatants, which sufficiently subdued them. On his way back he finished wiping his hands on a towsled head here and there with a certain patronising air, which identified him as a sort of monitor.

What mischief there was in those roving black eyes. Mexican children frequently have beautiful eyes—except for which this class suggested a band of monkeys.

A murmur of appreciation ran through the little crowd which shifted about, the better to see. In Orizaba was no better subject than the Painter himself, seated before his easel and surrounded by a cloud of admiring witnesses.

At length I bore him off to an inn close by, and the talk was of many things. He had left the stick to his umbrella in one town, and part of his easel in another—what painter has not? He had sampled every new kind of dulce on the street, as was his wont, and experimented with the various Mexican concoctions. The latest was a tequila punch, pink, and of flavour indescribable. I think it was his eye for colour which led him to investigate. The tequila punch looked like a solution of rose madder. He had the courage of the man who ate the first oyster. If he had come upon a drink of cobalt blue or apple green, he would have tried it. He had made some new Mexican acquaintances—

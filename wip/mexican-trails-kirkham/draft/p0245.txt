I am resolved to stand before the far-famed ruins of Mitla; to follow the long trail to Tehuantepec in the south, and to Colima in the west; to saunter in old Morelia, drift on the bosom of Chapala, and feel for myself the Syrian spell of Guanajuato. But first I shall ascend the mountains and cross the maguey fields to Puebla.

My train crawls up the edge of the table-land to the Mexico of the high plateau, so different from the Mexico of which Orizaba is a shining part. One naturally recalls in this journey the experiences of the Conquerors in their remarkable ascent from the sea, with their “cavalry” of twelve horses—or was it sixteen?—and their little blunderbusses.

Such is the vision before you as you follow the Atlacomulco road from Cuernavaca to the hacienda, with its rose-pink walls. Nearly four centuries have elapsed since Cortez with his Tlaxcalan allies defeated the Tlahuicas in Cuernavaca, their capital. All these years the descendants of that ancient Indian tribe have trod this highway. Doubtless they have changed but little. They receive some schooling now, to be sure, but if you visit their huts clustered above the hacienda, or at Juitepec, you will see the primitive dwellings and mode of life of antiquity.

As one rides daily through this enchanting valley, wrapped in ineffable silence, the impression grows that in such a land, where nature is at once sublime and infinitely charming, there should have sprung both a literature and a philosophy.

[[cont]] Once we landed amidst the rushes to pick the fruit of the granadita growing on the bank. Several islands dot the lake, and from a village on the steep slopes of Xanicho the sound of the church bell came softly over the water. It was thus that the Tarrascan kings came in their royal dugouts to the gaiety, or the solitude, of Patzcuaro. No pleasure craft float upon its waters now, no royal caieques. There are no tourists—no kings. Only the paddle of an occasional fisherman’s canoe breaks the silence. Along the shore are the primitive huts of the simple fisher-folk, and their nets hung out to dry. Gathering the rushes which grow at the water’s edge they make them into mats, their only occupation beside fishing.

The Indians paddled swiftly and we sped over the serene surface for three hours or more, rounding one headland after another.

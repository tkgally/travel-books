In the Zocalo the band plays for the peon; for the more cultivated ear there is music in the Alameda. He whose forefathers wielded the obsidian war-club, and ate the hearts of their captives, is here beguiled by the toot and wheeze of a brass band. What shades of brown and yellow time and dirt dye these sombreros, and what infinite rolls and twists may be given to the vast brims, like the myriad forms of autumn leaves. One rolls his thus, and another thus, as suits his taste, and always it is a fitting frame for his dark Malayan or semi-Mongol face, which becomes tranquil as he wraps his zerape about him and listens to the rag-time of the band.

So we studied the splendid old pulpit, the work of artists, and wandered into the sacristy, which is a storehouse of beautiful things—antique walnut chairs you would gladly loot, and a superb table which might have been designed for the Medici. The walls are covered with the really good paintings of Cabrera; while from their chests, the old sacristan took out copes, chasubles, stoles, and scarfs, all heavy with gold, which were brought long ago from France.

From the parapet, we looked again at the abandoned shaft and tunnels across the barranca. It is an ugly little red hill, yet it paid for the cathedral and made it possible. The Florentine carving, the iron work, the broidered vestments, the rich and massive stone work—everything but the blue and white paint—sprang from this little red hill.

To-day Gabriel has on a clean cotton suit, washed by Luz in the ditch in front of their shack, the ditch in which she rinses the few clay pots and dips their drinking-water. I know the house, and I shall pull up there and talk with the children. This being market-day, the villagers are strung out along the road. They come from Juitepec and beyond, little family groups, plodding along together, and neighbours chatting as they walk. Not one but carries something. Old dames, riding sorry nags, bring chickens to market, shortening the way with gossip and tobacco. Market-day is in effect the social affair of the week. Juitepec and Santa Maria meet and smoke together and tell the news.

A solid line of carriages comes up the Paseo and winds around Chapultepec. There are well appointed turn-outs in scores, for this drive is <i>the</i> amusement of the classes, as immutable an institution as the bull-fight or the pulque-shop with the masses. Social life is restricted; they do not play golf or tennis; they have no informal entertainments, and make little of the theatre. They have occasional balls and—they drive in the Paseo.

The little orchestra is playing in the casino. Tables are full; some people smoke with their soup and everybody tries to talk above the music.

But on the other side of the garden it is very quiet. Here the world does not intrude and I am left to the contemplation of the ahuehuetls and the violets growing at their feet.

But I came at length, after such mild adventures, to Chapala—one of the few towns in Mexico that may be called a resort. Like Cuernavaca, it is one of the idle places of earth—a place in which to loaf and invite your soul. You may wander along the shore or float idly on the lake; you may bathe in December. But if floating and dreaming are not to your taste, Chapala is not for you. Every one there belongs to the leisure class.

Lake Chapala has a languid beauty, according with voluptuous and dreamy moods. The scenery is not “sublime” but very lovely. A railroad circular says that Humboldt considered this unsurpassed by any lake in the world, and that it has over a thousand square miles of water. But Humboldt saw it in its prime. Like Popocatepetl it has changed. When I went to school Popocatepetl was twenty-one thousand feet high. Now it is less than eighteen thousand. But Chapala is still charming.

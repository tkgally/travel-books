While he chanted, my eye, roaming over the immediate hieroglyphics, lit on a serpent coiled in a chink in the stone, and as motionless as the rock itself. Of venerable aspect, he was perhaps himself the rightful guardian of the teocalli, as was the owl ot Tepoztlan. He seemed to accord with the place and to belong to it, or it to him.

Herculano returns at night to the village of Teclama, there being neither water nor food to be had nearer, and thus the teocalli is left to the serpent, the symbol of the great god Quetzalcoatl—to whom, perhaps, it was erected.

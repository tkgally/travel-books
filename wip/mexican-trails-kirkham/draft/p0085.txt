The seed must fall on good ground if it is to thrive and bear fruit—an environment un-American, unprogressive, Latin; where no one hurries, where to-morrow will answer as well as to-day, if not better, and the atmosphere is charged with a gentle, unobtrusive, contented spirit.

In Cuernavaca one basks not only in the matchless sunshine of Mexico, but in this genial atmosphere—as in a finer, more subtle light—into which no hurry or strenuousness has ever been injected. And if one must wait for one's coffee and be resigned, surely it is a small price to pay.

The key-note of this Mexican life is surrender, as with us it is assertion. There is a certain patio to which I turn, as to some delightful palace of dreams, to relinquish myself to a delicious lethargy. It might be written, “Who enters here leaves the world behind.” Only a faint hum penetrates the thick old Spanish walls.

He had made his peace with the good fathers, and they, it would seem, were ready to back him for a new venture. How or when he died I do not know, but the church at Tasco and the Jardin Borda, which dates from about 1750, serve to keep his memory green.

It was thus the Borda garden came into existence, the mines of Tasco and the brain of José de la Borda being in conjunction. Here he planned, perhaps, some of his ventures, and here he may have brooded over his losses; or it may be he came here for garden-thoughts, poetic fancies, and escape from the world. Since it was he invoked it, he must have come under its spell.

Adjoining the shop of the tailor is one rather larger than the rest and without windows. Over the door swings a blue signboard, bearing the inscription “<i>Casa de Prestamos.</i>” This is the government pawn-shop, and it is better patronised than any other store in the street.

Its tiers of shelves are quite full of carefully done-up packages, all ticketed. These neat rolls are rebosos, shirts, zerapes, and the cotton trousers of little boys. A peon will pawn anything he has, even to his machete. There is, for instance, one shelf full of carpenters’ tools, saws, hammers, and planes.

If your laundry is not all sent back, you have good cause to suspect the missing portion has been pawned. You have but to wait and it will be returned intact. Last week I could not get my clothes, which I took to mean they were among the neatly rolled packages, duly ticketed.

The difference between the Atlantic and Pacific slopes is remarkable, even on this narrow isthmus. Once the low range of mountains is crossed—a shrunken vertebra in the great backbone of the Cordillera—the palm jungle of the eastern slope gives place to the cacti and arid lands of the Pacific coast. At Tehuantepec it is almost rainless, while just over the hills, rain is frequent. This peculiarity is as marked in Mexico as in South America. In fact, all sorts of climates and conditions belong to Mexico, induced by altitude, latitude; and position east or west of the plateau; but none more remarkable than his, where in a few hours you pass from the damp, soggy jungle of lower Vera Cruz to the semi-dcsert of southern Oaxaca.

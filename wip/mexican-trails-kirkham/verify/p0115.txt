# Chapter VII

[[subtitle]] A Garden of New Spain

A<sc>bout</sc> an old garden with its subtle intimations, its web of romance, fancy-woven, there is a rare fascination; while if not neglected it has acquired that charm which is the gift of age alone, that harmony of wall and vine and rose, of the shaded walk and the trees which overhang it. There is a softening of lines, a confusion of identity, an intermingling into bowers. This is particularly true of old gardens in Mexico, which are very different from old French or Italian gardens where there is such evidence of care and the effort to produce effect. Here Time, more than any gardener, is the wonder-worker.

The Borda Garden in Cuernavaca is not of Mexico so much as of New Spain

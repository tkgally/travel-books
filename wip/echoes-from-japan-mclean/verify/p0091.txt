## Temporary Appointment.

I have accepted a temporary appointment in a Government school. Some of my pupils come to see me each Sunday morning, when an opportunity offers for Bible readings in English, which they seem to enjoy. The questions they ask about the sacred Scriptures show how entangled they are in the meshes of their Shinto, Confucian, and Buddhistic theories. It is laid on my heart to pray that <i>Jesus</i>, the “<i>Way</i>, the <i>Truth</i>, and the <i>Life,</i>” may soon become a living reality to them. My pupils at Nogé-Yama School are principally <i>Samurai</i>. The difference between them and the <i>Heimin</i> (common people) consists in their being more refined, and being, as a rule, book-worms. Their sketchings on the blackboards, as well as their neat figures and legible writing, give, at once, an idea of their capabilities. I am not allowed to teach the Bible during school hours, but I read it to myself at my dinner hour, when a bevy of them flock around me, to ask questions about it. I tell them it is to my soul, what the food I eat is to my body. I enjoy God’s presence to such an extent in this schoolroom, that I would give the world to be able to proclaim it to the whole of Japan.

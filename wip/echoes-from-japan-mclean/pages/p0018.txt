These celestial visitants are called, in mythology, Ten-jin-shi-chi-dai, or seventh generation of gods, <i>i.e.</i>, from Kuni-toku-tatsu-no-mikoto (lit. the God whose kingdom is everlasting). They are said to have had three children: first, Ama-no-terasu-no-mikoto (a female), Susano, and Hiruko (males). The fifth generation, and last of the gods terrestrial, was U-ga-ya-fu-ki-awa-zedzu-no-mikoto. From him, it is said, descended the primogenitor of the one hundred and twenty-three Mikados who have sat successively on the throne of Japan. The Emperor Jimmu was, in reality, a king, or chief of Kiu-Shiu. He and his army having gained a victory over the islands of Japan, he ascended the throne of that country, at a place called Kashi-wa-bara, and founded the dynasty of the Mikados. This took place when Manasseh ruled over the house of Judah. With the help of two of his provincial governors—Koku-zo and Agata-nushi—he ruled his realm with great ability.

As to a geographical sketch of the country, I feel that, in these days of enlightenment, it is unnecessary to draw your attention to a map of the world, and say that Japan is bounded on the north by the Sea of Yezo, on the east and south by the North Pacific Ocean, and on the west by the Sea of Japan and Straits of Corea.

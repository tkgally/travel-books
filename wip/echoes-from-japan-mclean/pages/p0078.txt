The following lines in acrostic, composed by a sailor on board H.M.S. <i>Thalia</i>, were given me by the Christians on board, when they left Yokohama.

<verse>
Time’s chariot swiftly bearing us—His smiling face to see,
Has in the past deep impress left, sweet to our memory.
A backward look to view the scene, and great that mystic sight,
Life issuing forth in victory, dispelling nature’s night.
In all life’s wanderings will we cherish dear,
A name by Him so blessed, to us so dear.
</verse>

On every Sunday evening, weather and “leave” permitting, the party, which soon became styled “the Christian band,” were in the habit of meeting for the express purpose of remembering the Lord’s death among themselves. The simplicity of this meeting had a beauty of its own, which I find it impossible to describe. They met <i>in the name of Jesus</i>, and His alone, and I always felt it a privilege to be permitted to be one with them.

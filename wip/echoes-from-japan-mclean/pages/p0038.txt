Many had already read and heard of the Lord Jesus, and were prepared to accept His love and His offered salvation; but of the many who clustered round the missionary it was not surprising to me and others, who were lookers on, to find that a large proportion had merely followed in imitation of others, a thing especially characteristic of the Japanese as a people.

My own work was principally teaching. I had gone to Japan with only a few dollars in my pocket, and therefore deeply felt the need of casting all my care upon Him who was my only hope and refuge in that strange land. Blessed be His Name, He then, and since, abundantly proved the truth of the words, “<i>He careth for thee.</i>” I had made known my willingness to give up a few hours daily to teaching English, but for a time my faith was sorely tried. One day a missionary called on me, bringing with him a young ex-Daimio and his vassal, who requested that I would give them two hours’ private instruction in English daily, for which they offered to pay me forty dollars a month. In this I saw the tender care of my Heavenly Father, for this liberal sum was twice as much as I should have asked had I made my own charges, and yet every dollar was needed to meet current expenses.

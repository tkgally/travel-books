She put it on, stood up in it for about five minutes, and—charged me five francs for doing so! Alas! the waves of this nineteenth century, breaking across Europe, have washed over the sands of her picturesque past, and have reduced all lands and peoples to one dead level of civilized sameness. But the Moslem remains <i>tel quel</i>, and long may he so continue!

One day, in a restaurant at Naples, we tendered by mistake some Tunisian pennies. They could not, of course, be accepted; but a waiter, who had once been there, overhearing the conversation, came forward and offered to change them. His face lighted up as he inquired, “Monsieur et Madame viennent de Tunis?” Who knows? Perhaps he, too, had the nostalgia of the desert upon him. Perhaps he, like me, had felt the spell of that marvellous land.

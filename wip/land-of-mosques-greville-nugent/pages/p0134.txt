But the afternoon wears on, and we must away to Bou-Zareah. The road which passes El Biar (<i>i.e.</i> the wells) is lined with the various wild flowers which grace this land—trails of clematis with greeny-white blossoms, a trefoil with wide-open yellow stars, a dignified member of the lily tribe from whose spreading leaves springs a beautiful tall spike of clustered white flowers, pink and brown fly orchids, giant hemlock, and a host of other beauties. Here and there along the road come strings of donkeys, carrying sand in baskets of green rushes, and pursued by the objurgations of brown-skinned drivers in tattered gandouras.

Here we come at last to the tombs, which stand in a wild garden laid out by the hand of Nature alone; and Nature in Northern Africa requires no gardener to help her.

All the same, you can dine exceedingly well in Algiers when once you have learnt not to order the fish or game of the country, and not to take the slightest notice of the Arab pedlars who persistently circle round your table.

“But where do they live, these <i>indigènes?</i>” you ask. Up in the old Moorish town, dear reader, where we will now proceed. For Algiers is, like a Neapolitan ice, in three layers. First you have the modern French part, representing the strawberry; then comes the Quartier Juif, which stands for the vanille; and beyond that the Arab town, which brings us finally to the pistache.

We will turn aside, then, from the lights and noise, and mount up these side streets by the Rue de la Lyre, and behold! a change has come over the scene, and Time has stood still for centuries.

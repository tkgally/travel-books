The women visit the cemeteries on Fridays in large numbers, and pray at the tombs.

A drive of about half a mile further brings us to a curious hill village, with an encampment of Kabyles. From the road nothing is visible but a forest of prickly pear immensely thick and high; but on looking about for an opening, we find a tiny path between the bushes and follow it, keeping discreetly in the middle, for the sharp thorns of these <i>figues de Barbarie</i> are not pleasant to tackle at close quarters. A few paces more, and we become aware of little low rude huts of rough stones, innocent of mortar, and roofed with branches and a very rough imitation of thatch. Each hut is surrounded by its own prickly-pear hedge, and seems a fitting abode for members of a barbarous tribe.

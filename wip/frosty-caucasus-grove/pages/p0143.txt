The dogs got rid of, Paul and I joined the others, and we plodded together along the path which, after rising for some distance, led us into a desolate glen with high, treeless sides of grass, and a strong stream roaring down through it. We crossed by a frail bridge, and making our way up the steep valley-side, were soon enveloped in a dense mist. Suddenly there appeared through it a phantom horseman, who shortly overtook us, and with many expressions of goodwill thrust his companionship on us, and whose doings during that day and the two following ones were to us a mystery and a wonder. His story, which he unfolded to the very unwilling ears of Paul, was that the chief of Kunim, reflecting after we had gone that we knew nothing of the country, had sent him after us to act as a sort of courier or dragoman, and to tell the men of Bezingi of our worth and nobility.

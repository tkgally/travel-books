In the evening the pleasant Mohammed came in for a farewell talk, and with the courtesy common to his family, earnestly begged us to come to Urusbieh again. ‘There is much to see near this valley,’ he said, ‘and plenty of game at no great distance from here. Between Suenetia and the Karatchai country there lies a mighty forest, the most wonderful I have ever seen. The trees are huge, and the flowers, which in places altogether cover the ground, are of the greatest beauty. Then the forest is full of game. There are deer, wild boar, bouquetin, and chamois,[[fnref 1]] all in plenty. A good hunter can kill much game there easily. Two or three men from here usually go over to hunt there during the summer season.’

<i>Myself.</i>—Are there any people or houses there?

<i>Mohammed.</i>—None whatever. The country is altogether without population. Anything that a man requires to live on besides the game he kills, he must take with him. You would much admire the forest, which is indeed a magnificent one.

What a picture this was!

The sheep are larger than the Welsh, though considerably under the size of the ordinary English animal. They have short curly wool, frequently black or grey, and are of the breed which furnishes the skins largely sold in England as cheap Astrakhan. The Caucasians are not at all particular about hanging their meat, but after killing a sheep usually skin it and cut it up at once with wonderful quickness, and then cook it immediately. There is an old saying to the effect that flesh cooked while still warm will be found quite tender, and this our experience altogether confirmed, for we found that mutton which had been thus rapidly dealt with was never tough. The native way of cooking was nearly always to boil. At Urusbieh there was an enlightened cook (probably a princess) who was acquainted with a form of pie, and could even make <i>rissoles,</i> but with this exception we found that the natives when in the villages never prepared their meat in any other way than by boiling it. They made excellent broth from the water in which it was boiled, which they were given to flavouring with a very strong kind of onion.

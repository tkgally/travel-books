## Saunders (Katherine).

THE HIGH MILLS. A Novel. 3 vols. Crown 8vo.

GIDEON’S ROCK, and other Stories. 1 vol. Crown 8vo. 6<i>s.</i>

C<sc>ontents</sc>:—Gideon’s Rock—Old Matthew’s Puzzle—Gentle Jack—Uncle Ned—The Retired Apothecary.

<blockquote>
“The tale from which the volume derives its title is especially worthy of commendation, and the other and shorter stories comprised in the volume are also well deserving of reproduction.”—<i>Queen.</i>
</blockquote>

JOAN MERRYWEATHER, and other Stories. 1 vol. Crown 8vo. 6<i>s.</i>

C<sc>ontents</sc>:—The Haunted Crust—The Flower-Girl—Joan Merryweather—The Watchman’s Story—An Old Letter.

MARGARET AND ELIZABETH. A Story of the Sea. 1 vol. Crown 8vo. 6<i>s.</i>

<blockquote>
“Simply yet powerfully told. . . . This opening picture is so exquisitely drawn as to be a fit introduction to a story of such simple pathos and power. . . . A very beautiful story closes as it began, in a tender and touching picture of homely happiness.”—<i>Pall Mall Gazette.</i>
</blockquote>

## Schell (Major von).

THE OPERATIONS OF THE FIRST ARMY UNDER GEN. VON GOEBEN. Translated by Col. C. H. von Wright. Four Maps. Demy 8vo. 9<i>s.</i>

<blockquote>
“In concluding our notice of this instructive work, which, by the way, is enriched by several large-scale maps, we must not withhold our tribute of admiration at the manner in which the translator has performed his task. So thoroughly, indeed, has he succeeded, that it might really be imagined that the book had been originally composed in English. . . . The work is decidedly valuable to a student of the art of war, and no military library can be considered complete without it.”—<i>Hour.</i>
</blockquote>

THE OPERATIONS OF THE FIRST ARMY UNDER GEN. VON STEINMETZ. Translated by Captain E. O. Hollist. Demy 8vo. 10<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

A very complete and important account of the investment of Metz.

<blockquote>
“The volume is of somewhat too technical a character to be recommended to the general reader, but the military student will find it a valuable contribution to the history of the great struggle; and its utility is increased by a capital general map of the operations of the First Army, and also plans of Spicheren and of the battle-fields round Metz.”—<i>Morning Advertiser.</i>
</blockquote>

These works form separate volumes of Messrs. Henry S. King and Co.’s Military Series.

<i>12th February,</i>—We are now looking forward to our New Year’s holiday, the 19th of February being the 1st day of what <i>we Celestials</i> call the <i>No.</i> 1. <i>Moon.</i> H. would very willingly spend the time quietly at home, which, however, he is not likely to be allowed to do, as we have various hospitable invitations to go to Foo-Chow, and I also hope to see something of the river scenery. I think after the great heat one <i>freezes</i> at a higher temperature than in Europe, for the thermometer has rarely been below 40, yet, clothed in serge and all the usual habiliments of winter, you would be surprised to see how much I have felt the cold, and to hear the good British grumble at the keen, dry, wind which has blown at times through our airy mansion. I often have had to go into the garden to get warm in the sun, as its rays never penetrate into our north rooms in winter, the very season when they would be welcome.

Letter XXX.

Foo-Chow, August 14th.

I am now staying with Mr and Mrs G.,—they having kindly come to the Arsenal to escort me here last Wednesday.

The unusually cool weather and charming breeze from the N.E. ended a few days ago in a Typhoon. We were at the time at a neighbor’s house which stands in a more exposed situation than this. Doors and windows crashed in all directions, and the poor trees seemed about to be torn up by the roots; a bit of glass from a window was driven nearly an inch into the roscwood case of the piano, which will give you some idea of the force of the wind, and yet I am told we only experienced the edge of the storm here. When the first tremendous gust came, one’s breath seemed absolutely to be blown away, for truly it was a violent seasoning to the idea of a husband afloat on the wild waves.

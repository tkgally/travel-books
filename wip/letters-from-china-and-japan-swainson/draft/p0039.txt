As yet all Chinamen seem to me to be exactly the same, but I suppose by and bye, I shall be able to distinguish a difference in their features. They are a most noisy race, so that when the work-people pass our house, which they do in hundreds, several times daily, I rush to the window to see what terrible catastrophe has happened.

I begin to understand, however, that all these tumultuous sounds mean nothing to excite alarm. Though they quarrel and use any amount of strong language, the combat is entirely oral and never comes to blows, but ends, generally speaking, in the antagonists running off in different directions, turning round and shrieking to each other as they go, their farewell words of abuse.

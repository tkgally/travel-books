The extent of the town is immense—indeed it is said to cover more ground than any town in the world except London, but many parts of it are quite deserted, whole streets being occupied by low, barracky-looking houses which were formerly occupied by the retainers of the different Daimios or Princes, when they visited Yedo. Under the Mikado régime, these princes are now requested to stay in the country, so their followers have no longer an opportunity of fighting each other, as they did in these “good old days” not long gone bye, for when H. was here before, almost every man carried two swords. We have seen a few who still do so, some of them very wild-looking fellows, but the custom has, to a great extent been given up, and many of the poorer <i>Yaconins</i> have turned their swords into shoe brushes, and become servants in the households of the European residents.

Some of the women we saw were rather pretty, in a pink and white doll style, but their faces were covered with flour or powder, and they gaped at us with an expression of face so utterly idiotic, I cannot understand how some people admire them so much.

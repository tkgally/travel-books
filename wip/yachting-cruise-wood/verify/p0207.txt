With the aid of their boat and our own towing us, we managed to get into the harbour, though it was quite dark, and anchored about 7 <sc>p.m.</sc>

In the morning we found we were lying about a hundred yards from the shore, on which stood the king’s house and a few others. Soon after daylight I received a large basket of fish from his majesty, and a quantity of bread-fruit and taro ready cooked. In return for this I found he expected a jug of coffee and some bread and butter.

This interchange of civilities took place every morning of our stay here.

The morning after our arrival the rain came down in floods and I was not able to get on shore till eleven o’clock. I first called on the king who had given us such a hospitable reception, and found him living in a most excellent house, furnished with a sofa and chairs, kerosene lamps, &c.

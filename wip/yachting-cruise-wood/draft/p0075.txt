On the 12th we moved down the coast, and anchored in Mataatua Bay, and while the photographic party was engaged on shore, I endeavoured to get some provisions, of which we were now very short, but I had no great success. The next day therefore we moved on again, but met with much calm weather, and not till Sunday afternoon did we make the island of Upolu, and drop anchor in the harbour of Apia, which is the white settlement of the Samoan group.

The town is pretty and very pleasantly situated, but I cannot say that I brought away any pleasant reminiscences of the place.

No doubt, if one arrived there as a simple passenger without any responsibility, a visit might be made enjoyable, for the resident merchants are most kind and hospitable. Here, however, the greater part of my white crew gave me constant trouble.

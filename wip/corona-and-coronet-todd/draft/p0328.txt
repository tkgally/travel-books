A native picture represents a man for an instant undecided whether he shall save parents, or wife and children. Characteristically seizing his aged mother, he is shown rushing with her in his arms to a place of safety, while wife and child, trying vainly to follow, are clutching at his dress in despair.

In one village the force of the wave was so great that one hundred pine-trees over ten feet in circumference were entirely torn away, leaving only their broken roots. But in other places men and women washed into the tops of trees were safely stranded—climbing down to the ground when danger was past.

More than one hundred and fifty victims were cast ashore upon Sankwan Island, and subsequently rescued. About the same number of convicts, released from jail at Okachi when the wave broke over the town, returned a few days after to the Miyagi jail of their own accord.

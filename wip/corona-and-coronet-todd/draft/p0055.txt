But a composite machine is possible, by which a hundred instruments are able to ask simultaneously a hundred different questions of the corona automatically, while one astronomer sets everything in motion, placing safe and implicit reliance in the precision of his mechanism. Fortunately, too, machinery has no nerves; for in the past, impressiveness of the scene at totality has been responsible for many a lapse in executing well-rehearsed programmes.

While the Coronet was buffeting Cape Horn swells and the great rollers of the Pacific, carrying tubes and mountings, the Astronomer was hard at work completing his invention in finer detail, until satisfied that the prospective minutes of total eclipse would be lengthened at least tenfold. Specifically, twenty telescopes and cameras were to observe and set down at the same time, all under electric supervision of one central mechanism; and exact records of the unemotional tool would be at hand after the eclipse was over, well adapted to patient study at leisure.

All along the eastern coast of the main island, as the Tairen-maru kept her steady way northward, were sad reminders of the tidal wave, now more than a month in the past. Supplies and money still being sent to the survivors, we stopped for two or three hours at Oginohama, near the southern end of the afflicted region, to leave a variety of necessities for the suffering people.

The town was dirty and sordid, but blossoming white lilies and purple hydrangea brightened it, and our own familiar clematis climbed all over shrubs and even trees on the mountain path leading past a pathetic little burial place. Just beyond was a Shinto shrine, full of <i>gohei</i> (sticks with fluttering paper prayers), a good many spirited drawings of a cock and hens, and a spherical bell with a thick red cord attached by which to ring attention from the presiding, but perhaps otherwise occupied, deity. A steep climb, accompanied by numerous little girls with babies on their backs, brought me to a larger Shinto temple with a mirror and rough drawings of horses. Overhead, tall cryptomerias shaded a spot doubtless charming in a sunny day, but rather too moist for comfort under gently falling summer rain.

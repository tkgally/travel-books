In the south the principal occupation was endeavoring to devise new balloon sails to catch every breath of the light trades, while on the return trip it was frequently a scramble to lower all sail in the shortest possible time. Instead of balloon canvas, the thought was to see how small a rag could be shown to the gales. The yachtsman who wishes to enlarge his experience and desires practice in handling his vessel under all conditions of wind and weather should cruise in the Pacific Ocean.

Leaving Yokohama the 2d of September took us to sea at the worst time of year. On the day before sailing a severe typhoon had passed up the coast, and three days later we encountered the edge of another which did immense damage about two hundred miles northwest of our position. It was, then, with a feeling of relief that we found ourselves at the end of a week's sailing beyond the reach of such unwelcome visitors.

From a study of the chart, one is led to expect a current setting along the coast of Japan and across the Pacific far greater in volume and strength than the Gulf Stream of the North Atlantic.

Although a late census numbers about 17,000 Ainu, a slight gain over previous years, the impression seems to be generally prevalent that they are actually and steadily dying out. Half-breeds, Ainu and Japanese, rarely survive the second or third generation. The race evidently lacks force, and will be entirely unable to hold its own in the march of nations. Bears are decreasing in number; many characteristic customs are forbidden by law, and will soon die out completely; and gradual extinction of the race will be a pathetic feature of the further development of the Hokkaido.

But sun and moon, in their inconceivable flight through space, were almost in line, the day was close at hand, and my interest in these singularly fascinating Ainu was lost in a study of clouds and weather conditions, the working of apparatus, and of celestial rather than earthly curiosities.

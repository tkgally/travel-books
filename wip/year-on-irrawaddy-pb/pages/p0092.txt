“Well any news?” he asks as he stretches himself luxuriously in a long chair, and sets down an empty glass.

“Nothing startling,” is the answer, “Anything happened down below?”

“I see that So-and-So has come to grief,” the Skipper replies. “I met him below Palow taking down a holed flat.”

“Quite time he went home,” the other man says, “he’s breaking up, and I don’t wonder at it,—”

And so the conversation goes on. For an hour, I sit and listen to undiluted “shop.” Sailors are well known to be good grumblers. Flotilla men live up to their reputation. If I am to believe all I hear, this is the worst low water season within the memory of man; never have the channels been so shallow and winding, never has the river taken such an awkward and unexpected course, and never has there been less water in the Irrawaddy than just now. The Skipper says that he can take his ship over a good heavy dew, but she won’t sail over dry-land, and he threatens to drop the “killock” and take casual leave, and go shooting until he gets water enough to float him.

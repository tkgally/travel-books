He may, possibly, get into another predicament. It is left to his discretion as to what draught the oil flats are to be loaded to at Nyounghla. Believing the rise to be permanent, he may order them to be loaded to five, or six, feet. Then, if the rise subsides, he will be left stranded, unable to get through the shallow channels, and the delay may be considerable. On the other hand, he may be too prudent, and bring down a light cargo, while the water continues to rise. This means loss of money both to himself and to the Company. These, however, are the difficulties of the low-water season. When the rains begin, the rise is permanent.

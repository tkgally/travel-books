“Munepoor ahoy! Say Skipper, is that you? Bring the Chief across and have a hand at Bridge. I’ve got another man here.”

“Can’t,” sings out the Skipper, “Memsahib’s on board. Come over here.”

“Oh! All right thanks! We’ll be across in a minute.”

The rather long “minute” is occupied I surmize in getting out of their lungyis or Shan bags into decent clothes. Meanwhile we await their arrival with interest, because we have not the least idea whose voice it was addressing us.

At length they come aboard, and the Skipper instantly recognises one of them as a man he bad last seen in Bhamo, near the Chinese frontier, about nine years previously. At present, he is having the rather strange experienee of travelling down the river on a raft of teak logs. The only accommodation he has is a little mat hut built on the centre of the raft, and this forms a rather inadequate protection from the noon-day heat the cold at night.

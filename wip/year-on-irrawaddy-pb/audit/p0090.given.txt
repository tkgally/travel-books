At last they begin. Each coolie has a sack hoisted on to his shoulders and a tally stick put into his hand. He trots along the gangway, passes the stick to one of the crew; whose duty it is to collect and count them, and then drops the sack on the upper deck.

A coolie always has such thin legs that one expects them to snap like a carrot across the shins under the load he carries, and the brown shoulders glistening with perspiration, look too narrow and weak to support their burden. The Burman coolies are slightly more muscular in build than the natives of India. The latter are a curious looking crowd. Some of them have absolutely faultless Greek prosiles; all of them appear to possess some degree of dignity and intelligence, yet their behavior is childish in the extreme.

At Prome, where we anchor for the night, we receive the home mail which has been sent up by train from Rangoon. One pilot is sent ashore, and the other, with the letters in his charge, is brought off. We anxiously watch the boat nearing the ship; and the purri-wallah, who knows that haste is expedient, stands waiting on the flat. He seizes the packet before anything else is handed out of the boat and runs with it to the “Captain Sahib” who is ready, knife in hand, to cut the string. He stands by while the letters and papers are sorted and then runs along with a goodly pile to the <i>Burra Maistri Sahib</i>. The arrival of the mail is a big event to him because his wife and the child which he has never yet seen are in Scotland and the weekly mail is all the solace <i>he</i> gets out of married life for five long years.

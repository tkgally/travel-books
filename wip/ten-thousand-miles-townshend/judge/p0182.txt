Descending into the gorge of the Platte, we rode down the bed of the river, which, though rapid, is not deep. The mountains approach so close on either side as to leave no room even for a game track. The scenery is very grand. The rocks are composed of red sandstone, or red granite and quartz, towering to an enormous height above us. Wherever the mountain receded from the river and long grass appeared, we saw numerous tracks of elk and mountain sheep. On the right we passed a deep canôn, into which, last spring, a party of hunters managed to drive a herd of twenty-seven elk. There being no exit, they shot twenty-six of them, the remaining one making his escape by breaking through the line of hunters. As it was late and far from camp, they left the elk where they lay, intending to return for them next morning; but having neglected to take out the entrails, they found them on their return at the time specified all rotten.

After crossing several small tributaries of the Platte, we followed one into a narrow gorge where the overhanging mountains nearly met above our heads; riding through this gorge a short distance, we came to a small piece of meadow-land covered with rich green grass, which presented the most tempting spot for a camping ground. A little back from the river, half-burnt logs and bones of antelope, deer, and elk, together with a nearly perfect head of a big-horn, or mountain sheep, showed that a camp of hunters had not long left the spot. We were not, therefore, surprised when on turning a corner, about a mile distant, we came upon a wild-looking figure dressed in a greasy buckskin coat, old felt hat, and long boots, into which were tucked a tattered pair of blood-stained pants. Beside him grazed a bony three-cornered looking mustang. On seeing us the figure sprang up; but discovering that we were friends he sat down again and proceeded with the occupation from which we had disturbed him, which was that of drinking a horn of whiskey and water, and filling his cheeks with plugs of tobacco.

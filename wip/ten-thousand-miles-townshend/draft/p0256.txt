As we steamed slowly down the bay, a small steamer followed with a band on board, playing “Auld Lang Syne,” “St. Patrick’s Day,” and other popular melodies. After rounding Telegraph Hill we lost sight of the Metropolis of the Pacific, and two miles farther on passed Fort Point, where a gun was fired from our ship. Proceeding through the Golden Gate, as the entrance to the bay is called, we reached the open sea and steamed rapidly over the broad blue waters of the Pacific Ocean.

The steamers of the American Pacific Mail Company run from New York to the Isthmus of Panama, thence to Mexico and California, and thence to Japan and China. The time occupied in sailing from San Francisco to Japan is about nineteen days, and to China twenty-nine. The steamers are side-wheel or paddle, of from 3000 to 5000 tons burden.

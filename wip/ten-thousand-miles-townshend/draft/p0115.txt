Videttes were stationed on all the surrounding bluffs, and sentries posted round the camp, a cold enough duty, as the wind sweeps across the plain as freely as over the ocean, and the nights were often bitterly cold,—ice forming an inch thick. Reveillé was sounded at 4.30 <sc>a.m.</sc>, breakfast for us at 5.30, and we marched off at 6.30, just about sunrise. The only bugle call which resembled any of those of our cavalry was the one to stables, which was exactly similar.

The chances of a night attack are very small, as the Indians think that, if killed during the night, they will always have to hunt in darkness in the land of spirits. If, however, they get a chance to stampede the horses and mules, and surprise a camp without loss of life to themselves, they will do so.

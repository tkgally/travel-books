In whatever way the manners of the Indians may have reacted on the Whites, to the detriment of the latter, there is one Indian custom which might with advantage be imitated by the Yankees, and that is respect for old age. Among the Indians the oldest chief and warrior is looked upon as almost sacred, and treated with the greatest reverence. His advice is eagerly sought at the council fire by the other braves, and his influence is in proportion to his age. Among the Yankees age is despised, while children are taught to consider themselves as the equals of their elders, and are rather admired for impertinences which in England would procure them a sound flogging.

After breakfast we strolled up to the quarters of the 13th Hussars, who have a squadron stationed here. They kindly invited us to dinner, and put down our names for the Saint James’s Club, where we had the treat of seeing the “Times,” and other English papers, and hearing all the latest news from the old country. Wednesday we devoted to seeing the town, where I was surprised to hear so little English spoken in the streets. This is the case not only here, but also in Canada East generally, as most of the lower classes are of French descent, and talk a villanous <i>patois</i> of their own. Neither pure French nor good English seems to be understood generally by them. The genuine Irish brogue is, however, pretty constantly heard; most of the cab drivers here, as well as in the States, being Irish immigrants.

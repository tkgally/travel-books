We were much amused when walking down the street to see the verandahs, which were the entire length of the different hotels, occupied by a closely-packed row of men reclining in their chairs, which were slightly tilted back, while the legs of the occupiers were, without exception, perched up on the balcony. Though the Americans do not, as I have heard laid to their charge, put their feet up on tables and mantel-pieces, they certainly have a universal habit of raising their legs off the ground as nearly as possible on a level with their seat. Go into a barber’s shop, and you will find a stool of the same height as the chair, for the convenience of the customers. In the halls of the hotels, in the reading-rooms, on board ship, everywhere the legs are seen propped up on the nearest available support. The facilities for indulging in this posture are, no doubt, a great addition to one’s comfort, and in a country where the fair sex are so scarce as they are in America, it is no breach of good manners towards them to avail one’s self of them.

A good deal of masonry has also to be done, as the frame houses are often built on stone foundations. These forts are generally defended by high timber palisades and a block-house. The parties detached to the railway stations throw up small earthworks for their defence The foundations are dug out and raised only a few feet above the level of the surrounding country. The sides are loopholed, and a species of bomb-proof casemate thus constructed, whence the small party can fire on a much superior force, and be themselves secure from the arrows of their savage assailants. Nor are these precautions useless, as frequent attacks have been made on the stations by parties of Sioux, Cheyennes, and Rapahoes out on the war-path, and every now and then, the rails are torn up and the trains upset and sacked by the Red Skins.

My weapons consisted of a breech-loading twelve-bore shot gun by Purdey; a breech-loading twelve-bore double-barrel rifle by Westley Richards, throwing a very heavy bullet or shell, as I had with me moulds for casting both; and an American cavalry Spencer carbine, carrying eight shots, one in the barrel and seven in the magazine. A revolver and hunting-knife were stuck in a belt round my waist. The best sort of kit for hunting, both on the plains and in the mountains, consists of stout waterproof butcher or snake boots, buckskin breeches, and buckskin or flax hunting-shirt, with flannel underclothing. The very strongest material is indispensable, as when forcing one’s way through the trees, while following game in the Rocky Mountains, one’s clothes are pretty sure to be nearly torn to pieces. A heavy topcoat, waterproof sheet, a thick blanket, two buffalo robes, and a light portable mattress are also necessary; especially the buffalo robes, as the sun shines with great power in the day-time, while the nights are bitterly cold,—water in the mountains often freezing inside the tents.

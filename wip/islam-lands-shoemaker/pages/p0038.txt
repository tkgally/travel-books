As our boat glides nearer I look around to see what effect the great temple has upon our company. Some of them are gazing as though they could not with one pair of eyes each see enough of it. The naturalist is fixing his nets,—what are temples to him?—his wife is tying her shoe. One lady is locking her maid up that she may not have too good a time while the mistress is ashore. Ali, the waiter, stands counting his tips with which he has announced that he will buy another wife when he returns to Assouan. He is but twenty-three and has two already. Well to the front of the ship, with these fantastic mountains, the sacred river, and that gorgeous temple before him, P——is calmly reading the <i>Spectator</i>. The sight is too much for my composure, I double up with laughter and make personal remarks to him. He merely gazes at me a moment over the rim of his glasses and continues,—reading the <i>Spectator.</i> I may anticipate here and say that throughout all our days in many lands he would always upon such occasions,—read the <i>Spectator;</i> until finally the appearance of that journal in his hands always caused us to look abroad for some object of interest.

Abû-Simbel is a spot to be visited with congenial spirits only. Therefore we have waited until even our little crowd is on its return to the boat.

It would not appear that those walls could ever have formed much of a barrier unless very strongly manned, though of course ten years of storm and sun have obliterated much of them.

Here the level desert stretches off on all sides and yonder on the left bank of the river lies the long, low straggling city of which the world has heard so much and for which so much blood has been shed, Omdurman, the city of the Mahdi and Khalifa, the capital of a vanquished nation and declining faith. Its low mud wall spreads northward for a distance of ten miles, and beyond, just on the horizon, one can discern almost the only hill hereabouts, and which overlooks the famous battle-field where the Crescent received the most severe defeat and setback since the days of Charles Martel, for the siege of Granada was but a following of the French battle, but for which Spain would never have shaken off the Moslem yoke.

Omdurman lies close to the river, whose banks rise gently and are to-day covered by the people's market.

[[cont]]a boy in 1873 during the days of Ismail Pasha I met the men of the Egyptian nation, met them socially. I remember many long and pleasant talks with many of them. Now they are absolutely taboo not only by women but by men. An English or American girl would not think of having anything to do with any of them. She would not dare because of instant and unfavourable criticism.

Yesterday Miss P—— came out on to the terrace and found a friend who had lived here for years talking to an Egyptian. After the latter had left, Mr. B—— said to her, “That was a man high in government circles, a most interesting and intelligent person,—you would have enjoyed meeting him, but of course it was impossible.” Why?

It would appear that save officially the Egyptians are as completely ostracised as the Eurasians in India, even we men here at the hotel have nothing to do with them. I could not discover that there was any other reason for this save that which holds with the Eurasians, viz., “they are touched with the tar brush,” that is, not all Caucasian. Why then does not the same hold with the Japanese?

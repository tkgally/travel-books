In a small hotel like ours one becomes interested in every one around. Yonder are two American women who to-morrow at four <sc>a.m.</sc> will climb into a wretched diligence crowded with Arabs and for two days of eighteen hours each will crawl southward over the desert toward Tuggurt, enduring the blinding heat, the choking sand-storms, the dirty smelly Arabs, in order to see what they call the “real desert.” To any one familiar with deserts it’s all nonsense, of course, as one can go five miles from Biskra and be as really in the “real desert” as a hundred would take one. Still, man has different methods of enjoyment, so good luck go with them.

There is much commotion just now amongst the French officers who dine here. Yonder party of four seem to be very happy and complacently eating their dinner while those near us are indignant over something which both the proprietor and white-capped cook are unable to explain away. It turns out that a specially selected dinner composed of game sent by friends has been by mistake served the other party and is by now all eaten up. Comment is unnecessary.

Straight into the mountain, themselves carved out of it where they stand, are two rows of gigantic figures, the grandest caryatids that the world holds. With arms crossed on their bosoms they face each other, two by two, and show the way to the sanctuary. What profanation to enter here as a tourist! What an outrage that guide! One regrets the days of Ismail Pasha when natives could be murdered at will. Certainly the will to do so is with us and we retire until he has completed his round with the others and departed. Then, in the solemn silence and hush which has reigned here for so many millenniums we feast our souls with Abû-Simbel. Pausing an instant in the entrance one gazes inward and, far off, confronting one, sit four dusky figures. Instantly to the mind come the old tales of enchantment so dear to our childhood, and for a moment we hesitate to enter. Perhaps the mountain may close on us, who knows? However, if you would obtain the full effect of the temple you will pass inward to where they sit. One is the great god Ra (the sun), gazing outward towards the living sun, and if you are fortunate to be here when the latter, rising over the yellow mountains, shines full in the face of the former you will never forget it.

# Chapter VII

[[subtitle]] The Trip to Gondokoro—Departure from Khartoum—English Officers—The Ride to Port Sudan—A Burial in the Red Sea—The Opium Trade—Customs of the African Tribes—Marriages—Suez and the Ride to Cairo.

W<sc>e</sc> had about given up the trip to Gondokoro, but to-night Col. Asser convinced us that we should go. Certainly it is now or never, and as we are not due anywhere at any time, it seems rather a shame not to do so. The land and the people, the wild game and the birds, will fast change and vanish with the incoming of our dear and necessary civilisation. It may be stupid coming down the river, but it cannot be all cakes and ale in this life. Personally I have always had pleasant times on river trips, and the journey will be most incomplete if I do not see the White Nile in its solemn reaches towards the heart of darkest Africa. While the tse-tse fly is there, he is not as yet dangerous.

If was the Oriental solution of the paradox of losing the life to find it, of the paradox of St. Paul, “poor, yet making many rich, having nothing, and yet possessing all things.” And so it became gospel of present salvation from all consciousness of the ills of existence to millions; alike of the poor and ignorant who in such a climate crave mainly the feeling of sensuous—not sensual—repose, and to the wealthy and powerful who were wooed by it to turn away from the greeds and ambitions that heat the blood and fret the spirit toward the interior enjoyment of those gentle and kindly feelings in which alone tranquillity and peace abide.

III.

Back in our own childhood days, many the moral drawn for our improvement from the bellicose propensities of cats and dogs in contrast with the loving spirit that should prevail in well-regulated households of little boys and girls. In India one often wonders whether the stupendous scale upon which this lesson is illustrated on the part of the whole animal creation has not had a vast deal to do with the depth of the national reaction in favor of mental peace. While the men, women, and cbildren are vegetarians, all the other members of creation, from the Bengal tiger to the most invisible gnat, are not.

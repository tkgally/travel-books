## III.

When Peter the Hermit and his fellow-pilgrims went up to Jerusalem, it was under volleys of curses and a hail of stones that tested the metal of their faith. To-day one sneaks up by rail. One cannot escape a haunting sense of humiliation. Not that curses and stones would not be plenty enough at this late date were the courage equal to the will. One is on Mohammedan soil and under the flag of the “unspeakable Turk,” but respect for the Christian’s cannon overpowers hate of the Christian’s creed. Fanaticism can only “think damn,” not act it out. Back in Armenia, happily, it is otherwise. There the faithful can slaughter Christian men, women, and children, even the soldiers lending a helping hand, and the governors diplomatically denying the facts when Europe begins to murmur.

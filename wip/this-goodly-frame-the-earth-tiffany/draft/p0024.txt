No! it is vain to struggle further with the wheat hallucination. Just as dolls are stuffed with bran, so the baffled traveler finally succumbs to the delusion that, should he tap a vein in the arm of one of the natives, there would flow forth, not a stream of ruddy human blood, but a stream of A No. 1 Pillsbury’s best.

## IV.

“It is wisely ordained,” says Goethe, “that the trees shall not grow up into the skies.” Only roll along far enough over the dead-level prairies, and at last is vouchsafed to weary man a rainbow of promise that not even the waters of wheat shall continuously prevail over the face of the earth. Faint indications begin to attest that, in the geological “struggle for life,” a perpendicular as well as a horizontal, a jackscrew as well as a flat-iron principle is at work in nature. Hurrah! a hill as high as a woodchuck’s burrow. It is big with prophecy of the Rocky Mountains. Vaster throes of an earth in labor succeed, and bring forth,—is it a huge barn? No; a veritable Alp as big as a barn. One is startled at the subterranean energies involved. But, so far, all is but prelude.

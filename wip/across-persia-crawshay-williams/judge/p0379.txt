There was sport about. A pigeon flew out from the rocks; but it was for big game that the place looked best fitted. The wooded slopes should hide panther and bear, and, likely enough, ibex too.

It was three hours before we reached Siawisha,—I thanked Heaven we had not attempted it last night,—and as the <i>charvardar</i> insisted on a cup of tea, I pushed on alone with the mules, telling him to catch us up afterwards.

On the whole, my experience as head muleteer was not unsatisfactory, although possibly I did not quite observe the rules of the road. Somehow, when Meshed-i-Astulla was in command, we always seemed to have to wait while other people passed. By a very simple expedient, I contrived that now the other people should wait, or get out of the way somehow, while my little string of mules wandered by. This effect was produced by my going on in front, and whenever a caravan appeared, waving my stick in the face of the leading mule, which made him run into the wall or down into the river, followed by his companions; and so we got along at an admirable pace.

I have no doubt, however, I should not have been permanently popular on the road.

The American railways are very comfortable, though they have nominally only one class. The Pulman cars, however, are attached to each train, in which you can travel at an extra fare. These cars, which have lately been introduced on the Midland, give you comfortable sofas or armchairs by day, and sleeping-berths by night. The American fares are, I believe, low in the Eastern States; but on the line from Omaha to San Francisco the charge is 100 dollars, which, though it is at a higher rate than we are accustomed to in Europe, is not unreasonable, considering the difficulties under which it has been constructed. The system which enables you to take a ticket at New York for San Francisco, with liberty to stop at any station you please, is a great convenience.

After sleeping at the Niagara Hotel on the American side, I started to visit the Falls. I should recommend any one with leisure to establish himself for two or three days at the Clifton Hotel on the Canada side, where the cataract can be seen to the greatest advantage. The transit from one side to the other is accomplished by a suspension bridge.

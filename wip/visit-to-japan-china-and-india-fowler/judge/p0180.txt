Officers knew their men, talked to them in their own language, took an interest in their pursuits, and, in return, were beloved by the sepoys. Complaints were made in the last years of the Company that the number of English officers was unduly reduced. It was said that the ablest and best officers were often taken away from their regiments and placed in civil employments. The mutiny ensued, and the fact that a conspiracy which must have been known to thousands and tens of thousands should have been kept a secret from the English is certainly one of the most astounding, if not the most astounding event in history. The Sicilian Vespers is the only event to which it can be compared, and the Sicilian Vespers was a conspiracy comparatively on a very small scale. It behoved the British Government to consider how far a native army was required, and what were the best races of Hindostan from which to recruit it. There can be no question that a certain proportion of native troops are necessary. The debilitating effect of the climate of India on European constitutions makes it important to save the English soldier every exertion which is not indispensable. English regiments should, as much as possible, be kept in reserve at hill stations, and, when employed from necessity in the plains, they should be spared exposure to the heat.

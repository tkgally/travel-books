As all hands have been permitted to go on shore in the latter country, they have thus had the privilege of seeing and learning much of that wonderful country and its people. In China, our duties were arduous: but all duties were promptly performed with credit to us all, and to the honor of our glorious country; and, as a general thing, I feel confidant that all hands will look back with pleasure to their late cruise in the “Mississippi.” To be sure, there have been many ups and downs, and our hardships in many instances have been trying; but in all cases it has, I think, been the aim of every man to do his best to make the cruise pleasant. And, in regard to Captain William C. Nicholson and his officers, I think every one will admit the fact that they have extended good treatment towards all hands, and every favor that could have reasonably been expected. Few ships leave the United States whose crews are allowed more liberty than was granted to our ship’s company; and it seldom occurs that so much money is served out during a cruise as has been served on board of the good old “Mississippi.” And, finally, it will be admitted that our cruise has been as pleasant as could possibly have been expected in such a miserable country as China; and all hands, in after-years, will look back upon Captain William C. Nicholson as a true sailors’ friend.

The ship has run a little over forty-seven thousand miles. There have been fired during the cruise sixty-five salutes, in all nine hundred and sixty-nine guns; besides nearly one thousand guns fired at targets and for other purposes. There has been burned on board of her seven thousand five-hundred and fifty tons of coal, costing not far from one hundred and twenty thousand dollars. Four hundred and eighty thousand gallons of fresh water has been used by the ship’s company. Among the heavy articles of provisions consumed during the cruise are seven hundred and eighty barrels salt beef, five hundred and eighty-five barrels of salt pork, one hundred and fifty-five barrels of rice, one hundred and thirty barrels of beans, two hundred and eight thousand pounds of bread, seven thousand one hundred and eighty pounds of butter; besides a vast amount of tea, sugar, pickles, flour, vinegar, molasses, and thousands of pounds of fresh beef and vegetables. And no ship ever returned from China with her crew in better health, or can boast of being called upon only seven times during the cruise to pay the last solemn mark of respect to departed shipmates. When she arrived, there was not an invalid amongst the men.

God grant, shipmates, that the remaining cruises that you make may be equally as fortunate! and, when this feeble hand shall be still in death, may you still be faithfully serving your country, with credit to yourselves, and with honor to those glorious stars and stripes that have so often waved above your heads in every clime, even in far-distant Japan,—in which beautiful and wonderful country you can all boast, and feel proud to tell to others, that you have trodden upon the soil! Farewell, farewell, shipmates!

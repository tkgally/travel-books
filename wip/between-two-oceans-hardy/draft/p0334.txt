# Among the Red Hills of Georgia.

O<sc>ur</sc> glimpse of the great and historic State of Georgia was only a brief one, but we carried away with us, stamped in our memories, two vivid pictures of her two chief cities—Savannah on the sea-board, Atlanta in the interior.

Savannah, as we saw it first on a spring morning, steeped in semi-tropical sunshine, a city of liberal distances, bounteously laid out on a large and lavish scale, evidently by those who had limitless space at their command, and no niggardly inclination to stint it—Savannah, with its wide streets running in accurate parallel and rectangularly across, with the chequer-board regularity so often to be found in America, north, south, east, and west—with its little grassy, wooded squares, dignified by the title of ‘parks,’ at the crossings of the chief roads—its boulevards and avenues and long, deep, shady vistas of green—well deserves its name of the ‘Forest City.’

## IV.—In the Indian Summer.

‘I<sc>t’s</sc> a fine day,’ observes Jack. ‘Let us go out. There’s a time for all things, and this is no day for bricks and mortar.’

We lean out of the window, Jill and I, and quite agree with him.

It is a fine November day, not the grey November of London, but the golden November of New York. The softness of Indian summer is in the air. Over our heads arches a sky of pure and perfect azure light. Opposite our eyes, brownstone houses compose the prospect, the beginning and end of our outlook. We have an excellent view into our <i>vis-à-vis</i> neighbours’ rooms, as they, being on the shady side of the way, have opened all their blinds and windows.

## XXX.—Life of the Rev. Edward Irving.

[[center]] BY MRS. OLIPHANT.

“A good book on a most interesting theme.”—<i>Times.</i>

“A truly interesting and most affecting memoir. ‘Irving's Life’ ought to have a niche in every gallery of religious biography. There are few lives that will be fuller of instruction, interest, and consolation.”—<i>Saturday Review.</i>

## XXXI.—St. Olave's.

[[center]] BY THE AUTHOR OF “JANITA'S CROSS.”

“This novel is the work of one who possesses a great talent for writing, as well as experience and knowledge of the world. The whole book is worth reading.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“‘St. Olave's’ belongs to a lofty order of fiction. It is a good novel, but it is something more. It is written with unflagging ability, and it is as even as it is clever. The author has determined to do nothing short of the best, and has succeeded.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

## XXXII.—Sam Slick's Traits of American Humour.

“Dip where you will into this lottery of fun, you are sure to draw out a prize. These Traits’ exhibit most successfully the broad national features of American humour.”—<i>Post.</i>

## XXXIII.—Christian's Mistake.

[[center]] BY THE AUTHOR OF “JOHN HALIFAX, GENTLEMAN.”

“A more charming story has rarely been written. It is a choice gift to be able thus to render human nature so truly, to penetrate its depths with such a searching sagacity, and to illuminate them with a radiance so eminently the writer's own.”—<i>Times.</i>

## XXXIV.—Alec Forbes of Howglen.

[[center]] BY GEORGE MAC DONALD, LL.D.

“No account of this story would give any idea of the profound interest that pervades the work from the first page to the last.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“A novel of uncommon merit. Sir Walter Scott said he would advise no man to try to read ‘Clarissa Harlowe’ out loud in company if he wished to keep his character for manly superiority to tears. We fancy a good many hardened old novel-readers will feel a rising in the throat as they follow the fortunes of Alec and Annie.”—<i>Pall Mall Gazette.</i>

## XXXV.—Agnes.

[[center]] BY MRS. OLIPHANT.

“‘Agnes’ is a novel superior to any of Mrs. Oliphant's former works.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“Mrs. Oliphant is one of the most admirable of our novelists. In her works there are always to be found high principle, good taste, sense, and refinement. ‘Agnes’ is a story whose pathetic beauty will appeal irresistibly to all readers.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

## XXXVI.—A Noble Life.

[[center]] BY THE AUTHOR OF “JOHN HALIFAX, GENTLEMAN.”

“Few men and no women will read ‘A Noble Life’ without feeling themselves the better for the effort.”—<i>Spectator.</i>

“A beautifully written and touching tale. It is a noble book.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

“‘A Noble Life’ is remarkable for the high types of character it presents, and the skill with which they are made to work out a story of powerful and pathetic interest.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

## XXXVII—New America.

[[center]] BY W. HEPWORTH DIXON.

“A very interesting book. Mr. Dixon has written thoughtfully and well.”—<i>Times.</i>

“We recommend everyone who feels any interest in human nature to read Mr Dixon's very interesting book.”—<i>Saturday Review.</i>

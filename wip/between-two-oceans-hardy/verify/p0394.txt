# The New and Popular Novels.

[[subtitle]] Published by Hurst & Blackett.

A BEGGAR ON HORSEBACK. By Mrs. P<sc>ower</sc> O'D<sc>onoghue</sc>, Author of “Ladies on Horseback,” “Unfairly Won,” &c. 3 vols.

MR. NOBODY. By Mrs. J<sc>ohn</sc> K<sc>ent</sc> S<sc>pender</sc>, Author of “Godwyn's Ordeal,” “Both in the Wrong,” &c. 3 vols.

TO HAVE AND TO HOLD. By S<sc>arah</sc> S<sc>tredder</sc>, Author of “The Fate of a Year,” &c. 3 vols.

ONLY YESTERDAY. By W<sc>illiam</sc> M<sc>arshall</sc>, Author of “Monsell Digby,” “Strange Chapman,” &c. 3 vols.

ONE FALSE, BOTH FAIR. By J<sc>ohn</sc> B<sc>erwick</sc> H<sc>arwood</sc>, Author of “Lady Flavia,” &c. 3 vols.

“This work is a mild composing draught, which may be taken with confidence and even comfort. It is pleasant reading. The scenes are very brightly and cleverly sketched.”—<i>Academy.</i>

DI FAWCETT: A Y<sc>ear of</sc> H<sc>er</sc> L<sc>ife</sc>. By C. L. P<sc>irkis</sc>, Author of “A Very Opal,” &c. 3 vols.

“‘Di Fawcett’ shows a remarkable increase of power. The narrative is lively, the action natural and rapid, and the main situation well introduced.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“‘Di Fawcett’ is, in most important respects, an advance upon former works from the same pen. It is fairly interesting, brightly written, and decidedly amusing.”—<i>Graphic.</i>

A CHRISTMAS ROSE. By Mrs. R<sc>andolph</sc>, Author of “Gentianella,” &c. 3 vols.

“Mrs. Randolph's new tale, flower-named, according to her custom, is a pleasantly readable society novel. All the characters are cleverly drawn.”—<i>Academy.</i>

“This capital novel will rank well beside the other works of the talented and amusing author.”—<i>Daily Telegraph.</i>

JONATHAN SWIFT. By a N<sc>ew</sc> W<sc>riter</sc>. 3 vols.

“This novel displays some power and a good deal of promise. The sketch of Swift's life at Moor Park and in his Irish livings is drawn with much skill.”—<i>St. James's Gazette.</i>

“The various points in the hero's character are balanced with nice judgment, and the whole book is so good that a long-accustomed pen might well be pleased to achieve such a result as we have in ‘Jonathan Swift.’”—<i>Literary World.</i>

GOLDEN GIRLS. By A<sc>lan</sc> M<sc>uir</sc>, Author of “Lady Beauty,” “Children's Children,” &c. 3 vols.

“‘Golden Girls’ is as pretty a story as Mr. Muir has written. There is a good deal of fine perception and candid reproduction of human characteristics, and the author's lively style secures his reader's interest.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“This novel contains a series of very clever, realistic, and vigorous character-studies, and is pervaded by a humour at once genial and satirical.”—<i>Scotsman.</i>

IN THE WEST COUNTRIE. By the A<sc>uthor of</sc> “Q<sc>ueenie</sc>,” “Miss Daisy Dimity,” &c. 3 vols.

“This is a very well-written tale, full of pleasing pictures of family life, and vivid descriptions of that most beautiful of English counties, Devonshire. In every way Miss Crommelin has the right to be considered an agreeable novelist, whose works are deserving of much praise.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

PEARLA. By M<sc>iss</sc> B<sc>etham</sc>-E<sc>dwards</sc>, Author of “Kitty,” “Bridget,” &c. 3 vols.

“‘Pearla’ can be safely trusted to teach nothing but what is good, even to its most youthful readers. It is written with a certain measure of elegance and ease.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

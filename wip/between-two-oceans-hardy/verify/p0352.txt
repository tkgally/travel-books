Bay Street is the business street; there are the banks, the offices, the shops, the latter good and plenty; you can buy anything you want in Bay Street. It appears that the article with which the tourist is supposed to desire especially to supply himself is a live alligator, for announcements of ‘Fine young alligators!’ ‘One hundred alligators just received!’ ‘The cheapest alligators in town!’ greet one at every corner. Next to an alligator, it is evident, by an inspection of the shops, that the tourist is expected to invest in sea-beans, shells, and fish-scale jewellery. The polished sea-bean looks like a glossy chestnut, and is prettily mounted in all manner of ways for ornament.

Greenleaf’s ‘curiosity-store’ is a place for the traveller to visit; whether he purchases or not, he is kindly welcomed. We had the pleasure of seeing a fresh batch of young alligators poured into a tank. Mr. Greenleaf obligingly set a fine well-grown one on the floor that we might see it run; the active saurian ran so fast that we ran too, and did not stop till he was picked up and put back in the tank.

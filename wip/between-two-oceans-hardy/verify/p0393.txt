# The New and Popular Novels.

[[subtitle]] Published by Hurst & Blackett.

SAM’S SWEETHEART. By H<sc>elen</sc> M<sc>athers</sc>, Author of “Comin’ thro’ the Rye,” “Cherry Ripe!” “Land o’ the Leal,” &c. <i>Second Edition.</i> 3 vols.

“A new novel by Miss Mathers is a great treat.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“‘Sam’s Sweetheart’ is clever and amusing. It is superior to its predecessors from the same pen: the plot is closer, and better constructed.”—<i>Graphic.</i>

A MAID CALLED BARBARA. By C<sc>atharine</sc> C<sc>hildar</sc>, Author of “The Future Marquis,” &c. 3 vols.

“This story is stronger and more romantic than the author’s previous works. She has drawn some good characters, and there is not a little pathos in the lives of the hero and heroine.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“A bright, pleasant, and readable novel. The characters are lifelike and the scenes are well developed.”—<i>John Bull.</i>

ADRIAN BRIGHT. By Mrs. C<sc>addy</sc>, Author of “Artist and Amateur,” &c. 3 vols.

“This novel will be read with avidity and keen pleasure by all epicures in fiction, who know how to enjoy what is good.”—<i>Standard.</i>

“There is much to interest and amuse in this life-like picture of the home of Adrian Bright. The story increases in interest as it proceeds.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

JUNE. By Mrs. F<sc>orrester</sc>, Author of “Viva,” “Mignon,” “My Lord and My Lady,” &c. <i>Third Edition.</i> 3 vols.

“‘June’ is as clever as any of Mrs. Forrester’s novels. There is closer observation and better writing than the author has hitherto put into her books.”—<i>The World.</i>

“This story is told in Mrs. Forrester’s best manner.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

SQUIRE LISLE’S BEQUEST. By A<sc>nne</sc> B<sc>eale</sc>, Author of “Fay Arlington,” &c. 3 vols.

“This novel can be recommended to those who are satisfied with an unaffected story gracefully told. It is healthy and high-toned throughout. The plot is well-imagined and neatly put together.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

RED RIDING-HOOD. By F<sc>anny</sc> E. M<sc>illett</sc> N<sc>otley</sc>, Author of “Olive Varcoe,” &c. 3 vols.

“This story is well written, as well as conceived with something more than the ordinary success.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“The best novel Mrs. Notley has written since ‘Olive Varcoe.’ It is a most exciting story.”—<i>John Bull.</i>

HER SAILOR LOVE. By Mrs. M<sc>acquoid</sc>, Author of “Patty,” “Diane,” &c. 3 vols.

“This is a good business-like novel of the homely sort. There is a great deal in the story to awaken interest, and not a little to afford pleasure and entertainment.”—<i>Illustrated London News.</i>

MISS CHEYNE OF ESSILMONT. By J<sc>ames</sc> G<sc>rant</sc>, Author of “Romance of War,” &c. 3 vols.

“A wholesome and entertaining romance of modern life. The plot is well-constructed and exceedingly dramatic, and the characters are sketched with that care and ability for which Mr. Grant is justly celebrated.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

THE SENIOR SONGMAN. By the A<sc>uthor of</sc> “S<sc>t</sc>. O<sc>lave</sc>’s,” “Janita’s Cross,” &c. 3 vols.

“This well-written story deserves the popularity assured to anything written by the author of ‘St. Olave’s.’ There is not a page in the whole novel which fails to command attention or to repay it.”—<i>Daily Telegraph.</i>

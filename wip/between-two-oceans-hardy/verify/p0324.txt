## IV.—Columbia.

I<sc>t</sc> was night when we arrived at the realisation of another dream—Columbia! Is there a heart in Carolina—nay, in the South—but throbs deeper at that name? Columbia, first in the field at sowing the wind of war! (But who reaped the whirlwind more terribly than she did?) Columbia, hallowed by her fiery ordeal, by the memories of that night of terror—the stories of which had so often thrilled us—had been the goal of our thoughts all day, as the train bore us at a jog-trot through the rice and cotton fields of Carolina, where, at this season, the coming crop was represented only by little grass-like stalks a few inches high.

It was a clear but moonless night as we drove from the dépôt, and, in the dim dusk of starlight, it seemed that we were driving through forest aisles and woodland avenues, on and on under great shadowing trees, with here and there lights gleaming through the branches. Trees—trees—we wondered when we should ever get to the city! And, while we so wondered, the omnibus lumbered round, and drew up at the door of the hotel.

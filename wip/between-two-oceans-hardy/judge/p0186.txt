They gazed after us with our Chinese escort, especially when we turned up a flight of sawdust-covered stairs, whither one enterprising tourist—whether he was from New York or London, I know not—looked strongly inclined to follow us.

Sam Lee led us into a large, airy, well-furnished room—the first such apartment we had seen in China Town—where a little Chinese lady, gorgeously attired, came forward and shook hands with us. She played hostess prettily, offered us chairs, and bade her waiting-maid bring us sweetmeats and wine. She could speak a few words of English, but to our attempts at conversation, she only replied by a perpetual ripple of laughter, looking shyly aside, too bashful to talk, but evidently thinking the position an extremely humorous one.

Her hair was wonderful to look upon, brushed off the temples, and standing out in large, stiff loops like glossy wire. On her head she wore a profusion of gilt leaves and artificial flowers.

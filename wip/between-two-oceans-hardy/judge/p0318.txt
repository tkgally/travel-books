How her father fell in love with her mother, who belonged to another master; how her father’s master, who was ‘the best man that ever lived,’ tried to buy her mother in order that the lovers might marry and live together on his estate; how her mother’s master refused to sell ‘Sue,’ and ‘Ole massa,’ he said, ‘Jim, Mr. So-and-so won’t sell Sue at any price. I’m sorry to part with you, Jim; but, if your heart’s set on Sue, why So-and-so offers to take you himself.’ Jim knew that So-and-so was a hard and cruel master, but ‘love will still be lord of all,’ and poor Jim made his life-sacrifice for love’s sake, and chose to be sold to the harsh owner of his Sue.

I was interested in this humble love-story, but was disappointed at the end of it, which was that Jim, cruelly treated and flogged for slight offences, ran away into the swamps, and was never heard of more; while wretched, deserted Sue took to drinking in her misery, and one day, when intoxicated, fell into the fire and was burnt to death.

There was another woman to whom we took a great liking. She was a mixed breed; white and Indian blood commingled with the African in her.

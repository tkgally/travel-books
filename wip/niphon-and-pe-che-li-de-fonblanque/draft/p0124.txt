Leaving our horses at the tea-house, while hampers are unpacked and breakfast prepared—for we are not yet sufficiently acclimatized to affect Japanese chow-chow—we walked, through lanes hedged by bushes of tea (which, for the first time, I discovered to be of the camelia tribe) and a magnificent park, in which Nature had most bountifully provided for the payment of gentlemen’s debts, to Inari—the Temple of the Foxes—the presiding deity of which is celebrated for his success in the cure of barrenness. None of our party required the services of this particular god, so we contented ourselves with buying, for a few pence, a stamped paper which was warranted to prove a certain charm against a violent death—no unwise precaution in these times.

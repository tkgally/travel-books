There is, perhaps, nothing more calculated to loosen the bonds of discipline, and to damage the <i>morale</i> of a military body, than the licence to plunder; and I fear I must add, that it does not tend to improve the character of British officers, but induces a mercenary spirit not desirable, nor usually met with, among gentlemen in our army. <i>L’appétit vient en mangeant</i>, and the taste of plunder in men, like the taste of blood in wild beasts, begets in them an insatiable craving, difficult to control, and most dangerous to gratify.

It was observed that troops who had served in India were far more expert at looting than others; they seemed to have reduced the business to a science, and they practised it like professors.

Before the Battle of Sinho, the Celestial Government had fired the valour of their braves by no less an indulgence than the issue of six months’ arrears of war-pay in hard silver.

[[cont]] neither the officials nor the people showed themselves well-disposed towards Europeans. At New-chwang their attitude had been particularly unfriendly, when a circumstance occurred which enabled us to assert our right to a better reception.

Mr. Davenport, Consular Interpreter at New-chwang, unmindful of the fact proverbially befalling those who in others’ quarrels interpose, and more particularly when the quarrel is of a domestic nature, on seeing a Chinaman furiously beating his wife, interfered in the lady’s behalf, and was immediately set upon by the combatants and their neighbours, who, armed with bill-hooks, scythes, and other agricultural implements, attacked the unfortunate interpreter, the injured wife taking a prominent part in the assault upon her champion. He was left for dead by the roadside.

On redress being demanded, the mandarin of the district defended the outrage, and declined to accede to the Consul’s request for the punishment of the offenders.

One kind, of a delicate yellow colour, with a green dragon emblazoned upon its surface, is only used by the Emperor; another, of a peculiar blue, is so rare that a broken cup is sold for five times its weight in gold. Yet both of these may be bought in Tien-tsin, and, in spite of their rarity, there is always a fresh specimen forthcoming as soon as the last “only one” has found a purchaser. I fear the curio-dealers formed a far higher opinion of our wealth than of our good sense: they must certainly have made small fortunes by us.

The picture-shops were an endless source of amusement: there you might see the English and French soldier in every imaginable dress, and in the most impossible attitudes; marching, fighting, marketing, and occasionally making love to a horrible creation of the artist’s brain, supposed to be an European lady.

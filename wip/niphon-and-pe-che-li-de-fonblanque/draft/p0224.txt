This latter wall, which is fifteen miles in circumference, had been built since our attack on the Peiho Forts in 1859, but its merits as a defensive work were not tested when we marched upon Tien-tsin. A sandy plain stretches as far as the eye can reach in every direction around the city, and there is something inexpressibly dreary and desolate in that desert-like level, the dead monotony of which is increased rather than broken by the mud villages which at intervals of three or four miles dot its surface.

If the country around Tien-tsin has no beauty to boast of, the town offers even less attraction.

“Your minister has heard, with the greatest surprise and alarm,” says Tsuan-Kin, “that in consequence of the failure of the attempt to bring the Barbarians to terms, Your Majesty had resolved on making a tour to Ye-hol. At such a moment as this, when the capital is the sovereign’s only proper place of residence, is it proper suddenly to propose a hunting-tour? This sudden departure, without any apparent reason, although called a hunting-tour, will bear the aspect of a flight. . . . In a period of public distress, the man of heroic character is prepared to die at his post.”

This is language which only under extraordinary circumstances a responsible adviser of the Crown would venture to address to a constitutional monarch.

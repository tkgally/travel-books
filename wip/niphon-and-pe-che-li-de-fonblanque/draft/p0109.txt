Mr. Alcock is a man of refined and literary tastes, and his <i>employés</i> are all, more or less, disposed to occupy themselves in the study of the language and institutions of Japan; but still, when I saw them spelling over an old copy of Kaempfner, or an unintelligible Japanese grammar, or feeding the gold fish in the pond, or looking westward at space through a telescope, I could not but congratulate myself that I was not a servant of the Foreign Office, doomed to pass the best part of a precarious life in Yedo.

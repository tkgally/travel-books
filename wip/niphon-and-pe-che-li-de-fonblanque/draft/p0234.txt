The frozen Peiho afforded some entertainment to our officers and men; and still more, perhaps, to the Chinese, who were inexpressibly delighted at our abortive efforts to learn their system of sledging. The sledge itself, upon which they convey heavy loads for very long distances, is simply a pair of runners connected by two cross-pieces, and covered with a piece of matting. The driver stands behind, with one foot on each runner, and, with an iron-shod pole which he places between his legs, propels his load along the ice at a marvellous pace, avoiding holes and fissures with as much nicety as an expert boatman steering his gig on a crowded river.

It requires long practice and a thick skull to acquire proficiency in this accomplishment, for a false move is pretty sure to send the driver flying backward on his head.

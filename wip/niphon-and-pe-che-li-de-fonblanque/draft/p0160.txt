I have said before that the actual condition of these girls is not well known: I never could get any reliable information on the subject. It appears certain, however, that they are generally the daughters of poor people who, unable to provide for them by other means, apprentice them out at a tender age as attendants at tea-houses, where they are taught their business in all its branches. These houses are under the control of the Government, whose officers regulate their interior economy, and which participates in the profits. After a certain age, the apprentices, unless conditions to the contrary should have been made, can be purchased outright or hired for a stipulated period, through the intervention of public officials.

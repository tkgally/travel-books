In rear of his chair, and immediately before mine, which is smaller, and in which, buttoned up to the chin, in full uniform, with a plumed cocked hat on my head, and spurs on my heels, I can neither sit nor lie down, six Yaconins swagger, armed to the teeth; two more are on each side, and four behind me. The chair of the interpreter, followed by a considerable guard, brings up the rear. And thus we proceed slowly, wearily, and very cold along the interminable streets and through uninterrupted lines of wondering men, women, and children, who seem in doubt whether to prostrate themselves before the well-known emblems of authority, or to stand erect in defiance of the foreigners. They probably know that the latter would be more agreeable to the Yaconins in attendance; at any rate, few performed even the common obeisance habitually made by the lower classes to the smallest officials.

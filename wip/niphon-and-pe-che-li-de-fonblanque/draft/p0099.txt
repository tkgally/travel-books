On one of these occasions, a thin-skinned trooper, who had fallen out of the ranks during the march, was met on his way home to stables by a party of unruly Britons from Yokuhama, who took possession of his horse, which was subsequently traced to the possession of a gentleman of the Hebrew persuasion. This youth could not, in the face of a clearly-branded V.R., dispute the identity of the animal, and expiated his “lark” (for nothing more serious was intended) in a Consular Court.[[fnref *]]

[[fn *]] This was the same young Israelite who, at a later period, was sentenced to deportation from Japan for shooting a Yaconin. The act was not intentional, but committed in a paroxysm of fear; but an example was much wanted at the time, and this silly, but by no means dangerous young man, became the victim. The sentence was hardly necessary, however; a braver man than our Jew would have deported himself after killing a Japanese official.

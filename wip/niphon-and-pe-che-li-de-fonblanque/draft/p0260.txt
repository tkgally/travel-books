Before entering Pekin, I paid a visit to the Summer Palace, and, meeting with no obstruction, found my way through the outer enclosure into the midst of the ruins of Yuen-ming-Yuen. The work of destruction seems, after all, to have been tolerably complete, and not the least effort has been made to restore grounds or buildings; shells of grotesque houses and pagodas, heaps of ruins and cinders, charred beams, and trees with their drooping branches singed to death, meet you at every turn, though I did here and there discover a building that appeared to have escaped detection, or, perhaps, was gutted without having been afterwards fired. At one of the principal gates, two monstrous bronze lions, valued at £5000 each, remain intact upon their massive pedestals.

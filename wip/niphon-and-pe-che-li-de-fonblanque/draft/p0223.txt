The force left to occupy the north of China and which, for some months to come, could, under no circumstances, hope for succour from without, was composed of three regiments of the line, one of irregular cavalry (Fane’s Horse), and two batteries of artillery, with a proportion of Engineers, Military Train, Commissariat, and Medical Staff, under the command of Brigadier-General Staveley, who thought—and, I believe, thought justly—that with his little army he might march through the length and breadth of China, and defy anything that could be brought to oppose him.

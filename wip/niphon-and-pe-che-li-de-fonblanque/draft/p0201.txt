It was tantalizing not to be able to land, but Mr. Alcock had particularly requested that we should abstain from doing so. We certainly received little encouragement from the population, who kept carefully aloof from us; even the fishermen’s boats, with which we would gladly have transacted business, gave us as wide a berth as if we had the plague on board.

<i>26th.</i>—The scenery still improves; and as we progress the charts become more and more incorrect. According to the Admiralty, we crossed a range of mountains this morning; breakfasted in the middle of an enormous rice-field; passed the greater part of the day in an excursion through the interior, and anchored for the night about twenty miles inland in the midst of a dense forest.

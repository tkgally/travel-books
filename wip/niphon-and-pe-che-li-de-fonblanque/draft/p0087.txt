The so-called pioneers of civilization are, like other pioneers, more noted for physical energy than for gentler or more refined qualities. It is not the skilled or scientific farmer, but the strong-armed labourer, who cuts down the forest; nor is it the liberal, enlightened, prudent, and educated merchant, but the daring, money-seeking adventurer, who clears the way for commerce. What have the Japanese to learn from such men? Do they set them a profitable example in morality, in decency, in religion, in probity, in intelligence, in industry, or even in the outward forms of social intercourse? [[fnref *]]

The French, who exclusively occupied the right bank of the Peiho, while our troops were quartered on the left, treated the <i>habitans</i> with less ceremony than we showed them. Their system was, to <i>order</i> whatever buildings they required for their troops; indeed, all their wants were supplied, in the same manner, upon a <i>requisition,</i> which the authorities did not run the risk of failing to comply with, and for which they rarely expected and still more rarely received payment; whereas we, who paid handsomely for everything, were served with far less alacrity, and were, upon the whole, decidedly less popular. It was curious to notice how the French soldier fraternized with the Chinaman, and how quickly and perfectly they managed to make themselves mutually understood.

Returned to breakfast, where met M. de Bellecourt, French Consul-General, who informed me that, upon the whole, he was really disposed to prefer Paris to Yedo; Mr. Townshend Harris, the United States Minister; and Mr. Euskin, his secretary and interpreter, who probably knows more of Japan and the Japanese, and speaks their language more fluently than any living European.[[fnref *]]

[[fn *]] Poor Euskin was assassinated a few months afterwards, while in the act of mounting his horse at the residence of the Prussian Ambassador, whom he had prominently assisted in concluding a somewhat unpopular treaty. This had, no doubt, rendered him obnoxious to the Conservative party. In private life, he was an universal favourite among all classes, and well he might be so, for a more gentle, kind-hearted man never lived. Although a Dutchman by birth, and occupying a subordinate diplomatic position, America is mainly indebted to him for its influence in Japan.

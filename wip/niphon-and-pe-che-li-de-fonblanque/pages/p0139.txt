[[cont]]the pantomime was sufficiently expressive to demonstrate the plot of the piece. This turned upon the amours of a fat priest, who endeavoured by various means—by presents, threats, professions, serenades, and violence—to win the favour of a lady who, though coy, did not altogether disapprove of his attentions. Her son, a precocious <i>gamin</i>, given to practical jokes and somewhat indecent gestures, was the evil genius, and showed as little respect for his mother as for the priest. After various absurd love passages, in all of which the poor suitor is made a fool of, now tumbling from a ladder as he attempts to climb into his lady’s chamber, now drenched with a basin of water as he serenades her under her window, or upset from a boat as he pursues her on the water, the heroine, with her scapegrace son, tired of his importunities, mounts a platform, and from thence stepping upon a rope extended across the theatre, they perform a <i>pas de deux</i>, the figures of which, if not graceful, are so expressive of contempt and derision, that the poor lover rends his garments in rage and [[corr depair|despair]], and the curtain falls

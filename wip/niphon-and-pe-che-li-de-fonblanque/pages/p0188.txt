The conduct of the people was excellent. The sight of eight mounted Englishmen must have appeared wonderful to them, who had never before beheld an European; but they never once allowed their curiosity to become offensive, far less were they ever guilty of the slightest disrespect. As we entered their towns or villages (and these consist of one long street, sometimes three miles in length) men, women, and children, flocking out of their doors, appeared to present a dense, impervious wall to check our progress.

This accident, it appears, was nothing less than the murder of two Dutch ship-captains, who had been cut down in the streets by unknown assassins. We immediately started for the scene. On our way across the Bay, the Governor’s barge, with its monstrous lanterns, passed us rapidly; he was going to face the storm of european indignation. In the streets of Yokuhama we met a strong picquet of Russian marines, who had been landed—armed to the teeth—from His Imperial Majesty’s ship Japonitch, and on entering the “hotel” (where the bodies lay) we where challenged by the hoarse voice of a gigantic sentry, before being admitted into the salle-à-manger; which had been converted into a guard-room, and where hairy Muscovites smoked in sullen silence. These fellows looked very dirty and savage, and vividly recalled to my recollection old Crimean days, when the same flat faces under same flat caps used to scowl at us.

Being dressed, I felt like a different being. The craven, shrinking, coward spirit which I had before gave place at once to one of independence and defiance. I began to feel indignant with the sleepers for making such a noise,—for the snoring, having served its purpose, was no longer interesting, and threatened to keep one awake all night; but, like all other noises, one can get accustomed to it, and at length in spite of it I slept in peace.

When I woke next morning the other travellers were sleeping still, and I had to stride over them to get out of the room. The yemschiks also, though moving about in the stables, were hardly yet awake, and took nearly an hour to get my horses yoked. When at last all was ready, and I was about to step into the conveyance which I had waited for so patiently, another tarantass came dashing down the road, and was pulled up at the station. Its occupant was a Russian officer, who no sooner alighted from his own conveyance than, with a word to the yemschik, he sprang into mine and at once drove off.

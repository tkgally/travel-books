Sometimes, and more frequently after we passed Irkutsk, we met other travellers going eastward—military men or emigrants; and at other times we overtook caravans, of perhaps a hundred or more carts. carrying tea to Europe. Almost every caravan we met going eastward was a caravan of big-wheeled barrels, in which vodka, the favourite strong drink of the Russians, was being conveyed from Western Siberia to remote settlements.

Occasionally when we thought we were alone we were startled by a sudden clank of chains, and, coming round a bend in the forest road, we met a gang of exiles on their way to the prisons of Kara or Saghalien. There were seldom less than two hundred persons in a gang—women as well as men. They wore long coats of coarse, earth-coloured frieze, and were chained together as they walked.

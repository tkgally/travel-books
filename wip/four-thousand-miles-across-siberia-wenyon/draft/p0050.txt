After a long spin through this wild scenery and wintry air, one can appreciate the comforts of the travellers’ room at the post-station. In summer these wooden shelters swarm with vermin, but from such torment travellers at this season of the year are free. There is no fire-grate to be seen, but a big brick bread-oven, whose mouth opens outside on to the stableyard, projects into one corner of the room. Whether the yemschiks are baking bread or not, Imperial order directs that plenty of good logs shall be burnt in this oven every day, and so, however intense the cold outside, the travellers’ room is always comfortably warm. In Siberia this is not so much a luxury as a necessity, for it would be impossible to cross these extensive tracts of country in the winter-time without the opportunity of thawing oneself now and then upon the way. There are no provisions to be had at any of these stations except milk and eggs and coarse black bread, but there is always ready for the traveller at five minutes notice a <i>samovar</i> of boiling water.

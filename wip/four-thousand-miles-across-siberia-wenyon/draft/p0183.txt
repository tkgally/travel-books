‘I have ordered the samovar for you,’ her husband said; ‘and here are the matches and some cigarettes.’

‘<i>Sobaka!</i>’—you dog—was all the thanks he got.

With no perceptible change of colour, and with no suspicion of tremor in his voice, he turned to me and said, ‘Shall we go for a walk?’

The poor woman’s anger had time to cool while we were out, but it had not taken advantage of the opportunity; and when her husband asked her, ‘Is your headache better?’ her only reply was the same uncomplimentary reference to himself. She could talk fluently enough to others, but, until a day had passed, she had for her beloved only that one expressive word, and he listened to it every time with the same imperturbable tranquillity.

These, with the Turco-Tartars, and a couple of hide merchants and myself, were the company which lived and slept in that one room during the weary days of waiting for a steamer.

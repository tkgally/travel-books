Several thousands of the residents are soldiers, and a large proportion of the rest are in the employ of the Government. Not less than four thousand soldiers from Europe and Western Siberia pass through this city every year on their way eastward, and, as very few of them return, the Russian forces on the frontier must be accumulating fast.

The journey as far as Tchita is performed on foot, but the soldiers are sent on from here by raft, for, the Tchita River being a tributary of the Shilka—which forms, by its confluence with the Argun, the Amoor—there is a continuous water-way for rafts from this city to the Pacific coast. The rafts are broken up and their timbers sold on reaching the lower part of the Amoor, so that a fresh fleet is required every year. Some of the Government officials manage to make a good deal of money out of this raft-building; having them constructed less than the regulation size, and of slighter timbers, and then pocketing the balance of the contract price.

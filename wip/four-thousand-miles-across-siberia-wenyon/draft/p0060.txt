How did I like it? Why, at first—to speak guardedly—not much. None of my joints were actually dislocated, though several of them felt as if they were, and for a week or more every bone in my body ached. Many a time I regretted that I had not brought more pillows with me. But the instinct of self-preservation led me at length to discover the position of most stable equilibrium; and I learned so to adjust myself—holding on to ropes and straps, with one foot pressed firmly against this projection and the other against that—and so to arrange the straw and pillows, that after a little time for training I could travel like this all day and all night, and from day to day, and even week to week, without feeling much the worse.

But it was trying to the nerves as well as to the coarser tissues, and my first few days of tarantass-riding were days of great anxiety. Often, as at headlong speed we shot down some deep descent, I thought we should be dashed to pieces; but as this did not happen, I came at length to regard these mad gallops with indifference, and at last with reckless enjoyment.

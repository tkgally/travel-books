No attempt was made to warn them against the danger of drinking contaminated water, and filters in Siberia are unknown. If I asked for water, they never brought me any that it was safe to drink. Sometimes it was green, and sometimes brown, and always tainted with decayed organic matter. But, as the average Siberian peasant only uses water to dilute his vodka and infuse his tea, the danger may have been less serious than it seemed.

There was no stint in the supply of disinfectants, and around every post-station between Irkutsk and Tomsk there was always a strong odour of carbolic acid. Every sniff of it awakened gloomy apprehensions in the people; but what disquieted them most was the rough wooden shed which, by Imperial order, had been erected on the outskirts of every town and village as a hospital for those who might be stricken with the disease.

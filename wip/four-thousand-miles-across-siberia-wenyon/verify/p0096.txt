It is only the poorer classes who habitually use this kind of tea, the dried leaf in its usual form being preferred by others. It is sometimes said that the best tea produced in China goes to Russia, but this is a mistake. The finest qualities of Chinese tea, which always yield a pale infusion, and are sold in native tea-shops at various prices, rising to upwards of five pounds sterling per pound avoirdupois, are entirely consumed by the Chinese; but of the coarser kinds, prepared expressly for foreign markets, and which mostly yield a dark infusion, probably the best is that which is carried overland through Siberia to Russia.

The tea the Russians drink is generally well diluted. A strong infusion having been made in a teapot, a small quantity (one or two table-spoonfuls) is poured into a half-pint goblet, which is then filled with boiling water from the samovar.

He dare not even meet a fellow man, except to overpower and kill him; and it is only by some such desperate deed that he can preserve his own liberty and life. Plunging into the depths of the forest, a month of continuous plodding will hardly be sufficient to bring him out upon the other side, and, apart from the other risks of such a journey, he finds himself at the very outset perishing with cold and hunger. So formidable are the difficulties which confront these fugitive exiles, that, of all those who escape from the convict stations, one-third come back and ask to be readmitted, and one-third perish in the forest, the remaining third only managing to maintain themselves in freedom.

These escaped exiles—called by the settlers <i>bradyaga</i> or <i>varnaks</i>—are the terror of the Trans-Baikal province. They are said to number not less than twenty thousand. One by one, as they manage to obtain passports, they set out for Europe, keeping well hidden by the forest while following the direction of the post-road. Most are content to run the risk of travelling with forged papers. An escaped exile who is determined to have a genuine passport must murder, rob, and personate a man who has one.

From the old town to Riobamba, we passed down a ravine, cut away by a torrent through an immense depth of volcanic tuff and scoriæ, showing the enormous quantity of matter that must have been ejected by the surrounding giant volcanoes in times gone by.

Modern Riobamba lies in a sand valley or plain of the great centre highland, surrounded by mountains—Chimborazo, Carguairazo, Tunguragua, and Altar, all being visible from its <i>plaza</i>; and those who are familiar with these names can well imagine the unequalled grandeur of such a scene, especially when contemplated by moonlight. The clearness of the atmosphere, already so frequently noted by travellers, is remarkable; but in a manner it detracts from the imposing dimensions of many features in the view, though, at the same time, it renders all lines distinct and definite. At night the heavens, when not overcast, were marvellously bright, and the stars appeared to have augmented their numbers tenfold.

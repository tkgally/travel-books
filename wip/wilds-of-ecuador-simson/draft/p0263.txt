There is a singular sameness in the appearance of the banks on the entire river, and although naturally the vegetation has the richest variety, as in the whole Amazon valley, this profusion throughout over such vast areas is wearying, and productive of a strong desire that one may meet with a rough, rocky bank, an open plain, a cascade, in fact anything to vary the monotonous flat banks. Nevertheless, I must confess that the endless beauty of the rich vegetation, the immense variety of palms, the graceful bamboo (always the thorny species east of the Andes), the wild plantain, the various ferns, and the innumerable forest trees, all intermingled with and bound together by an infinity of creepers, <i>lianas</i> and parasites, were a continual source of admiration and enjoyment.

Before closing here this narrative of my travels in Ecuador and up the Putumayo, I will give in another chapter a very short outline sketch of the history of Ecuador.

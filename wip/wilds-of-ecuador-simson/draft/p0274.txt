Such was the colonial administration, until towards its close; and the foregoing is a truthful picture of the conditions under which the Ecuadorian people had lived for generations. This cannot but diminish our wonder that their self-government should have proved a failure; indeed, expressions of surprise could only be justified had a <i>régime</i> introduced suddenly and under such circumstances resulted otherwise.

The independence from Spain consummated, Ecuador at first incorporated itself into the triple State of Colombia; but following the example set by Venezuela, separated from it in May, 1830 and formed an entirely independent State, under Flores, its first republican President. In 1832 the provinces in the north, Cauca, Buenaventura, and Pasto, passed over to New Granada.

To follow Ecuador as a Republic through all its political changes up to the present day would, although the period is little more than half a century, besides being a constant repetition of rebellion among the troops and people, with consequent numberless and complicated revolutions and changes of Presidents, Supreme Chiefs, Provisional Commissioners, and Dictators, be both tedious and foreign to this superficial sketch.

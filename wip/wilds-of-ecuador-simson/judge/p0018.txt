In travelling across the country it was only when Baños was reached, the last civilised village on the Pastassa, that the approximate number of days required to complete the journey to the Upper Napo, the means of transport and subsistence, the tribes to be met with on the way, &c., could in any measure be ascertained.

In Guayaquil, encouragement was offered in the vague admonitions of friends, amongst which starvation in misery, and the effective scalping, or rather dermic decapitation, of the Jívaros occupied prominent places. Warnings were gravely held out to us in the fate of Gonzalo Pizarro and his men, who lost more than half of their 4000 Indians and 220 out of 350 of the Spaniards,[[fnref *]] with all the horses and dogs, on the same journey we proposed to undertake, from hardship, exposure, fatigue, and want. Circumstances had not altered since that time, and that expedition was commenced with every apparent necessity duly provided for; an enormous stock of provisions and herds of swine.

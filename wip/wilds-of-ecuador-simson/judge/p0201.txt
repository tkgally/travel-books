The fires were always kept low, but never allowed to go out till morning; and during the night as soon as the embers began to cease to give heat the men felt it, and stirred and blew them into flame again, immediately afterwards resuming their sleep. At the very first streak of dawn, as if by one accord, the Záparos all awoke and began to move about. Even when the moon was full and commencing to wane, thus giving a strong light till morning, they in their sleep, as I had occasion to specially observe, seemed to know when day was about to break, better than I did who was sitting up and watching for it; though as yet not a streak or sign in the east announced the dawn, nor was any difference discernible in the light which the moon had supplied equally throughout the night.

We passed one night on a bank near the mouth of the Tiputini, a river entering the S.W. bank of the Napo, and famous for the number of jaguars said to haunt its vicinity; and in effect, on our “<i>praya</i>” we found quite recent tracks of a large and a small one following a deer, which had passed only half-an-hour or so before our arrival. After dark we distinctly heard the crafty cats in the forest hard by, and therefore laid out a peccary’s head, poisoned with cyanide of potassium, at the margin of the forest, in the hope that they might eat it. Morning however found it untouched.

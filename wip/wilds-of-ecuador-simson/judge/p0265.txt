Cacha Duchicela, the fifteenth Shyri, was killed on the field of battle; but the valiant Caras, instead of capitulating on the death of their chief, at once proclaimed his daughter Pacha their queen, and prepared to continue their resistance against the invaders. Huayna-Capac, finding such unexpected resolution in his adversaries, now betook himself to diplomacy in order to complete the work of aggrandisement so nearly accomplished, and the negociations terminated by his marrying Pacha; so that by amicable, or rather amorous, means he was able to assume the insignia of the Shyris, with the power they represented. So pleased was Huayna-Capac with his new love and his new conquest, that he no more returned to Cuzco, but thenceforward resided in Quitu during the further period of his long reign of thirty-eight years.

According to custom, ever since the time of the first Inca Manco-Capac, who announced himself as the direct child of the sun, the heir-apparent to the imperial throne had always been the eldest son of the Coya, or legitimate queen-consort, who, at least in the later reigns, was herself the eldest sister of the Inca.

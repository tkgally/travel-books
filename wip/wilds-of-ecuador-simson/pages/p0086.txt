The Topo rushes, or rather springs, down its bed at a frightful pace; and as it is filled with unevenly-dispersed boulders and rocks in mid-stream and in all directions, besides its irregular perpendicular banks of similar solid material, the rush of the waters can be imagined when they rise so as to almost cover the highest of the rocks in its centre, passing at a rate of some twenty miles an hour, and dashing their foam and spray thick and high up into the air. It reminded me vividly of Schiller’s description—

<verse>
“Es wallet und siedet, und brauset und zischt,
Wie wenn Feuer mit Wasser sich mengt;
Bis zum Himmel spritzet der dampfende Gischt,
Und Well’ auf Well’ sich ohn’ Ende drängt.”
</verse>

Of course the thought of crossing such a rapid in any way but quite out of the reach of its waters could not be entertained. I had heard much about the Topo; but my imagination never pictured so imposing a spectacle as it appeared the next morning when we turned out, after a wet, stormy night.

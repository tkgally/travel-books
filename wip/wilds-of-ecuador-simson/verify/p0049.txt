Baños is situated on the right bank of the Pastassa, at an elevation of 5904 feet above the sea-level, and enjoys the agreeable mean temperature of 63½° Fahr. It lies in a kind of large circular pit or caldron, enclosed by the steepest mountains, the only outlets being the narrow valleys of the river. At night, especially, one could easily imagine oneself to be in a large well or pit, surrounded by high dark walls, with the heavens visible only vertically, high above one’s head.

Just behind the village, the hills retire from the river, and so leave a little level ground which is utilised for the cultivation of sugar-cane. This, notwithstanding the rather low temperature, thrives, as it is irrigated by the warm water from several springs issuing from the back hills, whence also a pretty cascade falls.

The only industry and occupation of the inhabitants is the manufacture of spirit, to which almost all the cane is dedicated. As a natural consequence most of the inhabitants drink heavily and are good-for-nothing, lazy, and unreliable in the highest degree. Had it not been for the kind assistance of my friend, Mr. Lizarzaburu, we should have fared badly indeed at Baños.

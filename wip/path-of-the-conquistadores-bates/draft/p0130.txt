The ground is more and more hilly. At length, after a stretch of coconut palms, there appear the roofs of a straggly settlement of poor-looking houses, the village of Mayaro, in the south-east corner of the island.

Twelve miles of drive along the beach will take us to the Guayaguayare oil-fields, where the production of petroleum has been recently started. We must leave the car, which cannot negotiate the heavy sands, but a good mule and buggy are loaned us for the trip. While waiting for low tide, we lunch upon tinned goods and biscuit bought from a Chinaman who keeps a general store. All around coconut trees are growing, the nuts hanging a few feet overhead.

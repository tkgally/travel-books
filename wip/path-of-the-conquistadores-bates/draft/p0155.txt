“But that other city never got its police telegraph,” Fitzgerald remarks.

We go next day to the Venezuelan Consul, who has been appointed only three days. “They’ve bounced the Consuls four times in the last year,” whispers Fitzgerald. We sign many papers for clearance, and enrol at the Consulate as “captain and first officer respectively of the gasolene launch ‘Geraldo,’ 2½ tons burden, 24 feet long, crew of two, laden with ship’s supplies.”

The inwardness of the proceeding is this: A passenger is forbidden by the most stringent possible law from landing in Venezuela at any spot where there is not a “puerto habiltado,” or licensed port

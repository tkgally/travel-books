“We all drive in the cool of the afternoon,” says Mrs. Farrell, who is awaiting you. “It is the most important function in the day.”

We enter the carriage, drive out of the grounds and swing into the procession that flows past the gateway.

“Most of the ladies here do not get dressed until afternoon,” she observes presently. “Mother Hubbards and carpet slippers, you know. Now I will point you out the lions.”

A dark middle-aged man with a very pretty girl beside him passes and bows ceremoniously. “That is Mr. Siegert and his daughter; his place is beside the Queen’s Park,” says Mrs. Farrell. “He was a Venezuelan, but the revolutions drove him out. He came here with his family and makes the Angostura bitters which the monks used to brew.”

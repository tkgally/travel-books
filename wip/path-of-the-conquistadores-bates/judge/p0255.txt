[[cont]]Latin table, where Corsicans and Spanish flourish, where M. Mannoni himself sits and where another M. Mattey lays down the law amid difficulties. This table is in a constant state of effervescence, of explosions, of vivid words and far-flung gestures. By virtue of your letter to S. José Aquatella, you are seated here.

As you enter a great discussion is on. Mannoni has just exhibited an ancient revolver, with the proud statement that his great-grandfather carried it in Paoli’s fight against the Genoese.

“Mais, c’est impossible,” M. Mattey is affirming. “Revolvers were not invented until fifty years ago.”

Mr. Robert Henderson, the veteran United States Consul, from a small table of his own midway between Saxon and Latin, stands by M. Mattey. “We did not have cartridges such as would go into that revolver until the Civil War.

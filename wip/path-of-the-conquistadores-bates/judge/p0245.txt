In half an hour the storm has died down. You crawl out. Sail is hoisted, and with only another parted halyard we reach the spot where the negro peon is to land. His coriara, which was towed astern, is brought alongside and loaded with bananas and sugar-cane from the hold. With praiseworthy dexterity the crew steal several bananas and pieces of cane as they pass these down. The passenger counts out some money to the captain and pushes off.

Night comes on again, but afar off we see the lights of Bolivar. There is almost no wind. A slight drizzle of rain is falling. We go up a dangerous channel with rocks like a manatee’s back, close alongside. At last we cast anchor before the town. It is half past one: we have been thirty-six hours out from San Felix.

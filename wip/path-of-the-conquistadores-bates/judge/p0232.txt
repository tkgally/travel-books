He shakes his head dolefully. “But we can let off fireworks,’ he adds, as if on an inspiration.

When nine o’clock comes, the performance being billed, “a las 8 y media en punto” sharp, we help set off fire-crackers and sky-rockets in the hotel courtyard. Nobody bothers about the sparks which fly down onto the thatched roofs of the town.

In the next hour or so, some fifty people, a good half of them children, slouch in, bringing their own chairs. We, who rank as Charter Members and Patrons of the Arts, pre-empt rocking-chairs in the front row. The orchestra takes its place on a bench near the curtain.

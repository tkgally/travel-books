We slap at the flies and wait for the heat to diminish. About four o’clock the mules are brought out and we start off once again. It is still chokingly hot, and the miles of savanna spread out unbrokenly in the glare. Now and then granite rocks and thickets are passed, and far ahead a mountain range, the Parida, is dimly outlined.

A few birds are flying among the rare chapparal. On one bush is perched an <i>oripopa</i>, a sort of small vulture, so tame that we pass it 30 feet away, and an occasional <i>zamuro</i>, or turkey-buzzard, wheels in the sun far overhead.

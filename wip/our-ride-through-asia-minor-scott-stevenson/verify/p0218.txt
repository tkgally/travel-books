Now and then, we came on fearful heaps of rottenness; in one place no less than seven dogs in the last stage of decay lay festering by the roadside, as if poisoned wholesale; whilst heaps of offal and filth were piled against each door waiting for the night-dogs to do the scavenger’s work.

I thought I had never seen so filthy a town. The very drains were black as they oozed slowly past, and in all my experience of Turkish lands, I had never seen horrors like these.

And this was the highway whence travelled all the mighty caravans from the East to the West! This was the birthplace of all the richest and most opulent merchants of Asiatic Turkey! This was the town in which missionaries had dwelt since their societies first came to the East! I cannot express my disappointment. I don’t now remember exactly what I expected; but I had read of a beautiful fertile country, with forests and hills and flowing rivers, and a town rich and prosperous in the midst of smiling gardens—and we had come to this: to the filthiest and most ruined city we had yet entered! We could scarcely believe our eyes, and indeed inquired of the zaptieh if this were really the famed Kaisariyeh.

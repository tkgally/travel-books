I was parched with thirst, but felt too exhausted to move. I really thought I was going to die, and all sorts of strange ideas came into my head. Mary Hamilton’s song kept ringing in my ears and repeating itself over and over:

<verse>
Oh! mither, mither, when ye cradled me,
Little did ye think
O’ the lands that I would travel
An’ o’ the death that I would dee.
</verse>

I remember I was roused up once, by seeing a man on horseback peering down on me from the rock above. I raised the pistol and held it straight out; but I think to save my life, I would have been afraid to really fire it; and indeed I had forgotten which part Andrew had shown me to pull if I wanted to use it. The man fortunately moved off as suddenly as he had come, and whether Circassian or Kurd, I was too dazed to make out.

The hours seemed to drag wearily along when the sound of footsteps again roused me. I hoped this time that it was my husband, but feared it was the horseman returning with his friends to plunder us. Instead, I saw a string of baggage mules with two Turks in attendance. They stopped on seeing the débris below, and one descended halfway down the hillside.

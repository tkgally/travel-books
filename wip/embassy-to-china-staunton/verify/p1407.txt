[[cont]][[side: Cochin-china.]] A civil answer was returned to the viceroy, and a demand made of the property, that had been seized at Hué-foo. Promises were soon sent that it should be restored, and an interview requested. The person, however, by whom these promises were conveyed, privately mentioned the insincerity of such professions, and that, in fact, hostile preparations were carrying on against the vessel.

“On the twenty-fourth of November, the weather appearing moderate, the captain of the ship moved her nearer to the river’s mouth, about a mile above the spot where a prodigious high surge broke across the bar. On each bank of the river, thereabouts, were crowds of people busied in bringing down guns, fascines, and stores, and in erecting batteries, which, notwithstanding every effort to interrupt them, were soon completed, and began to play upon the vessel, tho with little effect. They were inexperienced in the management of guns, and took, as yet, bad aim. They ceased firing during the obscurity of the night; but in the course of it the vessel was exposed to another danger; a heavy swell drove her from her anchors, and several violent shocks announced that she was striking upon the ground; and it was to be dreaded that she soon would come to pieces.

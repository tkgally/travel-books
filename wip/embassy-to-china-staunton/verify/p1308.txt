They are said in that state to be <i>running a muck</i>, and instances of it are not more common among slaves, than among free natives of the country, who, in the anguish for losing their money, effects, and sometimes their families, at gaming, to which they are violently addicted, or under the pressure of some other passion or misfortune, have recourse to the same remedy, with the the same fatal effects.

A fondness for play, and a fondness for opium, are not uncommon among the Chinese also at Batavia; but the habits of restraint and moderation in which they are bred, and the cautious principles instilled into them, curb their disposition, and prevent them from falling into the same excesses. They are, indeed, much more capable of conceiving formidable designs against the government, and in the year 1740 a considerable number of Chinese, residing in different parts of the country, joined in a revolt under the command of a man, who said he was descended from an Emperor of China; and who, being joined also by several Javanese, attacked Batavia, but was repulsed.

The race of the Guanches, or aborigines of Teneriffe, is now almost extinct. They have not been, like the natives of South America, much diminished, by cruel treatment from their conquerors; but a society of people, less refined, always dwindles in the neighbourhood of one which is more so. The former, cramped in their possessions and excursions, daily repining at their dependence, and putting no limits to their indulgence in spirituous liquors, which the deleterious arts of their neighbours furnish at an easy rate, become gradually enervated in body and mind; and their race disappears, at length, from the surface of the earth. The very few Guanches, who remain, are entitled to some very trifling stipend, as a price of the submission of their ancestors, from the court of Spain, which they punctually, and with some sensations of pride, annually demand. Many of the dead bodies of those Guanches have been found in perfect preservation, in an erect posture, placed against the sides of caves dug into the mountains, the bodies wrapped round with several folds of goats’ skins.

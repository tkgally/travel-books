He felt also excruciating pain about the lower part of the abdomen; and a large swelling appeared, beginning at the ring of the external oblique muscle on the right side, and extending along the descending chord. He had often suffered under all these ailments, but seldom at the same time. The articular, and also lumbar and dorsal affections, generally recurred in spring and autumn; but the abdominal pain and swelling had been more frequent in their recurrence, and were shorter in their duration. The swelling appeared and disappeared sometimes on a sudden, but was greatest and most painful after making any particular exertion. These circumstances the Doctor learned from the Colao himself; who, however, was surprised at such a number of questions, which the other physicians had not thought it necessary to make. They drew their indications chiefly from the state of the pulse, in the knowledge of which they boasted the highest skill. According to their ideas, every part of the body has a pulse particular to itself, which indicates what part of the system suffers.

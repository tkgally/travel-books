Near this spot is situated an elegant gilt temple, called Luen-whang-miaw, or the yellow temple of the river Luen.

The Embassy had not proceeded far on the southern branch of the canal, when they arrived in the vicinity of the place where the Leu-tze, or famed fishing bird of China, is bred, and instructed in the art and practice of supplying his owner with fish in great abundance. It is a species of the pelican, resembling the common corvorant, but which, on a specimen being submitted to Doctor Shaw, he has distinguished in the following terms: “brown pelican or corvorant, with white throat, the body whitish beneath and spotted with brown; the tail rounded; the irides blue; the bill yellow.” It is delineated in plate 37 of the folio volume.

On a large lake close to this part of the canal, and to the eastward of it, are thousands of small boats and rafts, built entirely for this species of fishery.

# Chapter II.

[[subtitle]] Preparations for the Embassy.

[[side: Preparations for the Embassy.]] H<sc>owever</sc> flattering patronage may be considered to a minister, it becomes, on occasions of importance, a duty nice and difficult to perform. He is not more answerable for the measures he adopts, than for the choice of those to whom he confides their execution. As to the former, he may have credit given to him for the exercise of his judgment without any particular predilection; but, in suiting persons to employments, it behoves him, in order to escape censure, to be equally on his guard against his own partialities, and the solicitations of his most intimate connections; and he finds himself perfectly secure, only, when his private opinion coincides with the general voice.

Such was the case in the appointment of an Embassador to China. Lord Macartney was in the number of those whose reputation was established for talent, integrity, and an aptitude for business.

The bearings recorded in the journal of the Holderness were taken when at anchor afterwards, at a distance from the rock.”

The route, followed by the squadron, was between the Quee-san islands and a small cluster, named by the English the Bear and Cubs, lying to the westward of the Quee-sans and close to the continent of China. San-man, or the Whelps, were another group of islands lying to the northward, and in the middle of the passage towards Chu-san. Close to the westward of these the depth of water is five fathoms, and to the eastward seven. From them a north-north-west course leads between a cluster of small islands or rocks, surrounded with foul ground, called the Caulkers and Castle rock, and a small island to the eastward of them, called Kin-sa-hoia, or Starboard Jack, having a few rocks scattered on its southern side.

Within a mile of the seaport of Orotava is a collection of living plants from Mexico, and other parts of the Spanish dominions in America. From hence they are to be transplanted into Spain. It is an establishment of some expence, and, whatever may be its success, it shews a laudable attention, on the part of that government, to the promotion of natural knowledge.

The exterior practices of devotion, in every dependency of Spain, where the inquisition reaches, absorb, however, much of that leisure which might, otherwise, be employed for the purposes of instruction. Religion seems to be the principal business of the gentry of both sexes. Ladies of rank are seldom seen out of their own families, except in the churches at mass, at matins, and at vespers. The unmarried reside in convents, and are often cajoled to take the veil, by those who are already nuns professed, notwithstanding the bitter repentance which many of these feel for the vows themselves had made.

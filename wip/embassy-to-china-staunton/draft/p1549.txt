His Excellency knew that it was the intention of the commander to pay for every article received on board. It was possible, however, that the mandarines would conceive themselves bound by the general orders of the Emperor relative to the Embassy, to accept no payment for the supplies afforded to the Lion; but to charge for them in account with the imperial treasury, and perhaps not without exaggeration as to quantity and value; and as it was essential that the Embassy should appear as little burdensome as possible to the Chinese, he trusted that the commander would give particular directions, that no provisions or other articles be received on board, except what were solely for the general account, as absolutely necessary for the ship's use, or the health of the crew; and that nothing of any kind be allowed to come on board as presents to individuals.

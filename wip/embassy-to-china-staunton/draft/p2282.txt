The Legate, who had still the principal care of the Embassy, might have taken it, if offered, and would probably promise to forward or deliver it, as he had the papers hitherto confided to him; but would certainly have spared the Colao the trouble of reading it. His disposition towards the English was not softened by a disgrace which lately had befallen him, and of which, according to the report spread among the Embassy, it was innocently the cause. The Emperor happening to hear that the Embassador had his Imperial Majesty's portrait in the great cabin of the Lion, and flattered by the compliment, desired the Legate to give an account of it, that he might judge if it bore any resemblance to his person; but finding by his evasive answers that he had not seen it, not having been on board, as he had been ordered, he was instantly degraded for his disobedience. Such a power being possessed, and frequently exercised by the crown in China, with regard to all degrees of dignity. The Legate was reduced to wear an opaque white, instead of a transparent blue, button; and a crow's, instead of a peacock's tail feather pendent from his cap. Protected still, however, by Ho-choong-taung, he retained his authority and offices. None of the Chinese servants could be found with courage sufficient to carry a letter without his leave.

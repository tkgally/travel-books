The next day, the twenty-fourth of June, a large elevated rock came in sight. It is perfectly white, and, on that account, is called <i>Pedra Branca</i> by the Portugueze, who having been the first European navigators in these seas, many of their names were adopted by their successors. The latitude of Pedra Branca is twenty-two degrees nineteen minutes north, and its longitude one hundred and fourteen degrees fifty-seven minutes east. Fahrenheit’s thermometer at noon stood at eighty-four degrees, and the barometer at twenty-nine inches, seventy-three decimal parts. A current appeared to have set north by east during the last twenty-four hours, at the rate of about a mile an hour.

In the course of the next day, June twenty-fifth, the squadron crossed the tropic of Cancer; and the setting of the sun, that evening, was attended with an unusual degree of redness in the firmament. The quicksilver sunk suddenly in the barometer, and the wind increased to a fresh gale from the south-west.

Dark trousers and short cloth jacket, Holland blouse over flannel shirt, fastened at the waist with a blue <i>faja</i>, over which went cartridge-belt with revolver and ivory-mounted dagger given him by a Spanish friend (of whom more anon). The whole costume was completed by a broad grey felt sombrero, with silver cord and tassels,—the “<i>sombrero ancho</i>” of the song.

His horse was a beauty, cream-coloured,—the most esteemed barb colour—and looked very handsome with his Californian saddle, a Mexican bridle of white maguey fibre and red wool, and his repeating Winchester carbine slung at the saddle-bow.

29<i>th.</i>—We were going out driving, but an <i>agua cerro</i> came on which lasted an hour and a half. It began with thunder and heavy hail, which quite whitened the pavements, lying in heaps three inches high against the steps; and then turned into a torrent of rain. All the streets were inundated, and the S.’s coachman, who went out to get a carriage, came in with the news that the patios of the hotels were swimming in water a foot deep. So I had to wait till the water went down a little, and at seven Mr. S. walked home with me, and by means of an impromptu pathway of planks we managed to get across the patio at the Iturbide, which was still nearly covered, being a good deal lower than the street level, though the street was dry.

We found two rooms ready for us, with two little beds in each; or rather, in the one we occupied there was one of the coffin-like beds, and a sofa on which a bed was made up when we wanted to go to sleep. But all was reasonably clean, and the landlady was as kind as she possibly could be.

The key to our room, which opened into the lower hall, and very near the front door, was lost. But what mattered that in a country where robbers and burglars are unknown? Still, fresh as we were from countries not so blessed, we thought we should feel safer with the door locked. Besides, servants up there have a knack of bolting into your room at all times and at all hours without knocking, and if they happen to find you in dishabille, they reason, perhaps correctly, that from necessity everybody must be in that condition at least as often as once in twenty-four hours, and what matters it who sees them, so long as the fact is well known and perfectly understood by everybody?

# Chapter VI.

[[subtitle]] <sc>Steamer accommodations; cheapness of travel; Norwegian miles; the currency; rates of watermen; embarkation; reasons why docks not used; daylight at midnight; popular celebration of midsummer night; among the islands; their number; cod fishing, catching, drying and packing; meals on the steamer; the flat-bread.</sc>

H<sc>aving</sc> done the place the best we could in our limited time, we made preparations to go on board the Hakon-Jarl, the little steamer on which we were to proceed on our northern voyage. I had been on board the day before and secured the only state room on the boat for the ladies of my party, selecting a place on a side seat in the dining saloon for my own bed. These side seats were about six feet long and twenty inches wide, and were all occupied as sleeping berths, while other berths were suspended above them.

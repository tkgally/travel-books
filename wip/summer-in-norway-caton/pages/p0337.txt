The new road led us all the way to Hjœrkin, which is an old station on the old road, and is situated on a steep eminence.

Richards had reached there before us, in time to climb the eminence and make the necessary entry in the day-book, and was seated at his ease on a bench outside the house.

When we reached the foot of the declivity, our driver concluded not to drive up at all, but turn around there and bring the fresh horses down. The road was narrow and sideling. In turning short round, he brought the fore wheel quite under the carriage, which, without a load, would not have touched the body, but now, with the load in, the wheel came in contact with the bottom, and over we went down the hill. As I saw we were going over, I seized the lines as far forward as I could reach, and slipped from the box down on the rump of the near horse, and at the same time yanked the horses back, for a runaway at that time <i>must</i> be prevented.

When the female makes her nest she plucks down from her breast sufficient to line it abundantly. This the owner of the ground, after a time, carefully removes. She suffers this to be done without leaving. Again the bird plucks herself and lines the nest anew, which is again removed, when her pertinacity again prompts her to furnish her nest with a new lining. With this she is allowed to remain undisturbed, to complete the incubation. After she has left with her young brood another harvest is gathered from the deserted nest. This is much less valuable than the others, owing to its impurity. None, however, is fit for the market as it is taken from the nest; all has to be carefully picked out from the twigs and dirt with which it had become entangled in the nest.

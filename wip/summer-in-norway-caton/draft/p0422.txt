After three weeks in Paris without much rest, observing the changes which had taken place in the last nine years, and revisiting objects previously examined, we were off to London by way of Calais. In the depot at Paris, some light-fingered gentleman, perhaps thinking he could make better use of my notes of the journey than I could myself, managed to relieve me of my note-book, so that I have had to depend principally upon memory in giving the incidents and observations above.

Perhaps the reader should not regret this loss, for probably it has shortened the account, and so lightened his labors in the perusal.

We had an exceptionally quiet passage across the channel but had to climb high stone steps, covered with marine mosses saturated with water, when we landed at Dover, which was very unfortunate for some of the party.

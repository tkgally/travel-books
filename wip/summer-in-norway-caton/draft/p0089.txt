Indeed, I saw nothing else in Norway which I thought needed reform more than this. It constantly reminded me of putting the corn in one end of the bag and a stone in the other to balance it upon the horse’s back.

By the time we found ourselves comfortably seated on the upper deck it was midnight, or rather, I should say, <i>low twelve.</i> Still it was broad daylight—we could see to read the finest print, although the sun had been below the horizon for some minutes, and it would be some minutes before he would again appear. It was the longest day in the year, that twenty-third of June, and according to an immemorial custom the people were celebrating the event in a right good jolly way.

Formerly the elk were very abundant in Norway, and its flesh was an important article of subsistence among the <i>bonde</i> or peasantry. In deep snows they were pursued on snow-shoes or skates, called <i>skie</i>, which are quite different from our snow-shoes. They are made of wood, of the width of the foot, and about ten feet long, and capable of sustaining a man on light snow. The <i>skie-löber</i> does not attempt to walk with them, using them only as runners, propelling himself by a pole. In this way they can proceed very rapidly, and so they make long journeys.

Lately it has been found necessary to protect the elk by stringent laws, to prevent their extermination. The closed season is from the 31st of October till the 1st of August, leaving only three months, August, September and October, within which it is lawful to take them; and even then the owner of an estate may shoot but a single elk during the season, or he may transfer this right to another. Under this protection they are said to be now increasing in numbers.

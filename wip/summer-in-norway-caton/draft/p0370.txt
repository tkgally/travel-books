The rapids, cascades and falls, where the wild leaping waters rushed around large rocks and through deep and tortuous channels, formed a perpetual study, and I amused myself by imagining an American Indian in his bark-canoe, skillfully and successfully shooting the rapids.

We passed Holmen without detention, and rapidly; and now, as we had but one more change of horses before reaching Lillehammer, we were congratulating ourselves that we would terminate our journey by six o’clock, which was earlier than we had hoped for, at the best.

When we reached Fossegaarden we met with a vexatious disappointment. The station is some distance off the road, upon a steep bluff more than one hundred feet above it. We stopped in the road and Rose took off the horses and led them up the zigzag track to the station, and after awhile returned and said the horses would be sent down in half an hour.

The sun was beating down upon us in great force under the bluff, and we got out of the carriage and sought shelter under the fence and some shrubbery by the way-side.

I told Rose he must have that carriage back by seven o’clock in the morning or he must be answerable for the damages. Seeing I was very much in earnest, he put the young fellow, who had no doubt been the cause of all the trouble, into a carriole, and started him up the road, at the best pace he could get out of a sorry-looking pony, to overtake and stop the carriage, and as soon as he could change horses he followed with his old carriage.

About ten o’clock in the evening I had the satisfaction of seeing him drive up with my carriage. He said the boy had overtaken the party at the second station, about fifteen miles out, where they had waited till he had come up and changed carriages with them.

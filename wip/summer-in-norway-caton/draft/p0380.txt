In sailing through this lake, we saw by far the richest agricultural country we had seen in Norway, and it is rarely surpassed in beauty. The lake commences at what may be called the foot of the mountains, and it extends from the mountains to the plains. Its lower end is in the plains of Norway; its upper end is in the foot-hills, which gradually subside as we advance to the south. It reminded me more of the Seneca Lake of New York, and its broad, rich uplands on either side, than any other place I have ever seen, and I know not how to say more for the beauty of the lake and its surroundings than to give it this comparison. This only applies to the upper portion of the lake.

Besides the regular landing which the steamer makes, there are many pretty little hamlets along the shores of the lake, and fine rural residences close to the water, with their little boat-houses, probably occupied as summer residences, and they looked like quiet retreats from city life.

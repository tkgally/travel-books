But three days before, we had left England in a night which lasted long enough to give one a good refreshing sleep, and yet in so short a flight we had outrun the night, only the thin skirts of which still hung around us, holding a last struggle with perpetual day, which lay but a span before us. Already the strange sensation of so great a change began to creep over us, and it seemed as if we were at the confines of another world, where the laws of nature, as we had always known them, were suspended. Already the angel of sleep seemed to have taken his final flight to more sombre and more congenial regions, and left us to our fate, to struggle as we might for a short repose with that light so hostile to his sway.

We slept but little that morning, for morning had come so soon as evening had gone by. During the small hours the whistle blew for Christiansund, and I hastened out to get another peep at Norwegian scenery and the Norwegian people.

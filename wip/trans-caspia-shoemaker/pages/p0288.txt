# Chapter XX.

[[right]] July 24th.

ANY thing that moves by steam is marvelous to one after the slower tarantass; and how luxuriously smooth and comfortable these old, worn-out carriages appear! What a superb affair that whitewashed freight car that does duty as a dining car! One could travel to Cape Horn with such comforts. Surely, all things are comparative; at least it would seem so here, as I look back at my first impression of this same train. It is cool enough at night, and, though in our passage of the desert the thermometer mounts, in the car, to one hundred and ten (Fahr.), I do not feel it nearly so much as I have done eighty or ninety on a murky day at home, so perfectly dry and healthy is the air.

On the evening of the second day, we pass the ruins of ancient Merve, sharply and fantastically outlined against the “crimson glow of evening.”

I do not think, as he is human and not divine, that it is within the emperor’s power to do away with this present order of things. Nothing save a revolution, far-reaching and bloody—a revolution one shudders to contemplate—can bring light to the darkness of “Holy Russia.”

In the November days, that darkness deepens, and the end comes at Lavadia. Alexander passes before that throne that knows no difference between Czar and peasant, solves the “grand, sad subject of the immortality”; but I fancy, when the recording angel struck his name from the book of the living and entered it on the book of the dead, that he inscribed thereunder: “He hath done what he could.” Peace to the soul of the Czar!

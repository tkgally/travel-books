We are, for a time, much disheartened to learn that three Americans, Robinson, Thurston, and Watterman, who desired to enter Turkistan, have been turned back from Usin-ada. I had heard of them in our legation at St. Petersburg. It seems that they had been waiting at Batoum for the necessary permit, which had been telegraphed them. But they had nothing to show at Usin-ada save Mr. White’s telegram, which the authorities refused to honor. Therefore, they have recrossed the Caspian and are now en route to Ararat. The question arises as to whether we are much better off. We hold personal letters from the authorities in the capital to Mr. White, stating that permission has been granted and the military authorities advised of our coming. Will they do? If not, what will do? Have you ever butted against a stone wall? Then, come on this tour and battle with the underlings of the Czar. The further down they are, the tougher will be your experience.

These Europeans, outside of England, have absolutely no idea of real cleanliness or true sanitary arrangements. Here is a new and very handsome car, with roomy adjustable chairs, which make it a very pleasant sitting-room. At one end are three or four state-rooms, all one could wish for. But there all semblance of comfort or even decency ceases, and one comes to the lavatories, etc., etc. The former possess a faucet from which water will flow only when you hold it tightly, and when it does come it squirts up your sleeve, over your head, down your back—any place, in fact, save in the basin, which, by the way, is purposely so arranged that the fluid immediately runs out. The closet is simply vile, and it never enters the head of the porter to in any way cleanse it, although he will spend an hour on one window which shows outside. So it is in all hotels.

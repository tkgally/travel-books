The southern shores of the Black Sea are dotted their entire length with attractive-looking places. The towns appear prosperous, the land has a cultivated look, and although all is under the rule of the heathen Turk, it presents an appearance of happiness not to be met with in the dominions of Russia, and, in fact, reminds one very much of the Riviera.

Later in the day, we pause for an hour or so. The usual mountains, with a village scattered over their side and ending in a ruined castle on a hill; the usual bright blue waters, brilliantly painted boats, jargon of tongues, and jumble of colors. That is Terresco. Feeling sure that distance lends enchantment to the view, we do not go ashore. The day is too warm to justify much exercise. So I spend the time hanging over the ship's rail, trying to excite a score or more of boatmen to renewed quarrels. Judging by the noise, I am somewhat successful.

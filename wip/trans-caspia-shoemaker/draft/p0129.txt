He lives in a spacious mansion near the station, in one of those spreading, one-storied houses so common in this land, and aforetimes in Russia. I can not understand why the Russians so dread fresh air. When we called at his house in the morning, the outer air was fresh and delightful, but it was rigorously excluded from the house by double windows. Within it was simply stifling, and we returned in the evening with the dread of a dinner in such an atmosphere, but, on arrival, were conducted by our host to a side terrace, and there found the table. Prince G. and wife shortly joined the party, and we passed a charming two hours and had an excellent dinner. You must live on the stuff provided on the trains and at the cafés in Trans-Caspia to appreciate what that meant to us.

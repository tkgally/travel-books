The men “at home” wear as little as possible, but when out, wear, even in this torrid weather, a sort of thick double gown and a heavy felt hat. On leaving, I induced them to allow me to kodak the entire establishment, a picture which I hope will develop well. They are certainly a very different race from any I ever saw before. Rachmed tells me that they live here in a sort of imprisonment. The Russians will not permit them to enter their paradise, the Alai, and the Chinese keep them here. What a life! The place is on the banks of the dirty Kizil-Su and surrounded by towering yellow cliffs that blaze like a furnace from early morning to nightfall, and then the winds sweep down in icy blasts from the snows above, making, by their extreme contrast to the day’s heat, a place of torture.

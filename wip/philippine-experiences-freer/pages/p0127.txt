For two hours and a half each afternoon there was a practice class of children in two grades, which the <i>normalistas</i> taught in turn under our observation. After the dismissal of the children, the American teacher in charge pointed out the errors to the individual Filipino student in as kindly and sympathetic a manner as was possible.

An interesting fact in connection with this school was that the fifty young men and women represented three different peoples—the Ilocanos, the Gaddanes and the Isanays, the last named coming from the Dupax section. There was no one language that all could understand. A few spoke, read and wrote Spanish fluently, but these did not number more than ten; to the others Spanish was as strange a tongue as English.[[fnref *]] Hence, the announcements on the bulletin board, and verbal announcements as well, had to be rendered in the three dialects.

The linguistic ability of my fellow-countrymen, shown in their mastery of the Spanish language, impressed me deeply during the first few weeks of my wanderings. Everywhere I heard Americans of all classes, and particularly soldiers and teamsters, fluently conversing with the natives in Spanish, and great was my admiration. Such versatility was extraordinary. “What a wonderful people we are,” I thought, and my heart swelled with pride. But as time went on my opinion underwent modification, until, finally, I learned that what I mistook for Spanish was a remarkable jargon bearing no more resemblance to the correct speech than does Bowery slang to chaste English.

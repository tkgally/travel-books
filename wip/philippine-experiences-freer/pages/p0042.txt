I found there had been no public instruction in Aliaga since the insurrection which began in 1896 interrupted it, although there were easily four hundred children of school age living within convenient distance. The people were desirous of having the English language and American methods of teaching introduced, and the <i>presidente</i> had two buildings in view for school-houses, one for each sex; and schools were established under an American teacher a few months later.

The day after my arrival another tremendous <i>baguío</i> swept over the country, again inundating it. The quarters of the scouts’ officers, my hosts, shivered and creaked like a ship at sea; many dwellings and the barracks kitchen were blown down; our house was surrounded by water and the streets were flooded, so that the people were obliged to go about in <i>bancas.</i> The country-women coming to market waded through the water, waist or chest deep, each carrying a change of garments

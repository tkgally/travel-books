Viva Nuestra Señora de Peña Francia! Viva el Divino Rostro!”</i> is shouted by hundreds, thousands of throats. The greatest enthusiasm is manifested. The people on the banks add their cheers to those of the devotees in the boats and the water. The incense burning before the image and the sacred banner sends its smoke aloft. The barge moves up the stream, the various crews vying with each other in the labor of drawing it. At intervals men spring out from the shore to take their places beside the barge, so that a fringe of wet humanity two or three deep surrounds the float; for if a man be ill, he will be cured by such act of devotion. Some of my neighbors told me that, notwithstanding the hundreds of men and boys in and upon the water on these succeeding anniversaries, no accident had ever occurred; the Virgin protects those who thus serve her, hence nothing of the kind is possible. This afternoon a man was drowned; but the faithful say, “He died of very joy.”

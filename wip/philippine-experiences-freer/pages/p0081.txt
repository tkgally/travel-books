Yes, <i>that</i> is Fernando washing my shirts in the same useful stream. “But,” I reason, as I pass by, “the sunlight will disinfect them.” And so it did for over three years, for in all that time they were not boiled.

Most housekeeping Americans find themselves harassed by the general unreliability of the native help. For me the solution of the problem was easy, for the town lies close to the <i>rancherias</i> of the Igorrotes, who make excellent servants. I employed as cook one-eyed Clemente, who had lived in the valley long enough to acquire a Christian name and learn to write. He wore ordinary Philippine clothing, except on wash-days, when he was obliged to go back to the breech-clout. He cooked fairly well in the Spanish style, and readily adopted what changes I suggested from time to time, such as using less garlic with the fried chicken and cooking the rice a little more thoroughly.

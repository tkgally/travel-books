When the bride was asked if she would take the groom to be her husband, she hung her head and did not reply. The priest repeated the question, and, without response, the girl’s head went down lower. The third time the question was put, and the woman carried her handkerchief to her eyes.

By this time the <i>madrinas</i> and the bride’s mother had stepped up behind and begun whispering in her ear. “What is this?” said I to myself. “Is it possible that I am a witness to a forced marriage? This will never do. But what is to be done?” Then I reflected that the priest, whom I knew very well and in whom I had the utmost confidence, would not allow anything of that kind; and I decided to wait and see. In the meantime the whispering admonitions had continued, and the question was being put for the fourth time. The silence was intense. At length the bridal lips uttered a subdued “Yes,” and the ceremony went on.

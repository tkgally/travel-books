The small crop of rice, which for several years previous had been eaten by the locusts, escaped destruction, and the people took heart. The school filled to overflowing, still more furniture was built by means of government donations of rice, the new teachers were active and helpful, and Pili was a new town, in spirit at least. The following year an increased acreage of rice was planted and harvested; and if there be no serious backsets the town will soon again be on its feet.

The neighboring town of Bula, small and somewhat out of the way, had remained without a school since the insurrection, owing to the absence of a specific demand. But soon after Pili's awakening, the Bula people petitioned for a school and for aid in building a school-house. In response, fifty sacks of congressional relief fund rice were apportioned, and the active old gentleman who occupied the position of <i>presidente</i> made plans for a well-built and roomy school.

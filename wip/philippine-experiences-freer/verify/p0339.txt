Following this misfortune came the cholera, which took two of the children, the second and third in order of birth. <i>Gracias á Dios,</i> three remained; and Benigno was learning English from the soldier teacher in the school. Benigno would be a wise man some day, and then perhaps Pedro could find out some things that he wanted to know. Why were the white people rich—the Filipinos poor? He had never known a white man who was not well off; even the Spanish soldiers who remained in the province occupied better houses than the natives and had better clothing and more food. Why this unequal distribution of favors? Why were there no Filipino saints? In all his church going, in all the religious processions he had ever seen, never was there an image of a Malay saint. But Benigno was going to school and learning English—perhaps he would know some time.

He spent most of the time thrusting his head out between the bars; though he had plenty of drink and food and the boys kept the cage clean he pined for his freedom. Seeing that he continued unhappy I decided to let him have his way; and one bright morning he hopped to the open door of the cage, stood a moment to measure the space and be sure he was not deceived, spread his wings and, in a trice, flew away toward a large <i>talisay</i> tree near the <i>presidencia.</i> ‘<i>Adios, pájaro,</i>’ said I; ‘<i>ojalá que te suceda bien,</i>’ [[fnref *]] and I went about my work.

“That afternoon, just as I arose from my siesta, one of the policemen stood at the door and called my name. I went to him and, <i>diantre,</i> there in his hands was my bird; his feathers were disordered and a few were lacking, his crest was raw and bloody, an eye was shut and a leg was lame. In reply to my questions, the policeman said that while standing in front of the <i>presidencia</i> he had heard a fluttering and the cries of a bird under the big <i>talisay,</i> and had seen two large crows attacking a smaller bird which was entirely at their mercy; he went over and arrived just in time

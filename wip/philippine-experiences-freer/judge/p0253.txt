As I clung to his neck he continued his cautious way until we were in the deepest part of the pool two-thirds of the way across, when his strength gave out completely and he dropped me where he stood. The situation was so amusing that I could not help laughing heartily, which seemed to assure the much chagrined man that he was forgiven. In such manner I travelled among the towns of Indan, San Vicente, Labo, and Talisay and their <i>barrios,</i> having recourse at times to <i>banca</i> or <i>carabao</i>-sled if more convenient.

These Tagalog towns gave generous support to the schools. Of the <i>barrios,</i> even Matango, an impoverished hamlet lying at the head of one of the sloughs which form a network at the mouth of the Labo River, wanted a school. The people said they would willingly pay a teacher by private subscription if the government would but establish and supervise the school.

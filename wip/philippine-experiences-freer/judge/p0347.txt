# Chapter XXII

[[subtitle]] Some Filipinos I Have Known

[[subtitle]] Don Fulgencio and His Family—His Business—A Habit That Could Not Be Broken—Sociability—Capitán Domingo, the <i>Presidente</i> of Mirasol—Methods of Electioneering—Lax Administration—Gambling—Petty Grafting—Don Mariano, the Governor—An Efficient Officer—Public Scoldings—The Justices of the Peace—Their Tyranny—What Would Become of the <i>Tao?</i>—The Bird of Seven Colors—Fulgencio’s Application of the Story.

I<sc>n</sc> the course of my travels over the archipelago I came in time to the Province of Nueva Castilla, where I sojourned for a considerable period, making my headquarters at the home of Don Fulgencio, one of the <i>principales</i> of the town of Mirasol. Don Fulgencio was a fine example of the elderly Filipino, nearly six feet tall, well-proportioned, strong and vigorous in spite of his gray hairs and more than sixty years.

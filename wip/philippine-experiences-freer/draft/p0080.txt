A short distance farther on is a wide and deep pool delightfully shaded by a spreading bamboo; and in it are bathing three ungainly <i>carabao</i> which have just come in from the rice field for the thrice-daily bath. If this bath be missed the <i>carabao</i> cannot work, and may even become wild and unmanageable. At the edge of the same pool a girl is washing the smoke-blackened <i>ollas</i> in which the daily rice is cooked, scrubbing them well with the mud and sand from the bottom until they shine again. A rod away a flock of ducks is quacking and swimming, and some pigs stand belly-deep quenching their thirst. Here as we go are two more <i>lavanderas</i> working industriously, and a third woman cleaning some fish for the coming meal, immersing them in the stream as she cuts and scrapes them. A few yards farther two geese waddle away from the bank where a fourth woman has just come to bathe. She has draped herself in a single colored sheet, so arranged that she can complete her bath both efficaciously and modestly, even though an American Peeping Tom pass that way.

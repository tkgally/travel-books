Let me now portray a more pleasing character. Don Mariano had been appointed governor of Nueva Castilla, to fill the unexpired term of his American predecessor, who had been designated to fill a position under the insular government. He was short of stature and very dark. His small twinkling eyes could smile, frown or flash, as the occasion demanded. He had become wealthy in hemp and was reputed to be a millionaire. Before becoming governor he had the reputation of being the most able and energetic <i>presidente</i> in the province, and his town had been the best governed, as Mirasol was one of the worst. Like all Filipinos in public life, educated and trained under the Spanish <i>régime,</i> he was arbitrary and autocratic, but unlike most such, he did not abuse his authority.

The <i>comparsas</i> parade the streets in military formation; the men are brave in gaudy uniforms and the officers valiant with swords of tin, their artificial mustaches and goggles supposedly lending them a distinguished air. Presently they come to a halt in the plaza, and present themselves, one company at a time, to the <i>presidente</i> and judges occupying the bandstand. With each is a speaker, sometimes a man and sometimes a precocious youngster, who indulges in spread-eagle oratory accompanied by more or less affected gestures in eulogy of the occasion. The oration is followed by a dance by selected and drilled performers, by a chorus by the company, or by some other exercise, which, together with the general appearance and the manœuvres of the <i>comparsa,</i> is made the basis of the award of prizes. During the day and evening these performances are repeated in the streets in different portions of the town for the benefit of the appreciative citizens.

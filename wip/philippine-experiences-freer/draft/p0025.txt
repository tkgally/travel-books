[[cont]]rank upon rank of <i>cascos</i>[[fnref *]] tied to the sea-wall and to each other; whistling, turning and twisting to avoid the score of other craft puffing down the river—and at last we set foot upon Philippine soil and entered the portal of the untold future. The business of the customs finished, I bundled myself and baggage into a <i>quelis</i> drawn by a small, lean pony, and directing the bare-footed <i>cochero</i> where to drive, beheld with eager eyes that varied oriental life which is so intensely interesting to the Westerner upon first acquaintance.

The sounds of river and street life, the peculiar odors, the strange sights, were bewildering. The clouted Chinese coolies laboring on the water-front, the Filipino boys swimming in the Pásig, the <i>carabao</i>—unwieldy beasts with wide-spreading horns and sulphurous breaths, the odd vehicles and the emaciated ponies drawing them, the Filipinos, American soldiers in khaki, Sikhs, Cingalese,—all these made up the most interesting medley I had ever seen. That day and the few immediately following I looked and lingered, and looked again, held by a fascination I could not resist.

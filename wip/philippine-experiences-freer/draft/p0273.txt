Some persons entered and attempted to carry out the piano. By this time the crowd was very large; the governor and provincial officials arrived; our house had become a smouldering ruin with no part of the structure remaining except the burning <i>harigues;</i> several small buildings opposite had been pulled down so as not to provide additional fuel for the flames. The adjacent dwelling was half consumed, and the heat was great; the people were still striving with the desperation born of impotence to save the house of the <i>presidente;</i> if it once burst into flame it would be gone, but in a few seconds more the danger would be past. How we watched—how we hoped! The fire gradually became less; yes, the building was safe.

Then we breathed sighs of relief and sat down to rest and talk. How did the fire begin? That a spark had smouldered in the <i>nipa</i> roof above the stove from seven o’clock until past midnight did not seem possible, yet, how else?

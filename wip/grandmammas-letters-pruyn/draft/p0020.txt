[[cont]]but all he could learn was this: “Far off in some part of the world there was a place called Japan. No one could tell anything about the country, or the people, except they were very strange, and only once a year would allow a few Dutch traders to come there and buy some of their curious goods.” In that case were some of their beautiful things, brought away by some Dutchmen, and sold to an American merchant, who was exhibiting them as great curiosities that had come all the way from that strange and almost unknown land. What a change has come since that day! I suppose not one of the dear children who may read these letters is as ignorant now as the wise and grown-up men were when my brother questioned them. Japan is now our nearest heathen neighbor, and travellers from our country, and from all lands, are constantly going there to see for themselves the strange people, and the wondrously beautiful country.

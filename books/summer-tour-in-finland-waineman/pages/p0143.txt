A COUNTRY railway station in Finland is the fashionable rendezvous of the neighbourhood. Particularly on a Sunday, when, on the arrival or departure of a train, the platform reminds one of a massed choir at the Albert Hall—the women to the left and the men to the right. The sexes keep most rigorously divided, and a young man talking to a young woman is an unheard-of proceeding in public. On a Sunday the women discard their coloured kerchiefs, and for the most part cover their heads in sober black silk shawls, that generally have been handed down from mother to daughter for generations.

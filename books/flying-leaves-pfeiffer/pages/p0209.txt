We had gazed at the shops as we passed before entering the market, and, seeing what our former experience had been, I was surprised at the many objects acceptable to an exacting taste, which were to be found in them. However recently the majority of the inhabitants of San Francisco may have attained to wealth, it is clear that there exists a class well learned in the most approved fashion of scattering it in view of a fair return. On our way from the hotel we had lingered before the brilliant show in a jeweller’s window, and afterwards, at the polite invitation of the jeweller, had entered the shop, when he showed us a large collection of diamonds of the purest water, and permitted us to contemplate at our ease the fine decorations of the interior, and the many beautiful things displayed in the cases. What are here called the dry goods stores and the shops for “Yankee notions” are also tempting; the women’s fancies and furbelows cunningly set forth, and the vanity-fair business altogether well conducted.

In the afternoon we visited the Chinese quarter—Chinatown as it is called,—and saw all that was to be seen from the outside.

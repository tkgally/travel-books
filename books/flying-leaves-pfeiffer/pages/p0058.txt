These, too, are or can be made universal, but they have attained their position not through artistic self-renouncement but through the truth and depth of their personal passion. The one art is the outcome of a joy in creation, calm as that of the Olympian gods; the other, the result of the travail of the human soul with apprehended truth, as yet unrealized, perhaps for ever unrealizable.

Arriving thus far in my retrospect, it struck me that I had touched only the negative side of Greek achievement, and that no art or manner of thing whatsoever could attain to immortality through negatives alone. I then seemed to see that this Olympian repose made as it were the quiet field on which were revealed to the finely trained Greek sense, beauties of a nature and quality so subtle that they must have been lost in a disturbing medium. The government kept by these Titans of old upon the impulse of their genius may be compared to that with which a skilful rider regulates the motion of his hand, tranquilizing and restraining in order that the lightest imparted movement may tell with unerring effect.

Some of them were pawing the ice, endeavouring to break it, that they might get their morning drink. Some were lying down in the long grass, and others fed or played about. I watched them for some minutes before I crept a little nearer and broke up their pleasant party by sending a bullet through the heart of a fine buck antelope.

The difficulties of our route began on reaching the great belt of pine forest which clothes the main ridges of the mountains. The trail we were following, being only used by Indians and a few white trappers when hunting, was not wide enough to admit the passage of the waggons. The men had, therefore, to be set to the tiresome task of cutting a road through the forest. Not only had the living trees to be cut down, but the dead ones, which lay piled together on the ground, had to be removed, so that our progress was very slow and laborious.

The plank road ends here, the rest of the way being marked by a mere track with stumps of trees and rocks profusely scattered along it. The swampy places are crossed by what is called a “corduroy road;” that is, a road formed by a number of pine trees, cut down and laid close together across the swamp. As no earth is laid over the rough trunks of the trees, the jolting of the waggons when crossing them may easily be imagined.

As we proceeded on our journey the log huts become scarcer, and the clearings fewer, until at last the road ends at Martin’s Hotel, on the Lower Saranac Lake, where we were glad enough to arrive after the horrible jolting occasioned by the roughness of the road from Franklin’s Falls. At Martin’s Hotel, a comfortable wooden house, built much in the style of a Swiss mountain inn, were several parties bound for different parts of the Adirondack region, among whom were some English ladies just about to start on a fishing excursion on the lakes and rivers, which abound in fine red-fleshed trout.

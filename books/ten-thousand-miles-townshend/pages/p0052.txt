On the grass in front of the inn was an unfortunate land-turtle, endeavouring to make its escape from some boys who were torturing it by placing lighted sticks on its tail. We remonstrated with them on their cruelty, when they kicked it into the river, where it soon swam away.

From Round Pond we had our first “carry” of half a mile to the Upper Saranac Lake. These “carries” are frequent in the Adirondacks from one lake or river to another. Where no waggon can be procured, the guide carries the boat on his head, while the hunters shoulder as much baggage as they can carry, and, thus laden, make the best of their way through the forest to the nearest water, where the boat is again launched.

From Camp Douglas, two miles distant, where some companies of United States troops are kept to look after the Saints, the view is undeniably lovely, reminding one strongly of Damascus as seen from the slopes of Anti-Libanon.

The city is built at the foot of the great snowy range of the Wahsatch Mountains, which with the Oquirr Mountains, form a circle round the plain, or valley as it is called, of Salt Lake. Through the plain flows the Jordan River, uniting the fresh waters of Utah Lake with the briny waters of the Great Salt Lake. The latter lake is fifteen miles from the city, and is about one hundred miles long by sixty broad.

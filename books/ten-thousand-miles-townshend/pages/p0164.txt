It is hard to realise the fact that one is passing over part of the great Rocky Mountain chain, as the summit is reached by a succession of table lands so gradually sloping up to this height that the rise is almost imperceptible to the eye. Descending slightly from Sherman to Fort Saunders, which is seven thousand three hundred feet above the level of the sea, we got out of the train, and proceeded to the quarters of the officers stationed there.

We were received most kindly by General Gibbon commanding the post, and the officers of the 30th infantry, who promised us a hunt through the best part of the Rocky Mountains as soon as the bad weather, which was evidently coming on, should have passed. Meanwhile we gladly accepted their offer of lodging and entertainment at the fort.

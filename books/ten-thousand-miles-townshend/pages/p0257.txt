On the second deck was a long saloon, where five hundred people might easily sit down to dinner. There were some large state rooms aft. Forward were the barber’s shop, the bar-room, the butcher’s shop, and other offices. On the third deck were the berths of the second-class passengers, and forward those of the emigrants and crew. Below these was the passengers’ baggage, and at a still greater depth the cargo was stowed. The ship, when lit up at night, looked like a huge floating-palace, but had a deal too much top-hamper for our ideas of what is safe in a stormy ocean.

This consists of roughly-cut planks of wood laid on pine logs, forming a road which is anything but smooth, yet far preferable to the ordinary country roads, which consist merely of sand or mud, in which the wheels of the heavy coaches sink nearly up to the axle-trees. Half way to Lake George the road passes Glens Falls, where the river Hudson falls over a magnificent series of rapids, and where are laid many of the scenes in Cooper’s novel of “The Last of the Mohicans.”

Wednesday, the 26th, found us comfortably settled at the Fort William Henry Hotel, at the south end of Lake George. This lake is a beautiful sheet of water, thirty-six miles in length, by two or three in breadth. The surrounding hills are covered with forest, as well as the numerous islands, more than three hundred in number, which stud the lake. Trout, bass, and pickerell are freely caught in its waters, while in the neighbouring forests deer and wood partridge are still found.

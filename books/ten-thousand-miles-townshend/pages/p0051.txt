[[cont]]My fair countrywomen were extremely enthusiastic on the subject of tent life in the forest, which they were about to try for the first time. From our landlord, himself an experienced hunter, guide, and crack rifle shot, we procured the tents, provisions, guides, and canoes necessary to continue our journey through the forest; water being the only means of travel available through the wild country constituting what is called the Wilderness of New York.

At sunrise of August 31st, we each got into one of the light boats or canoes, built of white pine and weighing about eighty pounds. One of us sat in the stern of each canoe and paddled, while the guide, seated in the bows, sculled. The centre of the boat was occupied by such tents and provisions as each could carry. Passing among the numerous beautiful islands which are scattered about the lake, covered, as well as the shores, with a dense forest of cedar, maple, and pine, an hour's row brought us to the head of the lake, where we entered the Saranac river.

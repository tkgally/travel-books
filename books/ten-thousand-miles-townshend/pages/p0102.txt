As the conference had still some days’ work before it, we occupied the time in seeing the sights of Chicago, and in the evenings had a tolerable Italian or German opera at the large and handsome Opera House. Some of the operas were sung partly in Italian and partly in German, the effect of which was more novel than pleasing.

One of the monster torchlight processions, of which we had heard, was held in honour of Mr. Seymour, the Democratic candidate for the Presidency, while we were at Chicago. About ten thousand men walked in it, four abreast, each carrying a torch, and several of them bearing party flags and emblems.

The procession, which was enlivened by several bands of music, was a very pretty sight. Processions seem indeed to be a great institution of the country, as we saw several, both Democratic and Republican, in nearly every large town we visited in the States.

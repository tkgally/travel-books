My rifle appeared especially to excite the suspicions of the “colonel” who was told off to examine my baggage. He made me put it together, and then having examined it carefully, he asked me how much I had given for it. On my replying “eighty pounds”—four hundred dollars gold—he coolly remarked, “Guess you’d get as good a one in N’York for eighty dollars,” and then allowed me to put it up again in its case.

At the Custom-house we committed our luggage to the care of the express agent, and then proceeded to engage a hack carriage, which, as legal fares either don’t exist, or are totally disregarded, is an affair of a good deal of trouble and argument. However, we at last agreed to pay the heavy sum of eight dollars—twenty-four shillings English—and then drove on board the steam ferry-boat, which conveyed our carriage and several others over to New York, where we put up at the Clarendon Hotel.

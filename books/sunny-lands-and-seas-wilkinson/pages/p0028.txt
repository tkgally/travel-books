[[right]] Dec. 21, 1881.

We had a great downpour of rain last night, it being the first that has fallen here for ten months; the streets this morning are, in consequence, most filthy, and the air muggy and hot. There is very great excitement amongst us all, for we hear the block in the canal is removed, and that the <i>Ceylon</i> will be through in about an hour. We can see a crowd of ships away in the distance over the sand, steaming slowly through the canal, which is about three-quarters of a mile off. The <i>Ceylon</i> has just come in sight, so we must be off to meet her. She is to be easily distinguished from the others by her light dove-colour.

[[right]] S.S. <i>Ceylon</i>, near Bombay.

We joined our home for the next few months about three miles from the hotel, in the Gulf of Suez, after bidding hurried adieus to our friends, and the same evening we steamed away for Bombay. Every day since we left we have had hot, cloudless, and breezy days, and all along the Red Sea have been occasionally in sight of land.

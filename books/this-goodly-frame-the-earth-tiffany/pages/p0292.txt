Calmer hours of reflection will come later, in which memories of all you have seen there will emerge beautiful as reborn dragon-flies that have sloughed off and left behind their rent and desiccated Cook strait-jackets.

## III.

The departure from Cairo for one’s voyage up the Nile presents for the first two or three hours a succession of fascinating pictures. The city itself, crowned by the great citadel of Saladin, and at its summit by the five-domed mosque of Mohammed-Ali, with its two slender, sky-piercing minarets, smiles a gracious good-by and God speed for a voyage of wonders. Into the very city thrusts itself a great arm of the desert; and out from stretches of straw-colored sand rise, like exhalations, the ruins of the beautiful tombs of the Khalifs, to me the most charming of all the architectural glories of Cairo.

[[cont]]and gilded wood-carving; in the chasing of every brass jointure of a beam; in the superb gilded candelabra of lotus leaves and flowers; in gold-based screens covered with chrysanthemums, wistaria vines, wrens, herons—here lies the work in which the Japanese are as happy and at home as children reveling in the meadows and woods of springtime.

If, then, I were called upon to express my own personal feeling as to the impression wrought by the actual temples of Japan in comparison with that of the groves, avenues, and quiet, secluded gardens environing them, it would be in somewhat the following fashion that I should have to set to work. I enjoy immensely visiting many a temple; but I enjoy it very much as I should visiting a transfigured and sublimated curio shop, exquisitely harmonized in its details. I dote on lacquer. I am as crazy as any woman over embroideries. My finger-tips itch with kleptomania at the sight of choice bronzes. Screens painted with herons, wild geese, cherry blossoms, and wistaria vines, delight me with their life and grace; and as for a polished floor,—a limpid pool for the exquisite reflection of ceiling, gilded pillars, magnificent altar,—I could drown myself like Narcissus in it. Over the gilded and painted open-work, wood-carvings of flowers, peacocks, cranes, doves, monkeys, dragons, and griffins, I could delight to linger, in such places as Nikko, Tokyo, or Kyoto, a couple of hours every morning for a month, examining them bit by bit.

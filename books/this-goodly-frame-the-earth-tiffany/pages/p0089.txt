The man who can read this story without high-wrought admiration for such qualities of loyalty, courage, and fathomless contempt of self, has no sense in him of the heroic; as equally the man belongs back in the realm of savagery who does not shudder at such Moloch sacrifice, on a blood-reeking feudal altar, of all other graces and sanctities of life.

Here again we have Japan through and through, the paucity of her ideas, the limitation of her range of emotional response, her incapacity, so far, for the complex and synthetic, as clearly revealed in the narrowness of her heroic moral standard as in the lack of harmonic depth in her tinkling music. The wife of the Scotch Highlander outlawed in the service of his chief would unshrinkingly have faced with her husband starvation on the wintry hills; but once had it come to the issue of degrading her person, to supply him with means to carry out his fell purpose, heroically would she have cried, “Nay!

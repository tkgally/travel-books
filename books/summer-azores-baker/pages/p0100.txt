[[cont]]ran down at full speed from the top of steep hills to the very shore of the sea, meeting the cool breeze, and dashing through villages quaint and pretty,—and, alas! poor in direct ratio to their picturesqueness. Half-clad women, with folded arms, idle and inane but for the look of stolid despair on their otherwise expressionless faces, crouched on the floor of their squalid huts, which they shared with the hens and pigeons. Naked babies crawled about the doors, and an army of brutal and savage children ran clamoring after us for alms.

All along our route, old stone fountains babbled, and from their brazen throats poured cool mountain springs to refresh the weary traveller. Women were filling their great red water-jars at the spouts; others had dammed up the overflow in the road, and were washing their clothes in the puddle. Many were harvesting. Great heaps of corn lay on the <i>eiras;</i> and whole families were squatting beside them, braiding bunches of ears together by the husks. These the men hung up high to dry on four poles put together like a wigwam, mounting to the top by ladders. Beautiful in color were these rural pictures.

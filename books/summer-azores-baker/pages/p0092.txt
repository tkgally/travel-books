[[cont]]Myriads of gulls, aroused from their slumbers, flew startled from their perch on the anchored lighters, shrieking ominously.

The steamer fired her departing gun at two <sc>a. m.</sc>, and three hours later another gun announced our arrival at San Jorge.

<verse>
“Haven here
Was none for ships, nor sheltering creek; but shores
Beetling from high, and crags and walls of rock.”
</verse>

A stay here of two or three hours, while the ship takes in her cargo, is all that one cares to make; there being no accommodation for strangers. Graciosa is reached at noon. “Le plus gentil des isles,” as a Frenchman on board assured us, it is, as its name imports, a pretty little island, but with no special attractions for the tourist. It was a festival day in the islands. Bells pealed from the church-towers in honor of the “<i>consumption</i> of Mary,” so we were informed by a Portuguese gentleman who spoke English.

One sees here and there a child whose fair hair and blue eyes show its direct descent from the first families of the place. Road and river wind in and out among the high hills, whose steep slopes are cultivated to the very top. Some one said of this village, that its fields were set up on end, and cultivated on both sides.

Porto Pim is an adjunct of the main harbor, lying behind Monte da Guia and Monte Queimada, whose bases are connected by a high sandy beach. The opening between these two peaks makes a superb setting for the volcano beyond. It is prettiest at early dawn of a summer’s morning. Then the old Spanish fort and the windmill beyond are most picturesque. The mountains are in shadow; the rocks that guard the entrance to the harbor, black and jagged; the water, still and silvery blue.

# Coasting.

[[center]] <i>Sunday, Sept. 14.</i>

THE sled holds two people, and is like the <i>carro</i> cut in halves,—a wicker body on low wooden runners, projecting a little, and rounded at both ends, to prevent accident. Two men guide each sled. A stout rope extends from the point of each runner to the hand of the guide on either side.

The steep street, which is paved with small beach-stones set on their edges, and is worn flat, and polished by the constant passage of <i>carro</i> and sled, is unusually slippery to-day after the rain. The men dare not give the sled her head. It is very dangerous. With one hand firmly on the back of the sled, the other grasping the rope, they plant their bare feet squarely on the smooth pavement, and brace back with all their strength.

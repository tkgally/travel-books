Our life here was like a chapter out of “Quits.” Going to “The Top” before breakfast to drink new milk while the herdsmen milked, with a bit of black bread in our pockets, and alpenstocks in our hands, was like Nora’s excursions in the Tyrol. “The Top” was an alpine solitude, with here and there its cheese-makers, its fagot-cutters, and its charcoal-burners. It was like Nora anxiously waiting for the return of her cousin Jack from one of his adventures, when we went out in the moonlight, and heard the peasants hallooing and waking the echoes on the other side of the lake, and voices far away among the hills, and saw white figures stealing along the shore, and finally a dark object bounded up the path close by us, and it was our collegian returned from the <i>festa</i>. The miller and the forester, Seppel and Rosel,—we found them all here, but with Portuguese names.

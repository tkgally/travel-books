Different members of the same family are known by such a variety of names, that it is difficult to identify them as of one household. The wife sometimes takes her husband’s name: quite as often she does not. The oldest son appropriates some of the father’s ancestral names; the second son, some of the mother’s; neither assuming his father’s family name. The patronymic seems to be of little consequence. The personal name is the one to which importance is attached. Inquiring in a shop for the residence of the consul’s brother-in-law, we got no satisfaction till it dawned upon the proprietor that we were in search of the Rua de Senhor Jorge. A mother gave us her child’s name as Filomena das Angelos. Marias and Pias abound. In the post-office the letters are sorted according to the baptismal name,—a bundle of Antonios, another of Manuels, etc.

Now and then a trace of Orientalism appears. The women of the better class are seldom seen in the streets alone. “Would to Allah I might go home to my mistress!” sighed a tired model, posing for our artist.

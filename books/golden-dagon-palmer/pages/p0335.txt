A JOURNEY IN THE SEABOARD SLAVE STATES. B<sc>y</sc> F<sc>red.</sc> L<sc>aw</sc> O<sc>lmsted.</sc>

“Mr. Olmsted visited the Slave States for the purpose of studying the institutions and society of the South. He went not as a partisan, a fanatic, or a reformer, but rather as a student of Human Nature. He certainly had no foregone determination to detect the Sunny Side of Slavery, but he was quite as little under the sway of prejudices which would impair his integrity as an observer. The result is an instructive, lively, and most readable volume. It bears the stamp of honesty on its face. Everywhere it is direct and manly, equally free from the whine of the sentimentalist, and the growl of the sectarian. Its pictures of Southern Life are indeed generally far from flattering, but they are not painted in colors suited to irritate the sensitiveness of local pride. Not that he is squeamish of giving offense by the frankest expression of opinion—he shows no trace of trimming or truckling; but his severest comments are tempered by an essential good-nature that disarms them of their sting. With his turn for the comic side of things, he has gathered up a great many humorous incidents, whimsical anecdotes, and quaint reminiscences, which give a perpetual animation to his volume, and will force a laugh from even the crustiest Pro-Slavery reader that deigns to honor it with his perusal.”—<i>Daily Tribune, March</i> 15; <i>Weekly Tribune, March</i> 22.

It was from such sources as this that our fellows derived all their <i>loot</i>, as they called plunder; nor had the unscrupulous appropriation at any time a revolting aspect to even the most Quixotic minds among us. No popular affection was wronged, no religious pride insulted, no superstition alarmed. The Burmese are satisfied to let their gods and devils, from Guadma to the Sacred Goose, take care of their own images, never doubting that they can do that if they are fit to be gods at all; and though they do not go the length of licking them into good behavior, as some African tribes do, they would hardly fight for their silver outsides except the treasure were great, certainly never for their wood, or stone, or lead.

The manufacture of these gods is a popular branch of industry, and their sale a mere business transaction. Symes visited an image-yard where Guadmas of all sizes, and in every style of material and finish, were planted in rows like fancy gravestones, or stacked in piles like fashionable coffins.

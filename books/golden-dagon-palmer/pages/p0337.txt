## Household Words. C<sc>onducted by</sc> C<sc>harles</sc> D<sc>ickens</sc>.

This is the most popular periodical ever issued in England. It treats every topic in the most interesting and entertaining manner, the names of its chief contributors being sufficient guarantee of the character of the publication. In addition to the weekly articles of Charles Dickens, there are papers by William Howitt, Leigh Hunt, Mrs. Gaskell, the authoress of “Mary Barton,” “Ruth,” “Cranford,” and “North and South,” Barry Cornwall, Wilkie Collins, and all the rising English writers. In this country, the circulation is constantly increasing, and it is in every respect a safe, interesting, amusing, and useful Magazine for families, reading clubs, and the public generally.

“Household Words” may be obtained of all Booksellers and News Agents. Terms, 25 cents per number; Year’s subscription, $3.

## The Schoolfellow; An Illustrated Magazine for Boys and Girls.

Every number will contain original matter, from the best American Authors; with selections and translations from late English, German, and French Publications, and will be finely and profusely illustrated. It is the aim of the publishers to combine in the engravings and the letter-press of the “Schoolfellow,” the utmost possible beauty, variety, interest, and substantial instruction, with an elevated moral tone, and reverent spirit.

“The Schoolfellow” may be obtained of all Booksellers and News Agents. Terms, 10 cents per number; Year’s subscription, $1.

## The Gentleman’s Magazine.

With the number for July, 1856, the Gentleman’s Magazine changed publishers, but its general character will remain the same as before. More matters of interest will, from time to time, be introduced, and among all those readers who care to know the opinions of English Scholars and Gentlemen on the topics of the day, it will be as much looked for as usual; more and special pains will be taken with the Obituary, which will be made a faithful memorial of all persons of eminence. It will also endeavor to retain its position as a worthy organ and representative of Historical and Archæological Literature.

<i>We have made arrangements with the present Proprietors, Messrs. J. H. & J. Parker, of London, to publish the Magazine in this country, and will supply it with regularity.</i>

<i>Terms,</i> $5 <i>per annum, or</i> 50 <i>cents a number; those remitting</i> $5 <i>in advance, will receive the Magazine free of postage.</i>

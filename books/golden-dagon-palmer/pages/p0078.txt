After an hour of rattling through straight and narrow streets, between green ditches and smoky bamboo huts—the latter extremely ramshackle, and redolent of petroleum, ghee, and putrid fish—running over pariah dogs, and throwing naked brown brats into convulsions of glee, while their fathers and mothers squatted, and giggled, and smoked great green cigars, in their cane porches—we came, at last, to the river. Here, alighting from the garrees, we transferred ourselves and the “plunder” to ticklish canoes, and were paddled across the sluggish stream, thinking of crocodiles and hippopotami, to a bunch of tumble-down sheds in a bower of urchin banians, where some Burmese loafers, who were squatting as we approached, in knots of three or four, rising to the perpendicular when our boats stuck, a score of yards from the bank, ran down to bear us over the black mud on their backs.

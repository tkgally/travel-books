[[center]] <i>Notices of</i> “<i>Memoirs of Perthes</i>” <i>continued.</i>

“Perthes was the intimate and respected friend of Klopstock, the Poet, of Niebuhr, the historian, of William von Humboldt, the philologist, of Johannes von Müller, the statesman and writer, of the Counts Stolberg, of the Princess Galitzin, of Jacobi, and Eichhorn, and of Claudius and Heeren. And besides these he numbered among his confidential acquaintances, Goethe, Görres, Schlosser, Voss, the Schlegels, Schelling, Savigny, Varnhagen von Ense, and others scarcely less distinguished. He was in constant intercourse or correspondence with these, not on affairs of business at all, but on the great questions which agitated the church and state, or on the profounder problems of the inward religious life. Without pretending to a high degree of literary cultivation himself—always indeed holding himself forth as a simple bookseller—there was a charm in Perthes which attracted to him the best spirits of his day and generation, and, inferior as he was to them in position and attainments, he always met them with the frank and honest cordiality of an equal.”—<i>Putnam's Monthly, September,</i> 1856.

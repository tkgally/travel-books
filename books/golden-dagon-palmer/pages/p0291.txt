You remember the story of Mungo Park, and the African woman who brought him milk to drink, and sang to him the song of the “poor white man,” who had no mother, and “no wife to grind his corn?”

That water restored me, filled me with new confidence, made me quite calm and even thoughtful again; and so, seeing the great horror with which I inspired the woman, I left her and returned to my place outside. I took with me a carved rice-stick which I found in a pot, such as is used throughout that country to stir rice with while it is cooking. I waited with this in my hand, I should never be able to guess how long, or how short a time—it might have been hours, it might have been but a few minutes.

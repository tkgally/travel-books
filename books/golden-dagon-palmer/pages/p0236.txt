The landscape at Prome is Rhine-like in its picturesqueness. The lands, which have been low-lying and jungly ever since Rangoon, now suddenly swell into green hills, or are broken and upheaved in irregular piles of grim granite; and the outline of timber-crowned mountains is sharply defined against the Northern sky. The river, too, widens as suddenly, and becomes a lake-like expanse, flashing and canoe-dotted.

Then Prome has its golden pagoda, and its ruined fort, and its pillared banian, and a curious alley of palms. There are some very Westernish slopes opposite, that look as though they might be hiding a Christian barn behind them, in spite of three snowy pagodas not a long gun-shot off.

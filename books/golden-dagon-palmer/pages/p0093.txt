# Chapter IX.

[[subtitle]] Dacoitees—A Burmese House.

M<sc>oulmein</sc> had been always liable, if not to a combined attack, at least to the predatory incursions of the thieves of Martaban, an important village on the opposite side of the river. A year before, these <i>dacoitees</i> had been frequent and successful. The robbers crossed in war-boats, at night, the stream being narrow where it separates the towns, and, in formidable force and well armed, made successful descents upon the native quarters of Moulmein, occupied by friendly Burmese, and Bengalee and Chinese traders. A small force of Sepoys, which formed the patrol, could be easily intimidated or overpowered, and the suburbs effectually pillaged, before the alarm could reach the cantonments, and the troops be got under arms.

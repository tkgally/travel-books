But there were the two men, squatting side by side. I seized the dhar of the foolish-looking fellow, and tried to pull the cord which held it, over his head. Both had laid down their lances; their dhars were sheathed, and their hands pressed together. When I made the attempt on my foolish friend’s sword, he smiled—I remember how—a silly, childish, <i>Indian</i> smile, like that of a slave whose master condescends to jest with him. But his ugly comrade did not smile—far otherwise. He laid his hand upon the other’s dhar as I was in the act of removing it, and, holding it fast, shook his head in a threatening way, as if, less superstitious and more cunning than the rest, he had already half detected me.

He was very rich, had many wives, and two or three country houses, within a mile of Kabenda, built of wood in more than usual native style. He wisely adhered to the native dress, but it was always of good material; and native dresses, although they only consist of a shirt and loin cloth, are often very expensive. He wore a valuable ring on his finger, and would do his sporting (stalking sparrows!) with a double barrelled breech-loader of the latest fashion.

The Kabenda men are all a superior race for the coast. They are expert sailors, and navigate the coasting craft employed in collecting produce between St. Paul’s de Loando and the Gaboon. Many of them are fair carpenters, coopers, blacksmiths, and tailors; and their towns and villages are the neatest and cleanest to be seen anywhere. The houses are built of cane, and thatched with palm leaves; the space around each is kept cleanly swept, and the gardens are regularly laid out and well cultivated.

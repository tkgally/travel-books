I went on board a hulk at the entrance of the Muni, the fore part was crowded with produce being prepared for shipment to Europe, and India rubber and oil are not cleanly looking things; but the after part of the ship was comfortably and cooly furnished, and the usual hospitable reception ready from its occupiers. They reported trade improving and natives less troublesome. The natives look to be an inferior race. The canoes with which they bring down produce from the interior are hollowed out of very large trees, some of astonishing size. I saw some like them at Gaboon which had been captured formerly by French men-of-war, taking slaves to the island of St. Thomas, and I was informed had sixty men in each.

Our stay was very short. As we proceeded out of the bay, we sent a boat to examine the wreck of the <i>Macgregor Laird</i>, whose funnel alone was to be seen above water. While the boat was pulling round the wreck, a cask came suddenly to the surface. It was branded “Pale Ale, Bass and Co., Burton-on-Trent!” It was brought on board as a prize, and its contents directed to be issued to the men, the ownership of the cask being very doubtful. But alas for anticipated pleasure! The contents were very foul smelling and apparently bilge water!

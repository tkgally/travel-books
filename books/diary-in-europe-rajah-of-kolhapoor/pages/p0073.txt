29<i>th.</i>—Went to the India Office, where I left cards for some Members of Council. Then went to Mr. Green’s picture-gallery, where I left my name for an engraving of the pictures of the Derby Cabinet, and an engraving of the picture of the Prince and Princess of Wales’s Marriage. Went in the evening to Woodin’s. This is a small place. I was quite astonished to see him change his dress in such a very short time. He acts and sings pretty well, but I admire his changing his dress very much.

30<i>th.</i>—Drove to the Paddington Station, which we left at 10.15. Reached Oxford at 12 o’clock. Drove first to Christ’s College, and then to Mr. N——’s house, to find him out, but to our great disappointment he was not in the house. Then went to Dr. A——’s, where we saw Mrs. A——, and with her walked to the Bodleian Library. Mrs. A—— introduced Dr. Cox to me, and with him we saw the whole library, which is one of the largest in the world. This library is full of books and old manuscripts of several famous men. The gallery is full of the pictures of English sovereigns and statesmen. Then we went to New College, which is a large building. There is a church here which is beautiful. There are pretty grounds near the College which I liked very much. Then on our way to the “Randolph Hotel” we looked in on Dr. A—— and saw him.

Of course in a place like Kolhapoor all these changes and events could not occur without causing much excitement. Men’s minds were unsettled, and, consequently, some reforms that were introduced excited suspicion and gave rise to discontent and disaffection. At last, in 1844, the latent sparks burst into a flame. The garrisons of some of the hill forts belonging to the State revolted, and a serious insurrection arose, which had to be quelled by British troops.

These events led to important changes in Kolhapoor. Hitherto it had been looked after by the Collector of Belgaum, but now it was found that due supervision could not be exercised by an officer who resided at some distance and had his hands full of other work, and a British officer therefore was appointed Political Superintendent, and since then a Government official has always been at Kolhapoor, who administers the State during the minority of its Princes, and at other times discharges more purely diplomatic functions.

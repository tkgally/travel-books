The writer may be permitted to add that no one knew the Rajah better or mourned for him more sincerely than the officer who had been appointed to act as his governor, and whose special charge he had been for nearly four years. Captain West, as has been mentioned, accompanied his Highness to Europe, was continually with him while there, and watched with the most intense interest the effects of the visit on the Rajah’s mind. What these effects were may be gathered to some extent from the pages of the preceding Diary, but the Rajah was not given enough to reflection and analysis to be enabled to note mental phases and changes. Some things he did note; for instance, he wrote to a friend in India, not long after his arrival in England, that he had learned one thing—what a very insignificant person the Rajah of Kolhapoor was out of his own territory. This statement was made simply and without humiliation, being merely the result of enlarged powers of mental and moral perspective. But the Rajah gained much more than this knowledge.

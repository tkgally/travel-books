So grand and even solemn is it, that one would have thought its own intrinsic merits needed no further recommendation. That there could be any other incentive to its praises never struck me till I came across a sentence in a little French advertising guide to Tunis, published there recently. The Gallic cock must have his crow, and although Tunis is not really added to his poultry-yard, France having, as all the world knows, only a Protectorate there, still, the opportunity is too good to be lost. So he struts up, flaps his wings, and indulges in a prolonged cock-a-doodle-doo. Only hear him! He says, “Terminant l’Avenue de France, une porte monumentale de Tunis antique. Là s’arrêtait jadis la civilization. La France l’ouvrit et y passa le 10 octobre, 1881. Depuis lors, elle est restée debout; mais elle ne ferme plus. Ce n’est donc plus une P<sc>orte</sc>. C’est un A<sc>rc de</sc> T<sc>riomphe</sc>: celui du progrès sur la Barbarie! On l’a baptisée: <i>Porte de France!</i>” How truly French!

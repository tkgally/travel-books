On inquiring how he, an Arab of Northern Africa, could be descended from a line of French kings, he explained that in the Middle Ages a handful of French had conquered and held for some years a small settlement on the coast, under the leadership of the Duc de Beaufort, himself an illegitimate son of the then King of France; and, this son of St. Louis added, with a smile and a French shrug of his shoulders, “<i>Eh bien, madame, c’est arrivé comme cela!</i>” He is very proud of his lineage, went to Paris for the Exhibition, and always speaks of himself as a Frenchman, not an Arab.

Another cloudless day and calm night, and early next morning we reached Bizerta, and were dressed and ready to land by 6.30 a.m., for the ship only stayed here till ten o’clock, and there is much to see. Bizerta is a perfect specimen of an Arab town, absolutely untouched by European influence. The harbour itself contains some French torpedo boats, and is fringed with a few official buildings; but quickly passing by these, we reach the real old walled city, with its flat white roofs and cupolas.

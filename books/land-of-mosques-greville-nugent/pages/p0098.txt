Having therein borne his part in whatever strange adventure—be it the aid of a distracted beauty, the slaughter of a miscreant knight, the witnessing of a document of untold value, or what not—forms the basis of the tale, he is once more blindfolded and led away. His speechless guides place him in the coach which awaits them, and he is borne to his home, bound by strange oaths never to divulge what he has seen that night; and, finally, left alone to ponder and puzzle over the unfathomable occurrences which will haunt him for the rest of his life.

Very much like this did I feel when my “lady guide” made off, leaving me “planted there,” as the French say. I pinched my arm, to see if I was really awake, for my head felt in a whirl; gathered up my garland of orange-buds, and, hearing familiar voices within, summoned up courage to knock and enter.

The Marabout had not returned from Biskra. This fact would not have been surprising in the case of any one <i>but</i> a Marabout, as there were no trains due at that time of night!

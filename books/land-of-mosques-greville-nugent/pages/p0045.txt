# Chapter II.

[[subtitle]] Algiers.

[[subtitle]] The Arab town by day—The ghosts of the Kasbah—The “Pavilion of the Fan”—A maker of guitars—Moorish wares and their vendors—In a Café Maure—A dear old lady—“Eyes and no eyes”—The Jewish quarter—Examples of Moorish architecture—Old Salem the Soudanese—The Amiranté—The “Captives in Barbarie”—An Office for “Apostates to Turcism”—The end of the Algerine pirates.

T<sc>he</sc> Kasbah (or fortress), built in 1516, stands here, as in every Moorish city, on the highest ground, frowning down like a grim sovereign on the houses, which cower, as subjects might, beneath its gaze.

It covers a vast area, intersected by the road now, but once completely enclosed by those huge walls over eight feet in thickness, of which part remain.

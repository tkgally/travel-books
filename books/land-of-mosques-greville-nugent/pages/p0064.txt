The Rue Bruce, in which the cathedral stands, is chiefly peopled by brass-workers, who make the pendant lamps which are such a decorative addition to mosques and houses here. Here also Madame Ben Aben has gathered round her some Moorish embroideresses, who turn out fairy fabrics covered with delicate stitches worked by their henna-tinted fingers; an art which once formed the chief occupation in the harems, but which is now principally kept alive owing to her efforts. Here is also the Archevêché, formerly the palace of the sultan’s daughter (Dar bent-el-Sultan), and close by is the Musée-Bibliothèque, once part of the celebrated Djenina, or Palace of Gardens. Both these buildings are pretty nearly intact, and either will serve to give a general idea of a Moorish dwelling, which, though it may vary in magnificence, seldom varies in plan or style of architecture. The Archevêché has been restored in parts, and the principal apartments fitted up as reception-rooms, chapel, etc., for the Archbishop of Algeria.

The towns on the Algerian littoral are not interesting from an antiquarian point of view. Most of them have been founded by the French since the conquest; or if one sees here and there the crumbling remains of some bombarded Arab Kasbah, it is only by a rare chance. For the most part they consist of modern quays, a row of fishermen’s cottages, and beyond that a grand modern collection of shops, colonnades, and public gardens.

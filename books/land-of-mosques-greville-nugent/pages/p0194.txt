In the very heart of the souks, where four ways meet in a large central square, is the “Souk-el-Birka,” where once, as its name denotes, the ancient slave-market was held, and where human wares were bought and sold busily before the abolition of this horrible trade. The principal mosque, “Es-Zitouna,” supported on a magnificent row of pillars brought from Carthage, runs parallel with the Street of the Perfumers, so that it is easy for the Tunisian merchants to pass from business to devotion when the call to prayer is heard. And what is this oblong barrier rising out of the pavement that cleaves the throng of buyers and sellers in twain? It is a wooden coffin of the sacred green colour, having at one corner an iron socket to hold the candle which is lighted here, as at every sacred shrine, on Fridays and feast-days. For in the earth, below this symbolical coffin, lie the remains of one of the greatest Marabouts of Tunisian history, and the fact that his grave is there is believed to protect this street, and to bring good luck to the merchants in it.

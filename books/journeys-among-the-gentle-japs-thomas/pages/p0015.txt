# Preface

W<sc>hen</sc> Japan emerged from her isolation forty years ago, it is not surprising that so remarkable an event should have called forth a multiplicity of books describing from every possible standpoint the country which had remained so long a sealed empire; but it would have been strange indeed if much that was written of a land with a civilisation so unique, thus thrown open to the rest of the world at one stroke of the ruler’s pen, did not misrepresent the stern reality. For even now, four decades after we forced our acquaintance upon her at the cannon’s mouth, Japan—as no other country in the world—is apt to run away with the sober sense of the one who sets himself to write about her.

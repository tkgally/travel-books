Boscawen, M.P.) rightly remarks in a magazine article[[fnref *]] that, “alone of all Orientals, Japan has learnt not merely to do well under European tutelage, but to dispense with European tutelage. Other Easterns—Indians, Egyptians, Chinese—can fight and manufacture if led and organised by European officers and managers. Japan has learnt to do these things by herself. Her people have not been content to follow blindly and do what they were told, but they have gone deeper and learnt the reason why of our civilisation, and now they are applying their knowledge.”

A visit to a temple or two, a stroll through the great bazaar, which, with its labyrinthine mazes, is one of the favourite haunts of the people, and a tiffin on a balcony overhanging the river Yodogawa, with its countless quaint junks, and sampans, and barges, occupied the rest of the day, and in the evening I returned to my headquarters at Kobe, again in a crowded train.

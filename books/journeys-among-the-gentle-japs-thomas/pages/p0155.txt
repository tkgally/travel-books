Kobe is distant from Yokohama 358 miles, and (as there are as yet no sleeping-cars on Japanese railways) the journey is a fatiguing one, and is seldom performed by tourists without a break. A “stop-over” either at Shizuoka or at Nagoya is the rule. I booked to the latter place, although it was my original intention to go on direct to Kobe. I almost dreaded a night and a day in a Japanese train. Those who had done it and survived had told me that it was about as much as poor humanity could stand. They had travelled first. Though I was going to travel second, I thought I would attempt it, but at the last moment my courage failed me and I booked to Nagoya.

Sitting opposite me in the carriage (for he actually <i>sat,</i> and did not show an irresistible tendency to tuck his feet under him and squat) was a highly intelligent student of Tôkyô, who was bound for Kyôto, and who took no little interest in his English fellow-voyager. As we approached Nagoya on Saturday morning, I did not by any means feel as if I was nearing the limit of physical endurance, and thought I might venture to carry out my original plan.

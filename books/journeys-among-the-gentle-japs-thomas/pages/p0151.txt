It is commonly observed that the country roads of Japan, though well made in the first instance, are not kept in as good a state of repair as they might be. The road connecting Nikkô with Yumoto would be a very good one, and superior to most country roads in England, but for the occasional landslip or subsidence, which the authorities do not seem to think it worth while repairing. Sometimes one comes upon a large hole in the middle of the road, which it is not thought necessary to fill in or even fence in.

I have never heard the comparison made, but I should be inclined to call Lakes Chûzenji and Yumoto the Lochs Katrine and Vennachar of Japan. As I have walked the whole length of the two latter (including Achray), so I skirted nearly the entire length of the two former, and I was struck with a certain resemblance.

Though Ewa and Keo were humble domestics at an inn, I must not omit to give them their proper titles, which would be O Ewa San (“The Hon. Miss Ewa”) and O Keo San (“The Hon. Miss Keo”).

After a stroll through the village, I turned in for the night, but though the bed was as comfortable as any I had in Japan—the usual <i>futon</i> on the floor—precious little sleep did I get, owing to the noise of a stream close by, and the swarm of moths, caterpillars, etc., that shared the bedroom with me. The latter evil I succeeded to some extent in mitigating. Their presence was accounted for by the <i>mado</i> (rain-shutters) having been closed before the light (electric, by the way) was turned off. The consequence was that a number of those pests, which had been attracted by the light while the room was open on two sides, were shut in, and, of course, did their level best to annoy the sleeper.

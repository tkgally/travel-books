Being difficult of access, it is not often visited by the ordinary traveller, but, though the road is rough, narrow and precipitous, to a good walker the journey is not particularly trying. There is the alternative of the <i>kago</i>, but the jolting must make that mode of locomotion along such a road scarcely less fatiguing than walking. Taking an early train to Sumiyoshi—the next station to Sannomiya—and having reduced my <i>impedimenta</i> to the smallest possible bulk—a parcel of a few pounds—I set out for the hills. Before leaving the village of Sumiyoshi, I was relieved of my parcel by an American family who were also bound for Arima, and who kindly offered to bring it along in one of their <i>kagos</i>. Walking ahead alone, I arrived in much less time than the guide-book said was necessary at the summit of the Rokko-san Pass, three thousand feet above the sea, and, looking back to admire the glorious view, saw the <i>kagos</i> about three miles down the slope, winding their way slowly like a caravan. Coolies were met at frequent intervals returning from Arima with their empty <i>kagos</i>.

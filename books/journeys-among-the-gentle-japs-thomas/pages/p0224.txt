How long the party lasted I don’t know, as I left them when my tiffin was announced as ready, and there seemed no signs of its breading up when an hour later, amid a gentle shower of <i>sayonâras,</i> I made my “honourable exit.”

The profession of the <i>geisha</i> (literally, <i>artiste</i>) is not considered altogether respectable, though I observed nothing “forward” about the three at the <i>Wayo-tei.</i> They did not even share in the boisterous hilarity of their young employers, but sang and played and posed as a serious business. But <i>geishas</i> have the reputation of being frail, as well as fair. Many of them are girls with a past, as well as an ambiguous present. In their system of education manners are said to stand higher than morals. Certainly a great importance is attached to a study of the former. At the <i>geisha-ya,</i> they are trained from early youth, not only in the arts of music and dancing, but in all the etiquette of serving and entertaining guests. As to their mental equipment, I have read in a French paper that “les geishas japonaises, sorte de bayadères, sont en même temps les femmes les plus spirituelles et les plus intelligentes.

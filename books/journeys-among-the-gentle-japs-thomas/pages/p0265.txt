The death of Nobunaga was the turning-point in the history of the mission. His great generals, Hideyoshi and Ieyasu, were hostile to the new religion. In 1587 a great persecution broke out against the Christians, which lasted three years, and during which over twenty thousand were put to death. The Jesuits continued to send fresh missionaries into the country, in defiance of the rulers, who declared that, “should the very God of the Christians come, they would behead him.” About that time was instituted the festival of the “Trampling of the Cross.” I have already referred to the custom of trampling upon a cross, and other emblems of the Passion, by way of abjuring the proscribed religion. It is not many years since that festival was abolished. The last persecution of the Christians ended in the tragic incident of Shimabara, near Nagasaki. There, in the castle, the ruins of which still remain, the faithful had assembled from every quarter of the Empire for the purpose of a last and desperate stand against their persecutors.

The dapper little man in European garb, with pronounced Korean features, who was addressed on board as “Your Highness,” proved to be a well-known personality to those among the passengers who knew the Far East, having, though still young, played a great <i>rôle</i> in the government of his country. Unfortunately, his knowledge of English was very limited. A Victoria paper (<i>Daily Colonist</i>), in announcing our arrival at that port, gave the following account of him:—

<blockquote>
“Prince Pak-Yong-Ho, Japan’s firmest friend at the Korean Court, who was forced to fly from Seoul a short time ago, owing to the unexpected turn of the wheel in that much-disturbed city, reached Victoria by the <i>Empress of Japan</i>, and proceeds through to New York by to-day’s express. What his plans are he cannot himself explain in detail—the business of the moment is to find a haven beyond the reach of the indignant Queen,[[fnref *]] and such a shelter he believes America will afford. Troubles have come upon him in clouds during the past eventful year, and perfect rest, undisturbed by dreams of the assassin’s dagger, has now an incomparable charm for the fallen minister. To but one of the many charges of his enemies does he enter a vigorous denial—he was not planning, nor had he ever contemplated, the centralization of the government of the Hermit Kingdom in himself, and so was not guilty of treason to his country and his Queen.

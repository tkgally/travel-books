From Hiroshima I decided to cross over to the Island of Shikoku, of which tourists know very little. Their acquaintance with it is confined to the view which they get of its coast as they pass up or down the Inland Sea. Kyûshû in the south, and even Hokkaidô (Yeso) in the far north, are better known to European travellers than Shikoku. Except to the natives and a few missionaries, it is almost a <i>terra incognita.</i> And yet it is an island with an area equal to that of Wales. But though it is almost entirely virgin ground as far as the ordinary tourist is concerned, it is well worth visiting, and many interesting tours may be made either on foot, by <i>jinrickisha,</i> or on a pack-horse. The time at my disposal only admitted of my seeing a little of the north-western corner of the island, and I regret that I was not able to penetrate further into the interior.

The word <i>Shi-koku</i> means “four countries,” the island being so called from its consisting of four provinces, Awa, Sanuki, Iyo, and Tosa. Iyo is the part to which my visit was confined. In ancient times those provinces had somewhat fanciful names, Iyo being known as “Lovely Princess,” and Tosa as “Brave Youth.” Their modern names are more prosaic, but the charms of Shikoku are probably not fewer now than they were in that distant age.

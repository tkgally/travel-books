We pass numbers of paddy-fields, in which we see natives working in the mud, into which they have sunk so deep that only the upper portion of their bodies is visible. It looks like dirty work, and one might search far and wide to find a more unclean-looking set of husbandmen. We are told, however, that every one of these laborers will cleanse himself in a hot bath when the day’s work is done. If report speaks truly, the Japanese are a nation of bathers. The “daily tub” is a national institution; and that they think a bath must be a hot one, I have already been taught by my <i>boy</i> at the hotel.

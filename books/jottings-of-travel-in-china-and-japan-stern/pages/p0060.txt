[[cont]]we met a crowd of Japanese, who had just alighted from an arriving train, and whose wooden sandals made quite a clatter on the asphalt pavement. Everything in and about the station betokened neatness and careful management. The narrow-gauge tracks (the rails three feet apart), the small cars, and the diminutive-looking engines, suggested a railway in miniature. The coaches are arranged for three classes of passengers. The first, with their soft, cushioned seats, are warm, stuffy and expensive; the second are quite comfortable and are used indiscriminately by foreigners and natives; the third have simple wooden benches; as a rule, these carry only Japanese passengers, and at a very low price.

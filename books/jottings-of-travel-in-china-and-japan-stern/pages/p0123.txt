I propose to send you a letter on Japanese writing paper; that is, if I can manage to keep my pen from going through it. This is of the quality generally used by the natives, except that, because of the pictures, it is especially elegant. The Japanese write with a hair pencil steeped in India ink, and thus the flimsy texture of the paper does not bother them as it now does me.

We arrived here yesterday at daylight, and the intention was to leave at five o'clock in the afternoon. It stormed so severely all day that the Captain thought it would be wiser not to venture outside, and so our departure was delayed until this afternoon at four o'clock. If all goes well, we shall reach Shanghai in a little less than two days.

[[cont]]high noon, as at a sort of exchange. For comfort and completeness, the quarters of the Shanghai Club would do credit to any club in any city. In its large rooms the swinging <i>punkah</i> causes you to forget the torrid heat of the streets, and its excellent library and well-furnished newspaper room have helped me to while away many an otherwise tedious hour.

But I quite forget that you went to the club for tiffin. The meal was served in the large dining-room upstairs and, with the charming company assembled to discuss it, you could not but enjoy the meeting. As it happened, you did not get back to the bank until long after two o’clock; and when you did reach it you learned about another custom of the bankers hereabouts. They will wait on you after three o’clock as cheerfully as before that hour, and rarely get away until four. I suppose this is their way of averaging up the tiffin account.

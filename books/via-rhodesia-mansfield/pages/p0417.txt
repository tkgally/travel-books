It seems impossible to believe that chariots with three horses abreast ever dashed through the narrow streets, or that even a moderate-sized man or woman lived in any of the houses. The only places where it appears at all possible that more than two persons could have breathed at the same time are the stadium and the bath.

Apothecaries’ shops were apparently only the size of pill-boxes, and the only substantial remaining evidences that Pompeians really existed with the same healthy appetites as ourselves are the ovens of the public bakeries.

Everyone, after visiting Pompeii, must wish to possess at least one room decorated in black and scarlet, for the walls still existing in the house of the famous two brothers Vettii display how truly artistic such a scheme of colours may be.

Pompeii is too fascinating; one would wish to stay a long, long time and wander alone without a guide through its many silent streets, a wordless poem in stone.

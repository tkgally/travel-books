The distances by cart are:

George to Wilderness and return, 23 miles.

George to Oudtshoorn, 42 miles.

Oudtshoorn to Prince Albert, 60 miles.

Prince Albert to railway, 28 miles.

Take as little luggage as possible with you, and send the rest as parcels per passenger train to your next stopping-place.

Or, if you can afford the time and the money, hire a good motor-car in Cape Town, and proceed by train to Prince Albert Road, from where you journey in the opposite direction. From George you can easily visit Knysna; then proceed through the Prince Alfred Pass to Avontuur and return over the Long Kloof Road to George, where you travel via Mosselbay, Riversdale and Swellendam to Caledon. The next day you proceed via Houw Hoek and Sir Lowry Pass to Cape Town.

After a journey of twenty-eight hours Kimberley is reached. Here I was met at the station by the Crown Prosecutor, Colonel Tamplin, an amiable giant, and by Captain Tyson, one of the Directors of De Beers. These gentlemen kindly escorted me to the Sanatorium, which was the home of Cecil Rhodes during the siege and has now been renamed the Belgrave Hotel. Here also was staying Mr. Oats, the Chairman of De Beers; the latter gentleman, by the way, spoke to me most severely about the folly of my intended trip, and prophesied extermination or worse at the hands of the natives; but being a fatalist, advice of this nature counted little—one must die, when or how does not matter so much as morbid contemplation of it. However, the advice was offered in a kindly spirit.

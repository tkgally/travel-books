A native capitao, knowing English, has charge of the transport. I did not see him, he having gone to a beer-drink, at least so I was informed by a police-boy. However, the next morning he sent me fresh boys and the following letter:—

<blockquote>
“From the Head Caiptao, Resident,
“Liwonde, Upper Shire District.
[[right]] To the Mrs. Esqre
[[right]] A. L. Store,
[[right]] “Liwonde.
“S<sc>ir</sc>,
“I have the honour that I am sending to you the 16 carriers for your Mchila. Jim and I honour you that when you will reach at Zomba, singe the ulendo rate and return it with them.

It was indeed a faint and weary woman who arrived at Zomba, and very grateful indeed was I for the kind invitation Sir Alfred and Lady Sharpe gave me to rest a few days at Government House before proceeding to Blantyre. Lady Sharpe was a true Samaritan, for she literally clothed me, the boys not having arrived with my baggage, and, after the uncomfortable experiences since leaving the gunboat, Government House really seemed a haven of rest. I stayed there six days, and I am quite certain I should have been very ill had it not been for the great kindness I met with.

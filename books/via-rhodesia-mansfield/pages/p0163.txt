# Chapter XII

[[subtitle]] <i>Livingstone</i>

PROFESSOR BEATTIE left the Falls for Broken Hill a week previous to my departure (I having accepted the kind invitation of Mr. and Mrs. Wallace to spend a few days at Government House, Livingstone), and laughingly he assured me that he would jump all my carriers should they be waiting for me. That week the new train service began whereby it was possible to reach Broken Hill spending only one night in the train instead of two <i>en route</i>. This train did not contain a dining-car, and the picnics which used to ensue may well be imagined. Also, to enable the engine-driver, fireman, and guard to sleep, the train did not travel during the night, and camp-fires used to be lit by the side of the railway line. But the old order changeth, and now the run is made in a much shorter time. I really thought the amiable professor would have a good laugh at me when, immediately after his departure, I heard that the Kafue River had risen to such an extent that no more passenger trains would be allowed to pass through. It seemed I was to be left behind altogether. When I arrived at Livingstone, however, the Administrator kindly said he would see what could be done, for I urged that at the close of my visit I must go over the floods by boat if no other way was possible.

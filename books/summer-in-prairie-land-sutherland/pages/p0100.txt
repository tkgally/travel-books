Mind that boulder, or your foot may get a twist that the doctors will not be able to heal in a month! Look out for that tree, or you may find a case in which the “bark” is worse than the bite! But where’s our pack-horse? R-r-r-rip! Yes, there he is, just caught by a snag, and a long rent made through two stout rubber blankets. Well, we may consider ourselves fortunate in getting through such a road with no greater mishaps. And here we are at Ghost River again, sixteen miles from Morley, and a mountain thunder-storm coming quickly after us. We push on at greater speed, most of the time at a sharp canter, and after repeated fordings halt at a small clump of spruce trees just as the first rain drops begin to fall. Almost before I have time to dismount, McDougall and the chief have their horses unsaddled; in an incredibly short space of time the evergreen shelter on the storm side is thickened by additional boughs, the waggon-sheet thrown over a pole, a fire kindled, and by the time the rain is fairly upon us, we are sitting, figuratively speaking, under our own vine and fig tree, with supper almost ready. By the time we have refreshed the inner and rested the outer man, the storm has passed by.

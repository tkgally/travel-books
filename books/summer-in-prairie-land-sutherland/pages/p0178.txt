But just at this juncture the Indian in charge of the Company’s horses at the “guard” arrived, saying that one of the animals we had purchased on Saturday could not be found. Here was a fresh dilemma. But neither grumbling nor scolding could do any good; so I took Sam with me and started for the “guard.” We found one of the horses all right, but the other was missing. The Indian was getting his breakfast in his tent, but immediately after was ready to resume the search. He started out on horseback, accompanied by Sam; but an hour’s search resulted in nothing, and they returned to the tent. Again the Indian started in a new direction, and in less than an hour came in with the missing horse. So we returned in triumph to camp, got some dinner, and at 2 p. m. were once more on the trail. Early in the day I had been purchasing some supplies at the H. B. Company’s store, when Mrs. McKay, wife of the officer in charge, most kindly presented me with several loaves of delicious home-made bread! If she did not receive in return “the blessing of him that was ready to perish,” she received at least the fervent benediction of those who knew how to appreciate such a gift at such a time.

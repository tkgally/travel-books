The following year Mr. Case took young Henry to the school he had established at Grape Island; he also took him, in company with young Allan Salt, and several other Indian youths, on a tour through parts of the United States, where, by singing at Missionary Meetings, they excited a great deal of interest.

After remaining a year or two at Grape Island, young Steinhauer was sent to Cazenovia Seminary, where he remained three years, making commendable progress in his studies. He then returned to Canada and taught school for two years at the Credit Mission. Subsequently, he spent some time with his mother at Lake Simcoe, and in 1835 went to Upper Canada Academy, which he left the following year to teach school at the Alderville Mission. In the spring of 1840 he left Alderville to accompany the Rev. James Evans to the North-West, but was left by that devoted Missionary at Lac la Pluie, as Interpreter to the Rev. W. Mason, who had been sent to that point by the English Society. Bro. Steinhauer continued in this capacity for several years and then, by Mr. Evans’ instructions, he went on to Norway House, where he served as Teacher and Interpreter till the year 1850.

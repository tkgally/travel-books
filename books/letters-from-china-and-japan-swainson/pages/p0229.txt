## Bowring (L.), C.S.I., Lord Canning's Private Secretary, and for many years Chief Commissioner of Mysore and Coorg.

EASTERN EXPERIENCES. Illustrated with Maps and Diagrams. Demy 8vo. 16<i>s.</i>

The sketches contained in this volume are based upon Notes made during the course of several tours through the provinces of Mysore and Coorg. Information has been added from published official reports and such other sources as could be comprised within moderate limits.

“An admirable and exhaustive geographical, political, and industrial survey.” <i>Athenæum.</i>

“Interesting even to the general reader, but especially so to those who may have a special concern in that portion of our Indian Empire.”—<i>Post.</i>

“This compact and methodical summary of the most authentic information relating to countries whose welfare is intimately connected with our own.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

BRAVE MEN'S FOOTSTEPS. By the Editor of “Men who have Risen.” A Book of Example and Anecdote for Young People. With Four Illustrations by C. Doyle. Third Edition. Crown 8vo. 3<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

The lives have been chosen to represent marked varieties of character and their operation under different forms of effort. Success is here viewed in no narrow or merely commercial sense.

“A readable and instructive volume.”—<i>Examiner.</i>

“The little volume is precisely of the stamp to win the favour of those who, in choosing a gift for a boy, would consult his moral development as well as his temporary pleasure.”—<i>Daily Telegraph.</i>

## Brialmont (Colonel A.)

HASTY INTRENCHMENTS. Translated by Lieut. Charles A. Empson, R.A. With nine Plates. Demy 8vo. 6<i>s.</i>

“A valuable contribution to military literature.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“In seven short chapters it gives plain directions for forming shelter-trenches, with the best method of carrying the necessary tools, and it offers practical illustrations of the use of hasty intrenchments on the field of battle.”—<i>United Service Magazine.</i>

“It supplies that which our own text-books give but imperfectly, viz., hints as to how a position can best be strengthened by means . . . of such extemporised intrenchments and batteries as can be thrown up by infantry in the space of four or five hours . . . deserves to become a standard military work.” <i>Standard.</i>

BRIEFS AND PAPERS. Being Sketches of the Bar and the Press. By Two Idle Apprentices. Crown 8vo. 7<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

“Written with spirit and knowledge, and give some curious glimpses into what the majority will regard as strange and unknown territories.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

“This is one of the best books to while away an hour and cause a generous laugh that we have come across for a long time.”—<i>John Bull.</i>

<i>Oct. 4th.</i>—Arrived at Ningpo at seven <sc>a.m.</sc>, and were very kindly received by Mr W. H. went off to see an old friend of his who had just come from Hangchow, and arrange with him to accompany us over the city in the afternoon—which accordingly he did.

We went first to a very handsome Buddhist temple, the stone pillars of which, said to have come from Foo-Chow, are carved in the most elaborate manner, and the other decorations are also very striking.

It was built by the Foo-Chow people for the Fokkien residents at Ningpo, and, like other Chinese temples, is used as a theatre as well as a place of worship. Along the galleries are boxes for the better classes to occupy, when theatricals take place.

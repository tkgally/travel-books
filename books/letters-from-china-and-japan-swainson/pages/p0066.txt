When I mention food, your mind, I believe, instantly reverts to <i>puppy dogs and bird’s nests</i> as our daily fare, and you will be much disappointed to find that we have seen neither of these but live on commonplace beef, mutton and poultry. My practical housekeeping friends will like to know what we pay for them, and will ask why their lot has not been cast in China when I mention that the former, which is excellent, is only seven cents (3½d.) a lb., but let them not jump too rapidly to their conclusions, but wait till they have their domestic hair set on end by the more than counterbalancing price of mutton, which cannot be had for less than thirty or thirty-two cents, having to be imported alive from Shanghai. Large establishments import their own sheep, and smaller ones sometimes join together for the same purpose, by which they are supplied at a somewhat more reasonable rate, but in this <i>rural</i> district we are very much at the mercy of the Compradors who, in the hot season, do not kill sheep at all, unless they are sure of disposing of the whole animal immediately.

We “tiffined” with Colonel C. who lives in a very quaint abode—an old temple having been turned into a barracks. Such is the practical nature of the Chinese, they never like to waste any space, and Mr M. says he does not know how he will preserve his new church at Hangchow from secular purposes, for it being an agricultural district, the people are sure to think the church a capital place for keeping their ploughs, &c. His position there, cannot, I think, be altogether comfortable, though having his heart in the mission work, he does not seem anxious about his own safety. He acknowledged, however, that the principal security for himself and family, lies in the people not being able to agree among themselves as to the best means of putting them to death, and that if they ever should make up their minds on the subject, escape would be next to impossible.

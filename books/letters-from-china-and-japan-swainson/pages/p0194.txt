Fusi-yama, the great mountain of Japan, has been so obliging as to show the top of his peak to us on various occasions lately—a favour his cloud-capped majesty does not always accord to visitors. It is 13,000 feet high, and its conical peak is constantly covered with snow, except during the months of July and August. The Japanese are so proud of their truly grand-looking mountain, they manage to introduce it, like the sacred stork, on almost all their lacquer ware.

There was a shock of an earthquake two evenings ago, but it must have been very slight, as it did not awake us, though we were only slumbering quietly in our chairs after a long day's expedition.

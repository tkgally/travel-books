## Boguslawski (Captain A. von).

TACTICAL DEDUCTIONS FROM THE WAR OF 1870-71. Translated by Colonel Lumley Graham, late 18th (Royal Irish) Regiment. Second Edition, Revised and Corrected. Demy 8vo. 7<i>s.</i>

Forming a volume of Henry S. King and Co.’s Series of Military Works.

The principal task here undertaken is to describe the peculiarities to be noticed in the late battles, and hence to draw conclusions for the tactics of the present day.

<blockquote>
“We must, without delay, impress brain and forethought into the British Service; and we cannot commence the good work too soon, or better, than by placing the two books (‘The Operations of the German Armies’ and ‘Tactical Deductions’) we have here criticised in every military library, and introducing them as class-books in every tactical school.”—<i>United Service Gazette.</i>
</blockquote>

## Bonwick (James).

THE TASMANIAN LILY. Crown 8vo. With Frontispiece. 5<i>s.</i>

Believing that emigration to Australia, and especially to Tasmania would suit the pocket, tastes, and health of many in England, the writer has sought to picture simply but fairly in this tale the condition of colonial life.

<blockquote>
“An interesting and useful work.”—<i>Hour.</i>
“The characters of the story are capitally conceived, and are full of those touches which give them a natural appearance.”—<i>Public Opinion.</i>
</blockquote>

MIKE HOWE, THE BUSHRANGER OF VAN DIEMEN’S LAND. Crown 8vo. With a frontispiece. 5<i>s.</i>

This story, although a work of fiction, is a narrative of facts as to the leading incidents of the Bushranger’s career. The tale may therefore be regarded as a contribution to Colonial Literature.

<blockquote>
“He illustrates the career of the bushranger half a century ago; and this he does in a highly creditable manner; his delineations of life in the bush are, to say the least, exquisite, and his representations of character are very marked.”—<i>Edinburgh Courant.</i>
</blockquote>

## Boswell (R. B.), M.A., Oxon.

METRICAL TRANSLATIONS FROM THE GREEK AND LATIN POETS, and other Poems. Crown 8vo. 5<i>s.</i>

This book includes translations from Homer, Lucretius, and other Greek and Latin poets, in some cases as nearly as possible in the metre of the original.

<blockquote>
“Most of these translations we can praise as of very high merit. . . . For sweetness and regularity, his verses are pre-eminent.” <i>Literary Churchman.</i>
“Mr. Boswell has a strong poetical vein in his nature, and gives us every promise of success as an original poet.”—<i>Standard.</i>
</blockquote>

## Maurice (C. Edmund).

LIVES OF ENGLISH POPULAR LEADERS. No. 1.—S<sc>tephen</sc> L<sc>angton</sc>. Crown 8vo. 7<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

The object of this work is to bring into prominence men whose place in history has been either ignored or misrepresented. It has often been suggested that history should be written in biographies. Such a method throws greater light on obscure parts of history, and excites a more lively interest in ordinary readers than an ordinary narrative of events.

“Very well and honestly executed.”—<i>John Bull.</i>

“In style it is characterised by the greatest fairness and ability, and the picture of the archbishop is vigorously and firmly drawn.”—<i>Churchman's Shilling Magazine.</i>

“Well worth a careful study.”—<i>Jewish World.</i>

No. 2.—T<sc>yler</sc>, B<sc>all</sc>, and O<sc>ldcastle</sc>. By C. Edmund Maurice. Crown 8vo. 7<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

“The value of this little volume lies in its copious details with regard to the condition of the poorer classes in the Middle Ages, and particularly in the evidence which the author adduces of the close relationship between the dawning Reformation and the popular movements of the time.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

“Mr. Maurice's studies are highly praiseworthy.”—<i>Saturday Review.</i>

## Medley (Lieut.-Col. J. G.), Royal Engineers.

AN AUTUMN TOUR IN THE UNITED STATES AND CANADA. Crown 8vo. 5<i>s.</i>

The concluding chapters of this work deal with the subject of American Engineering in contrast with similar enterprises in India with which the author has been connected.

“Colonel Medley's little volume is a pleasantly-written account of a two months' visit to America.”—<i>Hour.</i>

“May be recommended as manly, sensible, and pleasantly written.”—<i>Globe.</i>

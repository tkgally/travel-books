13<i>th</i>.—Have been for two days at Kobe; one of them we employed in going to Osaka, the sea-port of Kioto, where, at the Imperial Mint, a very handsome new coinage is being prepared, which will soon completely supersede the dirty paper money now in circulation. The small steamer which conveyed us, took about two hours to go to the entrance of the river on which Osaka is situated: its banks on both sides as we approached the town, were lined with junks loading and disembarking cargo.

The streets are much narrower than those of Yedo—much more densely populated, and present a much busier aspect than any other town we have seen in Japan. It is, in fact, the greatest mart for merchandise in the Empire.

Mr S. and I walked, while H. canoed up the pretty creek, till we came to a stone bridge, over which some people and cattle were passing. The architecture of the bridge, and the whole scene, reminded us vividly of that depicted on the old-fashioned willow china which was once so familiar to our youthful eyes.

The long-tailed peasants were much amused by the canoe, which H. took close in-shore that they might examine it thoroughly. They were evidently greatly pleased by his doing so, and one man displayed what I suppose he considered his superior knowledge, by remarking when he saw it going very fast, that it went “all same steamer.”

Next morning we were up at six, and had a charming walk. Mr S. took his gun, but saw little to shoot, except a golden Oriole, which he tried to kill, but failed signally in depriving it of anything but a few of its feathers, which were thus brought back as sole trophies of our morning’s expedition.

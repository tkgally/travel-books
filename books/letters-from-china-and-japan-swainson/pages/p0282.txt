Sully (James).

SENSATION AND INTUITION. Demy 8vo. 10<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i>

“The materials furnished by a quick and lively natural sense are happily ordered by a mind trained in scientific method. This merit is especially conspicuous in those parts of the book where, with abundant ingenuity and no mean success, Mr. Sully endeavours to throw some light of cosmic order into the chaos of æsthetics.”—<i>Saturday Review.</i>

“The writer of such an essay (on Belief) must be ranked as a psychologist of no common order.”—Professor Bain in the <i>Fortnightly Review.</i>

“His remarkable collection of studies in psychology and æsthetics.... Two essays concerned with the æsthetic aspects of human character and its artistic representation display a fine critical tact joined to no common analytical power.”—Professor Croom Robertson in the <i>Examiner.</i>

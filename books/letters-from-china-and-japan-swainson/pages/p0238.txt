## Cornhill Library of Fiction, The.

[[center]] 3<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i> per Volume.

It is intended in this Series to produce books of such merit that readers will care to preserve them on their shelves. They are well printed on good paper, handsomely bound, with a Frontispiece, and are sold at the moderate price of 3<i>s.</i> 6<i>d.</i> each.

HALF-A-DOZEN DAUGHTERS. By J. Masterman.

“A charming little story.”—<i>Saturday Review.</i>

“Unusually bright and attractive.”—<i>Pall Mall Gazette.</i>

THE HOUSE OF RABY. By Mrs. G. Hooper.

“A work of singular truthfulness, originality, and power.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

“Exceedingly well written.”—<i>Examiner.</i>

“A well-told and interesting story.”—<i>Academy.</i>

A FIGHT FOR LIFE. By Moy Thomas.

“An unquestionable success.”—<i>Daily News.</i>

“Of the vigour, the sustained energy, the animation, there cannot be two opinions.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

ROBIN GRAY. By Charles Gibbon.

“Pure in sentiment, well written, and cleverly constructed.”—<i>British Quarterly Review.</i>

“A novel of tender and pathetic interest.”—<i>Globe.</i>

“A pretty tale, prettily told.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

KITTY. By Miss M. Betham-Edwards.

“Lively and clever . . . . There is a certain dash in every description; the dialogue is bright and sparkling.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“Very pleasant and amusing.”—<i>Globe.</i>

HIRELL. By John Saunders.

“A powerful novel . . . . a tale written by a poet.”—<i>Spectator.</i>

“A novel of extraordinary merit.”—<i>Post.</i>

“We have nothing but words of praise to offer for its style and composition.”—<i>Examiner.</i>

ONE OF TWO; or, The Left-Handed Bride. By J. Hain Friswell.

“Told with spirit . . . . the plot is skilfully made.”—<i>Spectator.</i>

“Admirably narrated and intensely interesting.”—<i>Public Opinion.</i>

READY-MONEY MORTIBOY. A Matter-of-Fact Story.

“There is not a dull page in the whole story.”—<i>Standard.</i>

“A very interesting and uncommon story.”—<i>Vanity Fair.</i>

“One of the most remarkable novels which has appeared of late.”—<i>Pall Mall Gazette.</i>

GOD’S PROVIDENCE HOUSE. By Mrs. G. L. Banks.

“Far above the run of common three-volume novels, evincing much literary power in not a few graphic descriptions of manners and local customs”—<i>Spectator.</i>

“Possesses the merit of care, industry, and local knowledge.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“Wonderfully readable. The style is very simple and natural.”—<i>Post.</i>

FOR LACK OF GOLD. By Charles Gibbon.

“A powerfully written, nervous story.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

“A piece of very genuine workmanship.”—<i>British Quarterly Review.</i>

ABEL DRAKE’S WIFE. By John Saunders.

“A striking book, clever, interesting, and original. We have seldom met with a book so thoroughly true to life, so deeply interesting in its detail, and so touching in its simple pathos.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

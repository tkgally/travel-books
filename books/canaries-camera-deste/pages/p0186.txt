# Chapter XII

AFTER three restful days at Guimar, we set out on our journey of exploration along the coast, our intention being to ride to Vilaflor—a village within two days' march—and from there to cross the <i>cordillera</i> again at a fresh point on our way back to Orotava.

We had arranged to keep our men and mules for the whole trip, as they were better than any to be had at Guimar, and on March the 15th we sent them on to Escobonál, where we joined them by carriage, thus saving ourselves a dull and unnecessary ride of two hours along a road. An hour beyond Escobonál we reached the village of Fasnia, where the road comes abruptly to an end, and as we took our way over the rocky desert towards the coast, we felt that we had now parted company with civilisation and its roads and telegraphs and black-coated waiters, and our spirits rose at the thought of our independence and of the unknown that lay before us.

A large portion of the meagre population of this district is troglodytic in habit, living in cave-dwellings scooped out of the sandstone rock; and you may see a whole row of house doors underneath a wheat-field, with a stratum of rock, eighteen inches deep, dividing the lintel from the waving crop overhead.

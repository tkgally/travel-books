The action of sun, air, and of the sea waves that break over the wall of the enclosure gradually disintegrates the clothing and the framework of the dead, and the residue is periodically burnt as the stock becomes too large.

The Towers of Silence of the Parsee are decent and solemn by comparison; yet so great is the power of the Church that knowing the fate in store for the bodies of their loved ones, mourners still commit their dead to a brief resting-place within the cemetery, fearing lest all should not be well with those who slumbered in unconsecrated ground.

On the 21st of May we went on to Firgas, a village lying up in the hills—our last stage on the way back to the Monte. Two comparatively quiet days at Guia had refreshed us, and the mere fact of having been able to talk to our host in English, instead of cutting our conversation to fit our Spanish, had been more restful than perhaps we were aware at the time. A very limited Spanish vocabulary is sufficient to support life upon, even in quite out-of-the-way villages; but that delightful fluency of expression of which I used to feel capable in moments when I was not called upon to speak, would unaccountably vanish as soon as I really had to do so.

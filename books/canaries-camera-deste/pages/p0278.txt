There is something awful in looking up and seeing the zigzag path that has been blasted in the rock winding like a tiny track worn by ants over the face of the precipice; the whole of the path is paved with slabs of rock, and the turns of the zigzags are so abrupt that the leading mule is often standing on a ledge perpendicularly above his companion pressing close upon his heels.

When halfway up we overtook a countryman leading a cow; all the islanders are extremely scrupulous about saluting one with “<i>buenos dias</i>” or “<i>buenas tardes</i>” according to the time of day, but this man went farther and pressed us to have some wine from the diminutive barrel slung from his shoulder; his surprise and disappointment were great when we regretted that we did not drink wine. “Is it possible!” he exclaimed.

But he would not be baulked in his hospitable intentions, and diving into the depths of his <i>alforjas</i> (saddle-bags) he produced a packet of most excellent dried figs, which he insisted on sharing with us. To anyone hailing from one of the villages to the north of the Timé, as did this peasant, it must indeed have seemed incomprehensible that we should not drink wine—for water drinkers would be in sore straits there; springs are very scarce in that part of the island, and the population relies almost wholly for its water supply upon the rainfall, which is carefully collected in covered tanks and is full of animalculæ and impurities.

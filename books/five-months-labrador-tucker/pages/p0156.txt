Immediately in the rear extends a wide spreading bog, intersected by natural ditches, in the summer season overspread with yellow aquatic lilies. The town being thus situated, the few straggling dwellings of the fishermen now resident there, backed by the range of mounds, are the first objects perceived by a voyager from the westward, even before entering the harbor.

Arriving on the area of the French city, it is found to be every where spread with a mantle of turf, and without the assistance of a native, it is not easy to discover the foundations even of the public buildings. Two or three casements yet invite inspection, appearing like the mouth of huge ovens, surmounted by great masses of earth and sod. These caverns, originally the safe-guards of powder and other combustible munitions of war, now serve to shelter the flocks of sheep that feed upon the ruins.

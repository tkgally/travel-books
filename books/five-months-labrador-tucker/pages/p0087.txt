After catching fish upon the banks, they are headed and opened by the fishermen, who place them in the hold of the vessel in an uncured,and consequently in some degree in a partially perishing state; and after having obtained a fare, they return with it to the United States, to be cured or dried, and prepared for exportation. Before this can be done or they can be landed, the fish is generally more or less deteriorated, becomes softer, and part of it makes an inferior quality of fish, called Jamaica fish. The proportion of this is much greater than it would be, were the fish dried and cured shortly after being taken, as is the case with the Coast and Bay fisheries.

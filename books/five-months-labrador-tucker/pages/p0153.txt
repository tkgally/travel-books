Here the rugged and perpendicular rocks, proceeding from the north side of the entrance, are succeeded by irregular hills, interspersed with groves of fir trees, and clothed with a few huts on the steep declivities next the harbor, conferring on this retired and sheltered arm a picturesque, but rather gloomy air. The surrounding land is evidently poor, in every part of Louisbourg, and the country seats and gardens of the French colonists, must have been created at great labor and expense. The country rises with a pretty quick ascent towards the interior; and a mile or two from the water, the quality of the soil improves greatly, affording timber and vegetation altogether different from the productions of the shores.

Upon a neck of land on the south side of the harbor, stood the ancient fortress and town of Louisbourg.

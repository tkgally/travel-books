The mass of stone and brick that composed the buildings of Louisbourg, and which is now swept so completely from its site, has been carried away to build up other places.

The remains of the different batteries on the island and round the harbor, are still shown by the inhabitants, as well as of the wharves, stockade, and sunken ships of war. On gaining the walls above the town, they are found to consist of a range of earthern fortifications, with projecting angles, and extending as already mentioned from the harbor to the sea, interrupted at intervals by large pits, said to have been produced by the efforts of the captors to blow up the walls. From these heights the glacis slopes away to the edge of the bog outside, forming a beautiful level walk, though now only enjoyed by the sheep, being, like the walls, carpeted by short turf.

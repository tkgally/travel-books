This Sudan under the fanatic Mahdi and Khalifa was decimated and laid desolate. Even to-day the traveller is constantly horrified by meeting poor wretches whose hands and feet were hewn off during that awful period, and they were left to live or die as the case might be.

To-day here at Gordon College one sees scores of bright-eyed, clean-looking boys, all interested in their work, and through them will come the hope and salvation of the land. We saw there the Mahdi’s grandson and the son of the Khalifa, a child picked up on the battle-field at Omdurman, a bright, pleasing fellow. On the whole they seemed a vastly more superior lot of students than the same number in any <i>black school</i> in America.

Personally, I do not consider that any good is done our black population by raising them above the working classes. They are inferior minded and will so remain. There may be a few exceptions, but the mass are only fitted for servants and are good for little when raised above that rank.

The old Arab city lay asleep in the moonlight; to my right the ancient mosque of Sultan Hassan cast sharp shadows athwart the square, while under its portals some white turbaned figures were bowing their adulations towards Mecca. In front twisted the lanes of the city, bounded by long blank walls, with here and there a jalousied casement breaking the monotony. What does not go on back of those walls? I fancy one would never again be heard of, having entered there, and would promptly drop backwards through the flying centuries to the fifteenth. We are in the twentieth out here, but it would be the fifteenth just inside there, at least you would never come out to tell the tale, and so I do not enter, but urge the donkey onward as I glance upward to where the mosque of Mohammed Ali gleams white in the moonlight, its delicate minarets piercing far upward into the blue dome of heaven.

But I have little time to contemplate all that.

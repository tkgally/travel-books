Passing onward around the outer walls we pause for inspection at several of the mosques before entering the city. The most interesting because of its beautiful tiling, of which there is a large quantity both in the mosque proper and in the adjoining arcades, is that of the Barber Abdallah.

This mosque, the Djemaät es Sehebi, is considered the most important building of Kärawân. It is not generally believed that Abdallah was really the barber of the prophet, but rather his companion; the legend arose from his having been buried with three hairs from the prophet's beard, one under his tongue, one on his right arm, and the third next his heart.

One passes through several courts, all decorated in beautiful blue tiling, with roofs and arcades supported by delicate marble columns, before entering the mosque, where sleeps the saint. His catafalque bears a black velvet pall, heavily embroidered in gold and surrounded by a high gilded railing. Our feet sink into beautiful rugs and the light filters softly downward through small windows of coloured glass. Small lamps of silver and ostrich eggs hang around everywhere.

He hastened home and saw his dog standing over the cradle covered with blood. Then he was wild with rage and shot at the dog with the magic bow; and the arrow flew and killed the dog, but pierced his son as well, and he came and saw many dead wolves around the cradle, and realised that his faithful dog had protected the child, and that he had lost his son through disobedience to the laws of the ‘Cunning One.’

The second tale is the Cree version of the Flood, in which the ‘Wanderer’ appears again, this time as Noah. The main outline of the story closely resembles the Biblical account, but it will be observed that the dove has been changed into the beaver to suit the local taste, and also that their habit of dam-building is accounted for.

Once the whole earth was covered with water, but the ‘Wanderer’ was in a big canoe with many kinds of animals; and after he had been on the water for many days without seeing land, he determined to send an animal to dive down to see if the water was still deep, or if the flood were abating; so he sent down a young beaver.

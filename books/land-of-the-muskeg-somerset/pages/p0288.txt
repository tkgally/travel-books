Below the rapid the river ran sluggishly, but here and there large boulders lay just beneath the water, and the stream rippled over them with a quiet splash. That night we camped near the house of a Chinaman, built after the manner of his country, and indeed we saw many of these people upon the river-bank digging for gold. A white man can hardly make a living from the gold in this part of the Frazer, but a Chinaman, who has few wants, lives cheap and makes money. As we passed these industrious people our boatman yelled out choice and entertaining insults both in English and in their own tongue. All the next day we paddled

It appears that they gambled long before the advent of the whites, and certainly to this day men will lose everything, to the very clothes on their backs, in a single game.

On the evening of the third day after our return from the mountains the rain stopped, and we determined to move on the following day. The only drawback to moving was Pollen’s sprained leg, which threatened at first to be a serious affair. But three days’ rest had done a good deal, and the time had given him considerable skill in bandaging it. When the time came to move, he found he was perfectly able to do his share of the packing, though compelled to ride on the march. The horses gave us much trouble, being very fresh after their long rest, so that it was late before we were ready to start. We said good-bye to the Chinooks, wishing them a successful hunt, and receiving many expressions of goodwill in return.

The country was fairly open, and we had gone quite a long distance, when, five hours after leaving camp, we came to a dense forest of small pines.

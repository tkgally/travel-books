I think that the real reason of this is that in each place the animals are called by different names. Thus in Wyoming the grizzly is known as silver-tip, and so on. In the north, at all events, there are only three kinds of bear. In the far north there is the polar bear. Next comes the grizzly bear, which does not differ from the grizzly of California or the silver-tip of Wyoming. Then there is the common black bear, which is sometimes brown. Daukhan told me that he had absolute proof of this, as he had found a black bear with a black and a brown cub, just as the silver and cross foxes, and indeed red foxes, are all found together in the same litter. It has been suggested that there is sometimes a cross between the grizzly and the black bear; but Daukhan said he thought that this was out of the question, as the grizzly persecutes the smaller animal. He declared that this was the reason why the black bear keeps so much to the wood, as it is able to escape from its enemy by climbing. He also said that more than once he had come upon the scene of one of the encounters, and had found the black bear literally torn to pieces, so that its skin was not worth dressing or curing; and in such cases the black bear was not necessarily a male.

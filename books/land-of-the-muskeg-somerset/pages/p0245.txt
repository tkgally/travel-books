I think we should have camped had it not been for Daukhan, who declared that he would push on, even if he went by himself. Accordingly, we moved on in the darkness, and luckily we had not far to go, for we soon found a muskeg. The rain was still falling fast, and our hands were so numb that we could hardly undo the ropes. The supper was prepared, but by this time the party were in too low a state to care much for their food. Nevertheless, the wolfish rush which was always made for the pan in which the portions were laid out was one of the most disgusting features of this wretched time. Drenching wet, and faint with weakness, we huddled into the tent and tried to rest.

<i>September 23rd.</i>—Torrents of rain were falling when we awoke, so we decided to remain in camp for a short time on the chance of a change in the weather. The tattered map was again produced, and again and again we scanned its familiar face, in the attempt to find out how far we might be from the Parsnip. We had that map by heart, and knew every line and every curve upon it; yet we always crowded round it as though we imagined that the position of the fort might have changed in the night.

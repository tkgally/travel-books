# Chapter V
[[subtitle]] On the Track of a Grizzly

D<sc>aukhan</sc>, for all his hardiness and strength, was utterly broken down from the continued damp, and had fallen a victim to excruciating pains of rheumatism. This was a pretty serious matter—for without him we should be hopelessly lost in this trackless wilderness. However, the Doctor did the best he could for him, dosing him with salicylate of soda, and finally building up a plaster of Paris support round his waist and back, which delighted Daukhan beyond measure, and I believe he kept the plaster in its place for weeks after he had entirely recovered.

As the country became worse, our progress had grown daily slower, and now that Daukhan was <i>hors de combat</i> we cast about for some remedy or help. About thirty miles from where we were there was a large sheet of water, known as Moberley's Lake.

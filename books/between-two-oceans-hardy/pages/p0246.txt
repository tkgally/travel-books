Mid-April, and not a leaf upon a tree. Not a flower or a bird seems to flourish here. No spring-blossom scents the keen fresh life-giving air; no warbler soars high up into the stainless sapphire sky. The leafless cottonwood trees stand out white in the flood of sunlight like trees of silver, their delicate bare branches forming a shining tangle of silvery network against that intense blue background.

The general aspect of Colorado Springs suggests a collection of wooden Noah's-Arks scattered and dotted about promiscuously. The population at a hasty survey seems to consist of blacksmiths, doctors, invalids, coach and stablemen. Small hotels and boarding-houses abound. If you want to hire a buggy, to have your horse shod, to consult a doctor, or to make arrangements for room and board, you can supply each and all of these wants at almost every corner.

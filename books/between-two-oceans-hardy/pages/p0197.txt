Thus the ‘Palace Hotel’ ‘day’ is Monday; the Mission Street day, Thursday, and so on: a great economy of time and trouble for the visitor, but scarcely, I should think, so agreeable for the resident Mrs. Jones, who would like to attend Mrs. Smith’s ‘Thursdays,’ and, living in the same street, must, if she would support her claim to be a ‘society-lady,’ be at home to ‘receive’ herself on the neighbourhood’s day. The term ‘society-lady’ is often heard there, and gives one the idea that society is regarded by its votaries in the light of a profession, and that they enter into it as they would into the Law or the Church.

The superficial lawlessness and social freedom, not to say liberty, of manner, which we had been taught by some Eastern friends to expect in San Francisco, we did not find. Except that at dinner-parties the gentlemen left the dining-room with the ladies—which we thought an improvement—society was very much the same there as in London.

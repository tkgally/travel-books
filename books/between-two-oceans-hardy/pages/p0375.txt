Often the forest closes over our heads and shuts out the sunlight; often the lower branches sweep the deck, causing a disturbance among the passengers. Generally we see the impending collision with some overhanging tree in time to retreat from the bulwarks; but sometimes we are taken by surprise, and a big branch crashes along the deck with furious force as we graze past the bank. Then and there is a cry of warning, a chorus of little shrieks, and a rush of the gentlemen to thrust back the boughs or drag the ladies out of the way. They tell of one lady who was dangerously hurt by a violent blow from ‘just such a branch as this.’

Once a splendid bough, laden with a scented snow of blossoms, strikes a young man’s umbrella out of his hand and his hat off his head, and sends him and his campstool rolling along the deck in a shower of torn white petals and stripped leaves. More than once I spring aside only just in time to escape a swooping tree that threatened not only my hat but my head.

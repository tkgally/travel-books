So our merry party breaks up, as the Rimouski tender swings away from the big ship’s side, and the <i>Sicilian</i> steams on her way to Quebec. We part with mutually waving hands and handkerchiefs, with cheers and good wishes, and—is there <i>one</i> tear shed? Good-bye to our theorist, his hobby forgotten, and only his kind and genial ways remembered! Good-bye to our artist and his sketch-book! Good-bye to our would-be Irving! Good-bye to the young man with the banjo and blue ribbon, whose lamb-like voice and meek face, with its expression of perpetual surprise, we shall never laugh at more! We shall never meet all together again on earth, that happy and harmonious little company, amongst whom even the <i>ennui</i> of sea-sickness sowed no division. I think that reflection was the only one that cast a shadow over the gaiety of our last day and night on board.

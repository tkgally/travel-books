‘Where is the hotel?’ we inquire of our attentive negro porter, having looked along the car in vain for any signs of it. He leads us through a door into a pantry a little larger than a doll’s house, where in the neatest of cupboards are symmetrically stowed away shining ranks of plate and glass, and snowy folds of linen. Beyond this we catch a glimpse of an ebon-visaged, white-aproned cook by a doll-stove in a doll-kitchen. This is all; and this is quite enough, as we discover when the dinner we order at mid-day comes to be served. A little table is set up in our section, a little cloth laid, and a little dinner that should satisfy the most exacting <i>gourmet</i> is spread before us by the willing hands of Sambo, who shows his white teeth in an ear-to-ear grin of satisfaction when we express our approbation. The same process is repeated in the evening, when our <i>petit souper</i> (of oysters stewed, oysters fried, oysters broiled, and washed down by sparkling draughts of amber lager) makes its appearance—and disappearance.

Arrived at San Quentin, we change to the little train of ‘North Pacific Coast Line’ narrow-gauge cars, which we find awaiting our boat. It seems a very trim, clean, toy-looking little train after the great ‘palace cars’ of the overland journey. We pass through the lovely village of San Rafael, one of the favourite resorts of San Franciscans needing a change from the city. It is an idyllic-looking cluster of pretty, white-and-cream-coloured wooden villas and nosegays of gardens nestling low among the hills. Beyond this we run into a perfectly beautiful and ever-changing landscape of wood and water, dark pine-forests, swelling green hills, and sunny valleys. In making its way along the shore and through the hills, our line plays the strangest tricks. It coils in and out, and round and round, disappears round corners, vanishes in tunnels, has a game of hide-and-seek with the hills, and comes unexpectedly to light again on a level.

It was a moonless night, and in the dusk we could only obscurely trace the vast, vague outline of the two great falls, divided by the blurred mass of shapeless shadows which we were told was Goat Island. As we looked upon them silently, and listened to the ceaseless boom like distant thunder, which shook the ground beneath our feet, across the snowy veil of the American Fall, to our left, shot rays of rosy light that melted into amber and shifted into emerald. They were illuminating the great waters with coloured calcium lights from the opposite shore. I wonder whose first brilliant thought it was to dress Niagara up in the attire of Columbine in the transformation-scene of a pantomime? It was like putting a tinsel crown and tarlatan skirts on the Venus of Milo!

But those green and crimson rays that played across the American Fall, and were turned on and off like a dissolving view, did not reach to the Horseshoe Fall away on our right. Vast, solemn, shadowy, we could just distinguish its form in the darkness, could hear the deep murmur of its awful voice. And there, between it and us, what was <i>that</i> we saw?

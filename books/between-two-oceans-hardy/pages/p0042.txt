All creature comforts we find admirably administered at our hotel, which, judging from the external aspect of things, we had scarcely ventured to expect. The veriest gourmet could not have been otherwise than satisfied with the delicate trout, the broiled salmon, the strawberries and cream, the dewberry pies—worthy of their delicious name—the corn-rolls, the sun-kissed peaches, the brimming bumpers of iced-milk—to which, on a hot day, no vintage of the richest province of France is comparable. Coming off the Atlantic voyage, where the only delicacies you are likely to crave for in vain are fruit, tea, coffee, and milk, (not that you cannot procure them one and all, but that they always seem to lack their rightful freshness and flavour on board ship), I must own that we enjoyed the Quebec breakfasts with a keen relish, and rejoiced that at our table sat neither æsthete nor theorist!

Of course we did our tourists’ duties in Quebec. We went over the citadel and over the Ursuline Convent; we went, in fact, to most of the places where our guide-book told us we ought to go.

We dash over trestle-works that look too fragile to support our weight, and, being in America, are of course so narrow that our cars overlap the track. We run along Tomales Bay—nay, run over it, for half the time the trestle-works are over the water. Presently we get into the Redwoods, which, at a distant, cursory first glance, present very much the aspect of pines. We have at this stage of the journey the car almost entirely to ourselves, and the conductor comes up to chat with us, and do the honours of the Redwoods to the strange ladies who have come from the city on purpose to see them. Getting interested in our society, he sits down on the stove, which causes me so much anxiety that I lose the thread of his conversation, as I have seen him but a short time before shovel an abundant supply of coal into the said stove, and I am apprehensive of the consequences.

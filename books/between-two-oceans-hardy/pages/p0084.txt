The Pullman sleeping-car by day does not strike us as being very different from the ordinary passenger-car or ‘day-coach.’ The beds are all put out of sight; and except that the seats are a trifle more roomy, and there is a little more paint and gilding about it, it seems very like the ordinary car. A closer inspection reveals a looking-glass, hat-pegs (and on some lines a little lamp), in each section; then the stove is shut up in a cupboard out of sight, and there is a ladies’ dressing-room (which is scarcely big enough to turn round in, by the way). The car is less crowded, there being seldom more than two people in each section of four seats, the atmosphere consequently purer; and there is a negro porter to minister to our wants.

There is a pitch of luxury even beyond the sleeping and drawing-room cars, the ‘Hotel Cars,’ which run on many of the principal lines.

In a lady’s dressing-room, I see amongst other ornaments on an elegant, lace-draped toilette-table, between a satin pin-cushion and a case of cut-glass bottles of perfume, a dainty little weapon.

‘What a dear little toy pistol!’ I exclaim, taking it up.

‘It’s rather a deadly toy,’ the lady observes; ‘don’t play with it too carelessly—it’s loaded. Why, my dear,’ she adds, smiling at my look of dismayed astonishment, and the haste with which I remove my eye from the barrel, down which I was squinting, ‘what would be the use of it if it wasn’t loaded?’

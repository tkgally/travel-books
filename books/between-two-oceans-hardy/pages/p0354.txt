[[cont]]Planting his brown arms akimbo, he immediately sets off into a wild jig, something between a reel and a hornpipe, stamping his wee, bare, brown feet, and finishing with a truly astonishing pirouette on one leg. Then he looks up at us with a grin, exhibiting two shining rows of ivory that seem to reach from ear to ear. We applaud, whereupon he announces, ‘I can sing, too!’ and breaks out anew into a dance, accompanied by a plaintive howl, in which a tune is dimly distinguishable, although the words are not. We reward his exertions by a small coin.

Two or three days afterwards, I was looking out of the window of another street-car, when I saw the little negro sitting on the sandy sidewalk. I did not recognise him, but he recognised me, hailed me with a shout and a burst of what he evidently intended for song, jumped up to his feet, and I left him dancing his wild jig under an oleander-tree.

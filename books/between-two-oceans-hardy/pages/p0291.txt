Good-bye, Uncle Henry! Peace be with you, old relic of an age that is being fast forgotten! survivor from days that are but history to us!

Next, we visited the studio of the celebrated Virginia sculptor, Mr. E. V. Valentine, and there spent a delightful hour or two inspecting the creations of his hand and brain, being especially struck by the wonderful expression in such heads as those of the ‘Penitent Thief,’ the ‘Nation’s Ward’—a saucy, rollicking little negro—and ‘Knowledge is Power,’ a sleepy little darkey with his book dropping from his hand. We had the pleasure and privilege also of seeing his latest, and then not completely finished, masterpiece, the Homeric group of ‘Andromache and her Son,’ and his recumbent figure of General Lee, the original plaster cast for the marble statue now upon the General’s tomb in Lexington. The commander-in-chief lies in his uniform, one hand resting on his sword, the other across his breast, in utter repose and peace, as if in sleep, not death. One old Confederate soldier objected to the recumbent attitude on sentimental grounds.

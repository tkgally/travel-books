Comparisons of long distances were evidently hopeless, so, confining my enquiries to the part of the route on which I required information, I then asked: “Ese Consacunti saliendo luna asi tiene, Carapaná llegando luna donde será tiene?” <i>That Consacunti leaving, moon so has got, Carapaná arriving moon where will be has got?</i> The reply to this was a wave of the arm westward, taking in at least forty-five of the heavenly arc, by which I was to understand the position of the moon at sundown; so, pointing my arm steadily with more precision where I thought might be meant, I said: “No será asi tiene?” <i>Will not be so has got?</i> And received for reply “<i>No!</i>” which, however, it must not be imagined meant “<i>no!</i>” but “<i>yes,</i>” being a negation of my negative question.

I could not arrive at any useful information in this manner, so at last I had to examine the two men separately, making them tell me each spot or bank they would halt at every night successively on both the upward and downward journeys; then, putting the two accounts together, I arrived at the conclusion that if I found no special objection afterwards, I should fix Orejones, Salado Grande and Incuisilla as the three stations before Consacunti. Fortunately this determination turned out well, and the distances proved equal enough for my purpose.

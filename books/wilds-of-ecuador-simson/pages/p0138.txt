[[cont]]top. The view down the precipice from such a frail summit on to the devastation of broken trees and earth all shattered below, was imposing in the highest degree.

At the N.E. base of the mountain we continued walking for about an hour down the pretty little stream Huascayacu, and then through level woods to the Cosano river, where we stayed a night, soaked through and through by the heavy rain that had been falling and continued till next morning.

As had so often been the case, the day’s work was commenced by crossing the river on whose banks we had passed the night, so that from the outset we started thoroughly saturated by fording the deep water caused by the heavy rains.

Two hours’ sharp walking brought us to the Arajuno, which flows into the Napo just below Aguano. The stream was too deep and broad to cross, the water for some distance being up to a man’s shoulders, so two Indians swam across to fetch a canoe from Aguano for us; and whilst we were awaiting their return four of the others decamped. This, however, was now of little consequence, as a canoe was at hand. In it we crossed the river and transferred the baggage immediately afterwards.

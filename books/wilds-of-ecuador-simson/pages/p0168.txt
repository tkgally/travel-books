[[cont]]Cotapino. The Concepcion men are generally finer—taller and more massive—than the others.

The dress of the Indian usually consists of short, close-fitting drawers, and sometimes a “<i>poncho</i>” of “<i>lienzo,</i>” the common raw cotton cloth made by the Indians of the highlands.

The women use a piece of the same cloth wrapped round their loins and reaching to the knee, and a jacket or shirt barely long enough to join the lower garment at the waist. The cloth is generally dyed by themselves, a dark dull claret colour, but also occasionally, with the wild indigo, blue. Ornaments of feathers, seeds, beads, teeth, &c., are worn, sometimes in profusion, especially on festive occasions; and for attending “church” some of the men have a pair of trousers. Bracelets and armlets of iguana skin are much affected, as in some parts of Central America, with the same association of their imparting bravery and pugnacity to the wearer.

For warfare, these Indians keep no arms; but for hunting they use “<i>bodoquera</i>” and lance; and though timid towards man show no fear of animals. The making of the “<i>bodoquera,</i>” or blow-gun, is a work of great patience and merit. A good piece of “<i>chonta</i>” palm, the hardest palm found in the country, is selected, and a groove cut into it with a knife, to a length varying from seven to nine feet. This groove is then scraped and rubbed with sand, finer and finer, until it assumes perfect smoothness. Another half tube, exactly similar, is next made; and the two are bound together with wax

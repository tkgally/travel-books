We told them that they must keep a good look-out for the “Aucas” all night, as we meant to sleep, and to wake us at once should anything occur.

The next day we passed inside the large Auhishiri Island and by the mouth of the river of the same name, the spot most feared on the Napo. In the evening we slept again on the right bank, nearly opposite Orituyacu, where there is a lake and settlement of Piojés; but still we saw nothing whatever of the Auhishiris.

Hence, leaving Tarapoto, already spoken of, on our left, we reached, in a tremendous thunderstorm, the village of Curarai, situated just below the disembouchure of the river bearing the same name, and with whose source we had become so intimately acquainted a few months previously. This little village is occupied by a few Indians from the Upper Napo—principally Santa Rosas, who have deserted their native places—and one white man, a Mr. Rodas, a Cuencano, occupied in trading with the Piojés and Záparos, at present collecting sarsaparilla with the horde of the latter who usually inhabit Sinchichicta.

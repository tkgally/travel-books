# Chapter VII.

[[subtitle]] The Jívaros.—From the Pintuc to Canelos.

T<sc>he</sc> tribe or family of the Jívaros is a large one, and one of the most distinguished, independent and warlike in South America. They alike withstood the attacks of the Incas and the Spaniards, as already mentioned in a previous chapter, burning the once-famed cities of Logroño and Mendoza, and massacring all the male inhabitants who did not succeed in making their escape; and even to this day they maintain the most thorough independence. There are missions at Macas and Gualaquiza, but they have as yet produced no practical results, nor obtained any authority over the Indians. A Jesuit <i>padre</i>, who had resided three years at Gualaquiza amongst the tribe, informed me that he had found it impossible to make any progress with them; and more than once his life was personally threatened. During those three years twenty-nine whites (<i>i.e.</i> half-caste traders) were killed by the Indians in the neighbourhood of Gualaquiza.

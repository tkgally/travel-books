[[cont]]A settlement called Cayaposa, a little higher up than the river Masan, on the opposite bank, is generally looked upon as the frontier, and the Peruvians appoint an official there; but the same individual, and others, have had at least the offer of the same official post from the Colombians. On the right bank, the little river Masan is recognised by Ecuador as its frontier with Peru; but the latter republic has pretensions, ostensibly based on some old treaty, to the Napo “as far as it is navigable.” On the Pastassa and the neighbouring northern affluents of the Marañon, the Peruvians have pushed forward on every possible occasion, and at one time there was a Peruvian “<i>cura,</i>” nominated by a Peruvian bishop, as far up as Andoas. This elicited some protests from Ecuador; but the apathy and ignorance of the Government with respect to its province “del Oriente” is so complete, that it can cause no surprise if encroachments gain upon their territory from year to year, and the rich and gold-teeming province becomes more and more lost to them.[[fnref *]]

The general outline of Ecuador is cuneiform, with a seaward base, off which the south-western corner has been cut, and placed below the wedge (whose apex faces east) a little farther to the eastward.

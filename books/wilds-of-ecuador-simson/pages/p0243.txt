We were therefore obliged to keep very carefully in the deepest channel, which was easy enough so long as the reaches followed regular curves, first one way and then the other, as many of them did. In some of the straight reaches, however, where the entire bed of the river spread out to an immense width, and no specially deep channel existed, navigation was most intricate and difficult.

With great care, and often losing a long time in searching for the deepest passage among the numerous sandbanks, we managed to get along without serious mishap. Some turtles were caught, and a number of their eggs, which formed a welcome addition to our commissariat. After anchoring at night, we also usually fished with some success.

On the evening of the third day from Carapaná we anchored in deep water near a sandbank, where we found a man called Firmin and a number of people, amongst whom the Indians informed us was a Piojé Indian who had formerly been working with Avelino, the man who had his leg bitten off by an alligator, and a boy who had been with Mr. Christopher Columbus, also before mentioned, and who was said to have assisted in his murder, a story, however, which I saw no reason to believe, though the boy was at Carapaná when the murder took place.

Thus short-lived was incipient civilisation and progress in the territory of the Pastassa and Paute. Logroño and Mendoza rose no more; and Macas, having set aside its more pretentious appellation of Sevilla de Oro, continued to exist, as it does to this day, an insignificant and almost inaccessible village of petty half-caste traders.

The Napo region was less unfortunate, or at least declined only considerably later on, and in a less terrible and sudden manner. A number of settlements were established on the main stream, and on the minor tributaries between the Napo and the course of the Coca. The natives, being of a milder and more timid disposition than the Jívaros, were not a constant, threatening danger to the colonists, whose community was further considerably swelled by the fugitives from Mendoza and Sevilla de Oro.

Before a long time had elapsed, missions were established under the Jesuits, who made Archidona their headquarters; and this period seems to have been the most peaceful and prosperous the province experienced, save only for incursions of the Portuguese from the Marañon to carry off Indians as slaves.

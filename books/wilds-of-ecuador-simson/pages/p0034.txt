# Chapter II.

[[subtitle]] Riobamba to Baños.

D<sc>uring</sc> our stay at Riobamba we started one day with a Señor Mosquera, on horses he kindly provided, for San Luis and Punin, villages about two hours’ ride from Riobamba. At Punin, through the goodness of the chief authority, we procured six Indians, much against their will, to accompany us to a ravine close by, where, the river having cut through a great depth of volcanic tuff and scoriæ, the bones of mastodons, an extinct horse, two species of deer, and other animals exist in great quantity, exposed to view in the sides of the cutting. The quantity of bones deposited within a comparatively small space is so large that they must probably have been washed together by some extensive flood, connected with the eruption of one of the neighbouring volcanoes, which have supplied the ejecta in which the remains are imbedded. The gorge is from 300 to 350 feet deep, worn through a homogeneous mass of scoriaceous tufa.

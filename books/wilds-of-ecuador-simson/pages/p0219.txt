After passing Copal-urcu (Copal mountain), Tambur-yacu (Drum river), both said to be inhabited by dangerous savages, and Puca-barranca (Red bluff), we came to Lagarto Cocha, which we entered. As its name implies, this <i>cocha</i> contains a great many alligators of the same species as frequent the Amazons, and quite distinct from the species infesting the Ecuadorian rivers west of the Andes. The former is a much more beautiful animal, its back jet-black, though when old frequently overgrown with a slimy green water-weed, and its entire underside yellowish white. Between the two contrasting colours, the connection is made in large mottles, either colour extending patches into the surface of the other, which gives the coat of mail a beautiful marble-like aspect at the sides. Besides the difference of colour, the structure of the Amazonian alligator is unlike that of the Western Ecuador species, especially in the formation of the head and jaws. The main feature of the <i>jacaré</i>, as it is called in Brazil, is that the upper jaw entirely overlaps the under jaw; whereas in the other the under jaw exceeds the upper; the snout is altogether narrower, and two of the front teeth pass completely through holes in the upper jaw, exposing their points above, besides other considerable differences in the comparative size and arrangement of some of the other teeth.

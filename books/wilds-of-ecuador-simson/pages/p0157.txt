We at once set out in our two canoes and met them on their landing-place, which they had no doubt chosen to reconnoitre the village from at night. Some of the men we compelled to accompany us to the <i>padre</i>, who censured them for having abandoned their village and him, and informed them that the next day three of them should accompany him to Santa Rosa and Loreto, where he feared one of his brother missionaries might also be abandoned and in want, without any company whatever. Another Indian was sent off to find the “<i>curaca</i>” and bring him to us, as he alone would be able to exercise sufficient authority to ensure the collection of more men; so the next morning the <i>padre’s</i> seat at our table was to our great regret left vacant.

At the end of five days’ patient waiting we were much pleased to welcome him back, as during the interval the time had passed much duller than usual without his pleasant company. Two of the three Indians were now sent again in search of the <i>curaca</i>, the previous emissary not having returned; but to ensure their reappearance on this occasion, one young man, a son-in-law of the <i>curaca</i>, was retained as hostage and chained to a post in our hut, as we were determined not to be left in the lurch again.

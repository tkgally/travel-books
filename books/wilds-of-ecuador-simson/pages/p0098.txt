Here I first heard the song of the “<i>flautero</i>” (flute-bird), among birds the mellowest and most beautiful songster that I remember having heard. His song is not quite the same in all individuals, but may be likened in tone to the most mellow, sweet-sounding flute; and the musical correctness of all his notes is astonishing and makes one imagine the sound to be produced by human agency. On one occasion, after this, I was utterly deceived, being out in the woods. I felt certain of being far from all my companions and other human beings, when I suddenly stopped, hearing, as I thought, someone with a knowledge of music whistling charmingly and clearly quite close to me. He is a very insignificant-looking, little, greyish-coloured bird; and, I was informed, always dies in captivity. He dances and performs strange antics before the female during his song.

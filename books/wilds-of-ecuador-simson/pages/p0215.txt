It was a type of peacefulness and solitude, with a powerful overhanging sense of gloom. The water was dark and sombre, though reflecting the vertical rays of a bright sun; its surface was utterly motionless, though continually disturbed by innumerable fishes; its depth looked black and impervious, though in reality clear and transparent as crystal; the vegetation was lit up by the sunlight, yet heavy, and casting gloomy, weird shadows on the marginal water; and although a deathlike stillness seemed to reign, the discordant chatter of the crows in the trees and the dull splash of the terrapins as they dropped from their seat on the dead logs into the water, were heard at frequent intervals. All these apparently anomalous sensations will, I think, be understood by those who have visited any of the numerous, still, mysterious, black-water lagunes or <i>igarapês</i> of the Upper Amazons.

After thoroughly enjoying this scene, being the first of these <i>cochas</i> we had entered, and unconsciously almost fearing to speak aloud for fear of disturbing the repose, we made our exit, and reached the encampment again towards evening, to find all in order, and our friends, the Santa Rosas, just arriving <i>en masse</i> to pass the night on the <i>praya</i> with us, for the safety of themselves and families against the much-feared infidels.

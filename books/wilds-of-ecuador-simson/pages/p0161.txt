I had often from previous knowledge of, and personal friendship with Jesuits, admired the self-sacrifice of their lives; but never was this so markedly realised as now, when I saw the <i>padre</i> busying himself among these miserable sufferers, attending to their physical requirements, administering remedies, cheering and giving them advice, whilst listening, with his face close to theirs—now distorted by the repellent disease—to their inquiries and confessions, and trying to satisfy their moral cravings by pointing out to them the road to that eternal salvation in which he had laboured to lead them to believe. Let this example show that nobody should lightly condemn any religion, or religious sect; for in all of them may be found similar true charity, which from its silent nature can but seldom come to the knowledge of the world.

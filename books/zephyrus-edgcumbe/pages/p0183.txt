For goods traffic there are all the vexations connected with the custom-houses, for not a bale of goods can be shipped for transit by river, even from one town to another in the same province, without as much investigation as if it were going to a foreign country. When duties are levied upon exports as well as upon imports, transit by river involves trouble both when shipping and when landing the cargo.

The portion of the Midland Railway which is about to be made between Salto and Paysandù will be about eighty-five miles in length. The country is precisely similar to that through which the North Western of Uruguay Railway runs, consisting of fenced-in cattle farms on undulating grassy plains, in part with deep rich soil, but mostly with a friable rock just below the surface.

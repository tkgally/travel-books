Thus it was that, on our coming to Salto, the first sight we saw was a long cavalcade of soldiers and national guards in charge of a large number of the most scrubby, miserable, dejected-looking wretches one ever would wish to see. Following the cavalcade came a train of waggons containing the wounded, who uttered groans as the great lumbering springless vans, each drawn by eight or ten horses, jolted horribly over the rough road. The streets were thronged, but dead silence reigned, for the sympathies of the people were with the beaten revolutionists.

It is very fortunate that the revolution failed so completely, for it is scarcely possible to estimate the evil effects of such disturbances upon the people of the country in the districts affected.

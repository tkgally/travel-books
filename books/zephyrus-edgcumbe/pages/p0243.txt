A cow or sheep is worth, in England, as many pounds as in the River Plate it would be worth dollars; so that, for purposes of comparison, a duty of a dollar in the River Plate is equivalent to a duty of one pound in England. There is, moreover, a tax of 6 per cent. upon all wool, sheepskins, hides, tallow, bones, horns, and meat exported. These taxes add something considerable to the cost of production, though, no doubt, they do not equal in amount that which the English farmer is called upon in the aggregate to pay.

One thing that struck me with some surprise in connection with the high rate of interest paid in South America, is the low rate of interest earned for their shareholders, by the English banks banking in South America. With such a field for their operations, I can hardly understand how it is they do not pay higher dividends.

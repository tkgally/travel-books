When travelling any distance, especially if time is important, several spare horses are taken, and as one horse tires, he is unsaddled and recovers himself by travelling unburdened. The horses are about fourteen and a half hands high. Their pace—they travel about six miles an hour—is very trying at first; it is an amble, the rider jogging on without rising in the saddle. The seat of the natives is that of the warrior, with long straight leg, riding by balance, not the bent knee of the jockey. It occurred to me that our military authorities might do well to mount a portion of our light cavalry on these horses. Any number of them can be got, which, including transit to England, would not cost more than from 15<i>l.</i> to 20<i>l.</i> each. The regular cavalry of the Banda Oriental, of which we saw a considerable number, were as well mounted a body of troops as one would wish to see.

To a slight extent the hunger of the passengers was satisfied by a meagre supply of biscuits from the liquor bar attached to the train, and by a hatful of raw eggs procured by the barman in a raid he made upon a neighbouring estancia, which he doled out at famine prices. It was past six in the evening before the dejected and hungry passengers reached a station where any food could be obtained.

When we were at Salto we visited Concordia in the province of Entre Rios (between rivers). It is a fair-sized town and of some importance, being the terminus of the East Argentine Railway. This railway (like the North Western of Uruguay) has been built to carry the traffic over the hundred miles of country which the Uruguay river, owing to rapids, does not serve. The East Argentine line was finished in 1876, and has obtained a good deal of the traffic coming from Rio Grande and South Brazil.

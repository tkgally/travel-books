When carrying even a cup of water, they place it upon their heads in preference to carrying it in their hands. The coffee planters have to a large extent met the diminishing supply of slave labour by additional and improved machinery; and they have been so far successful in this, that in the last seven years, although the export of coffee from Rio and Santos has risen from three million to eight million bags annually, I was assured that this increase has been accomplished without employing additional labour.

At one time an effort was made to procure Chinese labour, but the Chinese Government placed difficulties in the way and the project fell through. The need of labour, both more of it and of a better quality, is very visible in the coffee plantations. When once planted they are left too much to nature, and not properly tended. It is, as was remarked to me, ‘all plantation and no cultivation.’

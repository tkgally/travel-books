In the course of our travels I met many persons who were well acquainted with our colonies, and they all assured me that not one of them could be compared with the River Plate as to natural advantages. Their opinions appear to be confirmed by the fact, that I came across several persons who were settled in the Plate and doing well, after having tried their luck without success either in Australia, New Zealand, or the Cape. Only in the spring of this year (1887), I received the following in a letter from New Zealand. ‘Till we go home. When that will be, goodness only knows, with these wretched times for poor farmers with lots of cattle, sheep, and wool to sell, and no one to buy. I hope you eat frozen mutton to give us a helping hand.’ With a fruitful land, and good and cheap labour, it should be very long before bad times come to the River Plate.

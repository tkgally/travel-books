[[cont]]and in fact forming a sort of garden to it, is the cemetery. While we were in the lazaretto a brig came in with a small-pox patient on board. The poor fellow was immediately placed inside a long wooden box a trifle larger than a coffin, and carried by four men very slowly from the pier to the hospital. The ground is so uneven that they could only go at a snail's pace, and under a semi-tropical sun the temperature of the dark box must have been something frightful. Landed at the hospital with the cheerful cemetery as the only prospect from the windows, the patients see no doctors, for they do not visit the patients, for fear of infection, but prescribe by telephone.

We were somewhat fortunate in being allowed to prowl down to the cemetery during the first four days of our incarceration, for the hospital being then empty, they did not mind our going that way. This was a great comfort, for in the immediate vicinity of the lazaretto the smells of putrid and decaying substances made it most unpleasant to loiter there.

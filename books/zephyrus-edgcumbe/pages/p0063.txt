The middle-class Brazilian was in great force at Petropolis. He seems to be a most illiterate person. Book shops are almost unknown, newspapers even are rare, and as poor as they are rare. On two occasions only did I see Brazilians reading books; one was a professor at Pernambuco University, the other was a student preparing for an examination.

The Brazilian father and mother live with their children always about them, and spoil them to the utmost. A Brazilian child is worse than a mosquito on the war path. Brazilian houses have no nurseries, and, as it is considered cruel to put the poor little dears to bed during the day, one has the pleasure of their company without any intermission. One may be coming down stairs in a hurry and find the landing blocked by an admiring group, much too absorbed to let one pass, gathered round some little bellowing parcel dressed like an exhibition doll.

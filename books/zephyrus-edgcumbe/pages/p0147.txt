Horses were pressed in from all quarters, every horse in the country being at the disposal of the Government in time of war. An unfortunate milkman, who, ignorant of what was happening, rode into the town with his milk-cans dangling at his horse’s sides, was summoned to hand over his horse instanter. Instead of submitting, he clapped spurs to its flanks, and made a bolt for it. But a well-mounted orderly gave chase and soon had the milkman by one leg, when without any ado he hoisted him out of his saddle, and, leaving him shaken and sprawling on the ground, grasped his horse by the reins and trotted off with it for active service in the field.

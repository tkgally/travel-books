We visited Maccoll’s ox-tongue preserving establishment at Paysandù, the products of which are so well known on English breakfast tables. I was surprised to find how small a place it was, and then I discovered that of the 500,000 tongues Maccoll supplies to his customers yearly, only a tenth part (50,000) are cured and tinned at Paysandù. This is only what one would expect when one comes to reflect, for where the cattle are slaughtered there must the tongues be preserved. They cannot be carried about in a hot sub-tropical climate before they are tinned, especially as it is in summer that the saladeros work, when the cattle are in prime condition after feeding on the spring herbage.

The Japanese, whom the commodore had brought with him from the newspaper printing office at Hong Kong, requested the officials to walk down to the cabin, and they could not conceal their astonishment at being addressed in their own tongue, by a man to all appearance to them an European, for our interpreter was not very Japanese in look, but rather more like a Manilla-man, or an Indo-Spaniard.

On being seated, one of the officials, speaking in very tolerable English, announced that the great man present was the harbour-master of Hakodadi; and he presented a copy of the port regulations in Dutch and Japanese, and demanded the names, tonnage, flags, and complements of the crews of the three ships.

As each question and reply was made, two of the officials (having produced from the folds of their robes rolls of paper and taken from their girdles the pendant cases resembling spectacle cases, which contain their reed pencils and ink) minutely, and evidently word by word, took them down.

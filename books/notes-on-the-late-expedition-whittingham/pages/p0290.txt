A Chinaman, even if living in a climate prodigally rich in the means of existence, still retains the strong greed of gain; a character quite distinct from that of most eastern nations, and very different from the listless effeminacy of the Cingalese living on the production of their own cocoa-nut trees.

A walk through the regular and narrow streets of the little town, walled in by the old fort, gave us glimpses of the descendants of mixed Portuguese, Dutch, and Cingalee races, who seemed to live contentedly a quiet, unbustling existence, seated in verandahs or open doorways, and smoking as if without occupation. The old fort sustains its character of picturesqueness inside; its green slopes relieve the eye, suffering from the glare of the sun's rays; and its ramparts afford a good promenade after the heat of the day is past.

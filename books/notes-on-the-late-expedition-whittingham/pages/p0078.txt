We returned to the town over the lowest slopes of the hills, meeting every where signs of the fertility of the rich black soil, and we passed close to the tea gardens which are the scenes of so much that is peculiarly dissolute in Japanese manners. The use, or abuse rather, of saki, a spirit made from corn, is very common, and we were witnesses of a dispute between a young, pretty, respectable-looking, and intoxicated woman and her domestics, whether she was to be allowed to lie in a ditch as she wished, or be taken home. A policeman, who for the sake of learning English phrases had clung to us the whole afternoon, took no notice of the occurrence.

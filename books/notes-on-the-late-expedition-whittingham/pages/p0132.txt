The decks were sometimes dried by the dimly-seen sun, whilst shrouds, rigging, and sails were dropping continuously; and at these times the warmth of the sun was cheering and pleasant. On the third day, having lost all sounds from the rest of the squadron, the Sibylle’s bow was turned to the eastern shore; and in a few hours, from the mast-head, the three peculiar pinnacle rocks off Cape La Jonquière were descried above the fog, and were shortly afterwards seen from the deck at a distance of six or seven miles, whilst to seaward the fog prevailed as thick as ever.

We had the little inlet to ourselves, and landed in the evening soon after the Sibylle had anchored. The natives appeared to be less reserved than on our former visit, and showed greater anxiety to procure European clothes, exchanging seal-skins and seal-skin fishing boots for flannel waistcoats or seamen’s frocks.

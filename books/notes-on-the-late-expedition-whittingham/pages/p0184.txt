A well filled book had replaced the scraps of paper used as the record of the shipping in port, and numerous were the inquiries of the whereabouts of the several frigates of the allied squadrons. To the uninitiated, the frequent comings and goings of single ships, or of very weak squadrons, seemed unintelligible, but when we heard that four fifty-gun frigates, and one fine forty, had gone lately on a cruise, hopes again were high that, though unsuccessful ourselves, because never in the right place save once, when we were too weak to attack a larger force in a strong position, so fine a squadron of frigates, with one steamer, must have been directed against the only coast defences still armed against us, and near which the enemy’s squadron were known to be lying; and the evident apprehensions of such a proceeding in the minds of the Russian officers was transparent through the wit with which they veiled them, and though they laughingly inquired “Why so many large frigates?

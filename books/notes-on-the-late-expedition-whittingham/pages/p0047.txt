Two interpreters accompanied the lieutenant-governor, through whom,—speaking to them in low tones, which were listened to in attitudes of humblest respect,—he expressed “his hopes that the commodore and ships were well;” and through his own interpreter the commodore’s part of the following colloquy was played. “Very well,—thank the lieutenant-governor for his politeness.”—“The crews have had a very long voyage.” “Yes.”—“How many days?” “Twenty-one.”—“The lieutenant-governor is glad to see the commodore.” “The commodore is glad to receive the lieutenant-governor.”—“The officers and men may go on shore in small parties, landing at the harbour-master’s wharf.” “We shall avail ourselves of the permission.”—“No tipsy sailors are to go on shore.” “If sailors get tipsy, the police may bring them off.”—“The governor has been lately appointed, and only arrived with all the necessary officers some days ago.

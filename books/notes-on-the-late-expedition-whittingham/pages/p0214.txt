This is very small, narrow, and difficult of access, with reefs at no great distance from its mouth, and has more the appearance of a haunt for pirates, than a commercial port opened to the trade of the western world. Its proximity to Yeddo may have originally led the Americans to accept it as one of the harbours rendered accessible by their treaty and as a stepping-stone to cross over to the Gulf of Yeddo.

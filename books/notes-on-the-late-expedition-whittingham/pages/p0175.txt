There were no letters, no papers, no provisions for us: for the French small squadron some bullocks and other necessaries were brought, as their Commodore had, with national military “prévoyance,” freighted a transport to convey provisions and live stock to Hakodadi, from whence the Encounter carried them; and it will scarcely be credited that the only fresh meat eaten in six months by our squadron was owing to the generous courtesy of the French officers in command.

Light airs and a clear sky were a delightful change for a day or two, off the eastern coast of Sagalien. Just as the ships became once more dry, the fogs returned, with steady southerly winds,—similar to the constant breezes we experienced in the Sea of Japan and Gulf of Tartary; so constant, indeed, as to lead to the conclusion that the Chinese monsoons extend nearly up to lat. 50°.

We make some purchases, wherewith to beguile the tedium of another trip on the river, and then, as the tide is still running up, proceed to the Strand Hotel for tiffin. It is very pleasant there, away from the heat and glare, sitting under the punkahs with a lump of ice tinkling in the long glass by one’s side.

There are still a few belated tourists staying there, waiting for the homeward-bound steamer. There are unmistakeably Americans, and their wide sun-helmets with long gauze veils hanging down behind, their cameras, blue goggles and umbrellas look very serious and business like. All tourists in Rangoon exhibit one strange peculiarity,—they <i>live</i> in their topees. Even at dinner, long after the sun has gone down, they may be seen seated at the tables still wearing them.

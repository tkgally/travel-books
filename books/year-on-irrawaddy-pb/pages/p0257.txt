Christmas Day, however, was an exception. On our entrance into the saloon at breakfast-time, we found it gaily decorated with flags and foliage, and the table was further adorned with three, large, pink-and-white iced cakes, gifts from the crew. Later in the day we were presented with another weird concoction of food, which the Serang informed us with pride that he had made himself. It was served in a vegetable dish, it looked like porridge, and it tasted chiefly of butter, raisins, onions and semolina!

We fully appreciated the spirit that prompted the gifts, but I cannot say that we were grateful for the gifts themselves. They <i>looked</i> very nice, but we could not possibly eat them, and the difficulty was how to dispose of them. Some of the dishes, I must confess, disappeared overboard, when all was still at night.

By a combination of fortunate and fortuitous circumstances, it happened that three ships lay at Nyounghla on Christmas night;—ourselves just arrived, another oil-boat ready to depart at daybreak, and a cargo-boat on its downward journey.

Meanwhile the rest of the crew can spread the report that the Captain knocked him overboard, and caused his death. Then it is a case of one man’s word against another’s. Whoever swears hardest and most convincingly wins the day, but, in any case, it means loss of time and money to the Commander.

One marked peculiarity of the crew is their strange aversion from giving notice. If one of them wants to leave, he either runs away, and so forfeits at least a fortnight’s pay, or, with the help of a confederate, he adopts a subtler plan.

One day a telegram arrives for him, and he comes forward, with tears raining down his cheeks, and hands it to the Skipper. The Skipper opens it and reads:—

[[center]] “<i>Mother ill please come</i>”

He has read scores of similar telegrams before, so he tells the man to “<i>jow!</i>” and get on with his work.

Next day another telegram arrives.

[[center]] ‘<i>Mother dying come immediately</i>’

While the ship was under way, it created more or less of a breeze which, though at times like the blast from a furnace, was still better than the heavy, choking atmosphere that remained when the ship stopped. The nights in the hot weather were horrible. No words can describe them. Even though we had camp-beds put out on deck, the heat under the mosquito-nets was unbearable. The pillow was covered with damp patches wherever one’s head rested on it, and one was continually employed turning it over and over again, in order to find a dry spot. After enduring the heat under the net, almost to strangulation point, I would get up and wander round the ship in search of any little draught or current of air, only to be driven back to shelter again by merciless hordes of mosquitoes.

And so the nights passed. Sometimes it was impossible to rest at all, sometimes one got a little fitful sleep in the early hours, from two to four a. m.

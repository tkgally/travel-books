As we drive up to the entrance the purri-wallah jumps down from his perch to open the door, and the Skipper alights and enters the wide, dark door-way through which one occasionally catches a glimpse of waving punkahs and busy men in shirt sleeves passing to and fro, while unceasingly through the windows comes the liquid <i>Chink, chink, chink</i> of thousands of rupees being poured out of bags on to the floor to be tested and counted by the Burmese clerks.

While the Skipper transacts his business, I wait in the gharry and watch the passers-by. A hideously deformed beggar comes crawling and shuffling along in the dust whining for alms.

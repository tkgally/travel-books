The Skipper snatched the rifle out of my hands and blazed away, but all he did was to plough up the water about two feet behind the creature’s tail, and it continued on its way with unruffled composure. Before we could load again, both snakes were lost in the fog.

(I always tell this story with great pride, because it was the first time I had ever shot at a moving object. And I hit it!

The Skipper prefers to forget the incident; and he says that, anyway, there is only my word to go upon that <i>that</i> was the particular snake I aimed at.)

Towards the end of March the fogs ceased, and the hot weather began in earnest. I believe that it was cooler on the river than in Rangoon, but, even then, the heat was almost unbearable. I do not think that, for twelve weeks, morning noon, or night, I felt even passably, cool. It was one, long spell of discomfort, and the most unpleasant feature of it all was the constant sense of damp stickiness from excessive perspiration.

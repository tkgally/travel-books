Shortly afterwards a third telegram, somewhat in this style is received.

“<i>Mother dead wife dying house burnt down come instantly.</i>”

(Telegrams can be sent for four annas each in Burma). That settles it. The very piling on of the agony makes the Skipper suspect that it is all false, but what can he do? The man has evidently some urgent reason for going, and go he must, however inconvenient it may be to spare him at the time. So, after the advent of the third or fourth telegram, his persistence is at last rewarded, and he gets the leave, for which he has been scheming so long.

He <i>may</i> go home, or he may be met with next day, at work on another steamer, but one thing is certain, he will never, never as long as there is a drop of Chittagonian blood in his body, give the true reason for wishing to go.

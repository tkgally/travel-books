Barred windows project into the street, and now and again a face peers between the bars as from a prison cell. Roses and trumpet creepers hang over the road in profusion. The delicate perfume of flowers is wafted from beyond the walls, and always the birds are singing. Iron gates reveal charming vistas of patios within, of shaded walks and graceful pepper-trees. Here are the summer homes of wealthy Mexicans, while in the village goes on that primitive life all unconcerned. If perchance one falls to musing on the departed grandeur of Aztec and Spaniard, some bit of actual life presents itself which quickly dissipates any musty reflections.

My whim takes me beyond the village into the open country. In a sluggish stream on the borders of a maguey field women are washing clothes, and hanging them on the extended leaves of the maguey to dry.

Still another group of men in fantastic garb enact a sort of comedy, which includes a sword dance and suggests a lot of schoolboys in paper caps playing soldiers. There is an absurd solemnity about the whole performance, considering its grotesqueness, and it is plain these poor Indians are clinging to some rite whose significance they have forgotten. Meanwhile mass is being celebrated within and the little church is full to overflowing.

The village lanes, with their lava walls and flaming tulipans, afford pleasing vistas and entice one away from the churchyard out into the emerald cane-fields. Later in the day the youth with the gilt crown still capers among the maidens in pantalettes and congress gaiters, and the little boys and girls continue their stately rhythmic measure.

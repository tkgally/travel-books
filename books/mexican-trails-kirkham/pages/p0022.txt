In writing of this life I have, I believe, drawn a true picture of the Mexican Indian to-day, who, while he may be the despair of the business man, is none the less the joy of the traveller who surveys him with more leisurely and poetic glance.

From this Vale of Tempe nestling among the mountains of Morelos, my wanderings have taken me to Orizaba and Cordova, a day’s railway journey from Mexico City, to Puebla and Cholula, nearer at hand, to Oaxaca, a day and night’s ride, and to Mitla; again south to Tehuantepec, a full two days’ trip over the Vera Cruz and Pacific and Tehuantepec roads. Later I went westward to Morelia, Patzcuaro and Uruapam, some three hundred miles, and thence on horseback across the Sierra, coming at length to Chapala; westward still to Tuxpan and over the old trail to Colima and the Pacific.

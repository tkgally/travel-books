The corn was up six inches, where a few weeks before they had been ploughing. In the fields the cane was partly cut. Oxen were peacefully browsing by the roadside, the anis feeding within a few inches of their mouths, after the manner of our cow-birds. Presently we left the cane-fields of Atlacomulco and entered the <i>pedragal</i>—a river of lava which, flowing down from the Ajusco, cooled so quickly that the waves and the rapids retained their form as they became stone. This lava bed is as rough as a file and will wear the hoofs off a horse or the shoes off one’s feet. Over it grows an interminable jungle of nopal, maguey, organos, and low shrubs and creepers which have somehow found a foothold. The rock is honey-combed with cavities, many of them containing the bones of animals.

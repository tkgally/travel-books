He sits in the doorway of his shop, there being no windows, industriously plying his needle. When he can no longer see, he lights a solitary candle, and by its flickering ray continues his work. If you should pass his doorway then, you would find him intent upon some antique sombrero with worn silver band, his strong kindly face and his massive head with its shock of grey hair standing out in relief in the darkness of his shop, and you would say, as others have done before, “There is a Rembrandt for you.”

A hat-rack, extending the width of the little room and completely covered with sombreros, forms a fitting background to the picture. They are but dimly made out by the light of the single candle, but should you happen that way in the afternoon, when the sun shines in the doorway, you could run your eye over these ancient hats, dust-covered all of them and embellished with cobwebs as well.

The patio, at once dining-room, living-room, and bar-room, and all the people in it, might have been copied from some old print.

Small tables, with their ollas of water and curious long-necked vases of withered flowers, are attended by lank and unkempt waiters, with countenances like those in the drawings of Cruikshank. Exaggerated and unreal, they yet seem eminently adapted to their surroundings—like fish in an aquarium. Guests and habitués as well have the musty look of old books in an auction-room, and seem as remote from present-day life. Their coats, their hats, their shoes are all of an antiquated design which serves to heighten the pleasing illusion of the old print.

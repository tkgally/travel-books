With the pilgrims, journey those who feed the multitude, and at every halting-place make tortillas for such as have no women folks to cook for them, setting up a brassero and establishing a fonda by the roadside, where may be had frijoles and <i>chile con carne.</i> Naturally a man takes his calling with him if he has any. Does he make <i>guarachas?</i>—there will be customers; or sell dulces?—there are plenty of children, and he can surely turn a penny. Then there are the mixers of drinks, red, yellow, and green, of unknown ingredients and served lukewarm to thirsty pilgrims at a centavo a glass.

Thus is the pilgrimage a sort of caravan as well, bringing no ivory and spice, but buying and selling in its small way—the beginnings of commerce and reciprocity.

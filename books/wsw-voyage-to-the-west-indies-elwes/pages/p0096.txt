The vegetation was luxuriant, the lower part of the dell filled with great plantains, the sides of the hills covered with mango-trees; in fact, there are such numbers of these, that there are not enough pigs to eat the fruit that falls. The best mango in Jamaica is called “No. 11,” that variety being so labelled when it was introduced. The plantain, or banana, thrives everywhere; and if the people had any industry, I should think that it would answer to grow the other variety, <i>Musa textilis,</i> of the stalk of which Manilla hemp is made. The plantain suits a lazy people well. It requires planting but once; for the old stem being cut down after fruiting, numbers of suckers spring up from the roots. The fruit can be eaten raw or cooked; and it will produce more nutritive matter on a given quantity of land, than any other plant. The leaves are useful for a number of purposes; in Tahiti they will make a house of them in a few minutes; but here the blacks have lost the useful arts of savage life, and learnt but few of those of civilisation.

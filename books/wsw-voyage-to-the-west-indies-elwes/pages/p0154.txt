Although green, everything had a blue tinge over it, and it all looked like some gorgeous scene in a ballet, the illusion being heightened by the ladies of the party, who wore white muslin dresses and broad hats. We sketched, gathered ferns, and amused ourselves in different ways, and then returned to where the carriages were left, where the servants had prepared an excellent luncheon, with ice, beer, and every sort of luxury, and spread it under the shade of some tall feathery bamboos. Here we gathered oranges from the trees close by, and gave the owners 6d. for about fifty, with which they seemed quite satisfied. Then the Governor, Mrs. E., and the ladies who had riding-horses, rode up to the north post, from which was a fine view of the seacoast, outside the Bocas. I and the rest went to a sugar-mill, and stayed there till the party returned, when we went home thoroughly delighted with our day's excursion. We dined at “The Cottage,” and having before ordered a hack carriage to come for us to take us home, we started for the town soon after ten o'clock. The black driver, who had a friend on the box, went through the garden, and passed the sentry at a foot's pace, but when he got into the road, he still went on at the same slow rate.

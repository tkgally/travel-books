The town has good streets and shops; but as all passengers have to go there, and often to wait some days, it is particularly disliked by every one. It is, moreover, often visited by yellow fever, which may arise from the number of foreign shipping congregated there, and from the great heat of the climate, for, as it faces the south, when there is no wind it becomes like an oven. The sickness probably is increased by the stagnant state of the harbour, which is nearly landlocked; and the rise of the tide is so small (not being more than eighteen inches or two feet), that the water does not get often changed. Lately they have cut a small channel from the inner corner of the harbour to an adjacent bay, and that gives a little more current, and it is said that the health of the place is already improved.

The black population speak English (as does, indeed, everyone else), and are most amusing; the women dressing in the very height of fashion. The island produces nothing, the people being able to earn more about the streets and in the harbour, than they could obtain from field labour; formerly it was cultivated, and used to send home a certain quantity of sugar.

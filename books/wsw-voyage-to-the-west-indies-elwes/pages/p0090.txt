She went on: “You are going to England; may I go with you?” then we began to see what she wanted, and found that she wished to go with us, as a servant, to England, without wages or anything. That we could not agree to, as it was rather too far to risk carrying a servant of that sort, who would most likely be quite useless, or more probably a perfect nuisance when she was there; so we left her and drove off. It was market-day, and the street was crowded with negroes, and from one we bought twenty-five oranges for twopence; they were excellent oranges, very sweet and juicy, but with thick, rough skins. I at first refused them, choosing the smooth-looking ones, but a policeman, who was keeping order, showed me which were the best.

We went slowly through the Bog Walk again, I stopping to sketch, Mrs. E. to collect ferns.—The passers by all warned her against the insects. “Missus, you go into the bush you get plenty of wood-ticks,” and she found out afterwards to her cost that they were right. As we drove towards Spanish Town, a woman stopped us to sell star-apples; we bought some, and certainly they were a delicious fruit to eat in the heat of the day.

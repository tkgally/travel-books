Now the Russians have made a road from Tehran to Resht, and when we left Persia in 1900 we drove all the way to the sea; but until then one had to ride, and to cross a high pass which was under deep snow for months every year. On this pass, the Kharzan, in 1897, we were caught by a sudden snowstorm. Luckily we were close to a village, Mezreh, and able to get shelter, which consisted of a tiny hut just big enough to hold our two camp beds. My maid shared another hut with some hill women. The storm went on steadily for five days and nights, and there we remained until it abated. To go on would have been certain death, and no one would have followed us.

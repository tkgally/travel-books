[[cont]]end. The walls were smoke-blackened mud, but some wet wood was beginning to light, and one was at least on dry ground and out of the bitter wind. The cold I think prevented the smaller inhabitants from troubling, and after a time the fire blazed up, and the servants brought us some dinner. And then we had our camp beds made ready, and slept the sleep of tired people. The owners of the room came and peeped at us through the doorway, over which we had hung a rug, but they were happy in the sum they got for the accommodation they gave us, and were not uncivil. The Hizhabr ul Mulk had found another hut. Mr. Rennie bravely returned to the tents.

We were quite loth to leave our warm quarters next morning, when we stepped out on the frozen ground and remounted our horses.

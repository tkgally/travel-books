And this I found was right in practice as well as reasonable in theory.

The little upper deck was crowded by our people and those of the Hizhabr ul Mulk, who had one of the cabins, and the deck below was even more crowded with servants and native passengers and stacks of wood, for the <i>Shushan</i> burns no coal. But it was very comfortable and pleasant, especially after weeks of rough travelling on horseback.

All the afternoon we sat in cane chairs and read, as if we were on an ocean steamer, and then we had dinner, which was cooked on a stove somewhere among the wood below, and Captain Norton told us stories of his three years’ command of the <i>Shushan</i>, stories full of fun and cheeriness and common sense.

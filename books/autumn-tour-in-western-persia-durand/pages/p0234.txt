It is often little more than a big blanket stretched over a rough ridge pole and fastened down on both sides to a wall of piled stones. In fine weather, on the march, the Lurs often have nothing over their heads, but content themselves with putting up a circle of reed matting, about four feet high, which gives a family sufficient privacy.

These Lurs are wilder than their Bakhtiari kinsmen. They have no single chief or ruling family, and are very independent. The Persian Governor of Khurramabad told us they numbered in all 70,000 families, or from 350,000 to 400,000 souls.

After leaving Rajan we rode on up a very cold pass, and suddenly we saw beneath us the Silakhor valley, at the end of which lies Burujird. I have seen few things finer than the view at this point.

The water was lying in pools among the mud, and there we searched about for a comparatively dry place and pitched our tiny tents. Some of our horses and servants were awaiting us.

My husband and Mr. Rennie had to go off to shoot—“Must have some exercise before dinner”—so they climbed upon pack mules and disappeared into the sunset, and soon I heard some shots. Mr. Rennie came back with his first black partridge or francolin, a really lovely bird, much too beautiful to shoot, and I think they got some snipe. Next morning, after a quiet night, we said goodbye to Captain Norton and started for Shuster.

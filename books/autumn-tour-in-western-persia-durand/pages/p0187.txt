That night I remember Mr. Andrews, Messrs. Lynch’s agent, dined with us, as he had done each night we spent in Dizful. Poor man, he had been sent to Dizful to replace a Persian agent who had died, and was still there, after several months. It struck me as very sad. He was living quite alone, his main occupation the weighing and despatch of sesamum seed, with a cellar forty feet under ground to retire to when the heat grew unbearable. He had poor food, and nothing to drink but tea made from the muddy water of the Diz, and very little to read. Yet he was cheerful and brave, and laughed at his discomforts, and insisted upon presenting us with a box of fresh Egyptian cigarettes out of a batch which had just reached him.

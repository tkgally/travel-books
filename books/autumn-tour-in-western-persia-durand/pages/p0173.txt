I did not go into the town again. It was really too horribly dirty, and moreover the Shuster people, who seem to be largely “sayyads,” are not fond of strangers. They seem to have been much more friendly fifty years ago, according to the accounts of Englishmen who were then in Persia.

The next morning we started for Dizful, two marches distant, the last town before we re-entered the mountains, and we had a most unpleasant day. We were to camp at a place called Kovnak, about twenty miles off, and were told the road was very good.

We marched out of Shuster along the old river bed, under pebble conglomerate cliffs, and then got upon a plain with the mountains to our right; and it began to rain.

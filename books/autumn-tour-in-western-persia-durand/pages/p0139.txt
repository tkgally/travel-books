Since our crossing the work has been finished, and in the heart of this wild mountainous country an English merchant, helped by an English working engineer, Mr. Perryman, has succeeded in putting up a fine strong bridge of iron with material all brought from England. It is to be hoped that the energy and skill shown by these two Englishmen and their Hindustani workmen—for they had to import rivetters and stone workers from India—will result in the opening up of a satisfactory trade route.

Mr. Perryman has now, I believe, built another bridge, at the point described in my last chapter, Pul i Amarat.

The Indian workmen seemed much surprised and pleased at my being able to talk to them in their own language, and asked me at once to give them some quinine. We gave them plenty of this and some sheep for a feast, which seemed to make them quite happy.

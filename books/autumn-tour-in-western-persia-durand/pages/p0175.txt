This place, though rather imposing as one approaches it from the south, is another horribly dirty town, with another muddy river and another long bridge, which, strange to say, was not “kharáb shud.” It had been, but some one had repaired it, and though the parapets were down in places and the cobble pavement was in a chaotic condition, we were able to ride over it. I remember a small boy running in front of us and then suddenly jumping over, through a gap in the parapet. He must have fallen fully fifty feet before touching the water, but he came running up to us again before we were over the bridge. Our camp, as at Shuster, was at the other side of the river on open ground away from the town, and in comparatively sanitary surroundings. The Governor of Dizful has a fine garden house close to where our camp was pitched.

The Mullahs have great influence in Dizful. The town is full of small mosques, and, as at Kum, no wine is allowed.

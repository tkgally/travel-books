We camped at Kala Reza, where the road debouches into a fine broad valley, bounded on the left by the Kuh-i-Kebír, the “great mountain,” which our Derekwand chief said “went to Baghdad.” It really divides Luristan from the Pusht i Kuh, another wild mountain province of Persia. It showed us a bold wave-like slope, ending in precipices, like many of the Lur hills. One side of these hills is often a gentle rocky slope—perhaps dotted with oaks, perhaps quite bare. When you reach the top of the slope you find at your feet a sheer drop, sometimes of several hundred feet.

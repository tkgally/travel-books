The organization of the Khánehs had taken some time, but we had not given the first many hours start, and it was useless to go fast. It was, I remember, a lovely day, with a cloudless blue sky, as the Persian sky generally is near Tehran, and the ground being flat and free from stones the short march was very pleasant. We were still in the centre of civilization, for the English Bank in Tehran has made a road through this country, and a telegraph line under English management runs along it. The Hizhabr ul Mulk, a nice cheery man of thirty-five or so, with a great fondness for horses and sport, was in high spirits at getting out of town and into open ground again. He seemed as happy as a schoolboy, and I remember his beginning the day by galloping his horse past two doves sitting on the telegraph wire and firing at them as he passed. This shooting at a gallop is a favourite form of sport with the Persians. The doves flew away, I was glad to see; but it looked very pretty, and the Hizhabr ul Mulk only laughed.

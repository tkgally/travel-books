Hizhabr ul Mulk, by the way, was a northerner from the frontier province of Azarbaijan, and a sturdy Túrk, like his men. He spoke Persian to us, but his native language was Túrki.

He had, I remember, a charming expression for some of the birds we saw. He used to say to Mr. Rennie, “Why do you shoot that? That is a ‘chíz i bi maní,’” a thing without meaning. The Persians generally, I think, regard snipe as a “chíz i bi maní.” It is classed as a “gunjishk” or sparrow, and not worth powder and shot. But the snipe shot by the members of the Legation, especially the big “double snipe,” nearly as large as a woodcock, used to be a great help to me in Tehran, where game is not plentiful.

There are a few quail to be got there, “bilderchín” the Persians call them, but they are very few. There is also a delicious little bird called a “tihu,” a very small partridge, the Si Si of India, I think. There are also hill partridges and hares, the latter of which Persians do not eat.

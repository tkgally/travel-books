The next morning we got up early, while the nearly full moon was high in the western sky, and the dew was on the grass, and the sky of the east was red with the coming sunrise. We had a long march, over grassy undulating ground and in and out of deep ravines. I remember that we had our breakfasts by the side of a stream in the Tiktik or Chikchik valley. Above us, across the stream, was a clay cliff, perhaps a hundred feet high, with a broken waterfall coming down from it, and at the top a line of reeds, waving with the sunlight behind them, and maidenhair fern among the waterdrip.

We reached our camp in the evening, after a pleasant ride of six or seven hours. Behind us all day was a curious flat topped hill called Kala Kazi, or Kala Kasim, very long and very high.

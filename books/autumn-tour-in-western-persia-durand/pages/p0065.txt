After Kashan we struck across the hills we had been skirting. Most of the first day’s march was barren and bare—a hot stony glacis; but at a place called Kuhrúd, about 7,000 feet above the sea, we came upon a beautiful valley, full of fruit trees and walnuts and poplars and planes. They were just touched with autumn colouring. There is a considerable export of fruit and potatoes from this place to Ispahan, and even, I was told, to Tehran. With its green crops and trees the valley looked very fresh and inviting after the stony plain about Kashan. Kuhrúd is a populous place, containing about 650 houses in its two connected villages. So at least the Kedkhuda or village headman told us. He also told us that for six months in the year the valley was under snow.

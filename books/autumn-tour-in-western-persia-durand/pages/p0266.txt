[[cont]]had marched a little over twelve hundred miles, or at the rate of fifteen miles a day from beginning to end. This does not sound much, but over rough ground it is hard work for the animals; and I am bound to say that I was heartily glad to be in a clean and comfortable house again, after being so long “kháneh ba dúsh,” with our house on our back, as they say in the East. Above all it was a great relief to be within reach of telegrams, and to know daily something about the progress of the war. The want of news during the time of our absence had been our only real hardship.

I wish I could think the story of our march would be as pleasant to read as it is pleasant to remember.

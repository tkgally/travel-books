They were all away when we visited Susa, and their treasures were locked up. There was nothing to see but the fort and the deep straight cuttings in the mound, and one stray square of marble, perhaps a bit of the old marble floor of Ahasuerus, which was lying in the courtyard of the fort.

Wandering down among the cuttings we saw curious pieces of pottery sticking in the earthen walls. There were earthenware chimneys, or what looked like chimneys, possibly grain stores, and cups and odds and ends. Of course we touched nothing, for the French have been given the exclusive right of “fouillage,” as I think they call it, throughout the Persian dominions.

The merchants have difficulties too, and I was told that owing to bad harvests and the troubles consequent on the late Shah’s death, trade had been bad of late years; but the English merchants do not seem to be at all afraid of foreign competition, which is satisfactory.

Ispahan, the old capital, is a sad place to see. It is a city of ruins, ruined palaces with crumbling walls and broken carvings and faded frescos, ruined mosques with bold beautiful tile-work clinging in patches to the crumbling domes, ruined bridges spanning the broad bed of the nearly dry river, ruined gardens and avenues and squares, ruins, ruins, ruins, everywhere ruins. Alas for the beauty and splendour that has been!

Some day perhaps they will be again, for the country might be a paradise.

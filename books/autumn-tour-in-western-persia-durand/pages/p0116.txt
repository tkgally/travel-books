The planes or chinárs of Persia are very fine. The Legation gardens at Tehran and Gulhek, our summer headquarters, had great avenues of them. With their white or particoloured bark and clear cut leaf and tiny hanging globes, they always seemed to me one of the most beautiful of trees. But the chinárs of Persia are not, I am told, so large as those of Kashmir, nor do they take the same crimson tint in autumn.

As we marched away next morning it was raining a little, but the narrow oak-clad valley looked very green and picturesque, and we had our first sight of the real Bakhtiari highland villages.

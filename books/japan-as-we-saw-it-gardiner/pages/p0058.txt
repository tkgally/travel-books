All Japanese floor mats are about three inches thick and three feet wide by six feet long—the interior being of successive layers of plaited straws, with the upper surface of ornate woven matting, the commoner qualities of which Europeans and Americans are familiar with. This uniformity in size results in the floor areas of houses being constructed upon the basis of “mat measurements.” For example, referring to the size of a room, <i>we</i> would describe its dimensions as twelve feet square, while the Japanese would term it an “eight mat room.” As the “tabi” (<i>tah-bee</i>) or cloth stockings are the only footgear coming in contact with the mats, their surface remains clean and unbroken, while the uniform size admits of their being changed from place to place in a room, or to other rooms, as fancy dictates.

# Enoshima, Kamakura, and Yokosuka.

ENOSHIMA, Kamakura, and Yokosuka (<i>Yoh-koh-ss-kah</i>) may easily be visited in two days, and should under no circumstances be omitted.

Leaving Yokohama from the Imperial Government Railway station (fifteen minutes by jinrikisha from the hotel) by train at 6.50 <sc>a.m.</sc>, Fujisawa (<i>Foo-je-sah-wah</i>), twenty-two miles (fare sixty-six sen), is reached at 7.35, where jinrikishas will be found near the station. Thence by a very interesting and for the most part a shaded road, several villages are traversed, until at about three miles distance the hamlet of Katase (<i>Kah-tah-say</i>) is reached. Here we alight and walk across a half mile sand beach,—said to have risen from the sea by the action of an earthquake in 1216,—cross an arm of the sea on a bridge, the frame of which is made of bamboo, and enter the one street of the village of Enoshima.

[[image: sketch of the wooded island of Enoshima seen across the water, lettered “Enoshima in the distance”]]

Curios, seashells, beads, rope-sponges, corals, sweetmeats, etc., are exposed for sale in the shops, but passing these and the two tea-houses of the town, we ascend the hill of Enoshima by the stone steps running to the left. Two temples on the summit are interesting from the outside, but the particular attractions are the large cave on the far side of the island, said to be three hundred and seventy feet deep, and the magnificent ocean view had from the little tea-house on the extreme overhanging point. Thence back, winding down the hill by the path and steps ending opposite where the ascent was begun.

Just before reaching this point, however, a native schoolhouse on the left of the path attracts our attention. Stepping carefully over the dozens of little wooden ashida the scholars have left outside the door, we enter the schoolroom, and receive a pleasant salutation from the master. Rapping on his desk, he speaks a few words to the children, who rise from their seats, and bowing their heads say, in unison, “Ohayo” (<i>Oh-hah-yoh</i>) (Good morning).

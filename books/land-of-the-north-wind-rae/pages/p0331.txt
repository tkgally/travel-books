This hyperborean Fabius had mentally evolved some further idea, and hauled off, as a sailor would say, once more. He could get no boat to go to Dolga Schelje.

I could not help respecting the man’s unbounded truthlessness. Come with us, I urged, to Rusânova: we will go by to-night’s tide to Dolga Schelje, buy a boat, engage men, pay them twice what they ever earned before: you shall have twice what they receive, a handsome present in money when we reach Archangel, and the boat itself into the bargain. He ran to his house, we waited five minutes, the evening was coming on, the tide was already streaming up the river: I saw he was playing fast and loose, and followed Mr. Illyn into his boat in despair. I am ashamed of these people, he said to me: I will take you by to-night’s tide in my own boat to Dolga Schelje, and we shall find honester people there. I bethought me of the good decent men who pulled us down the estuary of the Kûloi on our descent to the sea, and rose like a trout at the proposal.

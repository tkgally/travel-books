# Chapter XVI.

[[subtitle]] Negotiations for Journey to the Interior—Reindeer Sledges—A Purchase and characteristic Incident—We start for the Tûndras—Our sledging Procession—Description of Sledges, &c.—Articles of Samoyede Dress—Ladies’ Attire—Mièsna—Beautiful Wigwam—Cleanliness and Courtesy of the Samoyedes—Tent Life—Adroit Hunters—We photograph and mystify a Group of Samoyedes—A Samoyede Deity and Place of Worship—Idolatry—Kânin Nôs as a Refuge—Wolf Traps—Samoyede Dogs—Sledging over the Tûndras—Unaccustomed Mode of Travel—The Tûndras—Swamps and Wilderness—Surprising Qualities of Reindeer and Sledges—Difficult Conditions of Travel and keen Enjoyment—Reflections on the Reindeer—The only Land for Summer Sledging—Travelling Eastward to Koroléva—Village of Tjoûma—The Smallest Church in the World—A Samoyede Grave—The Self-eaters—Reflections upon Self-eating and Sentimental Prejudices against it.

W<sc>e</sc> went on shore, dragged this time over the mud on a sledge by reindeer, and treated with the Samoyedes. We found there were but few Samoyedes at Torna, whither we had thought of going, and after protracted talking we arranged that we should be taken on sledges by reindeer to Tjoûma, a Samoyede village fifteen versts away: and thence to Koroléva, thirty versts farther as the crow flies, over the tûndras. Thence to Schoina by sledge, and down the Schoina river by canoe to the mouth, whither our boat was to go to meet us.

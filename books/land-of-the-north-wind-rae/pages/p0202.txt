We impassively absorbed the gold pieces, watched him tear his horse out of the tcheléga, and then quietly arranged with the little Jew that we should drive with him to Kulogôry, and engage a boat there.

We had got another tcheléga and were driving out of the yard, when the baffled station-master, beside himself at the thought of having in his folly thrown away so rich a chance, bounced into the air, shrieked aloud, and ran yelling to the gate, which he locked. A crowd had collected, taking the station-master’s part, and execrating and threatening us: while we stood with our hands in our pockets calmly regarding them. They are afraid, said the crowd, but the station-master knew better: I will open the gate for three roubles, he said: and when we, remembering the yamstchik at Koûzoniemsky, refused to be robbed again, and laughed in his face, he cursed us horribly. Show me, I said to the little Jew, where the Isprâvnik lives: and leaving the sturdy Doctor to watch the luggage amid that ugly-looking crowd, I walked over with the Jew to the Isprâvnik’s.

He proved to be out of town, and as his secretary was not out of bed, I had a long talk with a stout officer who seemed to have blunted his senses by smoking cigarettes, and who listened to me with an expression of massive imbecility: it ended, however, in my return-

We amused ourselves with our chart of the White Sea, mapping out our course upon it, and upon a perfect little German map of North East Europe, by the distinguished Arctic authority Dr. Petermann. The air seemed to die away, a great stillness fell upon the sea: the clouds and sea and sky became one sheet of milk, the only distinction being a creamy haze upon the water. We were sailing upon the White Sea.

<verse>
<i>The fair breeze blew, the white foam flew,</i>
<i>The furrow followed free:</i>
<i>We were the first that ever burst</i>
<i>Into that silent sea.</i>
<i>Down dropped the breeze, the sails dropped down—</i>
<i>’Twas sad as sad could be—</i>
<i>And we did speak, only to break</i>
<i>The silence of the sea.</i>
</verse>

Orloff undertook that we should have fair weather, but we mistrusted the calm, the sky became overcast, and the clouds began to change and darken. We watched the wind ruffling the surface of the White Sea behind us: soon a streak of golden lightning, splitting the black clouds, gave the signal: and the heavens over our heads crashed and shook with the thunder which announced the wind.

These creatures go through the same empty ceremonies with drums used by that extinct humbug the Lapp sorcerer, and in like manner they use those divining instruments to interpret Fate. To these poor benighted people no mission worth naming has ever come, no worthy Bishop Hvoslef has devoted himself: or, like their Europeanized neighbours, they might have shaken off the ignorance and superstition that press heavily upon them.

The Samoyede, we are told, has many good qualities: faithful in friendship, shrinking from dishonesty, incapable of crime. Often starving, he never stoops to beg. What a lesson to our well-fed, cringing, hotel and railway servants. For a glass of the irresistible vodka he will express a wish—indeed he can’t help himself—he is bound to ask for it or perish: an attempt to stifle this craving would leave his children orphans. This is a creditable lesson for his wealthy and mighty neighbours the Russians to have taught him, and it appears to be the only one.

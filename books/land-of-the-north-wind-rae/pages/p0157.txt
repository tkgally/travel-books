# Chapter IX.

[[subtitle]] Upsala and Old Upsala—Mounds of Odin, Thor, and Freya—Their Contents—Stockholm again—Magnificent Museum—Relics of Charles XII. and Gustavus Adolphus—Admirable prehistoric Collection, and Work in precious Metals—Sail for the Island of Gotland—The Mälar Lake and Södertelje Canal—Wisby—Interesting Remains of the Middle Ages—Disestablishment—Imposing Demonstration—Rough Passage to Stockholm—Morning Service—Depart for Copenhagen—The Museum of Northern Antiquities—Discouragement and a Friend in Need—Prehistoric, Runic, Mediæval, Ecclesiastical, and Icelandic Collections—Travel to Kiel—Railway to Hamburg—Renewal of a pleasant Acquaintance—Embark upon the North Sea—Rough Voyage—The Professor—Land at Grimsby.

U<sc>psala</sc> we reached only in time to go from supper to bed, and from bed at five in the morning, by carriage to Old Upsala. The famous mounds, of Odin Father of Gods, Thor his son, and Freya the universal mother, are most interesting. They are very uniform in shape and size—conical mounds of earth slightly flattened on their summits. In 1846 they were opened, two of them, by the Royal Antiquary of Sweden, who penetrated sixty or seventy feet before reaching the walls of the burial chambers. These were formed with huge granite blocks, two or three hundred of which the Royal Antiquary, we presume with assistance, removed.

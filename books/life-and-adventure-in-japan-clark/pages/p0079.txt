After the work of building was fairly commenced, the <i>roof</i> was completed in a month, and the heaviest part of the foundation in two months more; for you must know that it would be decidedly contrary to Japanese character to do any thing except in a manner directly opposite to all our preconceived notions on the subject. In a Japanese house the roof is always built first, and the other parts afterwards! With a kind of celestial instinct, they always commence at the highest point and work downwards. In all the lesser occupations of daily labor, such as digging, sawing, planing, cutting lumber, boring holes, or turning screws, the Japanese do just exactly the reverse of what people do on the other hemisphere; and it is consistent with the idea which children have at home respecting our antipodes, the queer folks that walk with their heads down and their feet sticking up, like flies on a ceiling.

After watching the manner in which they built the house, I felt as if they would be inclined to stand the whole thing on its chimneys, with kitchen and cellar skyward; but this was hardly convenient to do.

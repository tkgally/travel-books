Wherever the stream touched the fire it swept it out like a broom, and by the time I had emptied the first Babcock forty feet of the flames had been subdued. Meanwhile I was nearly extinguished myself by the thick smoke, and crept cautiously towards the hole, where I allowed the Jap pump to play on my face and head.

Thus revived, I took the second extinguisher, and nearly subdued the rest of the flames. As the third and last instrument was passed up to me, I heard a great noise on the roof over my head, and soon shingles and stone tiles began to fall, showing that the firemen were breaking in the roof in true Japanese fashion. In vain I called to them to desist, and not to let in the air; and I sent a stream of acidulated water into their faces to drive them away. Still they persisted in breaking in the roof, and at the same time men with stout poles began breaking up the thin floor beneath me. With the ingress of fresh air the fire started up again in several places. The treacherous cross-beams were some distance apart, and it was difficult to step from one to the other with the heavy instrument on my back.

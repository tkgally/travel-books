It is appropriate just here to say a few words respecting the various classes of society which prevailed in Japan before the advent of foreigners, and of the distinctions which are now slowly passing away.

In ancient times society was divided into four classes. The first constituted the literary and military class, called the Samourai. The second, strange as it may seem, was the agricultural class, or common farmer. The third was the laboring class, or carpenter and artisan. The fourth was the trading or money-making class, the merchant. These were the chief classes that existed from 1604 until 1868.

The Samourai stood at the head of the social scale. He was the gentleman—the soldier in war and the scholar in peace. He could wield either the sword or the pen.

[[cont]]sooner had the fair retinue risen from their seats than I raised the magic clock-lamp from one of the instruments, and shot a broad beam of white light, dazzling as the sun, down the long corridor through which the procession must pass. In an instant the Japanese lanterns glimmered like fireflies, and the darkness of the corridor changed to daylight. The Mikado and empress passed out first, followed by the ladies of the court, who walked quietly, two by two, and hand in hand. Their dresses were similar to some of those I had seen in pictures of the ancient Kioto court. The fair young faces turned one by one towards the brilliant light, which their curiosity led them to look at, and I noticed the little dots placed upon their foreheads, which designated the highest rank of nobility. Some of the ladies were very pretty; they wore their hair in thick tresses down the back, which style is only allowable for ladies of the court. Their eyes were slightly oblique.

The Mikado is a little taller than the average Japanese, with an open, fair countenance, having no decided expression except that of serenity. His profile is not very pleasing, but his forehead is high, and his eyes are manly and expressive. His dark hair curls a little at the temples. He steps with ease and carries his figure erect.

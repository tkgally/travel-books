When I reached the lake small ambuscades had been constructed of bushes and boughs of trees along the shore, and the surface of the water was literally covered with ducks “so wild that they were tame.” As the first volley of shot echoed among the hills a myriad of quacking creatures rose in a great cloud from the surface of the lake and wheeled over our heads in frightened confusion. The sky was darkened with the feathered fugitives, and the noise made by their wings was like a mighty rushing wind. Flock after flock swept in graceful curves through the air just above us, their white breasts flashing for a moment in the sun, and their wings in such rapid motion that they fanned our faces as they flew past and settled again slowly upon the surface of the water.

The lake had always been their quiet home, and they knew not whither to flee; besides, their enemies were completely hidden by the thick ambuscades. Another volley soon started them again, and they rose in the air leaving many of their dead and wounded companions upon the surface of the lake.

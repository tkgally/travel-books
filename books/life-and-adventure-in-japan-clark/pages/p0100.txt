Usually the rice is pounded into flour in the way shown in the accompanying illustration, where a man stands all day in the sun and pounds the rice in a wooden mortar with a large wooden hammer. It is very hard work, but he gets used to it, and simply stops now and then to wipe the perspiration from his naked body. The man in the picture has as much clothing upon him as most of the country people wear in the summer.

We passed Japanese farmers “ploughing” in the field. One man guided the plough, while another led the horse. But the men, the horse, and the plough were all in two feet of water and mud, and presented a ridiculous appearance as they rushed back and forth in the miry field, with the rain pouring upon them, and with only straw hats and straw coverings over their bodies. When the rice field is stirred up into a perfect mush of mud they consider it fit for planting.

My home in Tokio was near the second moat of the great castle, in the centre of the city; the house stood within a compound, or inclosure, adjoining the college buildings. Within this inclosure most of the foreign professors resided; their houses were nearly all of the same style as the one shown in the picture. The view of my house was taken in winter during a snow-storm, to show that we had at least a little snow in Tokio. In the foreground are two jinrikishas, or little carriages such as I used in riding around Tokio. The two little girls in one of the jinrikishas were great pets of mine; they were the daughters of a lady friend, who had charge of the Government Girls’ School. Two servants are standing in the doorway, and considerable snow covers the roof. The smoke-pipe on the left of the entrance shows that the Japanese carpenter could not be prevailed upon to build a chimney. None of the houses in the compound had chimneys, and many fires have originated from the careless way in which these pipes were put up.

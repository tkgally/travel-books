As the daylight came slowly on, we could see that the clouds were thickening about us, instead of diminishing, and that the mist and drizzle were changing to a regular storm of rain. However, we pushed on to the station, which we knew to be a little ahead, and just as the weather was getting the better of us we arrived at two old dilapidated straw shanties, where we set to work to make ourselves as much at home as possible.

As this was the station beyond which horses were not able to go, we had our goods unpacked and sent the horses away, retaining two coolies with us to carry things and make themselves generally useful. A small fire was burning on the floor of the shanty, and a forlorn-looking Jap had a scanty kettle of rice simmering over the embers. We piled on plenty of wood, and got ourselves well dried, and then spreading a straw mat and plenty of newspapers on the ground, we lay down and went to sleep. After a few hours we got up, and attempted to take observations on the situation; but as we were still in the midst of clouds and mist, we could see nothing, and the rain continued to pour worse than ever.

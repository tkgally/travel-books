With the exception of the twittering of the birds, the silence of the place was unbroken; but once we were startled by a chorus of yells, which came from a lot of ragged grass-cutters, who live a wild kind of life up here, dwelling in huts, and carrying hay down to the neighboring villages. Soon after we heard the loud report of a gun, as these fellows sometimes shoot as well as mow; and the Japanese tell great stories about the deer, foxes, wolves, bears, and other animals to be met with in the woods.

At last we arrived at the place where the regular ascent of the mountain begins, and beyond which the horses could not go. So we sent the horses and coolies away, and prepared for the hard climb before us. We had anticipated finding a place suitable for taking a substantial breakfast to start upon. But in this we were disappointed; for though there was a dilapidated shanty, not a person was to be found, and there was nothing to be seen save desolation and disorder.

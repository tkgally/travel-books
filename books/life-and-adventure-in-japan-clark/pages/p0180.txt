Hatakéyama said he had “piles of things” to tell me about his strange experiences in the various courts of Europe, and he afterwards gave me the most vivid descriptions of all that he had seen and heard. His official connection with the embassy afforded him rare opportunities of meeting many of the greatest men and princes in Europe. He did not forget to bring me some pictures he had promised from my old home in Geneva, Switzerland; and I also gave him some large photographs of Niagara, which he requested me to bring from the falls for his mother.

The new Director assigned me my duties and residence at the college, and I was soon settled amid the novel experiences of life at the capital. I took new courage, and began my labors afresh.

Shortly after, the Saga rebellion broke out in the southern provinces of Japan, and Hatakéyama was sent down there in company with the former prince of his province to try and conciliate the insurgents. The attempt was unsuccessful, and a short but bloody strife ensued, in which many noble lives were lost; among others, my former student and friend Katski, who studied with me in Albany, was beheaded with eleven of the leaders of the rebellion.

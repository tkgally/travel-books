But the old folks shook their heads, and were sceptical on that point; for they had been in the world longer than I had, and <i>knew</i> it was flat!

After most of the people had gone home I gave a Christmas dinner—or supper rather—to forty of my friends and the officers of the province. The long tables, bountifully spread, proved a climax upon their astonishment and their appetites, and they pronounced the bill of fare as good as it was novel. There were roast ducks and chickens, corn, peas, beans, tomatoes, succotash, and potatoes, besides plenty of pies, cakes, jellies, and sweetmeats. All of these foreign provisions were imported from London and San Francisco. The Japanese market is limited to rice, fish, and a few unpalatable vegetables. Piles of oranges, apples, and other fruit loomed up among the casters and dishes, and the long white tables, lit up by lamps and countless candles, presented a cheery appearance, rendered doubly gay by the reflection of the large mirror hanging on the wall.

All the metals and minerals I have mentioned may be, and will in time become, free exports; then Yezo will produce something. Awa-bee and erico, the celebrated “Bêche de mer” and “sea-slug,” are and have long been the great article of foreign trade. But Hakodate has other things to furnish the world with—viz. fish-oil, and dried fish; sea-weeds of many kinds, gall-nuts, planks, sulphur, lead, potatoes, even silk and tea (for, as I have said before, Nambu and Shanday are as near the northern port as the southern), deer-skins and horns, and many other things which help to fill up a ship; and when the merchant finds coal, sulphur, and lead for ballast, gold, silver, and copper to fill up the interstices, he will not object to topping it with sea-weed and fish-oil, or a few bags of awabee and other little things.

The harbour of Hakodate is safe and large; it is difficult of approach, but when the seas on either side have been thoroughly surveyed, the danger will have been more than half averted.

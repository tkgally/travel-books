In the city of Hakodate (for a village which in a few years has increased its population from a few thousand to 24,000, deserves that epithet,) there are ten wards or quarters; for each quarter there is an alderman, who is responsible, both he and his family, for any crimes or accidents which may occur within his gates. For the ten wards there is one Lord Mayor, who every morning receives the Ōtŏnōs or Aldermen, hears the cases of minor importance, and decides upon them with the alderman summarily.

Any grave case is reported directly by the Lord Mayor and Chief Police Officer to the Governor, who hands it over to the Procureur-Imperial: after that, European ken knoweth not at present how Japanese law is followed up.

These Ōtŏnōs post up the public notices in their quarters, take care of all the poor and sick in their wards; so well indeed, that when, on two occasions of local disaster, I volunteered to the Governor to open subscriptions and even forwarded my own once, both offers and subscription were politely but very gratefully refused.

When I first arrived at Nagasaki the circulating medium was “paper.” A printed bank-note called a “tael” was issued to foreigners at the rate of 4800 cash to the dollar, and much business was done with this imaginary specie; but no one would accept it beyond the pale of the Treasury.

In July, 1859, a new silver coin called the “half-itzaboue” was introduced, of the same standard as the Mexican dollar; but as, on one side, there was a notice to the Japanese not to take it, as it was only good for foreigners, its reign was short.

The cobang was once to be purchased for four itzaboues, or six shillings and eightpence of our English money: it is now worth nearly 1<i>l.</i> 2<i>s.</i>, for the immense quantity exported at such a remunerative benefice, and the enormous drain on this hitherto preciously hoarded metal, gave the Government reason to reflect, and calculate its intrinsic value.

It was then discovered that the thousands already sold had been sold 200 per cent. below their value; and when the foreigners could buy no more at their accustomed cost, they became clamorous. But the Japanese do not like gold to leave their shores: they have their superstitions or their fancies, and to prevent the ruinous export they equalised their gold coin to the standard weight of the foreigner's gold.

This checked at once a lucrative commerce; but surely the Japanese were, by every law human or divine, duly authorised to protect their currency. We were not the adventurers of a Cortez enriching themselves at the barbaric palaces of Montezuma; ours was a Treaty of friendship and reciprocity.

For some distance the path ran nearly level, this course being necessary to carry it over the upper part of a bold promontory. The mighty valley beneath was admirably seen from the track. On one side the vast bank was broken by great spurs and lateral glens, while opposite, the huge slopes, if less varied, were grand and striking from their steepness and vast size. Through the wide meadows of the valley's base rushed the Kuban, a powerful stream even at this early stage in its long course.

After passing high up round a portion of the promontory, we descended by a series of sharp zigzags its partly-wooded southern side into a lateral glen of some beauty, a short walk down which took us into the great valley. This we struck a little above the village of Eltiub, the largest we had yet seen in the Northern Caucasus, and possessing amid the native huts a house built after the Western fashion, which, though a poor dwelling enough, looked there a palace. In the middle of the village rose a large red-roofed building, which we decided to be a mosque, principally on the ground that there must be a mosque somewhere in a Mohammedan country, and that we had come to none yet.

The rain got lighter and then ceased, the mist overhead opened, and blue sky appeared. The clouds began to rise on the hill-sides, and within half an hour of the time when we had been in deepest gloom the sun was shining brightly, the ridges on either side were clear, and we could see a great mountain at the head of the valley afar off. It was a wonderfully quick passage from foul to fair. I never remember seeing the mists quit the hills in so short a space. Strange to say, the fine weather which came thus suddenly was no mere passing gleam, but lasted steadily for eight days. The Caucasus seems to have atmospheric laws of its own.

The valley of the Baksan improves much as its upper part is reached and Urusbieh approached. The hills on each side are bolder, the base is narrower, while at the head is seen the mighty mass of the great mountain Tungsorun. There are trees on the slopes; not as yet in large forests, but enough nevertheless to be very grateful to the eyes of men weary of a monotony of grass wilderness. This improvement in the country, joined to the sunshine, made us hope for pleasant days at Urusbieh, and we went on our way, if not rejoicing, at all events in a fair state of cheerfulness, while we steamed like apple-dumplings under the strong sun.

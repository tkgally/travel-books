[[cont]]also without food and in nowise sorry to be off, we waited only a few minutes to say farewell to the two hunters who came forth to wish us good-speed on our journey. Pleasant, genial fellows they were; and when in answer to a respectful request that they might see our firearms, a breech-loading revolver was shown to them, and then was loaded and fired, they were full of amazement and admiration, appealing vigorously to Sheitan, <i>i.e.</i>, Satan, who for some reason seems, in most parts of the world, to be invoked by men when they are very much surprised. Lingering for a minute or two behind the others, I asked the younger hunter through Paul what game he killed:—

<i>The Hunter.</i>—The two kinds of mountain goat.

<i>Myself.</i>—How two kinds?

<i>The Hunter.</i>—Why the big and the little (clearly the chamois and the bouquetin).

<i>Myself.</i>—How many do you kill in a year?

<i>The Hunter.</i>—About a hundred, (then, being obviously a truthful man), I did not kill as many as that last year, which was a bad one, but, taking one year with another, I kill a hundred. Down there by Dych-Tau is a wonderful place for game. Great big rocks difficult to go on—but a wonderful place, both for the big and the little.

<i>Myself.</i>—What is the best time of the year for hunting?

<i>The Hunter.</i>—Now: it is as good a time as any.

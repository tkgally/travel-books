But if there be ground for thinking critical judgment of little worth when the limited and comparatively simple work of men's hands has to be considered, how much weight can be given to it in any attempt to decide on the infinite glories of nature? Is it of much avail to try to rate and define the manifold beauties of mountain, glacier, and valley, and say which of two great regions is the fairest? Is not the task too difficult, perhaps impossible? I believe that the more a man studies nature, the less he will feel inclined to speak dogmatically of the beauty of one country as compared with another. Each may have produced a deep and lasting impression on him, but he cannot mark the points which admiration for them should reach, as an engineer marks the pressure of steam. We are not yet able to appraise beauty.

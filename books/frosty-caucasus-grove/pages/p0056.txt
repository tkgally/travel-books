Being interrupted after he had continued on this subject for some time, and asked what he demanded for the journey to Oni, he named an absurd sum.

<i>Paul</i>—That is too much by a great deal.

<i>The Dwarf</i>—Not in the least. Let the gentlemen understand I never speak anything but the truth. You see there is this brigand in the way. For any danger to myself I care nothing, being a man to whom fear is unknown, as many will testify. If the brigand should attack us, I shall probably make him prisoner. But remember my horses may be injured. If they are killed, I must replace them, and for the danger to them it is just I should be paid.

We were sceptical as to the existence of the brigand prince; but we found that the people of the inn believed in him, and the dwarf's terms were agreed to; whereupon, assuring us that such horses as his were rarely to be found, and such a man as himself never, he bowed himself out with much stateliness. He was the very ideal of an Eastern dwarf.

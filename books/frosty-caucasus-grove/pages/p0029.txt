After a long hesitation, our captain at last determined to put to sea on the chance of a smaller steamer meeting him somewhere down the coast. If this did not happen, we had the agreeable prospect of coming back and spending a week or so on the Abkhasian shore.

To everybody’s great delight, however, a little steamer, which was sighted next morning, made for us, and, with much gasping and roaring, managed to lay herself alongside. The passengers were at once sent on board her, to their great contentment. For us, indeed, there was double cause for exultation. A portion of the western range of the Caucasus is admirably seen from the part of the Black Sea we had now reached, and, as a most happy omen, the snow-peaks that morning were all clear, double-headed Elbruz, the highest of the range, being easily distinguished from his lesser brethren. To the south, over a great space of sea and land, were seen the mountains of Armenia.

There were some Georgian musicians on board the little steamer, who at first would play nothing but brassy French airs, which, with the vitality belonging to things bad and vulgar, had travelled thus far East.

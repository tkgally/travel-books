The road we wanted to go by is rather out of the beaten track; there are neither villages nor guard-houses, and only solitary khans of the rudest description. Furthermore, we had been told that the mountain passes are infested with robbers, not to mention the stray and truculent Circassian often to be met with. And although the escort of a zaptieh is no actual protection, still it proves to the ignorant peasant that you are travelling with authority, and at the same time it makes the Government responsible for your safety.

The same thought struck my husband and myself simultaneously—the caimacan had purposely omitted giving the written order, so that, at any rate, he would not be responsible for anything that might happen to us. Poor Nahli was quite in despair; and for the first time I suspected him of being rather a coward. He urged us to change our route, and go by the more frequented caravan road by Nigdeh and Misli. This we would not hear of; for it is not only a great deal longer, but we intended returning by both these towns and naturally did not wish to go over the same ground twice.

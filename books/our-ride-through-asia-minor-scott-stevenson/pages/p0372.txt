But their delight was as great and their welcome as warm, as though Andrew had been their brother instead of their judge. All seemed proud to be recognised; and several came with flasks of Cyprus wine, or cheeses, or some little island present, thinking we would appreciate the gift as coming from Cyprus.

When we had seen all our friends, we started to return the visit of the Caimacan, and to beg him to find us horses for next day’s journey.

From the khan we stepped into a square with a large carved fountain in the centre; shops with verandahs round three sides of it, gave it a piazza-like look, whilst to-day, the square itself was occupied by people selling their wares at little temporary booths, or simply from baskets. Numbers of women had come to the market with their homespun cloth for sale, and piles of white cotton ready to be weighed. Others had small stalls with beads, pins, imitation jewellery, combs, and such like odds and ends. Piles of brown and red chatties, heaps of dried fruits, skins of fresh butter or yaourt, sacks of grain with buyers and sellers busy haggling over them, gave a cheery business-like air to the scene,

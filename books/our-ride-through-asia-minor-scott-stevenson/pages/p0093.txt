# Chapter V.

[[subtitle]] Through Killis to Aintab.

[[subtitle]] A troublesome muleteer—A village of beehives—Arab <i>in</i>hospitality—A lesson in bread-making—Blue <i>v.</i> Red lips—Men will sometimes be boys; an unwilling stampede—The approach to Killis—A guard of honour—The observed of all observers; and the reason—An Armenian home—The town—Armenian architecture; size not beauty—A banquet; fingers before forks—A grand bedroom—The missing <i>douceur</i>—The Ballouk Sou—Turkish politeness—A country of vineyards—Aintab—The Persian consul's home—A crowd and its dispersal—Appearance of the town—Native wine—A novel sedan-chair—A cheerful Osmanli—Extreme kindness of the people—Gorgeous coverlets.

W<sc>e</sc> left Aleppo at 6 <sc>a.m.</sc> on the 16th of April en route to Killis, as we had decided to go from there to Aintab, and then across the Amanus mountains through a village called Pullanick and follow the Pyramus (or Jaihan) to Missis. The road to Killis is a dreary one, leading for many miles across the stone boundary that encloses Aleppo. The air was piercingly cold; our waterproofs over ulsters closely wrapped round, could scarcely keep out the bitter wind.

Nothing but palatial residences and elegant government structures are to be seen all through the whole of this long and well laid out street. At another part of the Ring Strasse is the Burgring, containing the rich Museum, lavishly constructed in Renaissance style, with a profusion of statues, and on the dome two colossal statues of Helios and Athena. Opposite is a beautiful park.

The river Danube itself does not flow through the town, but the town is divided by the Wien and the Danube canal, over which a number of bridges lead to the outer town. The Elisabeth bridge is the handsomest.

The manners and customs of the Viennese are slightly different from those of their neighbours. Their streets are perfectly empty and dark at half-past ten. Their hotels are really only furnished rooms, for they do not provide table d’hôte; the visitor must go to the restaurant for it. We dined and lunched at various places, amongst them the best being Sachse’s restaurant, a first-rate place, where we had an excellent dinner. The cafés also vary slightly from those elsewhere, and they are very good indeed. Ices are consumed in large quantities here, and the varieties are very curious; their quality is also better than in any other part of Europe.

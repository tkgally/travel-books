After having taken a drive about, we went on a small hill called the “Steamer Point,” where the Peninsular and Oriental Company have their buildings, etc. We dined here with Mr. Mermanjee, who was very courteous and kind during our short stay. We left Aden harbour at 9 p.m. We were most fortunate in our passage through the Red Sea. Up to this date we were told on all sides what terribly hot weather we should have through this sea—most likely also rough weather; but I am glad to say they were all false prophets. The weather was beautifully mild, the sea quite calm, and the passage, therefore, quite delightful. We came across two wrecks, and passed them very closely, and discerned the names. They were both steamers, “The Gulf of Finnland” and a West Indian trader. We passed Shadwen Island May 14, 7 p.m.

<i>May</i> 16. <i>Suez.</i>—Arrived 10 a.m. Consternation came on every one when informed that we could not land, but that the Egyptian Government had ordered quarantine to be made for three days. The Captain did all he could to get us off, produced a clean bill of health; but it was of no use. The mail bags were fumigated and taken ashore, and we were ordered off to Moses' Well, about five miles off Suez, and to anchor there.

Further on we view the ruins of the Hotel Manzi. This seems to have been the most fortunate of buildings, for, at the time that the earthquake took place, there were eighty-three travellers in the hotel, and not one of them was lost. In fact, it was only the kitchen which completely gave way, and buried some of the servants of the hotel, while the other part is cracked and partly broken, and, from day to day, is tumbling in more and more. The son of the proprietor was kind enough to show us over the wreck of his property, and we went through the lower rooms, which still looked safe. He told us that he was on the island during the catastrophe, and said it was about half-past nine p.m., when he was in the office, and a lady residing in the hotel asked him to go with her to the post-office. They had not left the hotel ten minutes, when suddenly they felt a terrible shock, accompanied by a noise resembling several claps of thunder or a prolonged cannonade.

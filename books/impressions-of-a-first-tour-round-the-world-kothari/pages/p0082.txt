The principal attraction here is the International Exhibition. On arriving at the station one could already see the town must be full. The trains were packed, and when we arrived at the hotel we found there also a great crush.

The first thing, of course, is to take a cab to the Exhibition, which is duly done, and we arrive at the grand entrance, which gives one a very good impression on entering. It is formed of two huge pyramids, about one hundred and fifty yards apart, carried by four huge elephants, surmounted with a peculiar combination of statues, busts, trees, fruits, wild animals, and snakes, the elephants forming the base, and the others the cone of the pyramids. Across these two structures an imitation of Indian shawl pattern is tastefully imitated, and the whole is, I think, meant to represent the Indian possessions of the Dutch.

The Exhibition, it must be said, is a private enterprise, and not as successful as it might be, more particularly that it is still in an unfinished state, although it was opened in May.

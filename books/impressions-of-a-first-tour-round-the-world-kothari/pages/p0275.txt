# Eastern Tour.

I <sc>left</sc> San Francisco on the noon of <i>22nd December</i>, 1883, with sixteen state room and about seven hundred (Chinamen) steerage passengers. The way they were quartered in the fore and after part of the steamer was sickening. They were packed like sardines in a tin box. The passenger agent of the company, who had come on board with his despatches, asked me to have a glance at these places before the steamer started, and at his suggestion, I did go; but I got sick on entering the place, for the air was so stifling, and people were jammed in their little berths so close, that any man of good strength could easily break down.

On the 23rd we had head winds all the day, which made the passage rather disagreeable; but since then it was much pleasanter, and all enjoyed the trip immensely.

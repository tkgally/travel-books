We took a drive through the town and first viewed the bridge that is just about being completed across the river. It is very handsome indeed, built of three large iron arches. When all the scaffolding is taken away this structure will compare favourably with any bridge in Europe. The cathedral we visited next. This is a handsome Gothic structure about four hundred years old. The organ is the chief attraction of the cathedral; it is a very famous one, and it was being played upon while we were there; its sounds are very powerful and very mellow. We now went to see the bears’ den of Berne, where Bruin is here kept since time immemorial at the expense of the Municipality. It is remarkable how fond they are of their bears; every statue, establishment, or sign, is sure to have a bear upon it in some shape or another. If I lived here I should think that continually seeing nothing but bears all over the town would make it quite unbearable. Only a small drive up the hill, where there is a beautiful garden called the Schamfi, and then off to the station, bidding Berne and its bears good-bye, where we take the train for Lucerne, arriving at 9 p.m. We stay at the Hotel National, a very good and comfortable hotel.

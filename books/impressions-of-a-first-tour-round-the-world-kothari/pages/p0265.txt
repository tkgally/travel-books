After going through this process, it is cut to pieces, salted, and packed, and in this way, some thousands are shipped to different parts of the world. I was informed that nearly nine hundred cattle and seven thousand hogs were killed daily in the stock-yard of Messrs. Armour and Co., and to carry this out, and work the business, they have to employ four thousand five hundred hands. When leaving this yard, I took a glance at the stores, and I saw some thousand cases of meat, some hundreds of barrels of lard, sausages, etc., ready to be despatched to any part of the world at a moment’s notice. Truck loads of ice were to be seen there to carry meat to different parts of the Continent. The other sight I saw in Chicago was the grain elevator. I here had an idea in what way the immense grain trade is carried on. There are about twenty buildings, all situated on the bank of the Chicago River, connected with the side trucks of the railway, where the grain is unloaded and shipped in steamers.

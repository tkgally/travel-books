Gradually the flat white roofs became peopled with women coming out for their evening airing and gossip. Negresses also appeared gaily clad in red and yellow, their black faces uncovered, and were most amusing to watch. Active as cats they climbed over the parapets from roof to roof and romped and played together like children.

Another day we made an excursion to the “Source of the Zakkah,” a trip which Salem regarded as his special privilege to make. He said he was the only guide from Tangier who knew of it, and although he had been there several times himself, we were the first travellers, with the exception of one Englishman, whom he had taken to visit it. The sensation of being a pioneer in these days when every corner of the world is sought out by the tourist is certainly exhilarating. One is, however, far from feeling certain of the fact, when one has only the statement of a <i>rusé</i> Tangier guide to rely upon. Still the jaunt proved a very delightful one.

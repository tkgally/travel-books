[[cont]]Where Rose landed I never did know, but the first I saw of him he was crawling up the hill from below.

I was in a tight place, for I had slid down behind the horse, and my energetic pull at the lines had jammed me close between the horse and the carriage, which now lay on its side. The horse, evidently not liking the position, made frantic efforts to kick me away, but I held him so firmly back, and was jammed so tightly against him, that, at best, he made but abortive efforts.

The moment Richards saw the carriage was going over, he made the best leaps he could down the steep hill, and in a very few seconds had the horses by the bits, knowing that the greatest danger was a runaway. A dozen men quickly joined him, and others ran to the carriage, taking off the roof and helping out the ladies.

Let me here say a word to their credit, that not a scream was heard from one of the ladies, but they kept very quiet till they could be carefully got out.

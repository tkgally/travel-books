And now I may speak of the currency of the country. This consists of specie dollars, marks, and skillings (pronounced <i>shillings</i>). One Norwegian dollar cost me, including commission, exchange, and everything, one dollar and twenty-one cents in greenbacks, when my drafts were paid at my home bank. At that time the premium on gold averaged about ten per cent. There are twenty-four skillings in a mark, and five marks in a dollar. Since I left the country a new treaty between Norway, Sweden and Denmark has gone into operation to unify the currency of the three kingdoms, whereby the Swedish <i>kroner</i> is made the standard, and the smaller coins decimals of kroners. Four kroners are equal to one specie dollar. The former Danish standard was equal to half a specie dollar, or two kroners. This change will be a great convenience to travelers in Scandinavia.

Let me mention here the tariff fixed by law for watermen, whose services are brought into requisition whenever one wants to go on board or land from a vessel.

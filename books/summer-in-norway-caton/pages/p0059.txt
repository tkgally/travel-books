He spoke English very well, was evidently well educated and intelligent. We walked together for perhaps an hour, while he furnished me a great fund of information. During all this walk the same salutations were exchanged with all we met. I asked him to show me where I could get some matches, and he took me to a tobacco shop. The man behind the counter was uncovered, while his hair was carefully dressed. The moment we entered the door my conductor removed his hat and remained uncovered till we left the shop. Of course I did the same—and this I found to be the universal custom throughout Norway. It is considered very rude for anyone—except he be an Englishman—to wear his hat in any store or shop, precisely as in the parlor of a mansion. Americans observe these things and conform to them much more readily than the English.

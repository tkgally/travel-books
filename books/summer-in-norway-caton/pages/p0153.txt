All that I saw were tanned with the hair on, and were made up with the hair inside. I saw some little chaps—I know not whether girls or boys—not more than two or three years old, toddling about the streets in full deer-skin suits. Their shoes are a kind of moccasin, made from the skin taken from the legs of the deer where the hair is short and firm, and much more durable than from other parts of the deer. They are constructed with the hair outward. They come up around the ankles, have a seam under the hollow of the foot, forward of which the hairs have a backward set, and behind which the hairs have a forward set, which prevents slipping. They differ from the Indian moccasin in having a regular sole, which, however, is but one thickness of skin. They are often ornamented with red and tinsel. These are made largely for sale, of various qualities and prices. The little store at Bosekop, where I got a pair, had many bushels of them. An ornamented pair costs a dollar.

After we had got inside, I questioned the captain as to the character of the navigation of the fjord. He informed me that in general the water is excellent, but there are some sunken reefs along near the islands, and it was to avoid one of these that he had insisted on the pilot’s keeping further west than he had chosen. I then inquired whether the pilot had the right to control the course of the vessel? “Yes,” said he, “theoretically he has, but no pilot shall run me on the rocks, if I know it, while I command the ship. I piloted here while he was catching codfish. There are many sunken rocks, some of which are not well marked on the chart, and my business is to keep clear of them, whether the pilot would avoid them or not. Suppose he is running me right on that island, am I to stand still and see him do it? There may be exceptional cases of which owners do not think when they make their general rules, and this was one.

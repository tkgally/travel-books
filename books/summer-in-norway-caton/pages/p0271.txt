In the morning when I went on deck I found we were passing along the same route we had pursued on our way north, but enjoyed a second view of the grand wild scenery almost as much as if it were entirely new.

The American reader is apt to associate the term <i>wild</i> scenery or <i>wild</i> country with a new or lately peopled country, where the hand of man has not yet had time or opportunity to destroy the natural features or native beauties impressed upon it by its Maker’s hand. Here these beauties and these features remain, not for the lack of time, but because man is too weak to deface them, and so they remain in their grand sublimity through the thousands of years during which man has been playing around them. He can never tear down or even deface these huge rocky monuments thrown up by convulsed nature in primeval time before man was.

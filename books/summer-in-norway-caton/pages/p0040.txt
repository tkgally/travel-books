From this it was inferred that they were not well up to Norwegian Custom duties, for of all things provisions are taxed more heavily than any imports admitted to the country, and could be bought outright for the duties which they would have to pay. Well, off they went, with the good wishes of their friends left behind, with a journey of thirty or forty miles before them to reach the river they had rented.

The rivers of Norway afford the finest salmon fishing in the world, and all are now rented to Englishmen. The rents are paid to the riparian owners, and the leases are executed by them. Some lease a whole river, others take half a mile, a mile or ten miles. Many have to go forty or fifty miles up the river to reach their fishing ground, where they have built comfortable lodges. We should consider the rents high for six weeks’ sport at the best, ranging from a few hundred dollars to several thousands.

This we were not disposed to yield, for experience had shown us that the greatest delay for horses occurs in the after part of the day, when those in the lead may meet with no detention while the next coming may have to wait for hours. At best, there must be several ahead of us who had taken the road at stations further on, and we could not afford to lose our advantage if we hoped to reach Lillehammer that night, which we had determined to do.

Although we had a right to take a week to make the journey in, yet if we could make it in the more usual time of four days, all the better. All had stood the journey so well that there was a general desire to get through that night, although it would make a longer drive than we had yet had.

We were off before the other carriage came in sight, and in fact we saw no more of them that day.

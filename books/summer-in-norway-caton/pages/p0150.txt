In quite recent times, the Lapp language has become a written language, with its own alphabet and grammar, the two most approved of which are by Stockfleth and Rask. Their alphabet is formed upon the phonetic principle, each letter representing a simple sound, which enables the beginner to learn to read much more readily than with our own barbarous and unphilosophical orthography.

Since the boon of a written language and printed books has been conferred upon the Lapps there has been a commendable progress in their education, and this is much stimulated by the governmental system, which makes the right of suffrage and other important civil rights dependent upon a certain amount of education. This the government places within their reach by sending the schoolmaster among them, who visits their towns and settlements far back in the mountains.

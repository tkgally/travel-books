In the background is a long low shed, its walls hidden by a collection of palm-oil barrels, and surmounted by a corrugated-iron roof. On one of the lower steps of the platform stands “Holliday” himself, scratching his chin; on his head is a Panama straw-hat, with the broad brim turned down. A green cord is fastened round his neck, threaded through a large single piece of red coral, which, hanging in the centre of the upper opening of his white linen jacket, takes the place of collar, necktie, and scarf-pin. A red-and-blue-check duster, wound round and round the waist beneath the coat, reaches a little below the knees, showing a few inches of bare black leg above the white cotton socks and black leather laced ankle-boots. Leaning over the balustrade to his right are two of his wives—one fat and thirty, the other equally fat, but not more than twenty years old, each dressed in a single piece of check duster material passed round the body under the arms, rather higher than a European low dress, but more than making up at the skirt for its superfluity above. Legs and feet are bare, and a checked handkerchief wound tightly round the head completes their attire.

Walking home, Mr Bewley gave me a description of a Greek funeral he had attended the week before. The coffin was taken in a boat from the town across to the cemetery, where, after landing, the friends removed the lid, exposing the corpse to view; it was that of a young bridegroom dressed in his wedding-suit, with his wife’s bridal wreath of orange-blossoms tied round his forehead, her bouquet over his hands. All the friends stooped to kiss him three times, and then in turn poured claret, incense, and ashes over him. The body was then lowered into the grave; and a friend being helped down, knelt on the corpse and tore the clothes from off it before screwing down the lid. To finish up this ghastly ceremony, they all drank liqueurs standing round the grave.

Just before lunch the Governor-General, Colonel Holled-Smith, came to call on us, and invited us to dine with him that night. In the afternoon Captain Lewis took us in a boat to visit the “John Pender,” on which we were shown the cables and dynamos.

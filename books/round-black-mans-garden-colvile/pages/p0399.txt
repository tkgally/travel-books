A short walk brought us to the shed in which the war-canoes were kept—huge unwieldy-looking things dug out of the trunk of a single tree, about three feet broad and fifty or sixty in length. They present, however, an imposing appearance fully manned, with the fifty paddles simultaneously flashing in the sunlight. Close by was the old <i>barracoon</i> in which the Portuguese used to store the slaves prior to embarkation,—a long, low, one-storeyed stone building without windows, a very dismal dungeon in which to spend the last hours on one’s native land.

On the margin of the creek close by, half buried in the mud, I saw an odd-shaped earthenware bowl, curiously ornamented with bosses.

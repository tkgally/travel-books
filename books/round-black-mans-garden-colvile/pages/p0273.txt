However, with the help of two of the men, we at last found ourselves on <i>terra firma</i>. While they went back for Harry and Monsieur Martini, the <i>simandou</i> and I walked on to try and procure lodgings in the town, which was about a mile distant. We accosted the first human being we met; and after a great deal of talk, he took us to a narrow door in a high palisade, round an open space, planted with mango-trees, in the centre of which was a large two-storeyed house. Our guide showed us up a steep flight of wooden steps, and we found ourselves in a sort of barn, divided into little rooms by thin partitions, in which were a number of sleeping men, over whom we nearly stumbled in the dark. Our beds having arrived, I took possession of one of the rooms, and by the time Harry and Monsieur Martini—who had been seeing to the safe landing of the goods—appeared, I had got the things fairly ready.

Up to the time they left the river-bank there had been no signs of the other canoe; so, not knowing what might be in store for us on the following day, we turned in, determined to get what sleep we could.

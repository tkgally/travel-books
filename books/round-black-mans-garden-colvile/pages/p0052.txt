Although so early, it was very hot, and the bazaar was more crowded than the day before by natives in all kinds of coloured costumes. We first went for our provisions to a bazaar I had not yet visited. If the heat had not for many days past deprived me of all wish to touch meat, the sight I saw there certainly would have done so, the butchers’ shops being simply black with flies. I speedily deserted Ibrahim, and turned my attention to the more tempting vegetable and fruit stalls, where he afterwards joined me. There were pyramids of pomegranates, some split open, showing their lovely crimson seeds; next to them were water-melons of curious shapes; while among the vegetables I seized upon an old favourite, the purple fruit of the egg-plant. Ibrahim complained that in consequence of my being there they asked him double the usual price for everything. This made him very angry.

Having got hold of a boy to convey his purchases to the boat, he took me into a narrow street where the metal-workers lived in dirty little shops.

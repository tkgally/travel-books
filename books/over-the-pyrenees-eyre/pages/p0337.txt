Its chief charm is the beauty of its site, the delicious purity of the air, and the gardens. They, however, though well worth keeping up in their present style as a sample of what Moorish gardens were, are nothing to a well-laid out English garden. They consist of small courts, the walls of which are hid by creepers, terrace-like, one above the other. In one are some very old cypress-trees, and here the guide pointed out the tree where romance says the Sultana met the Abencerrage, and was discovered; which meeting led to the destruction of the whole tribe.

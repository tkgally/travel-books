Just imagine, if you can, a grove of fine old elms; white marble fountains sending up jets of water that sparkle like diamonds in the morning sun, oleanders as tall as young apple-trees, with stems as thick as a man’s arm, and a crown of rose-coloured flowers; while the long vista formed by the trees, is bounded by a range of sky-blue hills, deepening here and there, in cleft or hollow, into shades of ultramarine or indigo; and crowned with eternal snow. That is Granada. Turn round, and look behind you. A long, long vista of triple rows of trees, behind and between which, apparently, rise foreign-looking white houses, with green jalousies and arches, and above them Moorish-looking towers, unlike any church towers you ever saw out of Spain; and over all such a bright clear blue sky. That, too, is Granada.

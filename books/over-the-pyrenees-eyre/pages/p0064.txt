It might be very interesting to English people who knew nothing about France and Spain, and not at all interesting to those who knew both countries well; if it was likely to be generally interesting, his friend was well able to translate it, and could, no doubt, make an arrangement with ‘mon éditeur,’ as a publisher is called in France.

M. Tolede asked if I was a good walker, and if I had strong shoes. ‘The roads,’ said he, ‘are so bad in Andorre, you will often have to dismount and walk.’ I told him I had a pair of men’s shoes. ‘Then,’ said he, ‘half the battle is over. Once in Spain your difficulties will be over. The Andorrans in general are a very dirty race. I don’t know how you will get on there, but in Spain you will find comfort and cleanliness.’ ‘But in the mountains,’ said I, ‘I am told the mountaineers live by <i>contrebandisme</i> and brigandage, and am afraid of being robbed and murdered.’ ‘N’ayez pas peur,’ he answered; ‘c’était comme ça autrefois, après la guerre, quand le gouvernement n’était pas assuré.

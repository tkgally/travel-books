The <i>douaniers</i> were satisfied. ‘Fige,’ which I suppose meant ‘put them in;’ but it might have been translated ‘fidgets,’ for it was fidgeting work to get them in again. At last the bag was tied and replaced on the mule, I having first duly explained I was <i>malato</i>, and had brought with me a little tea. If you openly state such a thing, the <i>douaniers</i> are always civil, and pass it. I had but half a pound, which I kept for illness. A great blessing it has proved to me. I asked for a glass of water; we exchanged adieus, and I and my guide walked on. I was in Spain. I looked at the mountain ridges and could hardly even then realise the fact.

[[cont]]I would give him five francs over and above the fifteen Señor Federigo had agreed I was to pay him at the close of our journey; and, moreover, I would treat him with cigars. Ford says, ‘It is desirable to keep your muleteer in good-humour, and the best thing you can give him is cigars.’ I had one real good Habana by me in case of tooth-ache—smoking being the only thing that ever relieves me; but as the critics are inclined to think ill of me already, I beg to inform them I abhor tobacco, and never smoke except to seek relief from violent pain. This precious cigar I determined to bestow on Joan, and to get him some more as opportunity offered. Ford says good smuggled cigars are always attainable in these mountains. He accepted the cigar, and seemed in the best of humours; but, ah! when we had to mount again, the cruel, faithless man, forgetting all his promises to Señor Federigo, that I should ride comfortably, had unpacked the comfortable seat Señor Andria had prepared for me, and left me nothing to sit upon but a sack stuffed tight with straw till it was as hard as a stone, and so loosely bound by ropes that I had no secure hold to cling to in ascending and descending precipices.

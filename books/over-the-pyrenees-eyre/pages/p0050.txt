Unquestionably we English are fifty times cleaner, in our houses, and in our persons, but we do not look so. Are they sprung from the ermines, which are said to die of grief if their fur is stained, and do they inherit from that remote ancestry a power of passing undefiled through all dirt? This morning—no later—I saw a French lady trailing a fawn-coloured gown over the floor of a dirty-looking lodging opposite, which I dare swear never knew scrubbing-brush, or even clout, and water. She has worn that gown ever since I have been here, and she has two children always clinging about her, and it has not a rumple or a stain upon it. I only got caught in the rain yesterday, and my gown is tattered and rumpled as if it was rough dry; but then I am an unhappy Englishwoman; my clothes will get dirty and tumbled, do what I will.

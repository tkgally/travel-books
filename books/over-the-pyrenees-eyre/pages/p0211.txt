It was genuine ‘Españolismo;’ they sympathised with, and approved it.

When supper was over, I asked the Frenchman to be good enough to aid me in settling with my guide; he assented willingly.

I turned to the treacherous Joan: ‘What sums have you paid for me, at the different <i>ventas</i>, where we stayed?’

‘Four francs.’

‘You agreed with Señor Federigo to bring me to Calaf for fifteen francs?’

‘Yes.’

‘Fifteen and four make nineteen; you will lose a franc by exchange; there is a Napoleon of twenty francs. Had you made my seat on the mule comfortable, I should have given you five francs more. No one ever loses by civility to an English person. If an English man or woman meets kindness in travelling, and has not means to repay it at the time, they will send a present months afterwards. The English never forget a kindness; but you did all you could to make me suffer, though you knew I had hurt my back by a fall in England.

There did not seem to me a decent shop or street in the whole town, and, like almost all Spanish towns, it has no shade, although, from the bridge one crosses after leaving the railway: where one sees a few green trees and shrubs overhanging the stream, and growing among the fissures of the rock; the river with its cascades of white foam, and the greenery; verdure, and water, both so rare in burnt, parched-up Spain, the irregularly-built, picturesque-looking, high houses on the steep ascent of the hill, and the towers of the two churches crowning all, it would form a very lovely picture.

I returned tired and hot to mine inn, and whiled away the time as I best could. There was only one other guest besides myself, and we two dined at the same table. He was of the industrious classes, as was proved by his espardines, or hempen sandals, no one above that rank wearing them in Spain, though one often sees very well-dressed men with beautifully clean white stockings and espardines.

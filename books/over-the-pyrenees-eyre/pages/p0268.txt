We reached the ferro carril. A woman whispered me the fare was ‘un reau,’ and a reau (about fourpence) I gave. I had been compelled to pay two pesetas (five reals) the day I arrived at Barcelona for the same distance and the same amount of luggage.

Properly speaking, I should say a real; but the common people, and even the shopkeepers, all talk of a reau and a piesette, instead of a peseta and a real—the proper terms. In fact, there are so many different kinds of money in Spain, and so many different dialects, that a stranger gets utterly bewildered amongst them; beside which, the people are not like the French, disposed to help him out of any difficulty, but enjoy his bewilderment, because for the moment they feel his superiors in knowledge.

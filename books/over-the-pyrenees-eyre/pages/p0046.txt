The air was deliciously sweet with the rich odour of the limes, and the spicy fragrance of the pines upon the hill-sides; it seemed to breathe fresh vigour into my jaded frame, and I was loth to quit the garden. At last I returned home to mine inn, and had a <i>plat</i> brought to my room for myself, and another for Keeper. I never dine at the <i>table d’hôte</i>. It is not agreeable to a single woman to dine among the <i>commis-voyageurs</i> and other indiscriminate company composing a <i>table d’hôte</i>, and I do not care about my dinner sufficiently to encounter all the noise and vulgarity one is exposed to, alone. If I were one of a party, it would be different.

I stayed that night at Nismes, growing more ill every hour, and started next morning for Montpellier. What can people find to like in that uninteresting ugly town, its shadeless environs, its white-dusted streets? I felt more feverish every moment; to eat was impossible.

A certain amount of comfort is become a necessity for me as a condition of tolerable health. A person liable to violent attacks of bronchitis, with no one to nurse her when ill, is obliged to be careful of herself. I put these remarks in for my reviewers, who have been pleased to wonder why I did not content myself with ‘even the humblest lodging in a country village in England, instead of wandering in this comfortless way.’ Gentlemen, I want to earn a home of my own once more; and, thank God, and a kind public, which buys and reads my books, I hope I am in a fair way now to do so.

Retournons à nos moutons.</i> About a mile and a half from the village of Encamps, I was painfully picking my way down a steep mountain-side alone—Tatine and the horse were of course out of sight and call—when I passed two shepherd boys.

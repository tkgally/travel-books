I saw, however, that he was trying to confuse me, for he gave me some silver back for my second twenty-five franc piece, then asked for it back, then gave me some again, three times. Again I went to the baggage-office to take my tickets; my luggage was weighed, and I put down the sum required on the book-keeper’s book. I had scarcely done so when the naked-shouldered boy seized hold of three francs, or rather pesette, jabbering something about ‘Kefé.’ I thought he had been sent from the <i>venta</i>, and said angrily I had had no ‘café;’ but at last the book-keeper made him put down the money and sent him about his business. Heartily tired of Venta di Cardenas; as soon as I had put Keeper in his cage, I asked a railway official if I might not take my seat, and on his answering affirmatively, did so. I had not sat quiet many minutes before a <i>gend’arme</i> came to the window, accompanied by the ragged youth, and both began talking about ‘Kefé.’ ‘Is there no one here who speaks French?’ said I, in utter despair.

# Chapter XIV.

[[subtitle]] Granada to Alicante.

I <sc>went</sc> one morning to the general post-office to inquire for letters, which obliged me to pass by the market-place. I lost my way, and asked it of a handsomely-dressed Spanish <i>donna,</i> and she very politely said she would show it me. We had not gone far before the usual hooting the unfortunate stranger commenced, and a rabble began to follow us, whom from time to time she waved back in a dignified way with her hand, once or twice rebuking them. ‘They are always so in Granada,’ said she apologetically to me; ‘it is very bad, very wrong; I am so sorry.’

Nobody even looked at me in Paris, or in any town in France, when I wore the same dress in which I was mobbed in Spain.

A <i>sergent de ville</i> passed, and he was appealed to. He shrugged his shoulders, and threw his arms up into the air, pathetically. ‘What could <i>he</i> do? It was not his place to interfere.’ ‘Whose is it, then?’ I asked, indignantly. ‘I am a writer; I will tell the whole world, in the book I am going to write on Spain, how uncivilised the Spaniards are.’ At length we worked him up to the pitch of speaking to the rabble, and at last they dispersed.

Tired and hungry, I turned down the street leading to the Rambla, and entering a baker’s shop, asked for a roll and sat down to eat it. There could hardly have been greater excitement if the hippopotamus from the Regent’s Park Zoological Gardens had walked in and seated himself in a chair to eat, and repose himself.

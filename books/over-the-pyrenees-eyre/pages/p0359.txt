Take madame there.’ ‘Madame had better drive first to a hotel,’ said the conductor, insinuatingly. ‘Madame can then refresh herself, and she will afterwards be in time for the steamer.’ ‘Madame will do no such thing,’ said I. ‘Madame will go on board at once. Be kind enough, sir, to tell the conductor the street where the office is, that he may drive me there.’ ‘Oh! he knows,’ said the Spanish gentleman; ‘it is the Calle—,’ I forget its name. ‘Take the lady there.’ He raised his hat, and bowed; and the abashed conductor, without further remonstrance, drove me to the packet office.

‘What is the fare to Barcelona?’ ‘Ten Napoleons.’ ‘Ten Napoleons! It can’t be. I was told it was cheap.’ ‘Well, I may be wrong; I don’t understand French money; sit down and I will see.’ It proved to be two guineas. I paid them joyfully; jumped once more into the omnibus, was driven to the quay, the conductor hailed a boat, I and my dog got into it. Huzza! Thank God I’m leaving Spain!’

The features of many of the figures have been completely effaced, but in other respects time and weather seem to have had no effect on them. Indeed, except where man has taken the trouble to injure them, all the sculptures and bas-reliefs of these ruins look as if they had left the sculptor’s hands a few years ago.

Chehel Minar, the prototype of Chehel Sitoon at Ispahan, was probably an open-air hall, having porticos on three sides, and being completely open on the fourth. Each portico was supported by twelve columns, in two rows of six, whilst the roof of the hall rested on thirty-six columns in six rows; the area covered by the whole building being 50,000 square feet. Of this forest of pillars there remain standing only thirteen. They are sixty feet high, accurately fluted, and taper slightly upwards from bell-shaped bases, beautifully ornamented with pendent lotus-leaves, to capitals composed of two half bulls or two half griffins, with their heads looking in opposite directions.

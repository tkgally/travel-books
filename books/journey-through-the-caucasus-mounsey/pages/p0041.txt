A further proof of the change that has taken place in their beds and the volume of their waters, may be inferred from the facts that the galleys of Pompey ascended the Rion, and that Seleucus Nicator had, some 2,000 years ago, a project for uniting the Euxine and Caspian by means of a canal.[[fnref *]] Such a project would hardly have been conceived had not these rivers then afforded greater facilities for navigation than they do at present.

Meanwhile innocent, no doubt, of all speculations like the above as to the future importance of Poti, 750 soldiers were working away at the harbour, sawing up timber in a steam-mill, probably the first steam-engine of any sort in the Caucasus, driving in piles, &c. There we will leave them to return in our barge to dinner at the “Colchide.”

But neither this modest expectation, nor another one in which I had often indulged during the miseries of my long ride, were to be satisfied. Arrived within sight of Tehran, I could perceive but a long low line of brown mud wall, capped here and there by the brown domes, very like ant-hills, of some insignificant mosques, rising above the snow-covered plain and backed by huge snow-covered mountains. Inside its gates I had to traverse bazaars as ruinous, and streets, or rather lanes, as squalid as any I had seen in any other Persian town; and on dismounting at sunset at the gates of her Majesty's Legation, I was shown to a room painfully similar to my quarters of the last week, the only apparent difference being that there were some panes of glass in the window-frames, a carpet on the floor, and a chimney which did not smoke. Thus are fancy's creations frequently dissolved: I had dreamt of tall minarets, gilded domes, and lofty bazaars; I found what I have just sketched. I had dreamt of a warm comfortably-furnished apartment, or at least of something like civilized accommodation; I found that I must camp on still. “Lazar, bring in my bed and go and see if you can buy me a chair.”

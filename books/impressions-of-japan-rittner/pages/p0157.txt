Hopscotch, tops, kites, battledore and shuttlecock, fencing, archery, puss-in-the-corner, and all manner of other games are played there as with us; in fact, almost every game except (at least when I was there) ping-pong. That, so far, has escaped reaching as far East as Japan—and they can do without it—because their <i>samisen</i> music makes up for the equally discordant sounds produced by ping-pong.

The Japanese are extremely clever jugglers, amongst the finest in the world, and even the smallest children are adepts at keeping several balls going at once. They run round a room with four or more balls on the move, throw them over their heads, and catch them again behind their backs. There is no limit to the amount of ingenuity they display at inventing new ball tricks, and it seems one of the favourite indoor amusements amongst the girls. Sometimes they have matches in which the losers pay a forfeit, and for every miss receive a dab on the cheek with a paint brush from the winner.

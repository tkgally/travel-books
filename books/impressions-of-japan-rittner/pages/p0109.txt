When in their houses they seldom wear shoes, but if they do, they use <i>zori</i>, straw sandals with a thong stretched from the front and attached to each side, forming the letter V; this fits in between the big toe and rest of the foot, and enables them to retain the sandals. When they go out they put on wooden clogs, with bars of wood about two inches high at the bottom, called <i>geta</i>, which keep their feet out of the mud and rain. The <i>shizoku</i> generally carry a fan, which fixes into their girdle, and is made of mulberry paper stretched tightly across narrow strips of bamboo. The Japanese are supposed to be the originators of the <i>ōgi</i>, as this folding fan is called. The old legend is, that a priestess cured a sufferer from fever in a temple by fanning him, and she is supposed to have been the inventor. Besides the fan they carry a <i>nécessaire</i>, called <i>netsuke</i>, in which they keep all the paraphernalia necessary for a smoker, as pipe, tobacco, matches, and cigarettes. We have not yet finished with everything appertaining to the dress, though this last has almost gone out of fashion—the <i>in-ro</i>, or medicine-chest, made of gold lacquer or carved red lacquer wood.

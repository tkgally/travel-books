She appealed to England in her anguish, and the only consolation she received was the advice that it was hopeless to resist the allied nations. The advice of Russia, France, and Germany, clothed in the robe of friendship, was to leave what she had gained to her superiors, and run away and play. This deep-laid scheme was shrouded under the title of “Peace and Civilisation.”

What a mockery! A dog is taught to perform tricks by the aid of a stick, and the man who puts him through his performance says it is all done by kindness: on this principle have the would-be civilisers of the world turned Japan out of what she so dearly paid for, and then say they have done it in the interest of civilisation.

If these men who fell fighting for the honour of Japan, who lost their lives out of loyalty for their country, could awake now and see Russia snugly nested in Port Arthur, and Kiao-Chou in the hands of Germans, how their souls would revolt against such injustice!

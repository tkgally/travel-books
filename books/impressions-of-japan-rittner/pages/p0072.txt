The Japanese women from their birth are taught to be artistic. A mother’s first care, after her child is born, is the preparation of its <i>kimono;</i> the younger the child the brighter the colour chosen for its dress. The richer the mother the purer the silk, the more unique the design; each child’s dress will have some befitting colour according to the month in which it is born, and no expense is spared, no sacrifice too great to attain that end. Small children of five and six years old will carry their smaller brothers and sisters strapped like a knapsack on their backs, and those who have no brothers or sisters will have a doll substituted; and so they are taught, whilst still almost babies themselves, to tend and care for the younger ones. Until they marry their great aim is to look beautiful, to attract the admiration of their friends, both men and women; their hair is artistically arranged, so saturated with oil as to make it almost impossible of untidiness.

The whole scene was one almost of overpowering solitude, no one near, no sound of voices, nothing but the view of Fujiyama, ever changing and yet ever more beautiful.

Numbers of poems have been dedicated to this wonderful mountain, and amongst the finest is one written about the tenth century <sc>a.d.</sc>, of which Mr. Basil Hall Chamberlain gives an adequate rendering in one of his books:—

<verse>
“What name might fitly tell, what accents sing,
Thine awful, godlike grandeur? ’Tis thy breast
That holdeth Narusawa’s flood at rest,
Thy side whence Fujikawa’s waters spring.

Great Fusiyama, towering to the sky!
A treasure art thou given to mortal man,
A God Protector watching o’er Japan:
On thee for ever let me feast mine eye.”
</verse>

The Americans, I believe, are chiefly responsible for the decline of Japanese art. They require—and unless they obtain it, will not buy these tawdry articles—a brilliant clash of colouring; the more <i>auffallend</i>—conspicuous hardly describes it—the colours, the better they are pleased. So far even has their art degraded in the past few years that one is shown an article, palpably, from the very inconsistency of its colouring, a modern piece of manufacture, and told it is hundreds of years old; they will even go so far as to guarantee its age and antiquity by offering written proof showing the temple or noble’s house from which it has been unearthed.

Another great industry worth mentioning is the <i>cloisonné</i>, of which Kioto is again the home. It is very much older than the <i>Satsuma</i> work, though, until last century, was never carried to any great pitch of perfection.

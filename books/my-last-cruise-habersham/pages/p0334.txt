<i>Japanese</i>, (with a look of command.)—“Put up your tent, put every thing into your boat, and sail away.”

<i>American.</i>—“We are going to sleep here to-night and sail away in the morning.”

<i>Japanese</i>, (look of command changing to one of affected terror.)—“You can’t sleep here to-night: if you do <i>my</i> head will be cut off by the governor.”

<i>American.</i>—“Oh, no! we have been to Si-mo-da and Ha-ko-da-di, and know that it is only one of your stratagems to get strangers away without resorting to force.”

<i>Japanese</i>, (with a most funeral-like expression of countenance.)—“It is true: we never joke: I must lose my head.”

<i>American.</i>—“I am very sorry: we will all cry very much. But, as the wind is against us, we will sleep here to-night, and sail away to-morrow if the fog clears away. If the fog remains, we remain.”

<i>Japanese.</i>—“How many of you are there?”

<i>American.</i>—“Fourteen men, fourteen rifles, and fourteen revolvers.”

<i>Japanese</i>, (with great vivacity of manner.)—“All right. You are going in the morning. Don’t go back into the country. Go to sleep. We’ll be back early. Good-night.”

# Lippincott’s Pronouncing Gazetteer of the World,

[[subtitle]] Or, Geographical Dictionary,

<i>Comprising nearly</i> 2200 Pages, <i>including a greater amount of matter than any other single volume in the English Language; giving a description of nearly</i> One Hundred Thousand Places, <i>with the correct</i> Pronunciation of their Names, <i>being above</i> 20,000 <i>more Geographical Notices than are found in any other Gazetteer of the World.</i>

[[center]] EDITED BY J. THOMAS, M.D., and T. BALDWIN,

[[center]] <i>Assisted by several other Gentlemen.</i>

[[break]]

## Testimonials.

[[center]] <i>From the Hon. Edward Everett.</i>

“This work has been evidently prepared with great labor, and as far as I can judge, from the best materials and sources of information. . . . The principles adopted in ascertaining the pronunciation of proper names (as stated in the Introduction) appear to me correct. This is a matter attended with some difficulty and uncertainty, but it is treated with great ability, and in a very satisfactory manner, in your Introduction. I have no doubt your Gazetteer will be found an extremely useful work, well calculated to supply a want which must have been severely felt by almost every class of readers.”

[[center]] <i>From J. E. Worcester, L.L.D., Author of Worcester’s Critical Dictionary.</i>

“Having made some examination of ‘Lippincott’s Pronouncing Gazetteer,’ more particularly in relation to Pronunciation, I take pleasure in expressing a concurrence, generally, in what is said by the Hon. Edward Everett, of the value and excellence of the work. The difficult subject of the pronunciation of geographical names appears to me to have been attended to with great care, good taste, and sound judgment; and this feature of the Gazetteer must add greatly to its value.”

[[center]] <i>From the Hon. Robert C. Winthrop.</i>

“I know of no Gazetteer so complete and comprehensive. . . . I entirely concur with Mr Everett in the opinion he has pronounced of the work, and sincerely hope that it may receive an amount of public patronage in some degree commensurate with the magnitude and costliness of the undertaking.”

[[center]] <i>From Washington Irving.</i>

“I fully concur with the opinions given by Mr. Everett and Mr. Winthrop of its merits, and with their wishes for its wide circulation.”

[[center]] <i>From J. Addison Alexander, D.D., Prof. Oriental Languages and Literature, Princeton Col.</i>

“On the subject of Geographical Orthography and Orthoepy, this is not only the best, but the only systematic work with which I am acquainted. In examining this work, I have received an indirect and incidental, but very strong impression of its great superiority, in fulness and accuracy, to any Dictionary of Geography with which I am acquainted.”

[[center]] <i>From Dr. B. Sears, President of Brown University.</i>

“Your work must prove an invaluable guide to the student of geography, and if generally adopted, could not fail to remove that discrepancy and confusion which now so generally prevail, in regard to the pronunciation of geographical names.”

[[center]] <i>From M. B. Anderson, L.L.D., President of the University of Rochester.</i>

“I hesitate not to say that it is altogether superior to any book of its class accessible to the American public.”

[[center]] <i>From Prof. C. A. Goodrich, of Yale College, Editor ‘Revised Edition’ of Webster’s Dictionary.</i>

“Your Pronouncing Gazetteer of the World appears, from the examination I have given it, to be a work of immense labor, very wisely directed. I consider it as of great importance to Teachers.”

[[center]] <i>From the Hon. George Bancroft.</i>

“I have formed a very high opinion of the merits of your Complete Pronouncing Gazetteer; especially for its comprehensiveness, compactness, and general accuracy. I wish you the success which you so richly deserve.”

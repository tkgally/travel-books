“Tax Collector Again!—Ow—Dis Money! Dis Money!! Why Jacob no pay him taxes I wonder. Ah! Old woman say—‘Tan far—see better’—You no know Jacob yet—him a goin’ a Kingston fa de races, and him putten’ up all dat money fe buy saddle from John Madonald shop by de <i>Marning Journal</i> office, where all de sportin’ gentleman buy harness. Jacob no pay no tax till after races oba’!! I know it!!! Jacob too trickify!!! But mother—you know hearie what Judge Blair tell Jim de oder day, when him beg fa’ two mont’ fa’ pay Massa Thompson? Him say, <i>so sweet</i>, to Jim—‘No Jim—you will be spendin’ you’ money fa’ Christmas and de races—and you owe Mr. Thompson dis money now—No Jim! you must pay it in one month.’ Jacob betta’ take care wid him saddle. Pay tax fuss, and buy saddle noder time!!!”

A store-keeper on the north side gives this,—

“Notice—To young men about to marry! A magnificent brass 4 post canopy bedstead just landed, will be sold a great bargain.”

A dentist heads his advertisement with a ghastly woodcut representing a set of false teeth, surrounded with extracted stumps of all descriptions, and all the instruments of his craft. A house-owner offers, in a curious misprint, “For lease or rent, that desirable family country residence, Depass’-Delight (Famished).” Bitterly sarcastic are some in their terms.

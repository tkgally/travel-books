On our short journey up East Street we passed “The Pride of the East,” “The People’s Favourite,” with a rather handsome coloured girl seated in it; “It shines for all,” “The Army and Navy,” “Something must be done,” “Self-help,” and “The Good Time Coming.”

In due time, and after a prodigious amount of tumbling and jolting, we were landed at the door of a large and desolate-looking building, which the driver informed us was the “Hall” (for by this grand name are inns known in Jamaica) to which we were bound. We entered upon a court-yard paved with brick, around which half-a-dozen men and women were idly sitting. Two wall-eyed horses were being rubbed down; sable damsels seated on the ground were washing ewers and basins and towels. A rug was being shaken from a balcony overhead. A couple of turkeys, three enraged Guinea fowls, some poultry, two goats, and a lean dog were wandering about at their own sweet wills; and the filthy condition of the courtyard justified the presumption that this litter had not been removed for a week.

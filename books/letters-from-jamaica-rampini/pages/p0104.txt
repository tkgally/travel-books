<i>W.</i>—And that I was too d—n fast, and I was too mannish.

<i>C. P. S.</i> (reading)—“He further added—You are too d—n fast, you are too mannish, or words to that effect.” Where did this occur?

<i>W.</i>—Please, my worship, I’m not done.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Answer my question.

<i>W.</i>—This was at the cross roads, sa! At Mr. Abraham’s shop.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Were you on the high road at the time?

<i>W.</i>—How high road, sa?

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Queen’s road, sir?

<i>W.</i>—Hi! Yes, sa! Queen’s road, to be sure.

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Many people there to hear what was going on?

<i>W.</i>—Yes, sa! Plenty people was da (hesitatingly).

<i>C. P. S.</i>—Were they quite near?

<i>W.</i>—Far enough, sa! (After a pause)—But not too far!

<i>C. P. S.</i>—What do you mean? Could they hear what was going on?

<i>W.</i>—Hi! Yes, sa!

<i>C. P. S.</i> (to Magistrates)—I think that’s all, your worships! (To prisoner)—Do you wish to ask the witness any questions?

<i>Prisoner</i> (to witness)—George Edwards!

<i>W.</i>—Sa!

<i>P.</i>—Remember you ’pon you’ oath, sa!

<i>W.</i>—Hi!

<i>P.</i>—Did I an’ you ever have any confusion?

# A Midsummer-night’s Dream.

<sc>Scene</sc>: Maiko, on the Inland Sea of Japan. A tea-house with verandah overhanging the sea. A lovely moonlight night, and the lighthouse on the Island of Awaji shinning brightly. A merry party on the <i>tatami</i> and on the verandah—foreigners and Japanese. The rooms lighted by shaded lamps, and dinner served. Geisha playing <i>samisen</i> and <i>kotto.</i>

<verse>
“I know a bank whereon the wild thyme blows,
Where ox-lips, and the nodding violet grows;
Quite over-canopied with lush woodbine,
With sweet musk-roses, and with eglantine:
There sleeps Titania.”
</verse>

The tide has just turned and lo! hundreds of boats hoist their white sails and float away into moonland. Silently they disappear, like phantom Vikings, and the night knows them no more.

“To sing us a song, O Mamezuru San, please, condescend,” softly pleads a young Englishman.

“<i>Go chumon-dori</i>” (just as you like) answers she of the <i>samisen.</i> And with laughing eyes and pouting mouth she sings:

<verse>
“Maiko wa yoi toko,
Asahi wo ukete,
O—Yama arashiga
Soyo—Soyoto!
</verse>

“Maiko is a lovely spot, facing the morning sun. The winds blow softly from these holy mountains.”

“<i>Arigatö,</i>” murmurs the Irishman.

“<i>Dozo,</i>” says my friend Matsujiro, “thou, with the sad eyes, O Koto San, tell us the story of the wife of the high-priest of Hinomisake.”

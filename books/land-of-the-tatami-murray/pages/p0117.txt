I presented my letter of introduction from the genial Japanese Consul-General in Shanghai, Mr. Odagiri, at the Hiogo Kencho, and was received with great courtesy by the governor of the province, H.E. Hottori, than whom there is no more pleasant and unassuming gentleman on earth. Armed with letters to the authorities and the military commander, I took train from Kobe to Himeji, along the beautiful inland sea of Japan, passing the famous places of Suma, Shioya, Maiko, and Akashi.

The great castle of Himeji, erected by Akamatsu Sadonori in 1339, with its five stories, 800 feet high, is towering over the entire city, which is the capital of the province of Harima and contains 33,000 inhabitants. It was conquered by the Ashikaga family and finally presented to the great warrior Hideyoshi by the Shogun Nobunaga. In 1600 it was enlarged by Ikeda Terumasa, who gave to it the name Himeji. It has fifty turrets and forty-eight gates. The approaches are magnificent; behind the moats, full of clear, running water, are the narrow, winding paths leading to the low, iron-bound portal. Majestic oaks and pines are growing among the ferns; wild flowers on grassy slopes give faint signs of pleasuances of long time ago, where the noble ladies of the castle in bye-gone days spent their Decameronian lives. In imagination I heard the rustle of their silk-brocaded <i>kimonos</i> and the platter of the wooden shoes. This is the place wherein a famous <i>daimiō</i> retreated after a bloody battle; his faithful retainers holding the gate to the death, until their master had time to enter his castle, write out a contemptuous message to his conquerors and then give himself the “happy dispatch” with his razor-sharp dagger, leaving to his enemies only his hatred and his bleeding corpse. Such were the men of those times!

The interior is absolutely bare and empty. There are no gilded sliding-doors, screens or panelled ceilings in this old castle. The massive oaken beams, the solid stairs, the receptacles for the ancient warriors’ swords and armours, the dark chambers and dungeons, are there—and nothing else.

But the grown-up people were quite pleasant, and would generally respond to a smile. It was only at any little delay in the streets that it came across one how difficult it would be to escape in any sudden riot.

Mr. Grundy having been thirteen years in China, and many of them in Canton, could talk Chinese fluently, and he told us that he heard the people saying, “We must be careful what we say, he knows Chinese!” He took us first to the Foreign Concession, a well laid out piece of ground near the river, and a great contrast to the narrow streets of the native city. It was sad to hear there was no regular English chaplain in Canton, the foreign residents being quite content that a Nonconformist minister should put on a surplice and read the English service to them in their Church on Sundays. The Roman Catholics, on the contrary, had obtained leave to build a Cathedral in Canton itself, and its graceful spires and fine proportions made us long that our Church should be equally well represented. Their efforts had not ended with the Cathedral, for, at the time of our visit, they were building a second Church in the Foreign Concession.

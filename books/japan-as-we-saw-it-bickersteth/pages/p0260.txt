# Chapter XI.

[[subtitle]] Nara, or an Ancient Japanese Capital.

<i>Oct.</i> 29.—On the morning of the 29th, the weather being beautiful, my brother said he would take us to Nara, a famous and very picturesque city, at one time the capital of Japan, and only twenty-five miles’ journey from Osaka. The railway line to it had not been injured by the earthquake; but about half-way, just before the train ought to have entered a tunnel, we were all turned out, and had to go by jinrikshas for a mile or so. We then went on in another train which was waiting at the other end of the tunnel. The reason for this was curious. The line had been made by Japanese engineers; but their calculations had proved incorrect, and the tunnels they had made in each side of the hill had failed to meet in its centre. They were rapidly mending the defect, and a luggage train had already been through; but the mistake afforded a good instance of the desire of the Japanese to manage everything themselves, even before they are in a fit state to do without foreign tuition.

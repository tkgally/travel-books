We had an interesting discussion on the present state of thought in Tokyo. She said (1) that the present anti-foreign feeling was political, not deep-seated nor spontaneous; (2) that the adoption by some of the ladies of foreign dress (which the Empress still orders to be worn at Court) had been a help to them in their effort to improve their social position, though, of course, from an artistic point of view, it was to be deplored; (3) that the influence of Buddhism had practically ceased among the educated classes, and a widespread atheism had taken its place; (4) as to her own work and friends, that she had persuaded some of the ladies to attend fortnightly lectures on Christianity by the Rev. J. Imai Toshimichi at the English Embassy last Lent, and that a magazine she edited had a good circulation among them.

Evensong in S. Hilda’s Chapel, and a general gathering of the members of S. Andrew’s and S. Hilda’s Missions at my brother’s house, closed the day.

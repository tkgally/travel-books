From Glacier House we had a day and a half’s journey to Vancouver. The scenery was again extremely fine, though we passed many of the most noted places at night, as our train was seven hours late. Such a delay sounds alarming; but on the C. P. R. only one train starts east and another west each day, and it is hardly surprising if they lose as many hours during a journey of 3,000 miles as an ordinary train would lose minutes in one of three hundred.

Vancouver, which five years ago was “solid bush,” is now a bright, well-planned city, with broad streets and electric cars, and every modern convenience except good footpaths. The energetic vicar, Mr. Fiennes-Clinton, of S. Luke’s Church, claimed my father and brother at once for a missionary meeting, and Sister Frances, a fellow-passenger on the <i>Parisian</i>, gave us a warm welcome the next day at the Church House, where she and other ladies conduct a hospital and other self-supporting work among the numerous emigrants and settlers of Vancouver.

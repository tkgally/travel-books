The articles to be decorated, whether plâque, vase, or incense-jar, were made in many different materials—bronze, china, pottery, etc. The pattern was then drawn on them in tiny pin-holes and lines, into which gold or silver wire was introduced, the wire rising a little above the general surface, and all interstices being filled in with oil painting. The whole was then covered with thick clay and “fired,” after which it had to go through a lengthy process of rubbing in order to remove the clay. Fine specimens would require a year or more of this rubbing, but the result was beautiful. Beneath the highly-polished surface appeared the sketch of birds and flowers, most true to nature, and with every vein in the leaf, or feather in a bird’s wing, delicately marked by the wire.

Some of the men were painting from nature, and each one seemed an artist. Others were not working at cloisonné, but were covering china tea-pots and vases with pale pink and blue dust. Designs of birds, grasses, and flowers, would be afterwards added, and their work also would be “fired.” The different specimens of both china and cloisonné that we bought have been greatly admired.

After leaving the pagoda we returned to the narrow streets, and everything seemed so quiet that Mr. Grundy said we might walk for a little way, instead of being hurried along as before in our palanquins. We thus obtained a capital idea of the various trades carried on in a Chinese city, and, though the colours seemed gaudy, and the houses exceedingly dirty after Japan, we had an amusing time peeping into them, and making friends with their pig-tailed owners. We began with a gilt-thread factory and embroidery warehouse, and went on to a button maker’s, in whose shop we bought little bunches of five buttons, this being the orthodox Chinese number, as every man wears five on his coat. We stood outside the shops of the mandarins, gay with gilt umbrellas and bridal crowns, and looked into the highly-decorated eating-houses, and butchers’ shops, provided with any amount of pork and long rows of ducks with outspread wings and curiously flattened bodies.

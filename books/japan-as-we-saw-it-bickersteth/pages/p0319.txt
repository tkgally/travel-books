“These words touch on some of the gravest difficulties which beset the missionary of the Cross here. The Protean forms of unbelief and misbelief which have troubled the Church of God in England and Europe, all find their counterpart in Japan. Buddhism has now only a feeble hold on the educated classes here, and our missionaries are seldom harassed by open hostility. If it breaks forth it soon subsides. At Nagoya, where I spent three days shortly before the late destructive earthquake, I addressed one Sunday evening in the Mission-hall a crowded congregation, mainly consisting of non-Christians, and among the audience was the ring-leader of opponents who six months ago had threatened to stone the missionary and burn the hall: that night he and his wife sat quietly listening to the message of salvation. The contest now lies between Christianity and infidelity. Of sceptics the name is legion. And hence the urgent necessity that our missionaries should be men of culture, and able to expose the hollow pretensions of agnosticism.

Mrs. Bickersteth and I left Osaka at 11.30 on the 31st. A great many of the missionaries kindly came to see us off from the station, and an hour’s railway journey brought us to Kobe, a large treaty port on the Inland Sea, with nearly as many English residents as Yokohama. It had not suffered like Osaka from the earthquake, but some of the shops were badly damaged, and a good many chimneys had come down, including all those of our host, Mr. Foss. He and his wife and their little son of six years old had rushed into the garden, and had fortunately escaped any injury from the <i>débris</i> of the falling chimneys. Like our friends at Osaka, they certainly allowed no anxieties of their own to diminish their unbounded hospitality to us.

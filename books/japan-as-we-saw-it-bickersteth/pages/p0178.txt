We lunched on one of the three fine stone bridges that span the mountain stream near the temples, and returned to Kyoto in time to pay a visit to one of its most famous temples and monasteries, <i>Chion-in</i>, the principal monastery of the Jodo sect of the Buddhists.

As we entered the temple we noticed a great crowd of Japanese seated before the rail which shut off the central shrine, and saying their prayers in a loud monotone “<i>Namu Amida Butsu</i>”—(“We worship thee, great Buddha”). The same words were repeated by all, and this for hundreds of times, with an accompaniment of wooden clappers, struck with most unpleasant effect by some priests and women in the crowd. But a few minutes later all was changed at the entrance of two priests, dressed, one in gold and vivid green, and the other in gold and brown. The senior priest said a few prayers before the altar, and then advanced to a high red lacquer pulpit chair placed within the rail. He seated himself with great dignity, and having arranged his robes, pulled down a scarlet and gold <i>ante-pendium</i> before the pulpit desk, and struck one blow on a clapper with truly instantaneous effect.

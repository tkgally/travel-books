English needlework, singing, and drawing closed the list of subjects—certainly a very long one; but the girls of Japan seem quite as eager as the boys in their thirst for learning.

IV. The College Class. This is for very advanced pupils and only included one girl at the time we were in Tokyo, as very few are allowed to stay long enough to attain to its standard, their help being needed at home.

Miss Snowden then explained to me the course of religious teaching given in the school. It was briefly this: <i>Sunday</i>; a voluntary afternoon Bible class of enquirers, which was often well attended, but many girls were kept back by home opposition; a lesson on the Prayer-book in the evening to the senior Christian girls. <i>Monday</i>; a lesson given by the Rev. Imai Toshimichi to the Christian pupils. <i>Wednesday</i>; a lesson by the Rev. C. N. Yoshizawa to the non-Christians. <i>Thursday</i>; the senior Christian pupils attend Miss Thornton's class for communicants.

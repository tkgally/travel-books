# Chapter XVII.

[[subtitle]] Last Days in Japan.

<i>Nov.</i> 13.—We left Kumamoto at a quarter past seven the following morning, <i>en route</i> to Nagasaki, from which we were to sail for England. Mr. Brandram, Miss Riddell, and Miss Nott came to the station to see us off, and we had a pleasant journey from Kumamoto to Saga, with constant views of Shimabara, the mountain resort of the missionaries during the great heat of summer. At Saga, which we reached at 11 <sc>a.m.</sc>, we took jinrikshas, and travelled in them for thirty miles, to a large town called Ureshino, the same set of runners taking us all the way. It was a beautiful journey, especially when late in the afternoon we went through one of the mountain passes. Its sides were clothed with white camellia-trees in full bloom interspersed with scarlet maples and deep crimson wax-trees, the vivid contrast between the two being made yet more vivid by the never-failing Japanese background of dark fir-trees and evergreen oaks.

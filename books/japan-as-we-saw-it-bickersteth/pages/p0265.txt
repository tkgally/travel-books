This bell was probably struck every hour, like those we saw at Nikko and Kyoto; but we were not fortunate enough to hear it while in Nara. The tone of such bells is most melodious, and reminded us of that produced by “Peter,” the largest bell in the chime of Exeter Cathedral.

The Buddha was also well worth a visit. The actual image is fifty-seven feet high, and seated on an enormous lotus flower. It is made of small plates of bronze, and the comparatively modern head is surrounded by a halo of gilded wood. It is much more effective than the one we saw in Kyoto, the figure being complete, but both evidently lack the artistic beauty of the famous one at Kamakura, not far from Tokyo. We had not sufficient time to visit that Buddha while at Tokyo, but even from photographs could tell that its face possessed a dignity and characteristic self-concentration of expression which was wholly lacking in those at Kyoto and Nara.

We started the next morning at six. Romano, the Gallego who had come with us to Barcellos, accompanying us to Ponte da Lima. A charming and most interesting morning’s ride, through a country luxuriantly fertile and grandly picturesque—cultivated valleys, and green dells watered by streamlets; wooded slopes, vineyards, and orange groves; the bright-leaved citron and dusky olive, and in the distance, high hills of fantastic outline, where the cedar and cypress mingle their dark shadows with that of the still gloomier pine. In the foreground, magnificent oaks, chestnuts, and cork-trees, scattered singly or clustered in graceful groups, white cottages, half-hidden amidst flowering shrubs and the thick foliage of overshadowing trees, with maize-fields, and pasture-lands where cattle are grazing, form a picture of perfect sylvan beauty. There is a dewy freshness in the air; birds are singing gaily in every tree, and the morning breeze brings to us the odour of the lavender-bushes, the jasmine, and the banks of wild mignonette. The dark-eyed peasants of this favoured “land of joy and beauty” have a bright, happy look, and salute us with “<i>vivas</i>” and cheery “<i>bons dias</i>.” The further we proceed the more luxuriant the vegetation becomes.

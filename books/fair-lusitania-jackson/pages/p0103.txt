But it is time that we leave the market. The Gallegos, who, though standing aloof have kept a sharp eye on our proceedings, make out that we have bought a dozen or two of prawns (if you like prawns, come to Lisbon; there are famous ones here), and applications crowd upon us for the honour of carrying them. “<i>Obrigado; obrigado,</i> Gallegos. We intend to bear this burden ourselves. Sorry for your disappointment. But <i>paciencia, homens, paciencia;</i> it is a sovereign remedy for all disappointments.” Poor Gallegos! What would the <i>Lisbonense</i> do without them, common as it most unjustly is, here as at Madrid, to call out, with projected lip and with upturned nose, of anything or anybody unusually stupid or contemptible, “Ah, quite Gallego, that;” “Only a Gallego could beat that.”

Yet there are few houses of any importance in which a Gallego is not the <i>major domo</i>, and the keeper of the keys; and rare indeed are the instances in which he has been found unworthy of the trust reposed in him.

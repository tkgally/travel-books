Few persons, perhaps, are aware what beautiful specimens are sometimes produced at those glass works. At least, I am inclined to think they are little known out of Portugal, though the manufactory was established so long ago as the middle of the last century, originally by an Englishman named Stephens, and under the patronage of the Marquez de Pombal. My own knowledge of it, so far as relates to its production of articles of great artistic beauty, was acquired very recently, while visiting a Portuguese friend who has a fancy for collecting rare and beautiful china and glass. It happened that an Englishman, a <i>connoisseur</i> of those things, was there at the same time. Naturally his eyes wandered often in the direction of the cabinets containing my friend’s precious treasures, amongst which was a glass vase of exquisite shape, delicately engraved with wreaths of flowers and arabesques. After a time it was brought out for inspection and admiration, my friend remarking, as it was placed before the <i>connoisseur</i>, “I bought it in Portugal, but I shall expect to learn from you its real nationality.”

A long pause ensued. “Very fine, very fine,” he then murmured slowly: another pause—“I doubt its being either French or Portuguese. I saw one at Vienna something like it; but that was from one of their own manufactories. This, I should say, is probably English.”

“How very provoking,” said my friend, smiling, “I always believed it to be Portuguese.”

It is otherwise accounted for by there being an echo in the building which repeats seven syllables.

A pilgrimage to the tiny convent of the <i>Santa Cruz</i> is <i>de rigueur;</i> and no less so is a visit, <i>en passant</i>, to the <i>Peña Verde</i>, the <i>quinta</i> of its founder. Accordingly, <i>burrinhos</i> are again in request for this mountain ride, and as the companion of the former one has returned to Lisbon, I join three other pilgrims, more of strangers than myself, and thus complete <i>uma partida quadrada</i>—too many by two, unless your <i>tête-à-tête</i> companion be a most uncongenial one; for rarely is any clear idea retained of an object of interest—at least, such is my experience, when visited under the distracting influences of a numerous accompanying sight-seeing party. However, in this instance, it is but revisiting old familiar scenes, and besides, the <i>quartette</i> is a sedate one; two parts Spanish, one French, and one English—a very happy mixture; not sufficiently acquainted to be too hilarious, all “unattached” and not likely to become otherwise, yet each disposed, as it seems, to do his or her best to play the agreeable.

The Mondego is apostrophized in glowing language, as a goddess rivalling in beauty Hebe and Venus. Regrets are poured forth at leaving Coimbra—“<i>Minha bella Coimbra;</i>” and sometimes “<i>Os olhos escuros</i>” of a “<i>graciosa senhorita</i>” seem to make the parting from <i>Alma Mater</i> more bitter. There are fragments of poetic thought, and pleasant random fancies, enough to fill a volume. Truly the bright Mondego literally deserves the name so often given to it—the “River of the Muses.” And it has another special charm in the eyes of the Portuguese—it flows through no land but their own. From its rise in the Estrella mountains to its ocean bed at Figueira, “the lovely Mondego is a Lusitanian river.”

Perhaps the <i>Sê</i> should have given place to the university as an object of interest to visitors. However, as it was the <i>festa</i> of some saint, it was doubtful whether we could see it. The shops, too, were closed, and people generally seemed to be taking their pleasure out of doors. From our veranda we looked down on some very gay groups assembled in the large open space by the river side, and embarking in boats for the opposite bank. Numbers were gathered about the old stone bridge, which is being removed, as it impedes the flow of the river, so sunken are its arches in the sand. This is the second bridge that has thus gone down; the former one, they tell us, lies beneath the present one, deep in the sandy bed of the Mondego.

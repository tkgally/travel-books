# Chapter IV.

[[subtitle]] Down Hill.

I<sc>t</sc> was ten o’clock ere I reached the hotel. The walk back was down hill all the way, <i>caloroso bastante</i>, but I took it very leisurely; noting things new and old as I passed them.

Of the former, the pleasant little square dedicated to Luis de Camões could not escape notice. It stands immediately opposite the opening known as the Largo das dois Egrejas—the square of the two churches—and on a piece of ground formerly occupied by the miserable, tumble-down tenements of a tangled web of the narrowest, crookedest, dirtiest little alleys and passages in Lisbon; and one of its greatest nuisances, being close to the central and best part of the business quarter of the city. This eyesore was completely swept away a few years ago, and the vacant ground, then surrounded by good houses, was encircled by a handsome iron railing, and a row of those gracefully-drooping South American <i>pimenteiros</i>—pepper-trees—which thrive here so wonderfully. There are seats all round this inclosure; and in the centre was placed, in 1867—218 years after his death—the first statue of the great national poet, erected by his compatriots.

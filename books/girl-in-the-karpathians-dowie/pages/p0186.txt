The poor fellow was all alone at his work, and saw no one, save at long intervals a chance passer-by. The previous day, a large bear had attacked and thrown one of his oxen. When he went to fire his pistol, the bear left the ox and came at <i>him.</i> He was just able to dash into his hut and barricade the slender door, while the bear, after hanging about a little, went back and finished the ox, and retired pleased and satisfied.

The herd bemoaned the fact that he had no slugs for his pistol—small use they would have been, except to ease his mind; but I suggested small stones, with a wad of dry moss on each side of them, and loaded the old-fashioned horse-pistol in this way, while the herd looked on with delight. He had a fair stock of caps and powder, so we fired a pebble charge or two into a tree, and he was able to follow the course of the improvised bullet with his pen-knife some few inches into the trunk of a big fir.

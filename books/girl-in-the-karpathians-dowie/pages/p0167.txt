The tie found, and distrustfully surveyed, and smoothed, and adjusted, I added a marigold for my button-hole, and felt that everything that could be done towards a Sunday toilet had been done. Strong in the consciousness of excellent intentions, I started off. That church, of the usual square cross pattern, with little glittering cupolas of wood and gilding in the middle, stood upon a slight eminence, and the road wound up to it: it was <i>surrounded</i> by some hundreds of brilliantly dressed peasants, all talking and posing in the most picturesque groups. At first I just feasted my eyes upon them as though they'd been a bed of annuals; but when it came to going up among them I felt vaguely in my eight pockets for a rag of courage, and, failing that, for the merest remnant of “cheek”—and found nothing. Positively, it could not be done. Would any humble cockchafer, all cased in dull and dusty browns, care to alight upon a patch of poppies white and red?

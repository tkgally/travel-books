When Jura came up, having tied the disgusted horses among the green dockens, all barren of any sustenance as these were, there was a little desultory conversation, and it transpired that from ten to a dozen people, as well as odds and ends of children, shared the hut at night.

With the rashness of the unexperienced and foolhardy young person, I glanced round the dimensions of the rooms, and then looked up upon the brilliant sky. It was early—there would be three more hours of light and one of navigable twilight before the luminant star-freaked dark.

Shavings would be fine sleeping, but—all those people, and no stream big enough to cleanse me in the morning! I preferred not to face it, so unhitched the horses, rubbed the kids’ soft noses for the last time, got a drink of water, nodded to the solemn unconcerned old cooper, and got upon my way.

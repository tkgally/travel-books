# Chapter II.

M<sc>orning</sc> and the market began simultaneously in Kolomyja. Down one side of the square, beside the row of little plane-trees, a rough railway line came unexpectedly. It went some seven miles into the country with a jangle of some great bell, and brought in the peasant people and their clean country stuff.

There is just one way that the sun filters through the opal of a dim-gold June morning, and when I looked out of window he was letting himself down the slim threads of the mist-web in light lines of shimmering yellow. Already the two-storeyed houses—like Swinburne’s strong sea-daisies—“with lips wide open and face burnt blind . . . feast on the sun;” but they blinked their eyelids too, for there was a flutter of shutters and stripey awnings upon them.

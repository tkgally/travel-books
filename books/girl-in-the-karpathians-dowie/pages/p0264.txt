It was blood fever, induced by the burning of a million flea-bites and—other bites. Every morning in the mountains I had awakened to remove lice from the inside of my shirt. No doubt this is very revolting, and there are readers who will wish it had been passed over in silence. Why? They are no worse than fleas or bugs, for they do not bite as much, and they are much easier captured, and—well, I won’t expatiate, but, in effect, they have their advantages, and it is well to look upon them with equanimity, for sooner or later the peasants’ blankets and persons will provide a few.

Any way, I had a skin and blood fever. It crept gradually upward, and my heart surprised me by pausing and doing cannon-thuds at odd moments. I reflected a good deal upon these symptoms, and realised a new sensation in lying grievous sick upon a mountain-top with such a wealth of space around me.

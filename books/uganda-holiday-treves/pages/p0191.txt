The doctor’s garden at Nakuru would surprise those who hold certain theoretical views of horticulture in the tropics. It had as trim a lawn as many in England, as well as a display of English flowers—roses, balsams, phloxes, and carnations—that would compare only too brilliantly with the appearance of an English garden in the depth of December. More curious still, a charming little girl, the picture of health, was running about on the grass, with bare feet, and without even a hat to cover her fair hair. All keepers of gardens are given to grumbling, but the only complaint that the brown-skinned gardener—whose wages were 1<i>s.</i> 4<i>d.</i> per week—had to make was that the zebras often played havoc with the hedge.

Once during our stay at Nakuru, at the hour of 3 <sc>a.m.</sc>, the roar of lions was heard in the precincts of the hotel. It was consoling to be told at breakfast that these disturbers of the peace were not specially in search of hotel guests as prey, but that they had followed a herd of cattle which had been driven into the town from up-country the night before.

The distance by rail from Nakuru to the Lake Victoria Nyanza is 135 miles. The time occupied on the journey is eleven-and-a-half hours, therefore the pace, including stoppages, may be reckoned as about twelve miles an hour.

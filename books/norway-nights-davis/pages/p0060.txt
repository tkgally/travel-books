The banks are pleasing, but have no salient features, until at the northern end the hills rise in height with evident emulation of their neighbors in the Gudbransdal region; forests of birch and mountain ash grow to the water’s edge, and pine and fir reach to their summits. All day we saw drifting down to Eidsvold rafts of pine logs felled in mountain forests, where they are shot singly down the Lougen (a tributary of the lake), over boulders and cataracts, as far as Lilliehamar; there they are collected and bound together for a new itinerary.

We stopped for the night at Lilliehamar, a prosperous but not a pretty village in spite of its pretty name (Little Hill) and its commanding position above Lake Mjösen.

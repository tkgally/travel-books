But although the first effect of these Russian churches dazzles the eye and kindles the fancy, we recur with increased admiration to the “petrified music” of Gothic architecture, the perspective of “long-drawn aisle and fretted vault,” and the personality of sculptured saints and apostles.

As an example of the realistic impression of the latter, I recall the solemnly dramatic burial of Pius IX. in St. Peter’s, which I had the privilege of witnessing. It was at night, and the vast edifice was in darkness save for the constellation of lamps that always burn around the tomb of the apostle, a few great candles that stood here and there on pedestals, and those borne by the mournful procession. Their gleams fell fitfully on white monuments in the indefinite recesses of aisles and chapels, and cast Rembrandt-like shadows and lights on sculptured hierarchs and apostles.

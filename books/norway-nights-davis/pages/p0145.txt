Among these straits are many dangerous currents, especially the Maelström, which formerly we were taught was a horrible whirlpool sure to engulf every object that comes near it. Though this is now pronounced a fable, the fact remains that when the wind blows from the northwest and meets the returning tide in the strait, the whole area of the Maelström becomes so turbulent that no ship could live in it for a moment, although outside the sea may be as still as glass. To add to the danger, the depth of the bottom very suddenly decreases and the whole weight of water from the North Sea is suddenly compressed between the two cliffs that form the strait; a ship must therefore inevitably strike on sunken rocks or founder in the fury of the waves.

The façade is elaborately adorned with statues, carvings, and vases, which, with the pedestals and capitals of the columns, were originally covered with gold. In the course of a few years the rigors of winter made serious havoc with this decoration, and Murray says that the persons who repaired it offered the Empress fifty thousand pounds sterling for the fragments of gold-leaf, to which she disdainfully replied, “<i>Je ne suis pas dans l’usage de vendre mes vieilles hardes.</i>” There are no traces of this lavish ornamentation left except on the cupolas of the chapel, the interior of which is painted in bright blue and gold, displeasing to the eye. But the ingenuity of even that reckless age was taxed to the utmost in the long succession of salons, all arranged as if for an immediate court pageant.

In rashly attempting to pass through this valley to Sweden they were fatally surprised by about three hundred peasants, who hurled upon them from this hill an immense avalanche of rocks, stones, and roots of trees carefully collected for the purpose. A small monument and a tablet in the rock commemorate the disaster.

The road now rises gradually; the valley becomes dreary and desolate; there are no farm-houses for several miles through a desert of stones, sand, and <i>débris</i> from the mountains, whose only vegetation is stunted pines. The few laborers’ cabins are roofed with birch-bark, covered with turf which is often prinked with bright flowers and occasionally affords footing for a small birch-tree.

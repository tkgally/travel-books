Turning from the splendor of these “lamps of sacrifice,” we drove to the spacious Foundling Hospital, which stands in its own pretty park. We sent our cards to the Superior, who received us courteously, and conversed in fluent French while she escorted us through the well-ordered establishment. The rooms are all of great size, clean and airy, but very simply and scantily furnished. In the first one a number of neatly-dressed children, from five to ten years old, were at dinner. They rose and saluted us like little ladies and gentlemen as they were—orphans, we were told, of good birth and property, but without relatives, and therefore sent here until old enough for school-education.

No one can study Moscow for even a few days without feeling an interest and admiration that few cities can inspire. Others are palimpsests of change and perhaps progression; Moscow resists new inscriptions and clings to its sacred past. We were more than ever sympathetic with it when we looked down from the “Sparrows’ Hill,” four miles distant, on the broad panorama of its stately architecture with its golden fringe of minarets and domes. This was the view which first met the eyes of Napoleon when he stood on this hill, seventy-four years ago, and watched the advance of his eager battalions from three different points. But no Russian forces appeared to contest the way, and an ominous stillness pervaded the air.

[[cont]]into the enclosure we address the first person we see with “<i>Godmorgen; vær saa god, Heste</i>” (“Good-morning; if you please, horses”). No one properly appreciative of Norwegian civility would think of omitting “<i>Vær saa god.</i>” Travellers who call out peremptorily, “<i>Heste, strap!</i>” (“Horses, quick!”) are in disfavor among these quietly courteous people, who from the highest to the lowest never enter a shop or meet each other without raising their hats; who invariably thank a host or hostess after a repast in the words “<i>Tak for Maden</i>” (“Thanks for the food”), and receive in reply a hearty “<i>Velbekommen</i>” (“May it do you good”), and who even express retrospective gratitude by the frequent phrase “<i>Tak for sidst</i>” (“Thanks for the last time we met”)! When a vehicle overtakes another on a country road, the driver who desires to pass the other invariably asks permission, and apologizes for so doing.

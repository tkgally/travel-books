He is seated in a chair, dressed in a faded and worn blue-silk doublet and hose embroidered by the peasant-wife whom he loved so well,—“my heart’s friend,” as he called her,—who retained her influence by not changing her native simplicity or putting on airs after she became Tsarina. Peter’s massive canes stand near him, and he looks as if he were quite ready to seize one of them according to his wont and lay it across the shoulders of servant or officer who might offend him. A heavy iron one among the number might well leave a lasting souvenir of the irascible owner. There are more creditable tokens of his personality in telescopes, mathematical instruments, turning-lathes, and implements for wood-carving; also a wax cast of his face, taken while he lived.

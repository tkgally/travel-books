Although this canal is well worth seeing, it must be admitted that its scenery is tame and monotonous after the fiords and mountain-ranges of Norway. It were better if possible to visit Sweden first. We left the steamer at Köpmannabro, where we took the rail to Trollhatten, occupying two to three hours.

At the hotel restaurant here we ate our first Swedish dinner, somewhat unique in appointment at the railway stations and ordinary inns. The table was nearly covered with from a dozen to twenty “appetizers,” or small dishes of as many varieties of salt and smoked fish, sausages, and pickled vegetables, amid which brandy and liqueurs were conspicuous.

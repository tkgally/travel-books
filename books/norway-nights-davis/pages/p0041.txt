A well-shaded drive up the hills brought us to the little summer palace called Oscar’s Hall, which seems to have no <i>raison d’étre</i> except the view, the rooms being small and nearly bare. On the dining-room walls hang a set of paintings by Tiderman, depicting with great charm of color and form Norwegian peasant-life from infancy to old age. The favorite subjects of this popular artist were taken from his own people, and probably no one has given the world so faithful an idea of their characteristics. On his crowded canvas is no indistinctness or confusion; the grouping is artistic, and the varied and marked expressions of face an admirable study.

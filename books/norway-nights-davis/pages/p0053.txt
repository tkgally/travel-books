A very interesting object is the rudder, which is in perfect preservation on the starboard—originally the <i>steerboard,</i> or steering—side, which here is on the right of the vessel. It was evidently a sort of movable blade or oar, not attached to the ship, but to a projecting beam of wood, and could be hauled on deck when the oars were used instead of sails. The one mast was also movable, and when erected was placed in a hole made in a beam at the bottom of the ship, and secured a few feet higher up by passing through another orifice in a heavy log of curious shape. There were no indications of seats for the rowers, who may have plied the oars in a standing position, in manner similar to that of Venetian gondoliers.

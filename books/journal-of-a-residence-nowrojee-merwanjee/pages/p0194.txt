# Chapter XIV.

[[subtitle]] Houses of Parliament.

W<sc>e</sc> copy from a periodical work, called Chambers's Edinburgh Journal, the following account of the British Commons House of Parliament:—“The House of Commons consists of six hundred and fifty-eight members, viz. sixteen barons of the Cinque Ports, eighty knights of the shire for England, twelve for Wales, thirty for Scotland, and sixty-four for Ireland; and three hundred and forty-three burgesses for England, twelve for Wales, fifteen for Scotland, and thirty-six for Ireland. By law these members, in all cases, ought to be elected by the people without any undue influence either from the crown, the peerage, or any other power.

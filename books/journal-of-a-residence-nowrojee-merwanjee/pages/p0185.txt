There is also a large hospital at Charing Cross, one in connexion with the London University, one called London Hospital, another for the Jews; there is also one for small pox, another for fever, and one for diseases of the eye, where the first-rate men of the medical profession give constant superintendance and advice; one for women when in parturition; and, in fact, an establishment for every species of disease.

C<sc>hrist’s</sc> H<sc>ospital</sc>.—We had an opportunity of inspecting this excellent and useful institution through our kind friend Mr. Rice of Chatham Yard, who gave us a letter to the Rev. Dr. Rice, Master of the Grammatical School, who introduced us to Mr. Brooks, the Steward, and the latter gentleman conducted us through the establishment and gave us all the information relating to it.

This institution was founded in year 1552, by King Edward the Sixth, and the place where it now stands was a monastery called Greyfriars. Charles the Second founded the GrammarSchool and it has since been encreased by voluntary contributions, eight hundred boys are here lodged, fed, clothed, and liberally educated.

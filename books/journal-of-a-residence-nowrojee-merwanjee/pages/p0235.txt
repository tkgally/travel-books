The hall (for so I was taught to call it) had its white wall almost hid by a curious collection of prints and paintings. On one side was a large map of London, a plan and elevation of the Mansion House with several lesser views of the public building and halls, on the other was the Death of the Stag, by the happy pencil of Mr. Henry Overton finely coloured, close by the parlour door there hung a pair of stag’s horns, over which there was laid a red roquelaure and an amber headed cane.

“When I had declared all this to be mighty pretty I was shown into the parlour, and was presently asked, who that was over the chimney piece.

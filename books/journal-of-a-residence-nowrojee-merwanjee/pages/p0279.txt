The new rope house is 1110 ft. long, and nearly 50 ft. wide, in which cables of all dimensions are twisted, some of which are 120 fathom long, and 22 inches in circumference; the labour of making these cables is partly executed by machines.

The sail loft is 70 yards long, and the other workshops are of proportional extent. The wet docks are four in number, all of which are sufficiently capacious for first rates, two of which are roofed over. There is also a stone dock upon a much larger scale than any of the others. There also are six slips on which new ships are constantly built, all of which have roofs over them that cost about £8000. each. The smith’s shop (where anchors of all sizes, some of which weigh nearly five tons, are constantly made) contains about forty forges; the fires are blown by a blast of air from a wheel turned swiftly in an adjoining building. The dock yard chapel built for the accommodation of the officers, artificers, and shipwrights and their families, was completed in 1811, and is reported to have cost upwards of £9000. It is a neat plain structure of brick, and the interior is extremely light, elegant, and pleasing.

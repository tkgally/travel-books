# Chapter XX.
[[subtitle]] Chatham, Rochester, and Maidstone.

C<sc>hatham</sc> is very populous, but very irregularly and ill-built; it consists principally of one long street, extending along the banks of the Medway; Chatham, Rochester and Stroud being one long continued street of above two miles in length. There are very good shops, at which nearly every thing can be procured nearly as cheap as in London.

In Chatham there is an excellent institution founded by Sir John Hawkins,—almshouses for poor decayed mariners and shipwrights, these were built in 1592, and twelve poor old sailors, or shipwrights, have a good house, a quantity of coals, and a few shillings each per week to make the latter end of their lives comfortable.

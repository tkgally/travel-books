[[cont]]Macassar, the inhabitants have to eat buffalo flesh until the next consignment arrives. Naturally the hotels are the first places to proclaim the scarcity of the imported article by offering their guests the local substitute. Now, in consistency, buffalo flesh is equal to the rubber rings that teething children are given to chew to assist nature; possibly also in taste, but it is so long since I tasted the latter that I have forgotten. There is growling at the table as a matter of course, and a deputation goes to the landlady to remonstrate, because if the beef is sent away the only thing to fall back on is a plate of chicken-bones innocent of flesh. The deputation is graciously and sympathetically received, and the process is repeated next day.

Some fine sunsets were seen while I was on the ramble. The rain would stop for an hour or so, and let the sun appear for a few moments before he dived into the sea. William Black would have gloried in the sight. Small islands in silhouette against a crimson sky and the sea like a broad dash of blue paint laid on with a careless brush. By the arrival of the s.s. <i>Ban Poh Guan</i> this morning, a definite period is put to my unwilling detention in Macassar, and I have no tears to shed at the prospect of departure. I felt homeless and friendless when the <i>Teresa</i> left for Singapore: since that time the hospitalities of the British community and a Dutch gentleman to whom I brought a letter of introduction have removed that feeling, but the weather has been so wretched that the prospect of escape is hailed with delight.

M<sc>acassar</sc>,

<i>March</i> 18<i>th</i>, 1896.

[[cont]]Budge thinks yesterday’s experience sufficient. So Neustadt, Ernst, and I, accompanied by Mr. and Mrs. Gordon, start out briskly at 9.30 o’clock. Mr. Gordon and Ernst before long take to horses, which they have brought along, Mrs. Gordon to a Sedan chair. Neustadt and I save the honor of the party and continue the ascent on foot. The road is good and we get on swimmingly. The mountain scenery is again grandiose, high rocky peaks looking down on narrow valleys. We get a first view of the lake, after a two hours’ march, and another half an hour brings us to its shores. As we suddenly turn a corner on the shore, Fujiyama presents itself in all its grandeur, about twenty-five miles distant. The mountain is entirely covered with snow, and with the clear blue sky above, Lake Hakone at its feet, and surrounded by rocky peaks, the impression is imposing. We enter a pleasant Japanese inn, situated in front of the lake, and from its glass-inclosed piazza we get a fine view of the entire scenery. The guides have brought a tasty luncheon from our hotel, to which, with an appetite stimulated by the rare air and the good march, we do entire justice.

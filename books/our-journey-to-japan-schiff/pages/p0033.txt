Finishing our breakfast, the other members of our party come from the steamer to join us, and once more the automobiles are called into requisition to take us to the residence of the ex-Queen. Here we find the stately looking old brown lady, surrounded by some of her ladies-in-waiting, who, we understand, are relatives, also Prince David, one of her nephews by marriage, and we are told she is thus maintaining the appearance and dignity of sovereignty, which in reality has passed away entirely; the United States Government is allowing her a small competence, with which she can hardly do more than keep body and soul together. The ex-Queen continues, however, to be much beloved by the natives and respected by the white population because of her dignified life, which she dedicates to good work, as far as in her power.

Then I make a visit to Mr. Laughlin, the secretary of the American Embassy, with whom we have become well acquainted, and returning to the hotel, it is soon time to prepare for the receiving of our guests, it being the custom in Japan for guests to arrive at any time within an hour before the time fixed by the invitation. The hour of our dinner having been stated as at half after seven o’clock, the first guests begin to arrive at seven o’clock, and by half past seven every one of the seventy-two guests is there. These are as follows: Count Inouyé (Elder Statesman), Count and Countess Matsukata (Elder Statesman), Marquis and Marchioness Nibashima (leaders of Tokio society and related to the Imperial family), Baron and Baroness Shibusawa (the first banker of Japan and Korea), Mr. and Mrs. Nagasaki (Master of Ceremonies of the Emperor), Baron and Baroness Mitsui (head of the renowned Mitsui family), Mr. and Mrs. H.

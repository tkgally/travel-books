The work proves most interesting, especially to the ladies, who have never seen anything of this kind, and the afternoon hour has well advanced before we are aware of it. At the outset of our inspection of the arsenal, we are joined by Chief Director Katakoga of the station and a number of the high officers, who desert the Prince’s party to accompany us, evidently more interest being shown in us than in the Prince. Nearly all of these officers speak some English, most of them having been at the Armstrong and other works in England. We learn among other interesting facts that the navy yard and arsenal employ 27,000 workmen, year in, year out, that it was started some eighteen years ago, and that its great growth dates from the Japanese-Chinese war, after which Japan evidently immediately began to prepare for a conflict with Russia.

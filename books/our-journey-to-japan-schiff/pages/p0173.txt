We are all specially impressed that our coming to Japan and the exceptionally open manner in which we were received by Japanese society meant considerable for the ladies of Japan, who, as we were repeatedly told, had before never appeared so frequently in social gatherings until our coming. The hour having become late and the morrow being the day of our departure at an early hour, we retire soon after the guests have left, at about midnight.

<i>Tuesday, May 15th.</i>—We have to rise somewhat earlier than usual, as Mother and I, with Ernst, intend to visit Kamakura—where the rest of the party had already been—before removing to Yokohama. We leave the hotel at half past nine o’clock, and reaching the railway station, we find a number of friends awaiting us to say good-by, among them the Marchioness Oyama and her daughter, Mr. and Mrs. Soyeda, Mr. and Mrs. Sonoda and their daughter, Mr. Okura, Mr. Nakano, the President of the Chamber of Commerce; Mr. and Mrs. Takahashi, and others.

The younger, a Miss Kawai, graduated only two years ago, and we learn that she has been a classmate of our friends, the Goldman girls. The elder is the principal of a young ladies’ school, and the younger has become one of her teachers. Mr. and Mrs. Soyeda, with a young daughter, are most pleasant, and they have converted their Japanese dining room into European style for our reception. We are served with an excellent dinner, after which we go into the upper rooms, where, however, we have to sit Japanese fashion. As no Japanese luncheon or dinner party appears to be complete without an entertainment, we soon listen to some Japanese music, after which we are treated to the tea ceremonial, which is a special honor in a Japanese home. The evening ends with some free-hand painting of panels by a young lady artist. As we enter our carriages, a little package is handed to each guest, and upon opening these packages upon our return home, we find each to contain an exquisite silk embroidery.

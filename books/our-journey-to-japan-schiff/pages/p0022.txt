We meet a number of acquaintances, some of whom have letters of introduction to me, such as Dr. Biddle, of Philadelphia, with his wife and three daughters, accompanied by Mr. Laughlin, of Pittsburg, pleasant people, who, however, leave the ship at Honolulu, where they are to remain for two weeks before proceeding further. Of the Gordons I have already spoken, and gradually we become acquainted with most of the passengers, some of whom claim to know me by reputation, and introduce themselves. Thus a pleasant intercourse springs up; there are no cliques, such as are frequently found on the Atlantic lines, and life on board soon settles down into a comfortable routine. I generally rise with the first gong at 7.30, take breakfast at half past eight, and then start for an hour's walk, finishing this shortly after ten o'clock.

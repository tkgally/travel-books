On the way we pass through an old, very interesting-looking Buddhist monastery and then stop at the pavilion built by one of the Shoguns, who was the originator of the tea ceremony, several hundred years ago. The pavilion is in reality a complex of buildings containing some interesting objects and is situated in a fine park, with some charming landscape gardening. After continuing our ride, we find ourselves before long at the hotel again, in good time for dinner, after which the evening hours are passed in the usual way.

<i>Thursday, April 26th.</i>—It seems that in Japan a rainy day invariably follows two or three fine days. The morning is gray with a sprinkling of rain and we have nothing particular to plan. Mother and I visit some shops and later the Art Museum, not a very great affair, but we pass through some charming nooks of old Kyoto and return to the hotel for luncheon.

[[cont]]twenty-one guns from the men-of-war made the harbor reverberate, day fireworks filled the air with brightness, and a special tiffin at the Grand Hotel (where well-remembered, dusky Cingalese in tortoise-shell combs and flowing white draperies still displayed their wares on the veranda) was attended by scores of pleasant people. Early in the afternoon various feats of juggling took place on the lawn at the landward entrance of the hotel, and a baseball game was played on a fine field, between a Japanese nine and another made up chiefly of sailors from the war vessels. The Americans won, but the Japanese played well, their running, and sliding to bases, being particularly agile.

In the evening various events of a social nature went forward on shore and in harbor, brilliant fireworks and illuminations flashed over the quiet sea; and our national holiday became in retrospect dignified and invested with a certain elegance as well as crude patriotism.

But Kobe and the Inland Sea would not come to the Coronet, so turning her bow lightly toward the south, good-bys were said to the friendly harbor, and the pretty craft sailed airily off down the bay, and along the coast.

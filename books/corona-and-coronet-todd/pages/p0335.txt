Hakodate seemed to have wide and fairly clean streets, the houses built evidently with reference to more severe winters than prevail in the main island. Stones, nowhere used for paving, were chiefly lying on the roofs. A pagoda, a few barren foreign buildings, and the graceful lines of a temple roof broke the general monotony.

In the light of a fair northern sunset the Tairen-maru cast anchor, and was surrounded at once by the usual fleet of sampans; and with colors dipping to her from numerous Japanese steamers lying near, she proceeded to settle herself for a twenty-four hours’ stay in the beautiful harbor.

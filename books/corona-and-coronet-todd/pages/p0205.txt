# Chapter XV

[[subtitle]] Japan Revisited

<blockquote>
Thank God for tea! What would the world do without tea! How did it exist? I am glad I was not born before tea.
</blockquote>

[[right]] S<sc>ydney</sc> S<sc>mith</sc>, <i>Memoir</i>, i. 383.

D<sc>anger</sc> of disenchantment lurks about a return to distant lands whose memory has been for years enshrouded in rosy atmosphere. The halo idealizing our recollections down the vista of years may dissipate into nothingness once the actual comes again in sight.

Will the air be as sweet as in those dreamy retrospects? Will the beauty be as all-pervasive, the charm as haunting? All the mistily bright June morning when the Coronet was beating up Yeddo Bay between green shores on either side, this unspoken wonder seemed to hover half-unconsciously in the sunny air.

For nine years the name of Japan had recalled pictures of dainty little women thronging its streets in bright dresses and gay parasols, and of shops full of fine old swords and other relics of samurai days, sold for a trifle, as being of no farther practical value in the modern life then beginning to overwhelm the beautiful land.

[[subtitle]] Preparation

[[right]] O<sc>sgood</sc>.

P<sc>rofessors</sc> of practical astronomy must always invent. No mental graces or acquirements can supersede a mechanical bent, whereby instruments of whatever sort give joy and all telescopes delight, merely in themselves, and quite independently of their performance in bringing heavenly bodies a few million miles nearer.

Since in this generation we cannot make sun and moon stand still, lengthening of the precious minutes of totality can be accomplished only in two ways. One astronomer might take with him ninety-nine others, each with telescope, camera, spectroscope, or other bit of apparatus to ask his own particular question of the calm corona as it gleams against the silent darkness. Or one astronomer could transport a hundred telescopes and cameras, if only each could make its own record.

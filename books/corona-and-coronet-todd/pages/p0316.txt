As to affairs out in town—there seems to be a great scarcity of small change. It is impossible to get even a <i>yen</i> changed. To make a small purchase at the shop near here, I had to leave the <i>yen</i> and take a due-bill for the balance, to be traded out afterward. . . . The others still run me a little about mess affairs. At the table when anything appears they say, ‘What’s this coming, Chief?’ As I haven’t the least idea what it is, I say, ‘A little surprise for you to-day.’ When I <i>do</i> say anything to the cook there seems to be a misunderstanding. Seeing onions for sale in town I suggested that we have some occasionally. The very next night, as a last course, when we usually have canned fruit or preserves, he served up two stewed onions to each of us. They were very nice, but why didn’t they come earlier in the meal? . . .

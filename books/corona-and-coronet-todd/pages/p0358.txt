B<sc>ears</sc>, barbarous Ainu, the Imperial Agricultural College at Sapporo, and the fine harbor of Hakodate, where men-of-war of various nationalities are apt to take refuge from the summer heats of Yokohama,—these are all that average travelers in the Mikado’s Empire connect with the great northern island, Yezo.

Containing nearly thirty-seven thousand square miles, practically all forest, the number of its trees is estimated at one hundred and twenty-nine million,—evergreens in abundance, and oaks, elms, walnuts, birches, maples, and other familiar northern species, the handsome ash, and a tangle of interlacing vines.

Already I had twice crossed the Pacific Ocean by steam, yet its magnificent immensity was almost unappreciated until this voyage in a sailing vessel. Distance, if not annihilated, is at least mastered by latter-day triumphs of steam; but an indescribable charm lies in leisurely traversing enormous ocean spaces, dependent wholly upon the wind’s sweet will; and when breezes depart, lying idly upon a glassy sea with sails hanging limp, a friendly sun flooding the decks with warm radiance, and a sky of softest, deepest blue brooding close above, affords one of the conditions yet remaining in this swift century when time seems of no value, and may be defied with impunity. As a rule, the Coronet voyagers were good sailors. Chief, an experienced naval officer, enlivened each meal with new and thrilling stories, and one of his inventions was a boon to the company,—a chess-board of ribbons woven upon a cushion, with pins in the bottom of the pieces to insure stability whatever the slope of the deck. Chief and Mrs. Captain, the Doctor and General soon became conspicuous experts, and many were the hours absorbed in this game.

# Chapter XXII

[[subtitle]] Yachting in the Inland Sea

<verse>

Crowned by a golden dome of mist.

[[right]] L<sc>ongfellow</sc>.

S<sc>ince</sc> it had been found impracticable to take the Coronet into the Inland Sea and she had returned to Yokohama, the Captain had chartered a native steamer, the Miyako-maru, for the Sea trip, a craft somewhat larger than the Coronet, and carrying a crew of twenty-eight.

Built exclusively for native use, the fittings of the Miyako-maru, including staterooms, height of ceilings, and galley appointments, were diminutive in scale; causing much merriment in the company, two of whom occupied the saloon, where they could stand upright if directly under the skylight. Ordinarily that apartment would carry thirty or forty native passengers. But the vessel was entirely new, and satisfactory, even if, as in Japanese inns, lesser toilet arrangements were in the public eye, and confined to one small brass basin, a pitcher, and a tumbler.

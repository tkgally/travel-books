We loosed from our moorings an hour or two after dawn on a murky, miserable morning, and the outlook from our deck was a very dreary one. The few trees on the banks were leafless, the surface of the lake to the northward was a mass of ice, and over all rested a cold depressing haze. There were no other signs of navigation, and no boats of any kind in sight except our steamer and its barge. As the lake was forty miles across we could not see the other side, and the expanse of water which stretched away before us might have been mistaken for the sea; all the more so now because of the big waves which the wind had beaten up upon its surface, and which made our little steamer roll and pitch in a very uncomfortable way.

All day long we were tossing on the lake, and for several hours were out of sight of land; but in the afternoon, against the grey, cloud-covered sky, the farther shore appeared—a long, low bank, sentinelled by a few ragged, weather-beaten trees.

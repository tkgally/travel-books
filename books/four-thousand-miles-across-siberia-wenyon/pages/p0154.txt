A few years ago the doctor visited America, and, after his return, his friend, Count Tolstoi, asked him what was the best new thing he had met with there. The doctor at once replied, ‘The Temperance movement.’

To Tolstoi this idea was a revelation, but he eagerly embraced it, and set himself at once to organize the first Temperance Association in what is—with the possible exception of England—the most drunken country in the world.

A picnic had been arranged for that afternoon, and I was invited to join it. In the bright, warm sunshine we drove out to a narrow and secluded valley a few miles distant from the town. The deep, clear water of the Ingoda flowed with a strong current through it, and on either side rose rugged cliffs almost precipitously from the river.

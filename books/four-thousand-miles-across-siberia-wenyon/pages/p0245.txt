Each of these confluent rivers receives its own navigable tributaries, the general direction of whose course is east and west,—and thus regions, not only far apart, but in all sorts of directions, are brought into communication; so that at certain seasons of the year, with a suitable boat, the whole of Siberia might be crossed from the Pacific Ocean to the Ural Mountains, with only a few days’ interval of land travel.

The present voyage commenced upon the River Tom, from the Tom we passed into the Obi, from the Obi to the Irtish, from the Irtish to the Tobol, and, last of all, from the Tobol to the Toora—travelling for considerable distances up or down these five rivers without a single change of steamer.

A fine country lay around us as we steamed up the Toora River. Farmsteads and villages were continually in sight, surrounded by cultivated fields, or tracts of meadow-land on which large numbers of cattle and horses, and sometimes sheep, were grazing; and there was nothing but the architecture of the churches to remind us that we were not in some of the rural districts of Western Europe.

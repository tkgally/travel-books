The climate of Tchita is peculiar. There is little rain in summer, and though there is a long ice-bound winter of over eight months, snow is as rare as rain; and, except on the rivers, wheeled vehicles take the place of sledges the whole year round. This scarcity of moisture is so seriously detrimental to agriculture, that the amount of grain produced is not sufficient for local requirements.

In the city of Tchita, and throughout this province, exiles are very numerous. Almost all the people engaged in menial employments—farm-labourers, herdsmen, and yemschiks—are ticket-of-leave convicts; and the Trans-Baikal is, in this respect, what Botany Bay and Van Diemen’s Land were at the beginning of the century—a cesspool for the rascality and crime of the home provinces.

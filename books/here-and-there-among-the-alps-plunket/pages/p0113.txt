Leaving Botzen by rail we followed the line of the Brenner as far as Franzensfeste, and from that station, after a change of carriages and a pause of about an hour, we turned to the right, crossed a very fine bridge, and passing almost through the fort we entered upon that new line of rail which has been opened within the last few years, and which, bearing continuously in an eastern direction, connects the pass of the Brenner and the western districts of Tirol with the capital of Austria.

The portion of this railway over which we passed mounts for the most part through a long and very interesting valley, the Pusterthal, till at a remarkably slight elevation the watershed is passed, after which the road descends gently along the banks of the Drave to the small town of Lienz. This being the place of our destination, forms also the limit to my knowledge of this newly made line, but if the remainder of the scenery it presents be as fine as that through which we passed, it is one well deserving of notice, and very superior in this respect to the generality of railways, by which a journey is so often monotonous and uninteresting.

Of course this is all quite right, and as it should be, and the spectacle of an untidy garden or farm is one that can give no pleasure; yet I fancy that on most people the sudden return from the perfect freedom of the mountain regions to the artificial world in which we live produces at first a slight shock, and the wild portion of our nature asserts itself for an instant through all the conventionalities which custom and education have woven into the tissue of our daily lives. Certainly, as we drove that evening through the paved and lighted streets of Botzen, it was a comfort to me to think that in a few days we should again be back among the mountains, and, sitting on some mossy stone, might watch the last faint rose of the after-glow fade from their snow-clad heights.

All the same, though it is very well to have fine feelings about the sunset, as we linger watching it from the mountain side, it must be allowed, on the other hand, that civilisation, with all its trammels, has its advantages too, and at the end of a long journey a comfortable, well-appointed hotel possesses charms in the eyes of the wearied traveller.

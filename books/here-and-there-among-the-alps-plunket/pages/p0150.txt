I must say in their excuse, that they seemed to be very busy, and to have more on hand just then than they could well manage. With great difficulty we got one of them to listen to our request for two bed-rooms; preceding us into a billiard-room, she pointed to two doors on the opposite side, and said, ‘There are the rooms, you can go in.’ This, having received the permission, we accordingly did, and found on inspection that they had no egress except through the billiard-room, in which a number of men were playing and smoking. A strong smell of bad tobacco pervaded these apartments, and this, added to the prospect of enduring the noise of the billiard-players till probably late in the night, was too much for us, so returning to the <i>Speisesaal</i>, we, after some trouble, succeeded in catching one of the attendant damsels, and making her stand still while we stated our objection to the rooms. Could we have no others? we asked.

‘No,’ she answered shortly, ‘there are no other rooms on this floor.’

‘Well, on the floor above?’

‘No, there are no rooms for you there.’

‘Why not?’ we persisted. ‘The house is large, they cannot all be full.’

‘These are your two rooms,’ she answered, evading the question, ‘and you must be satisfied with them, you can't have any others;’ after which statement she walked off and left us.

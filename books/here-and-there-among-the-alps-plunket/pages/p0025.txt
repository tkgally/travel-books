Those stupendous peaks that they have reached are to us forbidden ground; we may look up to them admiringly, longingly perhaps, but on their dizzy heights our feet can never stand; they are, like so many of the high places of this world, the reward and the privilege of those who excel, and mediocrity cannot obtain them. But because the foremost ranks are unattainable, it is no reason that one should not try for the second, and we, or any other ladies who choose to make the effort, may leave the great mass of idlers in the hollow behind us, and rising above the hot mule paths, may take to the snow and ice, and with the fresh glacier wind blowing cool on our faces, we may mount with alacrity as high as our limited powers will permit us.

Of course for those who aspire at all above the beaten tracks, it will not do to start very early in the year; the spring snows must melt, and the spring avalanches must fall before it is safe to venture too near them, and having reached Switzerland rather sooner than usual in the summer of 1874, we determined to take up our abode for a short time at Thun, and there await the beginning of July.

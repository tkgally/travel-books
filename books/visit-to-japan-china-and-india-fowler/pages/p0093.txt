After dining with Mr. Boyce, I went on board the “Thales,” Captain Painter, which did not, however, sail till next morning, the 27th. This was owing to the state of the tide on the Woo Sung bar. My host was going up the Yangtsee, but his steamer, though much larger and grander than the “Thales,” was built for the river and not for the sea, and, drawing less water, got off earlier. Steamers are the universal mode of locomotion in China, and where they are unable to run travelling becomes difficult. The fares are high, that of the Peninsular and Oriental Company and the Messageries Maritimes being $80 from Shanghai to Hongkong. This, of course, I paid; but in my case it seemed very reasonable, as it involved a week’s board in the steamers of Jardine, Matheson, and Co. to Foochow, and of Douglas, Lapraik, and Co. to Hongkong. In addition to their war-junks the Chinese are constructing an immense fort at Woo Sung to defend the entrance to the river. The navigation of the mouths of the Yangtsee is rendered difficult by immense banks of sand and mud, while the shores are very low.

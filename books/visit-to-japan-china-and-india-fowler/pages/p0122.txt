As regards Cuba, we have the evidence of Commissioners appointed by the Chinese Government to inquire into the condition of Chinese coolies in that island. These gentlemen, one Chinese, one Englishman, and one American, Messrs. Ch-en-Tampin, Macpherson, and Huber, have made an elaborate report dated October 20th, 1874. They say, “All investigations of Chinese were conducted verbally and in person by ourselves. The depositions and petitions show that eight-tenths of the entire number declared that they had been kidnapped or decoyed; that the mortality during the voyage from wounds caused by blows, suicide, and sickness, proves to have exceeded ten per cent.; that, on arrival at Havanna, they were sold into slavery, a small proportion being disposed of to families and shops, while the large majority became the property of sugar-planters; that the cruelty displayed towards those of the former class is great, and that it assumes, in the case of those of the latter, proportions that are unendurable.

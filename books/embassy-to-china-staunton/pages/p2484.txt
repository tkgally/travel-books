Both Chinese and Tartars value themselves on their skill in the use of this weapon. They hold it somewhat obliquely in the left hand. The string is placed behind an agate ring upon the right thumb, the first joint of which is bent forward and kept in that position by pressing the middle joint of the fore-finger upon it. In this situation the string is drawn till the left arm is extended, and the right hand passes the right ear. The fore-finger is then withdrawn from the thumb, which instantly forces the string from the agate ring, and discharges the arrow with considerable force. This armour consisted of an helmet of iron, in the form of an inverted funnel: the crest corresponding to the pipe of the funnel, stands six or seven inches above the head, and terminates in a spear. It is surrounded by a red tassel. The neck is secured by a piece of cloth stuffed, quilted, and studded with iron, which hangs forward round the face. On the body is an upper and an under dress of cloth, also quilted and studded with iron; the latter reaches below the calves of the legs; the former only a little below the waist.

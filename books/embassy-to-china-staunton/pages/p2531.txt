Water fowl are particularly plenty in this part of the country, and form a part of its resources. They are caught by various stratagems; but as they are never openly molested, or alarmed by the explosion of gunpowder, they are remarkably tame and familiar. The common white-necked crow of China, <i>corvus dauricus</i>, will suffer itself to be approached within two or three yards before it commences to take wing.

As the country receded from the Poyang, and the travellers got out of the province of Kiang-nan into that of Kiang-see, the prospect became more pleasant, and the lands more fertile. The usual populousness recurred. The progress of the Embassy was however slow, and the yachts were now moving against the current of a rapid river which came from the south-west. Inundated fields were here succeeded by extensive plantations of the sugar-cane. To apply the system of irrigation to those plantations, which were on a sandy soil far elevated above the river, it was necessary to raise the water to heights which could not be attained by the means hitherto mentioned to be practised by the Chinese.

Among the more innocent pleasures of both sexes were operas, plays, and masquerades, except when suspended on account of the indisposition of the queen of Portugal. Company often met at a public garden situated by the sea side, and at one extremity of the town. This garden was laid out in grass plats, shrubberies, and parterres, interspersed with spreading trees, together with arched alcoves or bowers of wooden frame-work, painted green, and decorated with passion-flowers, jessamines, and other fragrant plants. In these recesses, during the dry season of the year, the gay society of Rio, after taking exercise in the evening in the walks, and often after hearing songs and music, sat down to partake of banquets, accompanied, sometimes, by music and fire-works; and the company in this manner often protracted their pleasures far into the night. Towards the middle of the garden was a large fountain of artificial rock-work, with figures of two alligators, of no mean sculpture, spouting water into a marble bason, in which aquatic birds, well imitated in bronze, appeared sportively indulging themselves.

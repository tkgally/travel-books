The situation of these islands agreed, indeed, exactly with the sketch which had been given by the pilot; and behind them were perceived the masts of many junks. The place, however, was not examined, from the little hope that was entertained of finding any security for large ships there. A very slight view of the land surrounding the gulf was sufficient to shew that no secure harbour was likely to be found upon its shores. A good harbour is generally formed by the means of massy rocks, or at least of high and considerable mounds of compact earth, thrust forward by some irregular operation, or in some convulsion, of nature; and leaving within them an inlet of the sea, which those projecting points may protect from the violence of the winds and waves: whereas the country which terminates this gulf is utterly devoid of any solid and elevated masses capable of becoming a bulwark, behind which there might be a safe retreat for shipping.

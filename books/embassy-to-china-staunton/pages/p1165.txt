[[side: Teneriffe.]] Teneriffe is about seventy miles in length, and its mean breadth is about twenty-two miles; its surface amounts to one thousand five hundred and forty square miles, having, at an average, about sixty-five persons to the square mile. The number of acres is nine hundred and eighty-five thousand six hundred, which is, upon an average, about ten acres to every individual in it, the number of inhabitants being calculated to be nearly one hundred thousand; of whom, however, there is, annually, a drain to the Spanish colonies in South America, to increase the number of foreign settlers there, for the purpose of counterbalancing the multitudes, still remaining, of the natives, and for supporting the dominion of the Spaniards over them. The poor of Teneriffe are persuaded easily to migrate, as the proprietors of the land do not give them sufficient employment throughout the year; and they have not the resource of manufactures, except a trifling one in silk, chiefly, stockings. The price of labour is under a shilling a day; and, beside corn and roots, the principal food of the common people is confined to cod-fish, caught on the neighbouring coast of Africa, or imported from North America.

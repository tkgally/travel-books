Beside the wheat flour for making the soft bread or cakes, by the means of steam, as mentioned in a former chapter, much of it is used in forming the substance called in Europe vermicelli, much relished by the Chinese.

To each cottage is attached a small spot of ground for raising culinary vegetables; and about each are a few hogs and poultry, particularly ducks. The carcasses of many of the latter are spread out, salted and dried, and sent in this state as an article of commerce to many of the great cities. The art of hatching ducklings by artificial heat, has long been familiarly practised by this people. They were not, indeed, taught by the ostrich leaving its eggs in the sand to be hatched by the sun, that bird not being known to be indigenous to China, but perhaps by the crocodile, small species of which are found in some of the southern rivers of the empire.

In this part of the country, near the fields of wheat, were several patches of buck-wheat now in full flower. The produce of this species of polygonum is applied to the same uses as other grain, and its flour is remarkably fine and white.

Of the island of St. Jago “the south-west side only,” Doctor Gillan observed, “had any appearance of volcanic formation. About two miles from Praya bay is a very high hill, altogether composed of clay and sand, on which not the least marks appear of the action of fire. About six miles, near the road, from the town of Praya to that of St. Jago, is another hill, almost entirely composed of rich iron stone, of a deep blue or purple colour, formed of clay, calx of iron, and siliceous earth. In the rocks opposite the governor’s house, near Praya, are several narrow perpendicular veins of white spar. The beach is covered with a fine siliceous sand.”

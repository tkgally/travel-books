While he was formerly his Majesty’s Envoy at Petersburg, he concluded, with that court, a commercial treaty to subsist for twenty years, on such advantageous conditions, that the sovereign of Russia, at length, perceiving the balance to be too much in favour of Great Britain, refused, for a considerable time, to renew it. His Lordship had other occasions, afterwards, to exert both address and prudence, for the public advantage, in different quarters of the globe. He had since, indeed, declined the government-general of Bengal, of which the power and the profit exceed, no doubt, that of any other within the recommendation, or the gift, of ministry. But a visit to the court of Pekin was, on other accounts, so uncommonly inviting; it presented so much to an ardent and inquisitive mind, that, upon the first intimation, he readily engaged in the attempt.

# Chapter VI.

[[subtitle]] Tokio—Shinto Temple—Ueno Park—Museum—Asakusa Temple, and Park—False Fusi Yama—Mukojima Island—Cherry Blossoms—Ginza.

F<sc>eeling</sc> that we had visited the places of greatest interest, we turned our human teams towards a hotel, on our way passing by the Shiro or imperial castle with its massive wall and tower, its huge gateways and deep moats, not only obtaining a fine view of the same, but also of a large portion of the city. The gardens within the castle enclosure were prettily laid out, and many foreign fruits and flowers were cultivated in abundance.

The Tokio house was soon reached where, after a refreshing meal, excellently cooked and well served, we again loaded our carriages, put spurs to our steeds, and rolled along to the Shinto temple, which was erected in memory of the soldiers of the Imperial Army, who were slain in different wars for the defence of the Mikado.

Adjoining this temple is the Tokio race-course, and a park, both favorite lounging places where many Japanese resort to witness fireworks, which they manufacture and exhibit to perfection.

Where but in Japan could be seen theatres walking the streets, the actors unconcernedly playing their parts, the audience applauding, and the small boy shouting in the gladness of his heart? On one of the platforms was a tea-house in full play, the girls with painted lips and eyebrows shaved, hair elaborately dressed, and their silk wrappers and gorgeous <i>obis</i> (belts) tastefully arranged, all busily at work making and drawing tea, which with candies and fruits was passed to the crowd.

Then came a representation of all the trades in Japan. There were men from Satsuma plying their trade and polishing the beautiful ware of their province. Kaga and Hizen were not behind with their wares, which with the old and new porcelain of Imari filled the cars with beautiful effect. The Cloisenne ware was displayed from a car shaped like the heron or bird of longevity, and was one of the prettiest displays of the wonderful procession.

Other cars covered with food were, throughout the line, free for all to partake, but it seemed as if the abundance never grew less. In the rear were the coal diggers from Nogasaki, and the rice growers of the interior, with their scanty clothing and bungling implements of husbandry. From Hokadate came the hairy men, or bear worshipers of Yeso, leading their four-footed gods as quietly along the crowd as if they had been reared among a city populace.

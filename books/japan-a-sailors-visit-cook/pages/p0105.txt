The great majority of songs are sung by girls, and there are but few families in which the wife and daughters cannot sing and accompany their songs upon the koto or samisen. The men are not less fond of music than other nations and hence, no doubt, has arisen the custom of having at all the tea houses of pretension, professional singers to entertain the guests with vocal and instrumental music. The music usually commences with a low chant, weird and wild, which after a breath or two appears to have run down, then will suddenly start afresh like a cheap mechanical toy which has just been wound up, and the same operation is continually repeated, and in a short time you begin to doubt if any American ever admired Japanese music.

The religion of Japan is divided into two great parts or societies, which are denominated Buddhism and Shintoism. The first was evidently introduced from China, and is full of moral dogmas which are far from being cultivated by its believers. The Shinto religion is also claimed to have come from China or India by some, but as a general thing it is looked upon as a Japanese invention. The term “Shinto” is said to mean “The way of the Gods.”

The disciples of Shintoism say that the Chinese were a very immoral people, and Buddhism was invented by them as a check upon their great immorality, but that the Japanese being a good people, there was no necessity for any system of morals, as everyone acted aright if he consulted his own heart.

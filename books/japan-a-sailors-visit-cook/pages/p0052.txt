The proprietors will throw up ranges of miniature hills, trace winding paths about their base and to their summit, suggest beetling crags, purling streams, and silvery cascades by artistically selecting and arranging stones, and then give life to the scene by wonderful evergreens, gnarled and knotted trees, with here and there patches of brilliant golden and crimson flowers springing up, as it were, to brighten the tiny landscape at the right season of the year.

The villages are bright, with scrupulously neat and clean houses, each with its attached garden, and often a tiny pond of gold and silver fish, while the whole is made gay with gaudy flags and brilliant lanterns.

Early in the forenoon we arrived at the traveler's Mecca, the city of Tokio, the capital of this Island Empire.

Our guide soon engaged jinrikishas and men to take us about to see the wonders in the chief city of this eastern land. The city, which is over nine miles in length and eight in breadth, is built on an immense plain, so that the eye could at a glance survey the whole space if the vision would permit, and at every turn there would open to view new features in landscape, water, castles and massive edifices with which this prairie city of the Flowery land, is everywhere studded.

Although so much has been written about this Yedo of the past, it is in reality not an ancient city, but can claim an antiquity dating back no further than 1600, when it contained only one small castle surrounded by a few scattering villages spread about upon its flat, even surface.

And now that all are asleep I will append an incident which happened when in Kioto a year ago, and which, although not relating in the least to our travels, will be appreciated by many a lover of America, and in a manner illustrates the character of a certain class of all nations who are wandering through this empire, where they have acquired the significant name of Globe Trotters.

While spending an evening in Kioto with another Yankee captain, we strayed to the billiard room and engaged in play at the one table which the hotel afforded. Soon after, four English tourists came in, two of whom were gentlemen, while the other two were inclined to be snobbish. The two latter looked upon the game for awhile with disgust, wishing to have the table themselves, and evidently by their remarks and actions were looking for some way to mildly insult us. At last one of them backed up to the fire which was in an open grate, lifted his coat tails, with a deal of satisfaction, and remarked so loudly that we could not fail to hear—“This is the only thing an Englishman ever turns his back upon—a good fire in a grate.”

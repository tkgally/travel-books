# Chapter XXII.

[[subtitle]] Paolo.

[[subtitle]] Scene of the Persecution of Waldenses—Snow—Roosting with Cocks and Hens—Peeping Eyes—Hills of the Robbers—Tale of the Tower—Sanctuary of San Francesco di Paolo—Chapels of His Miracles—A New Friend—A Horrible Story—The Monks—National Manners—Departure.

T<sc>he</sc> distance to Paolo is twenty-one miles, and a public conveyance goes three times a week from Cosenza. The way at first lies through noble forests of chestnut-trees covered with mosses, then descends one of the hills where took refuge the Waldenses during their persecution in the fourteenth century; when part of the inhabitants, having become Protestants, were immediately exterminated by sword and fire: to this day no subject is allowed to belong to any other than the Roman Catholic faith. The road descended continually for twelve miles, and the downward trotting must have shaken the portly horses dreadfully; the carriage hung on lofty springs, looked like an old dowager in a monster hood, shaking her head over them.

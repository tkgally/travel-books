The peasants have taken refuge on the various little hill-tops, but the vapours rise and poison them there; their ghastly appearance in Lentini and Carlentini (the latter was built by Charles Quint) speaks of their sufferings and ague. This lake concludes the list of what the Sicilians consider the three wonders of the island before mentioned.

All the hills are now passed, the country around gets flat and dull, the moon’s light brings out no feature, throws no shadow, till a low ridge of rocks appears on the wayside. Separate at first, they afterwards join and wall in the road; dividing again, the openings show others cutting through the grassy plain. Where the moon strikes full upon them cavities appear boring their dull sides; for miles they stand monotonously continuing, till the coach pulls up, and—

“That is the tomb of Archimedes,” says Don Gregorio; “he lay in one of those top-heavy rocky piles which cower in the moonlight.”

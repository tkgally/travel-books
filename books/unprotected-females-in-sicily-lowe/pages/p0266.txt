A ladder of steps led up to a closed calash deeply encrusted with brass, and constructed with panels decorated by mythological subjects; in front projected a vast hood, covering the coach-box, and four jolly cart-horses with crimped manes and tails, were harnessed to it abreast. When this equipage spluttered up to the Albergo with two drivers, an armed guard, and three boys hanging on, the excitement was intense. At starting, as the four animals could not squeeze past in a line, two were taken off, and after turning the corner, put-to again, that they might gallop through the principal street, to the terror of the pet lambs and goats fastened at most of the doors. As usual, some gentlemen escorted us part of the way, then resigned us to the care of the “Urban guard,” who being attired exactly like his fellow-peasants, only looked more the bandit from bearing a gun.

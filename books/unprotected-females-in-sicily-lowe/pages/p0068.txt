The animals share the universal leanness; striped bull-dogs large as Cerberus, and which seem the pet race of the island, hanging their desponding heads and tails at the extremities of bodies unequal to the weight; oxen, whose monstrous horns are the only antidotes to the charm of the “jettatura,” or evil eye (and as such nailed up in all orthodox court-yards), gracefully bear their own hides on rows of starting ribs, like hoops of crinoline: the horses and mules, though trimmed with ribbons, bells, mirrors, pictures, and feathers, as if they had just been tarred, showed through their finery, like their owners, the leanness of their souls. Cruelty to animals seems unchecked by any authority; donkeys with clipped ears, and birds with eyes put out, to sharpen their pipes, are usual sights.

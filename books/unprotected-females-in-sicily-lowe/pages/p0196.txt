Don Vicenzo walked back with us to Syracuse, past the tombs, round which browsed the goats, while yellow evening crept over the distant town, of which I tried to take a melancholy sketch.

A steep street led to Arethusa’s premises. That lovely nymph is now a cistern built in between four walls, and surrounded by washerwomen in the daytime. Those who wish to see the original fountain must look through an opening left in the masonry for peepers, and they will find the clear water coming from the rock, half hid by hanging mosses, once the drapery of Arethusa, clothing it as gracefully as that did her fair limbs in life. It was night; no desecrating hand stirred the waters; her Alpheus’ self might have come and seen his face reflected in the bosom of his limpid nymph.

In summer their great pleasure is driving up and down through the Grotto of Posilippo, then supping in a boat on the sea.

The great San Carlo theatre seems to require the riches of London or St. Petersburg to make the quality of the performance equal its size. It has, however, presented a peculiar novelty of late years in the ballet department, by clothing the pretty legs of its dancers in semi-Turkish blue silks. The really national playhouse is the San Carlino, where some witty piece is always represented under the name of “Poncinello,” and the acting is so exquisite, that it tells its tale through all the mazes of the squeaking Neapolitan dialect. The subject of the piece was appropriately “Il Forestiere” (The Stranger); not our lugubrious Mrs. Haller, but a young man being initiated into some of the funny national ways, which were quizzed <i>con amore</i>. On paying a visit to his “intended,” the poor man was dismayed at not being able to get any other word out of her than “<i>per servirla</i>”

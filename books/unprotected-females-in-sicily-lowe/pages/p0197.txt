Don Vicenzo said we now had seen all the curiosities of Syracuse. For those who wish deep explanations, there is a professional guide who accompanies travellers to the ruins—quite a gentleman in brigand-hat and cloak, and who has a museum of antiquities, with a Venus of Phidias, but without a head, perhaps an Anne Boleyn of her day.

Turning sharp up Arethusa Lane, the Piazza flashed upon us with a thousand lamps; the Minerva Cathedral was a perfect constellation of luminaries. In and out of its portals pressed a crowd; we entered; the crimson, the gold, the silver, glittered and glowed to the light of countless wax candles in crystal chandeliers, of which the last was just being slowly wound up midst the cheers and hurrahs of dense masses. Santa Lucia stood on Minerva's altar, arrayed in a ball-dress; her silver face and arms, polished up, looked better by artificial than by pure day-light.

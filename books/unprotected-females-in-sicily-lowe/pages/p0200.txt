# Chapter XV.

[[subtitle]] Voyage Back to Catania.

[[subtitle]] The Scenery of the Coast—Excitement in Catania—Feast of the Mother of the Madonna—Explosions—A Warm Bath in Sicilian Style—An Earthquake—An Eruption.

F<sc>rom</sc> Syracuse to Catania is a beautiful voyage of three hours, past Agusta, which became an island-town in the irruption of 1693; so they have been obliged to build bridges there. The mountains towards Messina at times peep out; that headland which stands its ground so gracefully is Taormina—an excursion in store. Arrived at Catania, we were surprised and rather shocked, being in touring garb, at finding the streets full of smart carriages, whose occupants were arrayed in every exaggeration of Parisian finery, and tried to hide ourselves behind the porter who carried our two modest little bags to the Caffè Siciliano, where we meant to refresh ourselves before getting a carriage to Nicolosi.

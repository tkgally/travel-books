# Chapter XVI.
[[subtitle]] Journey to Messina.
[[subtitle]] The Bay of Ulysses—Acis and Galatea—Phœbus’ Sheep—The “Chestnut of a Hundred Horses”—The Oldest Tree in the World—Fudge—A Hitch in the Journey—Aggravating Simplicity—Consoling Beauties and Splendours of the Coast—Custom-Houses.

W<sc>ith</sc> the dawn we were off, and ere the sun had fully risen, were passing the Bay of Ulysses, in whose pellucid waters still stand the Lava Isles, hurled at his departing ship by the malignant Cyclopes. Mid-day brought us to Riposto, the scene of another stone-throwing of one of the same naughty monsters, Polyphemus, and which so disturbed the loves of poor Acis and Galatea, who roved through these very olive groves, and tended the great-grannies of those merino sheep now grazing among them. Talking of sheep, at little Fiume di Nisi, sly Eurylochus stole Phœbus’.

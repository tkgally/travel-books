Up! for another run as far as the outside shoulder of the Montagnuolo. We began to find the descent of a mountain has also its difficulties. Our knees were commencing to bend, and feel sadly tried by this new exertion. We endeavoured, as much as possible, to place our feet in the old steps, as it saved fatigue; the guides were bent on keeping the same path, that they might find a shawl dropped on going up;—here, reader, I will remind you of a homely, but useful habit; always to knot your wraps together when taking them off, as, thrown carelessly across the arm, they are sure to drop, and you can ill afford to lose anything in travelling. We soon required all our coverings again; the missing one was not retraced, and should a future Ætnean mountaineer find it drifted in some corner, and preserved by the pure air, he must keep it in remembrance of his piloteeress.

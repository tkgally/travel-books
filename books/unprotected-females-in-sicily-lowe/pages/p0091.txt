# Chapter VII.
[[subtitle]] Ancient Agrigentum.

A<sc>t</sc> ancient Agrigentum two temples stand, raised on the rocks from whence they were hewn, their roofless columns clear against the African sky; beautiful in ruin as their foundations are majestic. The other glories of the city lie prostrate, save where a few tottering cornices hesitate to follow their sister-stones of frailer tracery.

Concord! thy temple has outstood them all, and triumphs in the strength of simple unity. Of finest Grecian mould, its double row of pillars, perfect frontage, flights of steps, enchanting grace, can be seen in the mind's eye of those who poetically unroof the modern Madeleine, soften every stone, place it on slopes of tenderest verdure, clinging to a rock which stands in ocean, then fall down and worship the loveliness of ancient art, whose site is Nature's grandeur.

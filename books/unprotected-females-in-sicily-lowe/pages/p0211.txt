Approaching Messina, numberless beautiful little Madonna chapels peeped out among the trees, festooned with brilliant oranges, and here and there a fur-clad pastor playing his pipes fervently before her image. Wax “Bambini” (babies representing our Saviour), beautifully dressed and laid on laced cushions, were handed in at the carriage windows by young children, who received a few tornesi for their industry and taste. A still more certain proof of an approaching town were the custom-houses, of which the officers quietly came out and asked “if we were inclined to give anything, or should they make search?” A couple of tari quite satisfied them; and I often wonder the English can be so foolish as to give a piastre or more to Neapolitan custom-house officers, as they are but too glad to receive any trifle and be saved trouble into the bargain. Ten miles further on, more black-mail was demanded; but we did not grumble, as these, and a man stationed at every torrent-bed in case of need, were the only beggars.

It was too dark to see Scylla; but Charybdis’ waves made themselves heard upon the shore rather angrily, saying, “You are at Messina.”

Around the cloisters was “il Deserto,” a beautiful slope of fresh grass, where the youthful saint used to retire for prayer at the age of six years. The path up to it was bordered by little chapels containing images; twelve new ones had just been erected in fulfilment of a <i>voto</i>, or pious vow, made by some one saved from shipwreck. They were built of glossy white porcelain, and in front of each was portrayed, in coloured tiles, one of the miracles of the saint; such as his crossing the Straits upon his cloak, to the confusion of a boatman who had refused to row across without recompense: another was his marking eyes, nose, and mouth, upon the face of a child born without them: a third was his donkey kicking off its own shoes into the face of an unbelieving blacksmith, who had requested money for putting them on.

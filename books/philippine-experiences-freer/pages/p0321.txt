Thereafter, Pablito fetches the water from the river in two five-gallon oil-cans, one at each end of a pole resting midway of its length on his little shoulder. Under this burden of eighty pounds the boy staggers twenty or thirty steps at a time, until he can go no further, and sets the cans on the ground to rest and recover his breath. When he has finished his routine work about the kitchen he goes into Li-aco’s shop and sits down to learn to make slippers along with the two other boys whom Li-aco has acquired in similar manner. Here he toils away his childhood and becomes prematurely old.

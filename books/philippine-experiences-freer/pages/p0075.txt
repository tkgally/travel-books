My new quarters contained among other furniture several large marble-topped tables, which seemed remarkable from the fact that there is no wagon road leading into that region, and that everything imported into it must be packed over the mountain trails and passes on the backs of beasts or men. Don Sebastiano’s family and two or three others possessed pianos, and these, being too large to pack on animals, had had to be brought in at an enormous expenditure of human brawn. My friend Tomás, Don Sebastiano’s nephew, came often to see me, accompanied by his cousin Ramón, who lived opposite, and I returned their visits. For their benefit we spoke together in English, and, having made some progress with the assistance of the soldiers before my arrival, they advanced rapidly. It was not long until, owing to his aptitude, Tomás stood beside the newly arrived provincial governor in the <i>presidencia</i> and interpreted his remarks to the assembled townspeople and officials.

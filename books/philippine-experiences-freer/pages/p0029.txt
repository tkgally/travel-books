My visit took place not long after the surrender of General Cailles, who has been since and is now one of the most loyal and efficient provincial governors in the archipelago; and the province was not yet completely pacified. That night about one o’clock the colonel called me. I awoke, finding him standing at the window in the large <i>sala</i> of his quarters, and as I went near, he raised his hand and said, “Listen.” Then I heard the sound of firing, first two or three volleys followed by scattering shots. “That has been going on for ten minutes,” he said. Judging by the sound, the skirmish was taking place in the hills several miles away. In a few moments the reports ceased. That was as near as I came to the firing line, much to my satisfaction.

The next day the colonel took me with him in the ambulance to Pagsanjan, distant from Santa Cruz four miles along a road lined with beautiful cocoanut groves.

# Chapter XXI
[[subtitle]] The Biography of a Tao
[[subtitle]] Pedro—His Infancy and Childhood—Guarding the Rice—The Harvest—A Man’s Work—Times of Plenty—Family Thrift—Troublous Times—The Arrival of the Americans—From Bad to Worse—Pedro’s Reflections—The <i>Carabao</i> Found—The Decision of the <i>Juez</i>—Its Sequel—Earning a Living—The Taxes—Faith in the Americans—The Boys in School—Death of the Grandfather—A New Start.

P<sc>edro</sc> was a good <i>tao.</i> Though there were many things that he did not understand, he lived according to his light; and having been taught by his father not to fear hard work he had got along very well compared with his neighbors of the <i>barrio</i> of Marahay.

Pedro’s memory went back to the time when his grandfather took him to walk one evening to show him one of the most beautiful sights of the tropics—a tree alive with myriads of fireflies, whose soft lights, alternately glowing and disappearing, produced exquisite effects.

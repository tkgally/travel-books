There was nothing to do but to go for help; and fortunately I met a barefooted <i>tao</i> with a <i>bolo</i> in his belt a short distance down the road. Pointing to the jungle, I said, “My horse is in there and cannot get out. Will you help me?” Of this he understood only the word <i>horse</i> and the gesture. He shook his head and said <i>“Mayo caballo”</i> (“There is no horse there”). I insisted that my horse was there, at which he regarded me as if he thought my mind unbalanced. But I demanded that he should go with me, which he finally did; and when he saw the situation he labored willingly and diligently, cutting the creepers which held the animal with his <i>bolo</i> and clearing a passage-way in front. He then tugged at the reins before while I whipped from behind, and presently we all emerged in the road again. Imagine my astonishment when this <i>tao,</i> though evidently a very poor man, repeatedly declined to receive the good-sized silver piece I sought to press upon him with my thanks.

[[cont]]façade of the building is brilliantly lighted by scores of cocoanut-oil lamps, which at a distance present much the same effect as incandescent electric lights, outlining the belfry, pinnacles, gable, the windows, pilasters and arched doorways.

The inside illumination is with candles. The image is replaced in its own niche by the altar, while the clergy chant a Te Deum and burn incense in its honor; all classes of people throng the auditorium, not the least in evidence being the wet <i>bogadores,</i> some of whom may be seen chattering with cold. The same evening the municipal <i>presidente</i> gives a <i>baile</i> worthy of the occasion in the town hall—a function seldom surpassed in elaboration of adornment, elegance of costumes, abundance of refreshments and animation of the dancers—the social <i>pièce de résistance</i> of the year. So, for almost two hundred years, have the people of the Bicol region rendered homage to their most excellent patroness, Nuestra Señora de Peña Francia. In some respects the Spanish friars did their work well.

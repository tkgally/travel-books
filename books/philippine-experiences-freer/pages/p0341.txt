At the end of six months Pedro again sought Eugenio’s advice. The Don could only shrug his shoulders, throw up his hands, and tell the poor man that the <i>juez</i> could do as he liked; at least he knew of no way to oblige him to do differently—it had been tried and had failed. At the same time he said, “<i>Paciencia.</i> Some day it may come right.”

Pedro had faith in the Americans, and yet the <i>maestro americano</i> had told Benigno that now every man has equal rights under the government; and it could not be true. Else why was his <i>carabao</i> not returned to him, or at least half of the proceeds of its sale? If he had even the half of the animal’s value, he could buy a new one from Eugenio, who would wait for the remainder of the money until Pedro could pay it. Surely the <i>maestro</i> must be wrong.

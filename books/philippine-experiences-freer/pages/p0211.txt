Then bidding my friends and hosts <i>adios,</i> we set out, the boy preceding on foot. The flaring light made grotesque shadows and the aromatic smoke trailed streamer-like; the tall grass on either side struck against my pony’s flanks and wet my legs with the dew that had already fallen; the path meandered now through the <i>abacá</i> and again through the forest, turning and twisting, always revealing its intricacies to the light, but concealing its mysteries on either side in the darkness. When we reached Payatan after eleven o’clock, we entered at the side opposite to that I had gone out of that morning.

The journey to Caramoan was always interesting, as the route was mostly by sea and not unsafe during the northeast monsoon.

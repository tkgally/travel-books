# Chapter IX

[[subtitle]] Barrio Schools

[[subtitle]] The <i>Barrio</i> System—Wages of Native Teachers—Their Ability to Imitate—Supervisory Work—The Friday Teachers’ Class—A Visit to a <i>Barrio</i> School—The Siesta—Domingo’s Fears—New School-houses—Attitude of the People.

N<sc>ueva</sc> V<sc>izcaya</sc> was one of the first provinces, if not actually the first, where the <i>barrio</i> system of schools, since extended to every portion of the archipelago, was successfully organized and developed, under the American <i>régime.</i> The teaching of the children of the <i>barrios</i> or outlying villages had no place in the scholastic system of the Spaniards. Such schools as existed were accessible only to the children who lived in the <i>población,</i> or central and principal part of the municipality.

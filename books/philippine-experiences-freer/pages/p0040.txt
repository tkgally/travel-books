At another school which I visited the “rapid-fire” method was in vogue. The pupils stood on the floor in two lines facing each other, and the leading boy on one side asked the one directly opposite the first question in the lesson. The answer and the second question were fired back at frightful velocity to boy number one, who replied in the same way. The instant either hesitated or deviated in the slightest degree from the exact words of the text, the next opponent on the opposite side immediately took up the assault or defence and proceeded in like manner; and he who had failed was thereafter <i>hors de combat.</i> Thus the battle progressed down the line, while the unaccustomed spectator looked on at the wordy warfare confused and astonished, until all on one side were vanquished. In such parrot-like exercises consisted the schooling of the Filipino child during the Spanish <i>régime.</i> Arithmetic, geography, Spanish grammar and the catechism were all “taught” in this way.

Other pupils and neighbors happened in frequently to talk a little, to request some information, or perhaps to look at the illustrations in <i>Harper’s Weekly</i> which they enjoyed keenly. From these neighborly visits I obtained practice in Spanish, in my attempts to explain the pictures and political cartoons, and to translate the summary of the world’s news which I received daily by telegraphic bulletin. The towns-people showed their appreciation of my endeavors to be of use to them by sending in presents of food: sometimes it was a piece of fresh goat’s flesh; at other times a bunch of bananas, two or three pineapples, a fat pullet or a few eggs. Once a complete meal in courses was brought over by Don Everisto’s little servant. Often I attended <i>bailes,</i> wedding parties and family reunions; and thus, by work, study and play, my life in Solano was made agreeable and interesting and I found myself too busy to miss my fellow-Americans.

Yes, he has a “buen mula.” It is in the courtyard behind, and we go out to look at it. A capuchin monkey on the veranda roof, chained by his middle, reaches for your hat as you pass, and a guinea-cock jumps to one side.

The mule is brought out and looks all right, save for a saddle gall, which you object to.

“No importa,” says Señor Alvarado, shaking a long finger, “I will fix it. You try him.”

A couple of black boys go to get the saddlery. It takes two of them to bring it. Really, it is appalling what is put on to that little beast. First comes a blanket, carefully folded so as to leave a depression along the spine. Then follows a piece of sacking with a hole cut in it over the sore.

“A friend of mine—whom I will not name—managed it,” he goes on meditatively. “It was this way: A certain President of one of the South American Republics wanted a police telephone put in at his capitol. The price to be paid was twenty-five thousand dollars. The telephone was to cost about eight thousand, and five people were to split up the balance. We got a first payment of six thousand dollars, all in silver, from the National Treasury, and carried it away in a cart. The President of course got his rake-off in a separate bag, which we sent around first.

“Then the four others sat down, two of them Cabinet Ministers, to slice up their melon. It was a sight to see the Minister of Frumento, who was fat, puffing and perspiring in his shirtsleeves that night making piles of the pesos.

The gong sounds for seven o’clock.

“Allons diner,” says Mannoni.

“You should say ‘Allons souper,’” observes M. Mattey, correcting him.

Mannoni is wounded to the quick that his language and the regime of his establishment should be so questioned. He declares that it is “diner” and nothing else. The rest of the table at which he is now officiating is behind him. Mattey adroitly shifts his ground.

“But if you took your next meal at four o’clock in the morning, it would be <i>déjeuner</i>, and you would have had no <i>souper</i>.”

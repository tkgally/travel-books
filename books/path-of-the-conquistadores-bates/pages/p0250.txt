The booths of the Plaza del Mercado, past which you stumbled the night before, are full of women and peons chaffering for rice and coffee and cheap wares. A bust of General Tomas de Heres gazes fixedly upon the placid ox-teams drawn up alongside.

Beyond the <i>plaza</i> stretch the Barracks, where slouchy sentries, clad in rusty brown-cotton uniforms and armed indifferently with shiny old Mauser carbines and Winchesters, look unenthusiastically down on the market-place below. A guard of four soldiers and an officer are standing on the corner watching three convicts sweep the sidewalk.

“No mosquitoes here,” says Alvarado; “do not get out your net.”

None of the inhabitants of the house stir. After removing gaiters, shoes, and coat, you take wearily to the hammock. Towards morning you open one eye and reach for the woollen <i>covija.</i> It is actually chilly. Soon, however, you are awake for good, though the sun has just risen. Cocks are crowing, parrots screeching, cows lowing somewhere outside, and people moving about and talking. You look around and finally get up.

You are in a big room with the thatch of the roof rising to an acute angle above your head. At the doorway this is cut away like a bang on a child’s forehead, but along the rest of its length the roofing comes down shaggily to within about 5 feet of the ground.

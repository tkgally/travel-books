Now Barcelona, the last of Venezuela’s northern cities, had fallen, and all that were left to follow Bolivar were fifteen officers and a few peons as their servants. Help from abroad there was almost none. President Madison had issued an order forbidding any aid from United States citizens to the struggling Revolutionists. Great Britain stood apathetically by her ally, Spain. The feeble little Negro Republic of Haiti alone had lent support in men and money, asking in return only Bolivar’s promise, which he loyally kept, to give freedom to the slaves of Venezuela.

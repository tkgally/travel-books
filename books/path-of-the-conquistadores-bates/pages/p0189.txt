“Tiger come here later,” says Tom, and rolls a big gear-wheel into the smoke for you to sit on. “I cook dinner.”

Into a tin goes a most uninviting and scraggy piece of meat, then plantains and onions, sliced with the machete. This mixture is boiled over the fire. In another tin, black coffee is brewed. Fitzgerald goes back to the boat; he will have none of it. You do not want to hurt Tom’s feelings, for he has been as courteous as a grandee, and the tiger is, he asserts, due around. So you try his soup and some of the coffee with a piece of cassava bread. The hot coffee is not so very bad. The cassava bread looks like a flat bath sponge and tastes as it looks.

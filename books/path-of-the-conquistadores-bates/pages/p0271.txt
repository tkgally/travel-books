He takes you up to his residence in the Calle de Constitucion, to meet Señora Palazzi. Their home is on the second story of an old, thick-walled Spanish house, up the hill near Mannoni’s. In the hall-way beside the courtyard are a dozen deer-horns, trophies of bygone hunts. Light mahogany furniture is in the rooms. A gilt cabinet for little curios contains nuggets, carved ivories, and Dutch silver.

Señora Palazzi, a slight vivacious Caraceña, clad in the latest Parisian mode, greets you here. English and French she speaks perfectly, thanks to French governesses and two years at Convent, New Jersey. She is in touch with plays, books, and events as recent as the mails allow.

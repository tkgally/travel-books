We enter a road leading between suburban country houses. One cottage has over its door the name “San Buenaventura.”

A peon here salutes our host. He reins in his mule for a moment, and each man puts his hand on the other’s shoulder.

“Buenos dias, amigo,” says Alvarado.

Three or four other friends come up while we stand and are greeted in this way. Alvarado knows a good half of the people in the streets as we pass along, and for each one, high or low, he has this affectionate salutation.

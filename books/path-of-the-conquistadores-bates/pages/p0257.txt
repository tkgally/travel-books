In the interim you visit the Consulate of the United States. Mr. Robert Henderson supplies you with American papers only a month or so old and some grafted mangoes, grown on his brother’s estate, which taste like peaches.

Just outside the Consulate windows, in the hot sunlight of the river-bank, Wadsworth, the American electrical engineer, who came up on the “Delta,” is superintending a gang of twenty stevedores, busy hauling a section of the great flywheel of the Presidente’s electric-light plant. Many idlers are looking on.

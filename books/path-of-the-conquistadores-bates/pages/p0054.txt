In the latter part of March, 1817, a score of horsemen were riding towards Angostura from the northern sea-coast, some on mules, some on mangy horses. Most were sallow-skinned creoles clad in civilian dress, sombrero on head, sword and pistol at the belt; a few wore dingy uniforms. One, a gigantic negro, bore the insignia of an officer of the Black Republic of Haiti. Two, military of bearing, keen of eye, had the weather-worn red of the British Grenadiers; half a dozen barefoot peons in ragged ponchos rode behind with the sumpter burros.

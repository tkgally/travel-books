Long sections of “Don Juan” regale you now, read beneath the swinging lantern. At last Fitzgerald shuts the book regretfully.

“I used to write poems,” he says musingly. “Here is one which I wrote in Cuba:—

<verse>
“Roll on, roll on, ye wheels of steel,
You bear us on to woe or weal,
You bring the bitter and the sweet,
The flowers and the sugar beet.
Some are carried for commercial use,
Yon sugar-mill will use the juice
To start the smiles of your sweetheart
And ease the sorrow when you part.”
</verse>

“Can’t we make the last a little clearer?” you suggest. “Does the sugar-juice get made into sweets or rum? It really isn’t the thing to offer a young lady—rum.”

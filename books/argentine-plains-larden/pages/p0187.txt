It was curious to note what the locusts did <i>not</i> eat.

1. <i>Paraiso</i> trees. These were untouched. Yet they roosted on them, and so cannot dislike their smell.

2. <i>Canes.</i> This is a tall native cane, some 10 feet high, that looks not unlike maize. It was untouched.

3. <i>Onions.</i> Untouched.

4. <i>Cucumbers.</i> The fruit never touched, nor, I think, the leaves or runners.

5. <i>Sweet melons.</i> The fruit never touched. But it seemed both to me and to the Swiss gardener that they bit the leaves and runners [sometimes severing the fruit and so killing it], though they did not devour them.

6. <i>Sandías</i> (water melons) and <i>zapallos</i> (pumpkins). The case of these is strange. The locusts ate the leaves and runners; and one would conclude that the juices of these plants are not injurious to them. Yet, when they were eating dead locusts, wood, bark, and hard quinces, they did not touch these plump, relatively soft, and tempting fruits! Everywhere in the garden I saw untouched sandías and zapallos whose leaves and stems had vanished entirely.

7. <i>Certain flowers.</i> I think sweet-williams and foxgloves were untouched.

8. Of other trees, I understood that they are not very “keen on” the eucalyptus, and that consequently these are fairly safe from them if they have other things to eat.

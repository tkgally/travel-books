But all this time we are waiting at Villa Cañas station. Here it was evident at once that my brother was at home. In Buenos Aires the estancieros who have come in from the plains to have a good time are nobodies relatively to the wealthy men of business of the city; unless, indeed, they belong to some well-known family of enormous wealth, and are living as owners on one of the family estates. But here, out in the “camp,” the estanciero is a big man! We were met by a covered wagonette (or volanta) drawn by four horses for ourselves, and there was one of the huge two-wheeled wagons for my big chest and numerous other pieces of luggage, and all that of my brother and his family. Our coachman was a good-looking young peon with a marked strain of African blood in him, and the African love of smartness and brightness of colour in dress was much in evidence.

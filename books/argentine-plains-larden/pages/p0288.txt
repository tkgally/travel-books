Dr. Helbling found he was not at all in the right place for sleeping out, and came back in the dark.

Next day I went with him again to another place; and here I noticed the <i>penitentes</i> of which I had heard so much.

I may say at once that during these three weeks in the mountains in February, and another week afterwards about Puente del Inca, I never (with one unimportant exception) met with[[fnref 1]] any snow-patch that had not been converted into ice (an “Irish” form of speech, but clear in meaning), nor any such ice-patch, or <i>clean</i> glacier, that was not covered with towers, pinnacles, or blades of ice varying from a few feet up to 15 feet or so in height; and this whether the ice was on a level or on a slope. These white columns or pinnacles of ice are called “penitentes,” as resembling men in white penitents’ dress.

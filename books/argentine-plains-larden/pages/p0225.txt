On January 30, 1909, my brother noticed, in the midst of what had been (before the locusts came and ate everything) a dense growth of big thistles, a heap of dry vegetation. Suspecting that this concealed the remains of a sheep killed by thieves, he got down and kicked up the heap, which was 3 or 4 feet across. It was a mass of rotted stalks, &c., all warm and damp and caked together. A large iguana ran out, and inside he found concealed some fifty eggs and baby iguanas together. A perfect egg (which was flexible, though fairly stiff) was 4·25 cm. (1·67 inches) long, and 2·8 cm. (1·10 inches) in its greatest diameter.

A young iguana, judged to be recently hatched, was 21·5 cm. (8·45 inches) long, and of this the tail took up 13·5 cm. (5·3 inches). The young ones had a lot of vivid green about them, and their feet seemed very slender (in proportion) as compared with those of the old iguana.

## <i>The Escuerzo.</i>[[fnref 1]]

Needless to say that, being a toad, it has a great reputation for having a poisonous bite. I found that natives had a great horror of even a common toad; and certainly this escuerzo <i>is</i> alarming.

My brother told me that the plough sometimes turns one up. The only one that I found was in a marshy place. In July, 1898, the capataz discovered the cranium of an escuerzo entangled in the wool on a sheep’s back; the creature appeared to have bitten into the wool and to have been unable to let go. This cranium had been kept, and I photographed it; the small teeth in the upper jaw were visible. I measured the jaw round and it was 3⅜ inches; a close agreement with the measure of 3¼ inches that I had taken from the living animal earlier.

Of common big toads I found plenty in the garden.

## <i>The Vibora de la Cruz.</i>

Snakes are but seldom in evidence in the Pampas, and the only poisonous sort that one sees usually is the “vibora de la cruz” (“viper of the cross,” from the marking on its head). In a long visit in 1888–9, I think I saw only two of these and one harmless snake; in this still longer visit of 1908–9, I saw no snake at all save a vibora de la cruz that my brother had killed and brought in for me to inspect.

# Appendix I

[[subtitle]] Some Account of the Geography and History of Argentina, with Statistics Indicating its Present Condition

I <sc>had</sc> not intended to give any history of Argentina, or any statistics relating to it, in this purely personal narrative. But, on second thoughts, it seemed to me that it might be well to put within easy reach of my readers some general account, however incomplete and imperfect, of this Republic. For, huge though it be, it is little more than a name to the majority of Englishmen.

With this object in view I have dived into various books—the <i>British Encyclopædia</i> (“<i>B.E.</i>”) of 1910, <i>Chambers's Encyclopædia</i>, Keane's “<i>Central and South America,</i>” Dawson's “<i>South American Republics,</i>” the <i>Supplement to the Times</i> of December, 1909 (“<i>T.</i>”), the <i>Statesman's Year-Book</i> of 1910 (“<i>S.Y.B.</i>”), the <i>Argentine Year-book</i> of 1909 (“<i>A.Y.B.</i>”), <i>Whitaker's Almanack</i> of 1910, and three huge volumes of the <i>Cénso Nacional</i> of 1908 (“<i>C.N.</i>”), kindly lent to me by Messrs. Neild, Crane & Co., of River Plate House, London, E.C.

I must confess that I was dismayed to find how impossible it seems to be to feel sure of any <i>numbers</i> quoted being right.

Could the population of the city of La Plata be about 70,000 in 1899 (<i>Keane</i>), 45,000 in 1900 (<i>B.E.</i>), and 80,000 in 1908 (<i>S.Y.B.</i>)?

Can the area of land under vine-culture be both about 39,000 hectares (<i>A.Y.B.</i>), and also 122,000 hectares (<i>C.N.</i> and <i>T.</i>) in about 1908?

Can the number of sheep be 75,600,000 in 1907 (<i>A.Y.B.</i>, p. 166), 67,000,000 in 1908 (<i>C.N.</i>), and 120,000,000 in about 1909 (<i>A.Y.B.</i>, p. 33)?

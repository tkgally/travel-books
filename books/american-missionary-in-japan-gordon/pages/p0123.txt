The <i>oku-zashiki</i> (back parlor) was made our sitting- and dining-room, and my sleeping-room, as the evangelist who accompanied me, and who sat and ate with me, slept in an adjoining apartment. Everything about us was apparently new, and scrupulously neat and clean. As usual, there was nothing in the room except a single picture hanging on the wall, and a rug, with two <i>hibachi</i> filled with glowing coals upon it, covering the straw-matted floor. On entering, the wife, who had just brought the <i>hibachi</i>, came again with the <i>chadogu</i>, the hot water, the tray, the tiny teapot, the canister, cups, and other articles necessary for tea-making. The husband made the tea, first cooling the water below the boiling-point, so as not to take too much of the strength at the first drawing, then poured it into the tiny cups, which the wife passed to the guests.

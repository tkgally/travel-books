One woman made a journey of more than two hundred miles to Kyōto, and standing before the government building took her own life, as she said, “to atone for the nation’s crime.”

The popular feeling began to show itself in the newspapers. “‘Japan for the Japanese’ is a cry that has reason in it, but after all, it may be abused by ignorant men. Let us be more careful.” Later articles were published on “The End of the Reaction.” That time is not yet reached,—it may still be years in the future; but there is good ground for the more sober statement that “the tide is turning.”

There is reason to believe that the theological tide is also turning. One of the young men who, according to his own statements, felt his faith slipping away from him, is now publishing articles with these sentiments:—

“Christ was sinless; Christ was unique in knowledge and independence; Christ’s death on the cross was not simply an example but necessary to our salvation,—how, I don’t know.

But in this he leaves untouched the literature of the new life which has so recently burst forth in Japan,—a life which has transformed the nation’s ideas upon almost every subject. When the writer went to Japan, there was but one newspaper published in Japanese, and that was edited and published by a foreigner. Now there are hundreds of periodicals, all of which abound in ideas and words unknown twenty years ago. This has resulted in not only a new literature, but a new language for the new nation.

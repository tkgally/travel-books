In accordance with what has already been said, where the name of a missionary is given it is often understood that he has had Japanese to assist him.

The American Tract Society and the London Religious Tract Society have been the ready and efficient assistants of the missions in the publication of books and tracts.

The influence of periodical literature has been great. It began with the <i>Shichi-Ichi Zappo</i> (“Weekly Messenger”), started by the American Board’s Mission in 1876, and edited by Rev. O. H. Gulick. The <i>Kirisuto Kyō Shimbun</i>, of Tōkyō, is its successor. For a long time it had the field entirely to itself, but now all the leading denominations have their organs. The first paper for children, “Good Tidings,” edited by Mrs. E. R. Miller, and published by the aid of the Foreign Sunday-school Society, is still pursuing a successful career.

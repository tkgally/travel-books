And for a considerable part of the summer it is <i>mushi-mushi-atsui</i>, steaming hot, day and night, in a way difficult for an American to imagine. But after all, unless he be a Californian, the missionary is pretty sure to find more days in the year in which out-door life is both possible and pleasant than he has ever before known.

In the winter, on the eastern coast, where most missionaries reside, there is very little snow, and it is a rare day that the mercury falls below 18° Fahrenheit. In the spring the <i>ume</i> (plum), <i>momo</i> (peach), the <i>tsutsuji</i> (azalea), the unequaled <i>sakura</i> (cherry) and a host of other flowers, successively lend their hues to the mountain sides; and the valleys which nestle between them—green seas of growing wheat dotted with islands of the brilliant, golden rape—make a picture which, once seen, is never forgotten. And then the autumn! where are there brighter days, or balmier skies, or more bewitching sunsets!

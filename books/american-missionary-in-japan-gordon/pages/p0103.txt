Japanese etiquette comes in at these meetings. The preacher usually bows to his audience on beginning, and the people bow in return. At the close of the sermon this is repeated, with a much profounder bow from the people.

It must not however be inferred that the meetings always go on quietly and harmoniously to the end. Occasionally there are those who bring honest objections to the new and strange doctrines of the Christian preacher. More commonly Buddhist priests or their emissaries are there for the very purpose of making disturbance. At one time there was a persistent effort of this kind to break up the evening meetings at a <i>kōgisho</i> on Matsuwara Street, Kyōto. Pupils from Buddhist schools gathered among the standing hearers outside the door, jeered at the speakers, attempted to drown their voices by “Hear, hear!” “No, no!” and other cries, and rushed from behind upon the willing hearers in front, not stopping short of stone-throwing.

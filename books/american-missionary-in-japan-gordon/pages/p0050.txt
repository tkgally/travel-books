The double names of provinces, already referred to, such as <i>Chōshu</i> and <i>Nagato</i>, have a similar explanation. It is only American writers ignorant of this fact who speak of the <i>daimyō</i> of this province as “Nagato, Prince of Chōshu.” This doubling of words extends to the commonest things. Thus “parents” may be either <i>futa-oya</i> (Japanese) or <i>ryōshin</i> (Chinese).

Enough has been said concerning the Japanese language to enable the reader to appreciate the remark of “A Japanese Boy:” “The Japanese language has not been systematized; should one attempt it he would find it a tremendous task: after my dabbling in languages, in Japanese I prefer to be taught like a babe.” He will also understand how it is that the tyro in its study falls into many ludicrous and grievous blunders.

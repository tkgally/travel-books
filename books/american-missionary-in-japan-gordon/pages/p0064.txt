[[cont]]for the pronoun “I.” My country, my house, my sickness, are respectively the simple names <i>kuni</i> (country), <i>uchi</i> (house), <i>biyōki</i> (disease), with no possessive pronouns, honorifics, or other descriptive words; while, if belonging to the person spoken to, these nouns are loaded down with honorifics, <i>ō kuni</i> (august country), <i>ō uchi</i> (august house), <i>go biyōki</i> (august disease), <i>ō mi ashi</i> (august and honorable legs), always meaning your country, your house, your legs, etc. Then there are specially depreciatory terms. For example, while “your wife” is <i>go saikun</i> (the honorable lady), “my wife” is <i>gusai</i> (the stupid wife). Of myself I say <i>mairu</i> (humbly go); concerning you it must be some such phrase as <i>ō ide nasaru</i> (augustly proceed). A present made is always <i>somatsu na mono</i> (something coarsely made and of little value), lifted up towards the august recipient; conversely, a present received, however slight its value, is always <i>kekko na okuri mono</i> (splendid thing), condescendingly bestowed upon the bowed head of the humble beneficiary.

And for many years the ground of the most moving appeals, even to Christians, was not the condition of the unchristian[[fnref 1]] world, the wide-open doors, or the successes already attained: it was the hardships of the missionaries.

This period has long since passed away, but it is doubtless still as a rule true that the young missionary goes to his field with the thought of similar trials for himself and family as at least a possibility. If destined for Japan, however, he finds himself in his outward journey traveling under very different circumstances from those of Henry Martyn or William Carey.

[[cont]](literally, tea-price),—a tip, not to the servants, but to the landlord, for serving tea, sweetmeats, etc., in addition to the meals. For a cup of tea, when stopping for a rest by the wayside, one or two <i>sen</i> is sufficient; at a hotel it ranges from ten <i>sen</i> (cents) upwards, according to the size of the party and the length of time spent. The Japanese always pay this; and the missionary or other foreigner who neglects or refuses it, on the ground that he has “paid his bill,” violates one of the Japanese proprieties, and so, unconsciously but grievously, offends where he was specially desirous of pleasing,—another illustration of the importance of knowing the manners and customs of the people. One great objection to Japanese hotels is their lack of retirement and quiet. An occasional one will have a best room, separated from the rest of the house; but usually thin paper doors alone separate your room from two or three others. Sometimes your nearest neighbor will be a man who, unable to sleep, spends a great part of the night in reading aloud from some Japanese or Chinese book. Or there may be a party who call in <i>geisha</i> (low women who sing, play, and dance) and make night hideous with their revelry.

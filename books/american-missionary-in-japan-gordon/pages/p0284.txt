<verse>
“Kimi ga noru kisemu no keburi miyuru ma wa
Toku okuramu Fuji no shirayuki.”

Until thy steamer’s smoke is lost to sight
Thou’rt watched by Fuji’s snow so pure and white.
</verse>

Were these courtesies thought of as special to the present recipient, they certainly would not be chronicled in these pages. They are given, only because they show in a concrete way the touching attentions which the Japanese shower upon those who have lived and labored among them. Such attentions move the heart most deeply, and make strong the desire to add to our <i>sayonara</i> the words which so naturally follow it,—<i>mata mairimashō</i> (we will come again). And so, as we sail away, all other thoughts and feelings are overshadowed by grateful love for those who have thus poured out their kindness upon us, and our hearts respond to the affecting <i>wakare no uta</i> (poem of parting) so characteristically Japanese:—

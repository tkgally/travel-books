The unchristian villagers drove him out of the village; his brothers and other relatives deserted him; and he lived alone in a rude hut in the mountains, receiving a little aid from the prefectural government, and more from the Japanese Christians and the missionaries. At a little later period the Christians cooked his food and otherwise cared for him, but for quite a while he built his own fire and cooked his own rice, going, in his solitary blindness, to and from the spring that furnished him with water, guided by the straw rope which had been put there for that purpose.

The evangelist of whom I have already frequently spoken in this chapter, hearing of his pitiable condition, went to condole with him, but to his surprise he was met by the assurance that he was not an object of condolence; that his heart was full of joy, because <i>Kami ga shiju waga ushiro ni oru</i>, “God is always behind me.” Think of it!

The influence of missionary physicians directly upon the tens of thousands of patients whom they have skillfully treated, indirectly upon the native medical profession, and, by their benevolent work among the poor, upon the thought of the people at large; the advice given and plans suggested in regard to prison reform; lectures upon political economy; their work in various schools from the Imperial University downward,—all these indirect forms of service have scarcely been touched upon. Speaking of mission schools, Count Inouye, whose statement concerning the influence of Christian civilization is referred to on page <pref>229</pref>, stated in the same interview that reports universally agreed in putting the morals of their students at a much higher grade than those of students in other schools. As an example of the missionary’s influence as a teacher, take Rev. Dr. Verbeck, whose important work has already been referred to.

# Chapter VII.

[[subtitle]] Japanese Preachers.

T<sc>he</sc> Japanese have not been accustomed to public speaking except in a very restricted way. Besides that of the <i>shibai</i> and <i>joruri</i> (a kind of opera), which was not worthy of the name, there were only the <i>hanashika</i>, and the Shintō and Buddhist priests. These <i>hanashika</i>, as their name indicates, are simply public story-tellers who amuse and excite their hearers by tales of love and war. They speak the language of the common people, draw largely from the national folklore, and often show a good deal of power in moving their audiences. The Shintō priests go but little beyond the mere recitation of their mythology, and appeals, based thereon, to their hearers’ patriotism; for a religion that, in the language of Prof. Chamberlain, “has no set of dogmas, no sacred book, no moral code,” has little call to preach.

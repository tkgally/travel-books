Immediately above the village of Siloam stands the Mount of Offence as it is now called, or the Mount of Corruption as it was called in ancient times, where Solomon built altars for the worship of the idols of his wives.

Passing by this pool, which is in the Valley of Jehoshaphat, between Mount Moriah and the Mount of Olives, we rode through the village of Siloam, and took one of the roads that lead over the Mount of Olives to Bethany. After about an hour’s slow riding—a ride long to be remembered—we reached Bethany, a quiet little village, situated on the eastern slope of the Mount of Olives—a fit place for retirement from the bustle of a large city.

The history of Alexandria since his death has more than realised the dream of the world’s conqueror.

About an hour after we anchored a steam-tug came alongside the <i>Nyanza</i>, and took off those passengers and their effects who had taken through tickets for India, China, Japan, and Australia. So far as I was concerned the responsibility of the P. & O. Company ceased on the arrival of the steamer at Alexandria. I was therefore obliged to hire a boat to take me ashore. On landing I was very near losing my baggage, amid the wonderful crowd of men and beasts by which I was beset—Negroes, Arabs, Turks, donkeys, camels—in terrifying rivalry for my trunks. I was not disposed to allow anyone to touch my baggage until I could decide upon my destination. At length an Arab, calling himself Hamad, and representing himself as a professed dragoman, came to my assistance, and relieved me of the disagreeable pressure.

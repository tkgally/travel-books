Here one is brought into contact with the olden days—with David and Solomon, and the prophets; and with David’s greater son, who wept tears of sorrow for this very people, when, eighteen hundred years ago, he saw the sufferings which their blindness and obstinacy would entail upon them—wept as he foretold the desolations which have befallen their goodly country: “O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee; how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not! Behold your house is left unto you desolate. For I say unto you ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.” (Matt. xxiii.)

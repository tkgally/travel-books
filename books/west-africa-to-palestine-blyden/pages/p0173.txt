[[cont]]Bethesda, described in the gospel as being by the sheep market, in the margin <i>sheep gate.</i> Passing out of this gate we went down the path by which it is most likely Christ frequently went up to the city from the Mount of Olives. We passed over a bridge that spans the Brook Kidron, and came to the Virgin’s tomb. A little further on towards the right, and at the foot of the Mount of Olives, we approached the Garden of Gethsemane. I now felt that I was in the midst of localities where it is certain Christ frequently walked with His disciples. There are two roads that lead to the summit of the Mount of Olives from the Garden of Gethsemane, or St. Stephen’s Gate. We went up neither, but made a path for ourselves in a straight line from the Garden of Gethsemane, among the scattered olive trees, to the summit of the mount, where stands the Church of the Ascension—a small octagon chapel within the court of a mosque. There is nothing in it but a rock, with an impression on the side resembling, it is said, a human footprint. I could not discover any special similarity.

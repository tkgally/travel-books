From the “Well of Joseph” we visited the mosque of Sultan Hassan, described by some travellers as, architecturally, the finest in Cairo. I confess that—probably for want of sufficiently cultivated taste in that department of art—after having visited the mosque of Mohammed Ali, I could see nothing to admire in the old dilapidated structure of Sultan Hassan. The devotees about it, however, obliged us to take off our shoes—furnishing us no slippers—before entering. I fancy that my health was not very much benefited by walking thus exposed on the cold marble pavement.

I was struck with the rigid and impressive simplicity of the interior of the mosque. There are no pews, nor chairs, nor seats of any kind; no pictures or statues. They are intended for places of prayer—not of luxurious ease—whither people go, not to gaze around or criticise, but for devotional purposes.

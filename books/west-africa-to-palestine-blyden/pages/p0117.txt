# Chapter VIII.

B<sc>ut</sc> I must return from my long digression—which my countrymen, I am sure, will forgive, for the honour of the race—to the interior of the pyramids, looking at which brought on the train of thought in which I have indulged.

The heat was not so great within the pyramid as might at first be supposed; it seems to be ventilated from some quarter. Before the Arabs would consent to guide us out they insisted on receiving <i>bakhshish</i>—a present, corresponding to <i>dash</i> among the aborigines in West Africa. We had to promise them solemnly and earnestly that on gaining the open air we would satisfy all their desires. Had they left us, as they pretended to be about to do, it would have been utterly impossible for us to get out; and the idea of stumbling in the darkness, rolling down slippery places, and falling into deep holes, was harassingly frightful.

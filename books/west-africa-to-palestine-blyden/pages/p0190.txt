On the afternoon of the 11th of September, about three o’clock, I rode out of the Jaffa Gate, under an experienced guide, along the brow of Mount Zion, into the Valley of Hinnom to En-Rogel. (Jos. xv., 7, 8; and xviii., 16.) It is now called the “Well of Job” by the Arabs. The Valley of Hinnom is well known to Bible readers. It is on the south side of Jerusalem. The total length is about a mile and a half. In ancient times this valley was the scene of the barbarous rites of Molech and Chemosh, first introduced by Solomon. A monster idol of brass was erected in the opening of the valley, facing the steep side of Olivet; and there the infatuated inhabitants of Jerusalem burnt their sons and their daughters in the fire—casting them, it is said, into the red-hot arms of the idol. (Jer. vii., 31; 2 Chron. xxviii., 3.) Now there are innumerable graves there.

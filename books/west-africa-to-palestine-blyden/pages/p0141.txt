The ride, on the whole, was an exceedingly interesting one to me, everything being entirely new. During the latter part of the journey we pursued the same road travelled by Dr. Robinson[[fnref *]] in 1852. We passed over several of the lower ranges of Lebanon running parallel to the sea. We rode over ridge after ridge. Beautiful slopes, and deep glens covered with mulberry, olive and fig trees, now and then presented the most charming spectacle. Mountains, which, in the distance seemed wrapped in clouds and entirely inaccessible, were traversed by us with ease and rapidity; the loftiest and most rugged we found to be crossed by well-trodden roads, verifying the words of the poet—

<verse>
“The lofty mountains that uprear
Their frowning foreheads to the skies,
Are crossed by pathways, that appear
As we to higher levels rise.”
</verse>

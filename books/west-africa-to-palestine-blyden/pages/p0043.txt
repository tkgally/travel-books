By means of a volume of Palgrave’s Arabia, which he saw in my possession, and which he asked permission to look at, we became acquainted. I soon found out that, in addition to his other acquirements, he had a considerable knowledge of the Arabic language, from the ease with which he read and criticised the Arabic quotation on Palgrave’s titlepage.

As the object of my voyage was partly to get some insight into the Arabic language, I felt it a duty to cultivate a further acquaintance with this learned stranger. The following day we had an interview of two hours, and, by mutual interrogatories, exchanged statements as to our past history and future prospects. I ascertained that he was a graduate of Trinity College, Cambridge, where he had held a Fellowship, but, his health failing, he had withdrawn, and was in the habit every winter of visiting some southern country.

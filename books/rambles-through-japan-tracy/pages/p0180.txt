<i>August</i> 26.—The long day’s journey of thirty miles has brought me to the banks of Lake Biwa, and from this point I shall leave the Naka-sen-do for steamboat and cars. So far as scenery is concerned, the route to-day has been monotonous; the country flat, the villages generally poor and without interest. As we approach the ancient capital, one finds more localities identified with the national history or local tradition. Here is a ferry which Yoshitomo crossed when fleeing westward after his defeat in 1159. Nearer to Kioto are the tombs of two priests, put to death about 1198 because they had persuaded Matsumushi and Suzumushi, two beautiful damsels, to leave the harem of the emperor and become nuns. Incidents trifling as these are yet matters of remembrance and record. We pass near battle-fields, scenes of as bloody and momentous events as any in Europe, yet of which contemporaneous Europe never dreamed.

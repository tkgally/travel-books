# Chapter VIII.

[[subtitle]] Overland Toward Kioto.

[[subtitle]] A midnight start—Inquisitive fellow-passengers—Honjo—Stroll about the town—Prevalence of blindness—School children—The silk industry—Japanese bargains—Village industries—Crossing the Umezawa Pass—Arrival at Ozawa.

<i>August</i> 7.—It seemed to me that I had but just dropped asleep when the servant knocked at the door with the announcement that everything was in readiness; and that the man I had engaged to take me to the stage-office was waiting at the door. A few minutes later we were passing swiftly through the streets of Tokio. Nearly all the shops were closed; only a few dealers in <i>bric-à-brac</i> were displaying their wares on the pavement. Not a sound of noisy revelry to be heard, not a drunken man to be seen. How different will be the streets of London, Paris, or New York, a few hours later this Saturday night! But this is a heathen country, barbarous, uncivilized.

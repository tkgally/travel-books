And if we ask why it is that, despite industry and thrift, the people of Japan are becoming poorer every day, there can be but one reply. It is because the greed of Christian Europe still refuses to a sister nation the act of justice which she asks; the revision of a commercial treaty which she made in ignorance, and by which she gave away her rights without compensation.

That any ultimate injury to British trade would follow that act of justice I do not believe; but it is probable that sordid fear in the minds of statesmen alone hinders the reparation that is asked. But with Japan this is more than a question of finance—it is a question of honour as well; and she will not always be under a duress to which Canada or Australia would not for a moment submit. It is impossible that the honour and interests of a nation of forty millions should be perpetually governed by a selfish policy at home, or subordinated to the preferences of a few hundred Europeans and Americans encamped on her soil.

Indeed the traveller in the North-West, at least in the remoter districts, is almost entirely dependent on the H. B. Company for conveyance. On the more frequented prairie trails you may meet, during the summer, long bands of carts belonging to independent freighters, or you may at some points find that the “free traders” can forward you more quickly and more comfortably than the Company; yet the assistance of the Company’s officials, who are almost invariably energetic, hospitable and courteous, is of essential importance in traversing the remoter north, while even on the more familiar prairies they are the chief forwarders as well as fur-traders. At nearly every post the Company keep a large number of horses, for this costs nothing except the hire of a few men to herd them, as the horses find abundant pasture, both summer and winter; and if the agent at any post has no horses under his charge he can usually make arrangements with Indians, half-breeds, or, in some cases, with white settlers to provide them; and thus the traveller is forwarded by stages from the Rocky Mountains to Winnipeg.

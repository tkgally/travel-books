The two colonies were united in 1866, the one giving the name to the united colony—British Columbia, the other giving the capital—Victoria. The old rivalry, however, between the two capitals still exists, as New Westminster has not yet abandoned her claim to present and prospective superiority to Victoria. On the 20th July, 1871, the colony was united to the other Provinces of the Dominion, and Canada was thus extended from the Atlantic to the Pacific.

Victoria is British Columbia in much the same way as Paris is France. Originally an Indian village gathered around a post of the Hudson’s Bay Company, then a small settlement of traders, etc., it sprang forward rapidly under successive waves of excitement: first, on the discovery of gold on the lower Fraser; again, in 1860, when new and most profitable gold fields were opened in Cariboo; and, subsequently, on the discovery of gold in Cassiar in 1873. Its population like its prosperity has fluctuated, at one time swelling to 12,000, but now shrunk to less than half that number.

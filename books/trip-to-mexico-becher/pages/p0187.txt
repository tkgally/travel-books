The ancient name of the mountain, <i>Nauhcampateptl</i>, signifying quadrangular mountain, was given to it probably for the same reason. <i>Perote</i> is the plateau of country lying between the two volcanic chains of Popocatapetl and Orizaba, which strike north and south.

Humboldt, whose measurement I give you, and who, of course, knew most about it, says this immense rock is of pumice (probably dark porphyritic pumice, hence its being called <i>porphyry</i> in all the books but his), and that it was carried up during the upheaval of the mountain. He instances a similar phenomenon as having occurred in Vesuvius in 1834.[[fnref *]]

There was a large convent of Franciscan monks here until the law abolished them; this convent, which is a very fine building, and the cathedral are the most noteworthy buildings. Both are said to have [[corr beeen|been]] commenced by Cortés.

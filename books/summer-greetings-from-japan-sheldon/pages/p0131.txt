The latter is full of old treasures, especially rare old <i>kakemonos</i>, but I am afraid I am not quite up to some of these works. A clump of bamboos growing by the roadside, or an aged grandfather, with shaven head, carrying a baby on his back, or trundling it in a baby carriage, interests me more than all the richest old <i>kakemonos</i> I have seen. But the statues of the Buddhas always stir me, like the temples. There is something awe-inspiring about them both, though they are as remote from the real life of this people, just now, as the crucifix is removed from the daily life of the people of Europe. Only the higher natures have a feeling for the meaning of it all. I bought to-day one of those bamboo sticks used in cleaning the cups at the Ceremonial Tea, as I thought it would interest you; also the bamboo dipper with which the hot water is ladled out. I wish you were here to see all this. The shops would simply fascinate you.

[[right]] Thursday evening.

This has been mostly a day of seeing shops. Just as I was signing my name at the Mikado's palace yesterday, and putting down the date, I realized that it was July 4th. It came and went, but I had no way of being patriotic. This morning I called first on Mrs. Nitobé. They have a pretty little rented place close by. It all has the Japanese effect, though they have European furniture. Mrs. Nitobé called my attention to the sound of a slowly tinkling bell. It was that of a pilgrim on his way to one of the temples.

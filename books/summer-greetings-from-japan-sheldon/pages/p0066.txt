I noticed the party were not enthusiastic over Marquis Ito, yet I said in America we looked upon Ito about as he looked upon Roosevelt. Professor M. said that there had been 6,000 graduates from the University, and that, if they were taken out, it would take out a big slice of the new Japan. I mentioned the “women’s university” here, but they would not acknowledge it to be anything but a girls’ school. There is a rival university here, a private one, the Waseda University, founded by Count Okuma. I forgot to mention that we went to the place where the students practice <i>jiu-jitsu</i> and fencing. We saw them fencing with bamboo sticks. It looks fierce and furious. We also looked into a building elaborately fitted up for experimental psychology. Professor M. had never seen this before, nor the <i>hospital;</i> which was typical of the average university professor. I notice that even here when they want real tea with substance to it they drink Chinese tea, with milk and sugar. These names are awful to remember. They all sound like “kodak,” only stretched to three or four syllables.

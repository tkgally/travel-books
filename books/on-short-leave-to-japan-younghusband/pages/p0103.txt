The divisions between these pens are about a foot high, and access to one’s seat is gained along elevated gangways which traverse the theatre at intervals. Out of deference to our national custom, we were courteously allowed chairs, so placed as not to interfere with the view of our neighbours. Five ladies of the court, dressed in European costumes, also were seated on chairs. The stage, unlike the usual Japanese revolving stage, is a fixed one, and a huge curtain, tastefully painted with a winter scene, hangs before it, as in our theatres. The actors, instead of entering the stage from the wings, or back of the stage, make their appearance from behind the audience, walking along a broad causeway constructed on a level with the heads of the people in the stalls. The exit, except for dead men, is by the same causeway.

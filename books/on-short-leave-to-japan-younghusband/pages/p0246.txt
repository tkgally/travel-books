All arms and all branches of the service, with one or two exceptions, appear to be dressed alike, as in the German army: the band round the forage-cap being the only distinguishing feature. Thus, the Guards wear red bands; the Rifles black; the Line, Cavalry, Infantry, and Artillery yellow. No helmets are worn, all ranks being furnished with high, peaked forage-caps, as worn by German officers. In summer these have a white cover which leaves only the coloured band and peak visible. The Imperial Guard consists of some 6000 of all ranks, and is naturally the smartest and finest corps in the army.

The physique of the men is good, and may best be compared to that of the Goorkas of the Indian army. Small in stature, strongly built, sturdy, active, and with good marching legs, the Japanese soldier makes a stout little warrior.

An ample lunch-basket (observe the old soldier), stocked with all the necessaries of life down to a bottle of curry-powder, sufficiently supplies the deficiencies of the Japanese <i>cuisine</i>, and we eat and sleep in much comfort. Then up and away again in the merry rickshaw, with death’s-head in the shafts. Our next halting place, after a short run, was Numadzu, at another entirely Japanese inn. No one could be kinder and more hospitable than our hosts: the whole household was in a continuous bustle from first to last, doing its utmost to make us comfortable. Here was to be found that glorious institution, the common bath: this structure consisted of a large wooden box always full of water, at nearly boiling-point. It was placed exactly inside the main entrance to the inn, and by way of seclusion was surrounded by <i>glass</i> windows.

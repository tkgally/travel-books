As to the company, we preferred that of the second-class, for one does not come to Japan to be boxed up perpetually with parties of foreign tourists. As to the habits of the Japanese, the only one that was at all offensive was the expectoral activity of a small minority. To any one who has experienced the steady persistency of the Yankee in this respect, or has met the Frenchman in his native carriage, the experience, though disagreeable, will be nothing new. In all other respects we found our fellow-travellers clean, obliging, and the acme of good-nature. Pecuniarily, with heavy baggage, for which extra charge is made, the saving is infinitesimal. Lightly equipped, an economy of one-third is effected.

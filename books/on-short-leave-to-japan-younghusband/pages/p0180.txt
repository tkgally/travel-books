In the little village below, and within view of the lake, are sulphur baths, supplied by the ‘hell’s caldron’ above. Here we lunched at a clean little inn, and here we came across Adam and Eve in their pristine costumes. One old lady especially amused us, for, sublimely regardless of her own absence of clothes, she was transfixed with admiration for my leather gaiters. <i>Honi soit qui mal y pense</i>, say I: why on earth should we meddlesome Westerns trouble a guileless villager with our, as likely as not, false notions of decency? Adam and Eve were very happy and comfortable till the serpent arrived and inaugurated the era of top-hats and bombazeen gowns. I see no reason why we should hand on our heritage of woe and clothes to people who are perfectly happy without them. In this connection the Zulu maiden always occurs to me as a case in point.

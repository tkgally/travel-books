# Chapter V

[[subtitle]] Japan—On First Acquaintance

T<sc>he</sc> captain said that he had passed through the Inland Sea forty-five times, and only twice had he experienced fine weather. We were therefore fully prepared for anything but fine weather, and were not altogether disappointed. The sea is 240 miles long, its greatest width being 40 miles, but throughout its length it is constantly broken up by groups of small islands. All round these is deep water, so that ships of the largest tonnage steam at full speed in and out, and pass quite close to them. Perhaps writers and narrators have exaggerated the narrowness and intricacy of these passages; or perhaps one's own imagination had exaggerated them in anticipation.

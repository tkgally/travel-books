The language is an exceedingly easy one to get a smattering of—sufficient, indeed, for travelling purposes—but a difficult one to master. An hour a day at words and sentences out of Murray’s <i>Guide</i> will very soon supply one’s linguistic requirements. Very handy little vocabularies are also to be purchased.

A general retrospect leads one to reiterate Charles Dickens’s advice against entering a new country with preconceived opinions about it. In Japan this is especially advisable. Old residents call it the ‘land of disillusions.’ We need not go so far as that if we avoid loading ourselves with illusions before we land. Some writers have ‘gushed’ egregiously, no doubt; and the traveller who has only read such works runs a fair chance of being disappointed.

[[cont]]thoroughly cognisant of the land and its people. A little experience in Eastern travel, and Murray’s book, make the services of the ‘pestilential guide’ unnecessary in Japan. It is impossible to carry about many books when travelling lightly; but Mitford’s charming little <i>Tales of Old Japan,</i> and <i>Things Japanese</i> by Professor Chamberlain, would squeeze in anywhere. To read Mitford’s tale of the <i>Forty-seven Ronins,</i> and then to visit the scene of their death, is one of those intellectual feasts which go to make up the charm of travel. Professor Chamberlain’s book is a fund of quiet humour, combined with solid instruction, from beginning to end.

This was the end of June, and the weather had cleared and become bright and fine. The heat was nothing more than that of a hot summer’s day in England, 73° to 75°; but we heard the inhabitants groaning, and saw punkahs at dinner-time in some private houses.

Quite so; the principle is excellent, and not one of us would for a moment grudge a handsome ‘tip’ in recompense for the extra service received. But, unhappily, in these days of erudition the very people these instructions were <i>not</i> issued for, the hotel proprietors, have got hold of them, and deliberately charge double, treble, and even five times as much as they would a Japanese traveller, and in justification of the charge calmly point out the passage in the guide-book. In fact, we all resent being mulcted, as a right, of a sum of money which is really a gratuity. Imagine our feelings, for instance, at being asked $5 for a room, without food, for which a Japanese would pay 90 cents, and having our own guide-book hurled at our head, as a voucher, so to speak. Yet this happened to us at Hikoné (not to be confused with Hakoné).

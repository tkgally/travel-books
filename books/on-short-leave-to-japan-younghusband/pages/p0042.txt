Against external foes Singapore is, they tell me, practically impregnable, by reason of its submarine mines and coast batteries, whilst a very nasty-looking fort completely sweeps the roadstead and anchorage. Of course, these works do not profess to block the straits—that is a naval matter—but merely to secure absolutely the coaling station. Coal seems a prosaic enough article, and it is hard to connect it, in imagination, with great deeds and great battles. But nothing impresses on one more forcibly the immense power of the British navy than the consummate skill with which its coaling stations are dotted over the world. It is no uncommon thing to hear our navy compared in numbers and strength to the navies of other nations. That seems to me to be a false estimate of comparative strength.

It seems to an outsider merely a form of insurance, for certainly without modern works and modern guns the Singapore merchant and his business would be in a very precarious position in time of war.

These warlike reflections were suggested by the current rumour, at the time of our visit, that the relations between France and England were much strained in reference to Siamese affairs.

Let us return to peaceful Singapore. We drove out to Tanglin barracks and the public gardens there. The former, unlike ordinary barracks, were very picturesque, and more especially so the officers’ quarters and mess—deeply thatched, with cool broad verandahs, and buried in every sort of tropical vegetation. The gardens are beautifully kept, and are very lovely.

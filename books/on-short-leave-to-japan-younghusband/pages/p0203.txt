A semi-Japanese inn gave us shelter here—an inn which aspired to chairs, and tables, and chicken cutlets, but the bedrooms were small and stuffy. Perhaps the best way to see Lake Biwa is to work from Kyoto, making a day or two’s excursion of it; but to us it seemed at best an uninteresting sheet of water.

One and a half hour’s ride by rickshaw, or a pleasant morning’s walk, and we reach Kyoto, the ancient capital of the empire, and for many hundred years the place which contained the gilded cage in which generations of Mikados were born, and lived, and died, unseen of their subjects, unknown to them, semi-deities whilst they lived, and yet nigh powerless for good or ill.

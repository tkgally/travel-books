But, putting aside <i>kagos</i> and paving-stones, the views during the first few miles from Hakoné are well worth a little tribulation. It is very much easier, too, looking at views going down hill than toiling up. Every yard of the road the traveller can enjoy a prospect which includes sea and land, mountains and plains, rivers and forests. A nice breeze is blowing, and the weather is just pleasant for walking. My <i>kago</i> men considered me a most magnificent bargain, for only during a short spell in the day’s march did I trouble them. Toiling up the steep hills many men and pack-ponies passed; the latter, like their masters, shod with grass sandals, to prevent their slipping on the knobbly pavement.

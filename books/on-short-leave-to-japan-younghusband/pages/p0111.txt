I forgot to mention that during the whole while a distinguished-looking lady, evidently the wife of the young daimio, had been sitting unobtrusively close by. At this juncture she is suddenly taken ill with violent pains, and is borne off into a room in the cottage. The young daimio immediately sticks a dagger into the wall, hangs the dead lady’s scroll on it, and sets to work to pray steadily to it, regardless of the havoc he is playing with his fine clothes. His prayers are evidently answered, for, heigh! presto! the door opens—and he finds that he is the father of a fine boy. Subdued applause from the ladies of the audience. The precocious boy has, meanwhile, afforded intense amusement to his little contemporaries in the pit by neglecting his prayers at frequent intervals in order to steal to the door to see what it is all about.

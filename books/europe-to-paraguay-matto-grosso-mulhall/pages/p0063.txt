On crossing the Sierra we got a view of Cordoba, beyond the Rio Primero, this river being marked by frequent plantations. We halted at the Calera, property of Mr. Allende, who receive us very hospitably, my husband being already acquainted with him. Close by we saw some Englishmen at work, who told us they were building a hotel for the people of Cordoba to come to in the summer months, the Rio Primero offering good facilities for bathing. We rode along the banks of this river for a couple of hours till reaching a delightful island with a farm-house surrounded by orchards and plantations, the abode of a Frenchman who married the daughter of the original owner, with whom he chanced to be a fellow-passenger from France when the heiress was returning from school to South America. The guide tells me they are greatly esteemed by everybody. Still following the river we came to Saldan, belonging to another of the Allende family: it is at the foot of the Sierra, and possesses probably the largest walnut tree in the world; under its shade 200 horsemen, they say, can rest. A few hours ride brought us back to Cordoba before sundown, having travelled about 50 miles since yesterday morning.

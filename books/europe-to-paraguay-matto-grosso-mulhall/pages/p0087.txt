Many of the Indian customs remain amongst the Paraguayans. We went to see a ‘velorio’ or wake of a new born child. The body was laid upon a table, dressed in white satin embroidered with silver, satin shoes etc.; it had a wig of long black curly hair, surmounted by wreaths of artificial flowers. About forty women sat around, smoking and laughing, all barefoot and their only dress the embroidered chemise. When a child dies the mother first holds the feast in her own house. Afterwards other women borrow the body from her for a night until they [[corr aré|are]] obliged to bury it.

The Gomba is an Indian dance which on certain festivals is kept up two and three days: some trunks of trees are put on the ground in the form of a cross, and covered with skins; the women form a circle and the dance is supposed to be a rejoicing on the return of their husbands from the chase. Notwithstanding the manner in which they perform the Gomba it is curious how well and gracefully these women dance European dances.

All the ancient forts were built of such materials, and the Amir’s palace of to-day, a very large structure, is entirely composed thereof. His highness was away on a visit, but through the kindness of Mr. L. the palace was thrown open for our inspection. It consisted of the usual number of courts, arcades, Turkish bath-rooms, and numerous small rooms, all decorated in the usual gay taste of the Sarts of to-day, who seem to me to have entirely lost the taste and talent that produced such peerless structures as the Taj Mahal, the Delhi Mosque, the Alhambra, and the beautiful Medressés of Samarkand. The same holds true of the modern architecture in India. Bokhara carpets of rare quality and great value were tossed here and there, while some absurd French glass, a brass chandelier, or some cheap English furniture, were kept carefully covered and only exposed for our admiration.

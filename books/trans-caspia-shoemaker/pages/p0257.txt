# Chapter XVIII.

ABOUT an hour from camp we met the first of Jorneoff’s forces—horses and donkeys, most of them, but each and all ladened with the necessaries for a three months’ tour of the Pamirs. Then came the foot soldiers, about three hundred in all—Cossacks, of course—and a ragged, dirty-looking lot they were. No inspection in this department, or such uniforms would not be permitted. They are supposed to consist of black boots, red trousers, white jackets, and caps of linen; but to-day, including the faces and hair of the wearers, all were of a dust color, though there is comparatively no dust here. The men did not look simply travel-stained, as a marching army must, but were dirty with old dirt and grease.

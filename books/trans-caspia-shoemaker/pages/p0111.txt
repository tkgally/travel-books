As long ago as the tenth century this city of Central Asia was spoken of by travelers, and it was old, very old even then. Here “in the winter of <sc>b. c.</sc> 328, in the ‘Royal Chase,’ undisturbed for generations, Alexander the Great and his officers, slew four thousand animals, and here Alexander himself overcame a lion, Samson-like, in single combat.” Not until 700 <sc>a. d.</sc> did Bokhara emerge from the darkness, since which time it has been conquered and reconquered so many times that any thing like a permanent rule was impossible. In 1400, Timur—Tamerlane—established a Tartar dynasty lasting one hundred years, after which tribe after tribe conquered Bokhara down to the present day, when, under Russian rule, she is more prosperous than she has been for centuries.

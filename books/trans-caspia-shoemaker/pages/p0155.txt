It stands where it has always stood, on a platform under a pagoda, the pagoda having been for the past twenty-two years inclosed in a Russian fort, only to be entered by special permission. We had such, and were also escorted by an officer; so we not only entered, but were allowed to photograph the stone, though I fear the light was not favorable. It is said that, in the days before Tamerlane, a bright blue stone surmounted this, and on that was placed the throne chair, now in the museum of the Kremlin, at Moscow. I saw it there some years since—a square structure, heavily carved and inlaid.

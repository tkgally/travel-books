[[cont]]“With the help of God the Lord, the great Sultan, conqueror of kings and nations, shadow of God on earth, the support of the decisions of the <i>Sunna</i> and of the divine law, the ruler and aid of the faith, Ulug Bek Gurugan—may God prolong the time of his reign and rule!—undertook a campaign into the country of the Mongols, and returned from this nation and these countries uninjured, in the year 828 (<sc>a. d.</sc> 1425).”

The Ulug Bek mentioned was grandson to Tamerlane, and the founder of Samarkand’s observatories and colleges. The second inscription is of the victory of Abdullah Khan, a century and a half later: “Let passers in the waste and travelers on land and water know, that in the year 979 (<sc>a. d.</sc> 1571), there was a conflict between the army of the lieutenant of the Khalefate, the shadow of the Almighty, the great Khakan Abdullah Khan, son of Iskinder Khan, consisting of thirty thousand men of war, and the army of Dervish Khan and Baba Khan and other sons of Barak Khan.

However, one pardons roughness here if it means progress; and it does mean progress until we roll into the last post, thirty versts from Marghilan, and are met with the news: “No horses until 2 <sc>p. m.</sc> to-morrow.” We are not entirely unprepared for this, having encountered the mails, westward bound, just outside the village, and knowing that they use up the horses. There is nothing to be done save to pass the night as best we may, which I do in the tarantass, and B. on the door-step of the post-house.

Three <sc>a. m.</sc> brings in two officers, who, possessing a “podorozhnaya,” take our horses, and our departure is put off four hours more. I confess I lose patience. The whole army and their relations are provided with these passes, which enable them to delay every one else.

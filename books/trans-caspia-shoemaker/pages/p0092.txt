I was told flatly to return. There were many consultations with white-capped, ceremonious officials, much bowing and scraping, much intercession by an officer we had met on the ship. I showed my numerous papers: first, my own American passport; second, my Chinese pass; third, my letter of admission from the officials at St. Petersburg; and fourth, my telegram from the governor, Kuropatkine, at Askhabad, stating that I was to be admitted. “No, it must be an error.” At last, it was arranged that the chief here wire to the governor at Askhabad, and make inquiries in my behalf. So they vanished, and left B. and I looking each other blankly in the face.

“What shall you do?” he asked.

“Go back, I fancy.”

We are not long in taking to our boat, and are soon once again on the ship, feeling sure that, when she moves, we shall not be left behind. The two other vessels near us both spent a month here last year, at which time some four hundred persons died at this station alone. Situated as it is at the center of the southern coast, it is the grand depot for all this section. A Turkish ship is here for a week even now, her cook having succumbed to the pest. Of course, no intercourse is permitted with either of them, and, in fact, they look as though they were deserted. It is all very depressing, and one hurries the day along as something to be done with and forgotten. Yet I have no doubt that those who were cast here during last year's epidemic met with much more hospitable treatment than was accorded the passengers on the “Normannia,” in the same year, at Fire Island.

By lunch-time I had used up my day’s supply of dry plates, so in order to carry away with me any remembrances of this strange country I was reduced to the use of my pencil. I had been assured before starting that I was to have a good <i>hajeen</i>, or riding-camel; but in this I had been sadly deceived. The beast on which I found myself mounted, when it was too late to change, was a most unmistakable baggage-camel, with all the slow movements, dishabitude of control, and browsing habits of his tribe. I had counted, when anything of interest was passed, on being able to stop, take a photograph, and catch up the caravan; but mounted on this brute this was impossible, and if I wanted to stop, the whole procession had to wait for me.

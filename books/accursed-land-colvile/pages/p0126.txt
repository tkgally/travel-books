The same ranges of hills which wall in the gulf continue in an unbroken line to the northward, and until the low shingle bank which I have mentioned shows itself above the horizon, it is hard to believe that the sea does not still flow through the long vista of hills which dies away in perspective to the northward, as one knows it does through that which stretches as far as the eye can reach to the south. At the head of the gulf, and for the first few miles up the valley, the range on the east is composed entirely of granite. That on the west has a granite base, overlaid in turn by red sandstone, limestone, and chalk.

On reaching the shingle bank which runs across the head of the gulf, one sees that the battle between land and water, which has raged for hundreds of centuries, for possession of the Arabah, is still drawn.

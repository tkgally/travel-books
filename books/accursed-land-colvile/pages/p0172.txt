[[cont]]down came the pole with its weight of wet canvas between Peacocke’s shoulders. Peacocke, who up to this time had not been brought as much into personal contact with the sheikh as myself, had always maintained that at all events he was a very fine-looking old man, and was, I believe, rather inclined to think that I was unnecessarily prejudiced against him. This little incident, however, completely converted him to my views, and “I am certain that old brute let down the tent on purpose” was pretty certain to crop up in any conversation which we had on the many failings of Hammid Jad.

Four times I had seen my dromedary saddled, and four times the saddle had been taken off and another put on in its place; so as soon as the fifth saddle had been put on his back, I put myself upon the saddle and steadily refused to move, and my example was followed by Peacocke and Cash.

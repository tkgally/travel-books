Wetting his feet is the one thing which a camel cannot stand, and in muddy ground he becomes quite useless, his soft splay feet spread out in the slippery mud, and split, and he becomes, if not permanently injured, at all events very lame for some time. The camels had had no water now since leaving Ain Arkhdar five days ago, and, although they are supposed to do without water for seven days as a rule, and ten at a pinch, they were all quite ready for a drink, and it must have been very tantalizing for the poor brutes not to be allowed to put their noses in the tempting clear water in which they were walking.

In one place, the cliffs running straight down into deep water, we had to make a long <i>détour</i> over the hills, through the Wady el Hoïmradâd; and a very bad bit of road it was, taking us nearly an hour and a half to march, and barely advancing us a mile along the coast-line.

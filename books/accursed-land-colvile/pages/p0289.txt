[[cont]]hidden among the ruins. Around him were grazing the camels of the Beni Hammidi, and conspicuous among them the far-famed Haloueh. A long-legged, long-backed, fawn-coloured beast, with a thin, muscular neck, and hair as short and fine as that of a thorough-bred horse. Beside her lay a little soft-haired, fluffy ball, from out of which projected four phenomenally long legs, and a neck to match. This little ball was Haloueh's latest tribute of affection to her spouse. It was with glee that the wicked Imri saw it. He knew well the difficulties before him, and had felt more than doubtful whether, after jumping on Haloueh's back, he would be much further advanced in his vile purpose than he was before. When one jumps suddenly on to the back of a strange horse, the probabilities are that whatever else he does he will not stand stock-still. With a dromedary it is different. That animal does not kick, or rear, or run away, he simply roars, and bites, and lies down.

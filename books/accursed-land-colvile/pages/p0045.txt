Water also was a serious matter. Twice we had five days’ hard marching without a drop on the road. As a camel can go seven days with ease, and ten at a pinch, without water, this would not have mattered if it had been certain that I should always go straight ahead, but when arranging for the transport I had made it distinctly understood that I was to be able to go when I liked and stop when I liked, and as long as I liked at any place, without any consideration as to the proximity of wells. I had been led to take this precaution, knowing as I did from experience that the necessity for water is the great lever which dragomans and sheikhs employ to force the traveller who has not got the country thoroughly at his finger-ends.

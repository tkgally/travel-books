After about four hours’ marching, a halt was called for lunch; and as the sun was setting we caught sight of the tents, which, with the baggage, had gone ahead during the midday halt.

I have been accused of slurring over descriptions of scenery. Let me see if I can give any idea of this, the scene of our first camp. In the centre of the panorama are four white tents, faintly tinged by the after-glow of sunset. Round the tents lie groups of ill-clad Bedwin, shivering under their camel-hair blankets—for there is no wood to make a fire—and wistfully eyeing in turn the cook’s charcoal brazier, and the carefully padlocked barécas, from out of which their small daily ration of water has just been served.

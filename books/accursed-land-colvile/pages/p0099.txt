Not a very great feat one would think, but twenty-two and a half miles a day, at two and a quarter miles an hour means ten hours’ steady marching. This is not much when the days are long, or when one has plenty of forage for the camels; but our supply of beans had run out, and our beasts had to be content with the scanty <i>rims</i> bushes, which were dotted here and there along the beach, and for some reason best known to himself a camel does not seem to thrive on what he gets at night. The strongest reason, however, against our getting to Akabah in two days was the fact that it was against the custom. From Nawibi to Akabah was a three-days’ march, I was told; nobody knew why, but so it was. I had the presumption to make up my mind that in this case it was going to be a two-days’ march, but, as will be seen, custom as usual got the best of it.

“Remember that you are not to halt before sunset,” I shouted to Petrōs, as he left us at the midday halt.

[[cont]]Salaam said that it was time to be going home again, and went to fetch the camels, upon which Peacocke with great promptitude started off on foot up the valley. He, it seems, had already found out a weak point in Salaam’s armour, which I had yet to discover, namely, his mortal dread of losing one of us. He certainly was not actuated by any feeling of personal affection, at this period at all events; nor, I am quite certain, was he moved by any feeling of honour or duty. It must then have been fear, but I have no idea how he would have been punished or who would have punished him if we had come to an untimely end. “Where is the gentleman?” he asked, when he returned with the camels. “Gone up the valley,” I answered, and off started Salaam at his best speed, in the extremely vain hope that he was going to lead back Peacocke like a naughty child who <i>will</i> play in the field where the mad bull lives.

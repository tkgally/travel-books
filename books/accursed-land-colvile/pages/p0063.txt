An eight-hours’ ride brought us to the foot of Jabal Sarbout el Jamal, under which we passed through a narrow gorge called Ben Sudr, entering on the other side a fine amphitheatre of rocks, in the middle of which our camp was pitched. This amphitheatre is formed by the closing in of the walls of the Wady el Hamr, or red valley, so called from the high sandstone walls which bound it; its more easterly portion, which runs through limestone cliffs, being called Wady el Baida, or the white valley. On the north of these valleys is the southern wall of the Tih plateau, on the south a long low spur of Jabal Serabit.

In the bed of the Wady Jerafeh, the action of the water having removed the alluvial deposits of the surface, the substratum of chalk is laid bare. In this part therefore we have the direct evidence of our senses that it would be through this formation that the cutting would have to be made. In the neighbourhood of Akabah, we see as I have said, that the western range, having a granite base, is composed of strata of red sandstone, limestone, and chalk; and as we ascend the valley we see that each of these formations, in turn, is buried beneath the surface till at the Wady Jerafeh only the chalk is left exposed. At Rishi we find a saddle, covered with an alluvial deposit, lying between two limestone hills.

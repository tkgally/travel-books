The word Akabah means an obstacle. The town, I am told, stands on the site of the ancient Eloath, a statement which I have no reason to doubt, but it certainly does not stand on the site assigned to it on the modern maps. Apart from the question of latitude, which I shall go into later on, it is undoubtedly placed on the maps a good five miles too far from the head of the gulf. Its fort is a substantial stone building, capable of holding 600 or 700 men; it is screened from the sea by the palm-grove, and commanded by the high ground on the east. An inscription over the gateway states that it was built by the Sultan Selim in the year 1122 after the Hajera (1762 <sc>a.d.</sc>). Round the fort are clustered a few wretched mud huts, inhabited by the families of the garrison (about twenty men).

At 8 a.m. on the morning of the 29th I rode into Akabah, having left England exactly three weeks, and Suez twelve days.

There was plenty of wood about, and we soon had a good fire, and with the saddle-bags for pillows made ourselves very comfortable as far as our outsides were concerned. Internally I felt the strongest possible evidence that it was dinner-time. I had had a very light and hasty lunch while I was taking the sun at noon, and had emptied the water-bottle before leaving the eastern watershed. “Is there food in the saddle-bags?” I asked of Aïd as he took them off the dromedaries. “Makl mafeesh, wa moyeh mafeesh” (there is no food and no water), he mournfully replied; so I lit a cigarette, and curling myself up by the fire was soon asleep. After about an hour I woke, feeling very cold between my shoulders, which were turned away from the fire, and abominably hungry.

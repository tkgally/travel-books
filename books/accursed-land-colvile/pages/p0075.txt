It is hard to believe that this is not the work of man, so striking a resemblance has it to the ruined gateway of some Eastern town. Passing through the gateway one rides for 100 yards or so through a narrow, sunless gorge, as bleak and desolate as any in the whole peninsula, then suddenly turning round an angle in the wall such a scene of light, and green, and beauty bursts upon one as after a week or more of utter brownness fairly takes away one’s breath. On either side are still the rocky cliffs, here rather lower and farther apart, but, streaming in at the ends of the gorge as the sun does, they only suggest the idea of pleasant coolness, instead of the impression of dreary coldness which the gorges that are altogether sheltered from the sun give. Nestling under the cliffs on the right is a clump of graceful palms, and opposite to them a single palm surrounded by a circular green patch of tobacco.

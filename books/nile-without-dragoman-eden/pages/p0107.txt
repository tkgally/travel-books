Mahomet, the wit of the ship, who had hitherto done nothing but aggravate us by his unintelligible Arabic jokes (it is most disagreeable to see men loudly laughing at gibes one does not understand, and sometimes suspects to be made at one’s expense)—Mahomet, whose conduct had been constantly idle, and sometimes almost mutinous, actually climbed the yard and helped to furl the sail; whilst Ibrahim became grotesque in his endeavours to forestall our wishes. His hitherto continuously bad behaviour had earned for him the nickname of the Ruffian. Now we were enabled to change his soubriquet, and call him the Reformed. But, alas, it was not long before, his nature reasserting herself, he became the Relapsed; and so by quick alternations from bad to worse, and from worse to bad, he won for himself a dozen names, until he finally took rank under Mr. Bruce’s Act, as “the Habitual.”

On the 26th, we stopped to victual at Wishna. We were attracted by the crowds of country people and masses of country edibles, we saw hurrying and being hurried to the market. Over and above both produce and people, and representing perhaps a little of both, or at least a link between the two, were three, what our fathers would have called, Barbary apes.

We soon passed them with their neighbourly lines of sycamores, and the unadorned ugliness of the country confronted us. But we were in luck. Before the men were tired of their frolic, a breeze sprang up, our sail filled, and, as the boat ran up to them, they jumped on board, screaming with joy as one of their number, an old fellow who was beat by the pace, slipped in springing from the bank, and fell into the water. An Arab bears his own misfortune well, but takes a genuine and unconcealed pleasure in that of his fellows.

The Mahmoudéëh Canal constructed by Mohammed Ali in 1819–20, is a straight water-way with a bend in it, running from Alexandria to Atfeh, a village on the Rosetta branch of the Nile. It is forty-two miles in length, some twenty yards in breadth, of a not very varying depth of about four feet, and of uniform ugliness. Twenty thousand men are said to have contributed with their bones to the formation of its banks. Poor fellows! they have found a plain resting-place, and do not seem to have agreed with the soil. The mud still lies in little heaps on either side, retaining almost the shape given it as it fell from the shovels, sacks, and baskets which carried it from the bed of the canal. Grass does not grow on it, weeds cover it barely, and the unshod feet of the men or donkeys which pass over it have had no power to fashion and shape the uneven surface.

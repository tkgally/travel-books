In one place near Ibreem, soon after the date groves, for which it is famous, had been passed, we came upon a very arid scene, and were much struck by the similarity that exists between two such dissimilar things as the sand of the desert and the snow of the Alps. In shape, the hills we were looking upon much resembled the crest of an Alpine ridge, and the sand that lay amongst them, coating the peaks, and filling the hollows, was in position and lie marvellously like drift snow and glaciers. Precisely as a black rock rises in Switzerland from a snowy bed, crops out in Africa a granite boulder from a drift of sand. So too with the tracks. The effect of a footstep in the snow or sand is identical. As we looked at the trail left by a number of camels we saw passing over the shoulders of these hills, it required the sight of the animals and the colour of the sand to convince us that it was not on snow that the tracks were made.

The man-of-war proved to be the “Garibaldi” from Tasmania, bound to Japan. She had put in here to refit, while a surveying party was engaged in making a chart of the harbour. This seemed to me rather a work of supererogation, for it was accurately surveyed by Captain Denham of H. M. S. “Herald,” in 1856. Perhaps, however, they had some reason, for the entrance has certainly narrowed much since Captain Denham’s visit. He describes it as being one-eighth of a mile wide: “It led into a basin having sixteen fathoms of water, with swinging room for two or three frigates, and a shore well adapted for coaling, if ever deemed desirable as a dépôt for passing steamers.” Strange to say, in spite of this recommendation, a harbour on the island of Kendavu, though much inferior to it, has been selected as the port of call for the mail steamers plying between the Australian colonies and San Francisco. The only reason for this is, that several of the more influential settlers in Fiji have bought land round the Kendavu harbour, and are anxious to improve the value of their property.

Fortunately I had with me a boy from that part of the Pacific, who had been with me to England, and spoke English well, so that I was enabled to understand all they said to me.

It appears that for some years an emigration has been going on from the crowded and somewhat unproductive island of Tamana to the more fertile and thinly populated island of “Nui,” one of Ellice’s group.

It so happened that a Fiji vessel, in search of “labour” for Fiji plantations, called at Tamana, when a number of the natives, thirty-five in all, men, women, and children, requested to be taken as passengers to Nui. The captain consented, and they paid for their passage in pigs, fowls, mats, &c. It appears, however, to have been the captain’s intention to run them to Fiji and sell them as “labour;” and the natives were much alarmed when they found the vessel had passed their intended destination.

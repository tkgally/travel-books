There was nothing slow about the way we were whisked off the minute we had stepped into ’rikshas. All was so dark and still, just the patter-patter of our two-footed horses’ feet, their “hai” as something got in their way and an occasional tinkle, like a bicycle bell, when the need was urgent. All the houses seemed closed, and no one about except our fellow-passengers, hurrying in various directions so rapidly that friends lost sight of each other and parties became separated. We were expected at the Oriental Palace, where we finally drew up, and were well taken care of. Two double connecting rooms, with bath, for twenty-five yen daily, maid four yen. The proprietors are English and French and the table and service (the latter limited conversationally) as good as any hotel in France. Evian water seemed like a long lost brother and worth its weight in gold, which it nearly cost—one yen (fifty cents) a bottle. The Japanese line carries only charged waters, but Pacific Mail Steamers have still Poland.

The cold wet weather is a disappointment, but nothing can really mar the charm.

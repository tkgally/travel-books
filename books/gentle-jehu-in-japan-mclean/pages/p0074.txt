It was almost hanging over the Inland Sea, was carpeted with beautiful matting and absolutely devoid of furniture save for the inevitable kakemono (hanging scroll) in the tokonoma or alcove, one small table six inches from the ground and in one corner a beautiful chest of baby-drawers with a glass on top—intended as a dressing table, but looking more like a cabinet. Half a dozen pretty, chattering and giggly maidens quickly supplied us with cushions on which to sit and things resembling padded sleeve boards to lounge against. We were offered European chairs—nailed to flat pieces of wood to protect the floors—but scorned them as being out of keeping and spoiling the effect.

Next we were each presented with a Hibachi or portable charcoal fire and finally tea and cake.

Some sound ground once more enabled us to get on terms with the pack; but even here, especially when we got at all low down, there were traps for the unwary, and again and again a soldier who knew the country would shout a warning to others who, like myself, had not ridden across it before.

<verse>
Pit where the buffalo cooled his hide,
By the hot sun emptied and blistered and dried;
Log in the plume grass, hidden and lone;
Dam where the earth-rat’s mounds are strown;
Cave in the bank where the sly stream steals;
Aloe that stabs at the belly and heels;
Jump if you dare on a steed untried—
Safer it is to go wide—go wide!
<i>Hark from in front where the best men ride:—</i>
“Pull to the off, boys! Wide! Go wide!”
</verse>

Again we were among crops and floundering on, crawling over the little “bunds” or small banks, our M.F.H. on his raking, yawing chestnut still going great guns and heading the right-handed division. An unseen hole put M. down in a soft, earthy bed, but a good-natured man picked her up and held her mount while she scrambled into her saddle.

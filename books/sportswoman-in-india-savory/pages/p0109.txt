Arithmetic on the frontier is a one-sided affair; as Rudyard Kipling says, there is not only the chance of being “potted” at at any moment from behind a rock during—

<verse>
A scrimmage in a border station—
A canter down some dark defile—
Ten thousand pounds of education
Drops to a ten-rupee <i>jesail</i>—
The crammer’s boast, the squadron’s pride,
Shot like a rabbit in a ride!
</verse>

but dysentery and enteric combine to pile up heavy odds against Tommy: few at home realised how many that summer, in the Bara Valley, at Ali Musjid, and in other camps on the frontier, might have said,—

<verse>
We’ve the fever here in camp—it’s worse than forty fights;
We’re dying in the wilderness the same as Israelites;
It’s before us and behind us, and we cannot get away,
And the doctor’s just reported—five more deaths to-day.
</verse>

While we were finishing lunch a conjuror—a real juggler—appeared, one of the camp-followers, and squatting down on the ground, wearing nothing except a loin-cloth and his turban, with therefore no possibility of concealment, began his tricks.

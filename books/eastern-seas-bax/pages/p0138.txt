Teuxham is a walled city, and is the capital of the northern portion of Formosa, being the second city in the island. It is governed by a prefect, and contains about 40,000 inhabitants. Some of the shops are good, and there is a fair trade inland and also by sea, in junks from Heong-san, a village and bay on the coast about three miles off. This bay is well sheltered for junks by a sand spit, but it is too shallow for foreign vessels to lie inside. The valley round the city is well cultivated. We went to the house of the headman of the Peppo-hoan tribe, who was obliged to keep a house at Teuxham, and to reside there for a portion of the year. As the headman was in the country, his wife and brother did the honours of the house for him. They were both Christians, and were delighted to see Mr. M. The reception room was well and gaudily furnished, like that of a petty mandarin, but the sleeping rooms were very small. As soon as it was known in the neighbourhood that Mr. M. had arrived, a great number of Christians and others came to see him and to ask him to cure them of various complaints.

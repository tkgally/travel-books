The path had gradually been getting worse ever since we left the sugar mill, sometimes leading up the bed of a stream among the rocks, and at other places it became so steep that it required the use of both hands and feet to scramble up. There were few camphor trees on the Chinese side, but after passing the frontier we entered a most beautiful forest composed principally of large camphor trees that looked as if they had never been disturbed. Creepers were twining about them in the wildest profusion, and many beautiful orchids grew on the branches; we saw very few birds in the forest, but occasionally we could catch a glimpse of one or two savages gliding along among the trees and watching us, making one feel what little chance a hostile force would have against them in these woods, on account of the ease with which they could be shot down without being able to see their foe at all. The path had now disappeared, and we went along simply by the guidance of one of the Peppo-hoans.

We had to cross a range of mountains about 2,000 feet higher than Mi-a-nosta, and then to descend into a beautiful plain or valley which was rich and well cultivated and about 1,700 feet below the top of the range we had just crossed. We put up for the night at a very nice tea house at a village called Go-tem-bah which was about fourteen miles from Mi-a-nosta, making a very pleasant walk. Here we got an upstairs room, which we found a great luxury, in consequence of its being quiet and well clear of Japanese travellers, and the cooking arrangements, which are all carried on in the large public room downstairs. We found the Japanese very primitive and simple in their habits. At this tea house, men, women, and children washed in a large tub of hot water in the evening in the public guest room without any idea that there was any indecency in the performance; after the tubbing operation, the old women and little girls shampooed the arms and legs of their guests.

# Chapter X.

[[subtitle]] Nagasaki—Effects of Typhoon, August 20th, 1874—Shanghai—Ching-Kiang—Nanking—Ningpo—Hongkong—Relieved by the New Ships Company, November 30th, 1874.

O<sc>n</sc> our return to Takow we found that H.M. ship “Modeste” had arrived from Hongkong, and had brought orders for us to proceed to Nagasaki, for which place we accordingly started, and arrived on the 25th of August, after a very pleasant passage under sail.

On entering the harbour, we were much surprised to see that many of the finest trees had been violently uprooted, that a great number of houses were in ruins, that several steamers and ships had been driven on the rocks; the masts of a Japanese ironclad (“Stonewall”) and of several junks were showing above the water, and there were wreckage and débris all about the beach in every direction. This havock was caused by the terrible typhoon that had taken place four days previously.

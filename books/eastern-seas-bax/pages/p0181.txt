[[cont]]Japanese scenery. The climate was pleasant, but very foggy near the sea, although the fogs do not prevail far inland.

H.M. ship “Elk” joined us on the 3rd with a mail; after which we proceeded to the settlement of Vladivostok, where we arrived on the 5th, having had to grope our way along in a fog. I have often noticed on this coast, that the fog lifts a little close to the land, and as the cliffs come down pretty steep into deep water, with few outlying rocks, the navigation is not so dangerous as it otherwise would be.

Vladivostok, also called the Golden Horn, can be entered from either end of the channel called the Eastern Bosphorus, and is a deep and safe harbour, well fitted for defence and to become the principal naval station in Eastern Siberia. It is about seventy miles from Possiette Bay. For a great part of the distance, an inner channel leads by a very good passage inside the Eugénie Archipelago of islands, which form a sheltered inland sea for vessels and boats trading in the numerous bays on that part of the coast.

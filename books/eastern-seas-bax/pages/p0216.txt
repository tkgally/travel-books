# Chapter VII.

[[subtitle]] Ascent of Fusi-Yama Mountain in Japan.

O<sc>n</sc> the 6th of October, 1873, the captain of the flagship, admiral’s secretary and myself, having obtained the necessary leave of absence from the Commander-in-Chief, started from Yokohama for a cruise inland, to see the country and refresh ourselves after our late sea voyage.

Through the kindness of one of the officers of the Royal Marines, who was stationed in the camp, we had managed to engage the services of a Japanese called “Gong,” who was to act as interpreter, pay our bills, procure us good quarters and provisions, and to see that we were not cheated by anyone besides himself. He turned out to be a very useful and civil man, always ready to make the best of everything, and kept our expenses down to a very reasonable amount. We had another Japanese called “Shouski,” who acted as cook, packed up our luggage and bedding, secured it on the packhorse, and then walked off in charge of everything to our next halting place.

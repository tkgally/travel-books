This sacred relic is often a written sentence, to be got from any priest; or the model of a little dog, or fox, and is put into the richly ornamented charm bag, already tied round its waist. In addition to this, they carry what we in England might imitate, viz., a pretty embossed card with the child’s name and address written in full, which, in case of its straying from its home, would enable the police, or any humane person, to gladden the heart of the distracted and distressed mother and child. Children, when they begin to walk, do not get their little pates <i>entirely</i> shaven, which is always the case during the first year; but the barber, at short intervals, shaves a round patch on the crown, leaving a band of hair to grow in a circle. This custom varies according to the sex and age, and the varying fashions of the different localities. Japanese juveniles are, like other children, fond of toys, and their parents rarely grudge the money spent on them. The frank and bright countenances of both boys and girls are, I feel sure, due to the pains parents take to make their children happy, and by their gentle and thoughtful ways avoid spoiling their tempers, a thing often done when childish mistakes are visited by hard words and cruel blows.

It is well-known that the American missionaries are systematic and hard workers, and have the talent of provoking others to good works.

The American missionaries seem also to realise the importance of a native agency; for no matter how well the missionary may speak the language, one learns more and more that the Japanese mind must be <i>handled by a Japanese mind.</i> They have been educated to reason things out, and there is less time wasted when combated by one from among themselves.

## The Japanese at Home.

I had frequent opportunities of visiting the homes of my pupils, and it was then that I unlearnt false ideas which I had imbibed about Japan from the reports of sensational writers.[[fnref *]]

I saw how necessary it is to know them in their daily life before they can be rightly understood. Very often we see the lady of the house waiting on her visitors at meals. She takes to this work in a cheerful, matter-of-fact way.

[[fn *]] “A snail overturning the castle wall with its horns” is a Japanese proverb, expressive of <i>impossibility.</i> A Japanese scholar quoted it one day in my hearing, in reference to those who wrote books on the domestic life and moral condition of the Japanese, before they had learnt the language and the character of the people from personal observation. A thing which cannot be done in a day!

New Year’s Eve, 1874.—We gave a tea and watch meeting in the Church schoolroom. The Captain of H.M.S. <i>Thistle</i> has lent us the Flags of all nations. We only asked for two—the English and American. He also sent a detachment of men to put them up. A petty officer on board is an earnest Christian, and helps on the work. Several of the Missionaries came and addressed the meeting. We had a good time. The Chaplain, Rev. E. W. Syle, was so encouraged by eight marines recently converted, applying to him for admittance to the “Lord’s Table,” that he sent me a truly kind Christian epistle, and three five dollar notes, saying that my visit to Japan was a venture of faith which God had evidently owned. <i>See R. M.’s letter on page</i> <pref>298</pref>.

My hands are full of truly spiritual and happy work among the sailors and marines. My pupil, Hirano-san, is a great help and comfort to me. He grows in grace. The sailors love him. He visits the ships with me, carrying books and tracts, and enjoys our meetings on board as well as those on shore. As I have the means and opportunity, I send a note to one or more ships, inviting a small party of sailors to tea. The Christians on each vessel are glad to pilot the unsaved ones to my house.

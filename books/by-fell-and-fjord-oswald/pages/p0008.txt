# Note.

A few Icelandic words are used without translation, as—

<i>à</i> (pronounce as <i>ow</i> in prow), river—as Thjorsà, Thors river; Ellidà, Ellid river.

<i>Bondé</i>, yeoman, peasant proprietor, but often used of the head of a family even when not a landowner; hence our word husband—<i>i.e.</i>, hus-bond.

<i>Bœ</i> (pronounce <i>by</i>), dwelling.

<i>Fjall</i>, fell, mountain.

<i>Jökull</i>, glacier.

<i>Prestr</i>, priest or clergyman.

<i>Tun</i>, enclosed grass-land round a farm, equivalent to the Scottish <i>toun.</i>

<i>Vellir</i>, valley.

Icelandic is pronounced as written, with Italian vowel-sounds, besides others peculiar to itself. The consonant <i>Thorn</i>, or <i>th</i> English, and the soft modification of the <i>d</i>, which is pronounced <i>dh</i>, both represented by different characters in Icelandic, are here printed <i>th</i> and <i>d</i>, not to confuse the page.

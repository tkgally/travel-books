<verse>
[[cont]]“Life for life has this conquering brand
Counted my dead out of Flosi’s band.”
<i>Ever shall murderers meet their doom.</i>

“The feud is fought to the bitter end,
And yet we may meet as friend meets friend;”
<i>The homeless winds are abroad alone.</i>
“And yet he may only thank the wave
That spares his foe for a bloodier grave.”
<i>’Tis quiet below the sculptured stone.</i>

“We fear no foe nor any fate,
Let us then knock at Flosi’s gate;”
<i>Hark to the wind on the upland fell</i>—
“We are worn-out men and the night is blind,
We’ll prove the mettle of Flosi’s mind.”
<i>Hark to the dash of the thunderous swell.</i>

Flosi came to the wind-shook door,
“What voice is that in the tempest’s roar?”
<i>Oh fires within are bright and warm</i>—
“Who stands so dark against the snow?”
“Kári it is, thy deadliest foe.”
<i>Hold fast the doors against the storm.</i>

“Thou com’st unarmed, a shipwrecked guest,
Kári, avenger, come thou blest.”[[fnref 1]]
<i>Cruelest storms at last will cease</i>—
“Revenge is past, and love shall live;
Ah, doubt not, but the dead forgive.”
<i>Under the grass mounds all is peace.</i>
</verse>

From Bergthorshvol our day’s ride was to Odde, which from the most ancient times has been a well-known place. The first author who <i>wrote</i> books in the country, Sæmund the historian, lived here. He is supposed to have compiled the ‘Elder Edda.’

Here Snorri Sturlason the historian was brought up by Jon Loptson, also a man of mark. Mackenzie found a good house here, and a handsome church, in 1811, when Iceland was at its lowest ebb. We were a large party, and had a charming evening, entertained by the kindly and merry old Dean, Sira Gudmundr.

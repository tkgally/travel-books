It rained in the morning, except when it was blowing very hard indeed; so, after breakfast, we went over to the neighbouring <i>bœr</i>, or farmhouse, and into a cavern called the <i>eldhus</i>, or kitchen, where, with patience, a person who can stand steadily on one foot may dry the other over the few sticks that, crackling between two rough stones, count as the kitchen-fire. Nice people were there, who made much of us, and gave us excellent cream. About one it cleared up, and we cantered off merrily, sometimes over sound turf, but sometimes plashing through bogs, which had drunk so much rain that I feared they would swallow us down as something solid. At length we came out from the hills on a sloping plain, which, except where one snowy peak showed beyond it, melted into misty distance. Near us it was dotted here and there with clouds of steam rising from the ground. We rode over the soil blasted by sulphureous vapours to the farthest and biggest of these clouds; and this was Geysir, and here we dismounted on a bit of greensward, and prepared to pitch our tent. This may not read as if the place were pretty, nor was it—it looked odd, and perhaps a little mean; but then the day was overcast, the lamps were not lighted. Soon we found much that was curious. Hunger impelled us to the accredited kitchen, Blezi, which is a pool like a wide, deep <i>crevasse</i> in a blue glacier: the boiling water is so exquisitely pure that the silica rocks below become bright blue through its medium. To Blezi we confided a joint of lamb, a tin of preserved fowl, and a fish, tying them in strings and lowering them. I regret to say he swallowed our fish; the string breaking, we beheld it deep blue in the depths, and no doubt it is now a petrifaction. Round the margin of this pool ran a charming fretwork of petrified leaves, chiefly silver-weed; many we took away, but, like fairy-money, when we unpacked them they were dust.

The crater of Geysir is a mound of siliceous rock, which has been aptly compared to an inverted oyster-shell. The pool is about sixty-five by fifty-five feet in diameter, and there the transparent water simmered quietly, looking perfectly incapable of any such frisky proceeding as springing into the air.

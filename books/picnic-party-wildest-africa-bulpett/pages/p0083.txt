# Chapter VI

[[subtitle]] Kaig Camp

[[subtitle]] A permanent camp—Numerous baboons—Bathing under difficulties—The Doctor guards the launch—Excellent sport—Our daily routine—Lost dogs—“The sheep and the goats”—Mohammedan Christmas—Work for the Doctor—Native gratitude—The effect of a gramophone—Nuer physique—Characteristics—Distant excursions—Value of signal pistols—How <i>not</i> to fry bacon—A giraffe hunt—Ostriches—A magnificent shot—The “Mem Sahib’s” buck—Peculiarities of hippos—Return of the launch with stores—Arrival of mules—Galla porters—Difficult navigation—Itang—First view of the Abyssinian highlands—Abyssinian looters—Pokum—Olea—The parting of the ways—A lovely country.

As soon as the boats could be unloaded and their contents piled beside the river on the high bank, we sent the larger launch with the barges back to Nasser to bring up our reserve of stores. It was a long and tiresome journey to Nasser and back; and as it turned out it was three weeks before we saw the launch again. But we knew that we should have to remain at our present camp for a considerable period, so we made our arrangements accordingly.

We had no fear that the time would hang heavily on our hands, as we could see herds of antelope of different sorts on all sides of us, and we immediately began to set up our camp around a large gemaisa tree close to the spot where the Greek traders were encamped.

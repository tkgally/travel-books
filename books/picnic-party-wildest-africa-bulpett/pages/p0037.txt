It is well to remember that in most parts of the interior of Africa, salt is worth more than any other commodity, so that you can hardly have too much of it. It will buy food when nothing else will do so. A large supply of the well-known American cotton cloth will be most useful for trading and paying purposes; and brass and iron wire, of the thickness of the common telegraph wire, has always a good exchange value, though it is heavy to carry. Beads nowadays are rather at a discount; and as each tribe has its own particular fancy as to their size and colour, it is often very difficult to know what kind of beads to take. But they are generally found useful for small purchases, and if taken, a very varied assortment should be had. The large amber-coloured beads are as valuable to natives in the far interior of Africa as pearls to us in Europe.

It is not very easy to make an exact estimate of the cost of a well-equipped expedition, say for a period of six to eight months. A good deal depends on circumstances; prices vary in different localities. If mules and donkeys are available, a large primary outlay may be necessary for buying these animals; and as all your transport animals are liable to die, and often do nearly all die, there is sometimes a dead loss here.

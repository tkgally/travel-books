We owe her far more than the value of her theft that day for putting into the awful perfection of the scene the bit of imperfection with whose faults our poor humanity could find something akin and social. It was a positive relief from the mighty condescension of the hills to meet somebody who was not above us. Her smiling presence helped also to dispel the gloom which settles on these waters of destruction, as we recall the slaughter of extermination with which Kamehameha choked the valley in the day of his conquest of Maui.

For it was in this terrible strait that Kahekili’s army was utterly destroyed. There on the sides of the great rock in this Coliseum of nature they fought and fell, and the Wailuku dragged their lifeless bodies to the sea. It is this association of human tragedy with a tremendous scene in nature which turns awe into something like fear.

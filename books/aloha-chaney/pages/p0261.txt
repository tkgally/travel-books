I shall never blush or retaliate if I am ever called a donkey. After that trip, in which the highest human virtues—courage, patience, self-sacrifice, long-suffering, the bearing of others’ burdens, and meekness altogether trans-Mosaic—were so conspicuously shown by the little beasts that conveyed us through that limbo of wrought-iron and clinkers, I should feel honored by comparison with them.

“Is there no water in this wilderness to cool their innocent tongues?” I am mentally asking. “Oh for some miracle by which my cupful could be made to water them all! I would gladly give it to them.”

“Yes, there is water,”—“<i>pau wai</i>,”—Jacko says, “a few miles away.”

“Let us go there.”

“Yes, we are going.” The surveyor gives the order. And how I longed, every step of that wrenching, racking, twisting, crucifying way, to have my old mule and his fellows know that we were going to get them water! We found it at last,—a great bubble cavern, in whose cool depths the waters found a natural cistern. The barrels and portable tanks were filled with it.

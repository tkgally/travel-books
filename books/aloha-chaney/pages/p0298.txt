Its faith in itself may awaken faith in others. In Honolulu, if anywhere, it should prosper. The English residents and visitors might sustain it there.

But it may prevail after all, if Congregational Protestantism should lose the sense of its priceless liberty or omit the safeguards of its traditional order. The form of Episcopacy, however, which has been imported into Honolulu, being what is called high-church, will have no advantage over Romanism in its appeal to the natives. Already the Church of Rome has made many converts among the impressionable Hawaiians, and it may be that its firm hand will yet take hold of the people whom the Congregational Church holds with ever-loosening grasp. If all the prelates of the Roman Church have as much candor and common-sense as one of whom I heard the following anecdote, their personal influence might go far to neutralize the superstitious influences of their church.

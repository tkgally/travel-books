The regular beating of the engine, whose monotony had become a positive sedative, now grew fitful, and finally ceased. Orders and responses, and the scuffle of hurrying feet, the flapping of sails as they came bellying down, and the rattle and squeak of pulley and rope; the jargon of half-roused natives breaking through the chrysalis cerements in which Death’s brother had wound them; the love-pat of the sea upon the ship’s side, so different from the hard slap it had been used to; the reviving crowing of the ship’s live fowl, answered from afar by kindred voices; the little crowd by the ship’s side, brought out in glowing distinctness by the lantern they held; the thump of the lowering boat as it struck the water; and the sound of oars and hospitable voices on the dim sea,—this stir of noises all predominated by the sudden rush of escaping steam, whose long, shrill hush, as if to quell the racket, was the most rousing of all, brought us gradually to the consciousness that we had arrived somewhere, and were at anchor. We were in Lahaina harbor, and it was four o’clock in the morning. A vision as of a creamy star suddenly falling out of darkness into darkness more profound, and we realized that our prince had gone down over the vessel’s side, to take the boat for shore.

If a skilful surf-bather can get his board to the front, where the roller first begins, and keep it just on the edge of the advancing wave, he will be carried with increasing swiftness on to the final break-up of the sea in the surf upon the shore. A very slight maladjustment of the saddle on these watery coursers will unseat the most accomplished rider. Again and again, amid shouts of laughter from the native crowd on shore and unnecessary sighs of anxiety from the uninitiated foreigners, these surf-riders were upset and submerged in the rushing waters. Hardly would they disappear before their shining heads would come up again in the rear of the wave that had thrown them, and they would push out further into the sea, to mount the next convenient roller. They rode their surf-boards as boys coast downhill, flat upon their faces, and seemed to keep themselves in place by a timely stroke or holdfast of their hands. The toil of swimming off with their big boards after each ride, and beginning again, would have spoiled the sport for less easy swimmers. As in coasting, one needs the excitement of a rapid descent to sustain the labor of returning to the starting-point; but to these natural swimmers it was all sport.

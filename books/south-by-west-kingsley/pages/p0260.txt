# Chapter XVI.

[[subtitle]] Guadalajara.

[[subtitle]] The Paseo—Barricades—The Belen Cemetery—Attractive baths—A fortunate escape—The Cathedral—Confessionals—<i>El Hospicio</i>—Señor Menesses—A clean kitchen—Embroidery—The <i>Cuna</i>—A wonderful contralto—<i>Helados</i>—A wicked bull—Pottery—The opera—The States Prison—An embarrassing present—Mexican troops—How to make a <i>pronunciamiento.</i>

<i>Guadalajara, April</i> 10.—We are in perfect Paradise here after “roughing it” for so many days. Our hosts are kinder than words can say; and one almost forgets that there are such things as robbers and revolutions till one sees all the gentlemen and the coachmen going about with revolvers, and barricades across the streets for fear of the rebels making an attack.

The 8th was spent by Mrs. P. and me in resting and enjoying our host’s excellent German piano, while the gentlemen received all the authorities, who paid them a long and satisfactory visit. Everywhere as yet the idea of a railroad has been received with acclamation.

In the afternoon at five we took a drive in a comfortable carriage, with a pair of fine mules. We went along the Paseo, a boulevard planted with trees, which runs all round the town. There are now only two points of egress from the city to the Paseo, as all the streets are barricaded with strong <i>adobe</i> (sun-dried brick) walls, very well done.

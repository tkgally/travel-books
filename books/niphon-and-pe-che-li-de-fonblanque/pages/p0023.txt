The flag-officer commanding the American squadron, Commodore Tatnall, who was about proceeding to Japan to receive on board, for conveyance to the United States, the Japanese Embassy, was obliging enough to offer me a passage in the <i>Powhattan</i>, and I accordingly proceeded to Kanagawa as his guest. It was he, a brave and accomplished sailor and a thorough gentleman, who, during the disastrous affair at the mouth of the Peiho, in the preceding year, on seeing the crews of our disabled and stranded gunboats exposed to a deadly fire, forgot every consideration but that of humanity, and carried his ship into action to the rescue of our sailors.

The French funeral ceremonies differ widely from those of our army, but are not less impressive. The band of the 102nd Regiment played Verdi’s “<i>Miserere</i>” with admirable taste in the chapel. During the procession to the burial-ground, there was no music but the Zouaves’ “Quick Step” (the general had commanded a regiment of Zouaves in the Crimea, and led them in the storming-party at the Malakoff); and their <i>reveillé</i> were the last notes over his grave.

Service was performed on this occasion by three missionary bishops; two of them Frenchmen, one a Chinaman, but all wearing the Chinese dress, pigtail included. The effect was novel if not imposing.

The addresses spoken over the grave were short, pithy, and soldier-like; that of the army chaplain concluded thus:—

“<i>Messieurs, je n’ai plus rien a vous dire;</i>” then, with a wave of his hat towards the open grave, “<i>Adieu, mon Général, au revoir!</i>”

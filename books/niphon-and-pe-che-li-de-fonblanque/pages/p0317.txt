The men of infantry regiments seemed to have been converted into cavalry, and might be seen tearing over the plains in all directions, with that utter disregard for every law of horsemanship and humanity peculiar to the British soldier. Mounted sailors would suddenly dash out of the most unexpected places, at a furious pace, and nearly run you down, replying to your remonstrances with a blow from a small spar over the horse’s head, and an assurance, accompanied by strong oaths and vigorous hauling at the tiller ropes, that “the devil won’t answer the helm!”

English shopkeepers and their assistants, too, deserted their business for a time, and, in top-boots and breeches, their bodies bent forward after the fashion of jockeys, and their toes turned out after a fashion of their own, went into a desperate course of training for some future Derby.

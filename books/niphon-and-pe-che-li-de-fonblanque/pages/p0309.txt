Kin-shau, uncle-in-law to the late Emperor, Mu-jin, minister of war, and several others, were banished to the colonies; and Prince Kung, having thus removed all obstacles, assumed the regency in concert with the Empress-mother, who is said to be a remarkably strong-minded female, and extremely favourable to European intercourse.

We are not used to such summary measures in England. What would be thought of Lord Derby if, some fine day, having concerted his plans with the Duke of Cambridge and Mr. Disraeli, he sent Lords Palmerston and Russell to the Tower, with permission to hang themselves, executed Mr. Gladstone in Charing Cross, and sentenced the other ministers of the Crown to penal servitude for life? But it is not, after all, so very long since things almost as outrageous were done in England, and even in the present day there are civilized countries in Europe where a similar course might take place

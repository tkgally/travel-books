That most candid of charlatans, Mr. Barnum, tells us in his memoirs, that the real genuine mermaid, which he exhibited in his museum at New York to admiring thousands, was obtained from a Yankee ship-captain, who reported having procured it in Japan.

In the course of a shopping tour in Yokuhama, I came across several specimens of the same kind, and I had puzzled over them for a long time, utterly at a loss to reconcile their strange incongruities, partaking, as they did, of man, beast, fowl, and fish, before I discovered, or rather suspected—for discovery required a dissecting process—that this <i>lusus naturæ</i> was ingeniously manufactured, by the hand of man, out of a composition of paper, pulp, leather, and glue.

The shopkeeper, who dealt principally in live-stock, dogs, chickens, singing-birds, &c., did not attempt to deny the ingenious fraud when taxed with it, but assured me that he sold a great many to the Funi-Yaconins (ship-captains), who never doubted his word when he assured them that the nondescripts had been caught in the interior of Japan.

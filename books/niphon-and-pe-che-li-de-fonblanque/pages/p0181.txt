We had now accomplished over two-thirds of the ascent, but the worst was yet to come. Hitherto the path, though steep and rugged, had afforded a tolerably firm foothold; but the rest of the way was over loose pieces of lava, scoria, and cinders, and at every few yards the ascent became more precipitous. It was curious to remark how some of our party, who had before shown themselves somewhat insensible to the beauties of nature, would now stop every few minutes to admire the scenery, generally seating themselves to do so; but some allowance must be made in consideration of the rarified state of the atmosphere, which rendered violent exercise somewhat difficult and made us gasp very painfully. At first, we met with little snow, but as we advanced we found large patches here and there, and on reaching the summit, after four hours’ toil, the tubs of water near the temple were frozen into a compact mass. Still, the cold was not anything like what we had been led to suppose it would be, the thermometer at mid-day showing only 54° in the shade, and the mercury boiling at 186°.

On our arrival at Atamé, than which a more lovely little sea-coast town does not exist on the face of the earth, we were not met, as we had hoped, by the <i>Camilla</i> (she had, it is now supposed, been lost five days before this, on the 9th September), but H.M.S. <i>Berenice</i> (Indian navy), by which we received our English mails.

By an odd coincidence, on our opening the papers we found, in a late number of <i>Once a Week</i>, an article by Sherard Osborn, on “The Pilgrimage to Fusi-jama.” Of course, we swaggered a good deal over it, and pitied his ignorance, and wondered how a man who had never been there could presume to write on the subject, misleading the public, &c., &c., &c.

Nevertheless, we remained in ignorance of the true meaning of “Fusi-jama,” which Sherard Osborn and other writers (who have never been there, and, consequently, cannot know anything about it!) choose to translate, “the matchless mountain.” That “jama” means mountain, we know; but no Japanese would allow that “fusi” meant matchless.

The sale by auction of a thousand horses was an event of considerable importance in Tien-tsin, and gave rise to some amusing scenes.

Among the troopers of Fane’s Horse were some very vicious ones. They were manageable enough while with their Sikh riders or Syces; but when they fell into the hands of English or French soldiers, or of Chinamen, their true character would break out. One unfortunate French officer, having paid five pounds for a beautiful little Gulf Arab, found his purchase so utterly unmanageable that he finally offered a Chinaman a dollar for removing the horse from his premises. Another horse was no sooner in possession of his new owner than he positively refused to allow any white man to enter his stable; and the groom had, at last, to resort to the device of blackening his face and wearing a red turban, as the only means of approaching his charge. It was also reported that a powerful Cape horse had been seen scampering through the streets, with head and tail erect, and carrying his owner, a fat Chinaman, between his teeth.

“Your ministers have to-day read the vermillion decree, stating that the arrangements for Your Majesty’s proposed hunting expedition are to serve as preparation for taking the field in person. . . . . They admire the awe-inspiring demeanour and the strategic ability thus displayed. <i>But the common people are extremely slow of comprehension; they easily suspect, and with difficulty appreciate;</i> they will say that the Barbarians are to the south-eastward of the capital, and that the sudden change from a hunting-tour to taking the field in person should induce Your Majesty rather to proceed to Tunchow for the support of San-ko-lin-sin; that the taking up a post to the northward of the capital <i>would be desertion from the seat of war;</i> that what in name was a campaign was in reality a hunting-tour. . . . . . Your Majesty is familiar with the maxim, that the prince is bound to sacrifice himself for his country.”

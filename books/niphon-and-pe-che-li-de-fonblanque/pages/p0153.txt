There were two very old women; one unmistakable old man; then Paterfamilias with his wife; lastly, some half-dozen boys and girls, from about eighteen to six years of age. They were all <i>stark naked,</i> and, to all appearance, perfectly unconscious of the fact, as they stared at me in open-eyed wonder. After having passed some little distance, I looked back; they were still at their doors quietly chatting, and, I suppose, criticising my appearance. I wondered whether they would dress for dinner.

How are we to account for these things? Some enthusiastic admirers of Japan insist that this absence of shame is the natural result of perfect primitive innocence, and that the Japanese are really as little aware that they are naked as Adam and Eve were before the Fall. But, alas! we know too well that they have long since tasted of the forbidden tree; nay, eaten so heartily that scarcely a single fruit remains to tempt their appetites.

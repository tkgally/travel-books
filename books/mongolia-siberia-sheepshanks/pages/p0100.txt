In this temple the idols were placed in a press, or cupboard, which at the time was open, and the Lama, who was disposed to be very friendly, gave me the names of one or two of the divinities without ceasing his work. One, he said, was a representation of Maidra, and one of Burchan Bukshee (the Master Buddha), but the lower orders of the Lamas seldom, I think, know even the names of the divinities represented. I visited the temples with gentlemen who understood the Mongolian well, and we generally obtained conflicting replies to our queries.

The most curious and most richly decorated of the temples that I visited this day was the last. The house containing the idols was not connected with the hall of assembly, but was in an enclosure behind, the gate of which was shut. I found, however, some simple Lamas in one of the gurrs within the enclosure, and made friends with them by allowing them to handle my clothes, and examine my boots and watch-chain, and ask questions which I answered as well as I could. One of them, at my request, opened the door, and I was admitted into the sanctum. The shrine was built in the Chinese fashion, and ornamented in front with bright colours tastefully handled.

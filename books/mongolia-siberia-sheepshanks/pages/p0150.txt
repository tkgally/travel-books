# Chapter V

[[subtitle]] ‘Shamanism’

T<sc>he</sc> old, old religion of Asia, which, it might well be thought, would have been driven by advancing light into the limbo of forgotten superstitions, has not yet by any means died out. It has been dispossessed of its fairest provinces by Christianity and Mohammedanism in the west. It has been ejected from Southern and Central Asia by various forms of belief, notably Buddhism and Brahmanism; though it still retains its hold upon the præ-Aryan tribes of India, who, remarkably enough, have never been conquered by those more thoughtful systems of belief, but have still clung to their ancient and ruder notions. It has been supplanted in China by the various beliefs of the “Celestial Empire,” and among the Mongols of Gobi by the strange religion of the Lamas; yet upon Buddhism and Confucianism it has left its undoubted impress.

The food was coarse, bad, but plentiful, and the wines of Austria, which were served free with the meals, were pure and invigorating.

On our way to Trebizond, a journey which took us about three days, we made three stoppages at the three only Turkish ports of the Black Sea, besides Trebizond, namely Ineboli, Samsun, and Kiresun, all of them little better than villages. At each of these we stopped several hours, and got rid of some of our passengers. I made a practice of landing in the curious boats which looked exactly like the viking ships one sees in pictures, and are probably built very much on the same plan as the galleys of the ancients. The jabbering of the natives was like pandemonium. At Ineboli I saw a sentinel, sitting down in front of his sentry-box, his gun leaning up against it, and he himself busily engaged hunting for vermin about his person. It was a sickening sight.

Asia Minor is the land of promise flowing with milk and honey; flanked by Mount Ararat on the east, and Mount Lebanon on the west, it embraces a most fertile country. Rich in every natural resource, grain, wine, sheep, and other products, Asia Minor is nevertheless on the verge of bankruptcy, and this is due entirely to the iniquities of Turkish rule.

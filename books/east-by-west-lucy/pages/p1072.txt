The rancheman, though now one of the wealthiest men in Texas, was born and bred a cowboy. With another lad he had, at the age of twelve, gone into business on his own account, with a stock of a dozen cattle. He had never been to church, as, indeed, he had scarcely ever lived a day off the ranche. He had the vaguest idea of what a clergyman was or did. But he loved this woman very much, and, saddling his horse, he rode straight off fifty miles to the nearest hamlet, and brought back “a preaching man” almost literally at his saddle-bow.

The rancheman assembled all his cowboys to witness this strange ceremony. As they stood by the open grave the preaching man, whilst offering up prayer, knelt and closed his eyes. The rancheman was aghast. He had brought this man over, and felt personally answerable for his safety; and here he was on his knees with his eyes shut, and scarcely two paces off a score of the blackest rascals in Texas, not one of whom had ever been known to miss his aim!

When we reached the tea-house the coolies washed their feet, covered their semi-nakedness with their cotton blouses, and sat down, contented and happy, to their evening meal. This consisted of two soups, which always introduce a Japanese dinner, a bowl of rice, some eggs, and a dubious vegetable; a meal not too heavy after the day’s work, and with the prospect of one on the morrow equally exhausting. For liquid refreshment they had had a cup or two of tasteless tea, the banquet being rounded off by three whiffs from their liliputian pipes.

As for us all preconceived notions of personal discomfort, and even semi-starvation when travelling in the interior, were agreeably dispelled. We had two rooms on an upper floor, spotlessly clean, the straw matting shining with polish, and the walls partially formed of painted screens. There were a table and three chairs, which looked grotesquely out of place, but were nevertheless acceptable.

But he thinks he has a reasonable right to demand that he shall know a yen note when he sees it, and shall not confound a fifty-yen note with one valued at twenty. Formerly yen notes were recognizable at sight, having the figure “one” printed on them in numerals. Now there are yen notes of various colours, sizes, and designs, with no figure of denomination printed on them. The fifties are the same size as the twenties, and are exactly the same pattern save for cabalistic signs in the corners—of course plain enough to the Japanese, but worse than Greek to the foreigner. It is true that if you know where to look for it, and have a microscope handy, you can discover the figure “twenty” printed on the tenpenny note, and “fifty” on the two-shilling one. But these are not conditions always realizable, especially at night.

I heard of a recent visitor to Japan who had only a month to see the country in.

But there is no doubt the thin end of the wedge has been inserted, and within twelve months this exceedingly modest approach to the ballet will be further pursued.

The dance itself was to the Western taste a melancholy and soul-depressing performance. Forty-three damsels, dressed precisely as if they had walked off a Japanese tea-tray, moved in a single file across the stage and adown the gangway running at right angles with the stage, and passing through the mass of the audience in the pit. This is a peculiarity of the Japanese theatre which is really very effective. Instead of the actors and actresses entering from right or left of the stage, or from behind the scenes, they walk on to the stage, as it were, out of the pit. The green-room, instead of being at the back of the stage, is at the front of the house, and there are two gangways, one for approach and the other for exit.

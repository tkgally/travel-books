# Chapter XVI.

[[subtitle]] Tokio—The Shiba and Ueno Parks—The Asakusa Temple—The Yoshiwara—The Forty-Seven Ronin.

O<sc>ur</sc> next move was to Tokio where we were landed at the Shimbashi Station after a fifty minutes’ run along the coast. Tokio boasts of two hotels—the Imperial and the Métropole. The former is a cold palatial looking edifice run by a Japanese syndicate chiefly interested in letting out their banqueting hall for official dinner parties. The company is also said to receive a subsidy from the Government, in return for which they accommodate any official guests whom it may be necessary to entertain at Tokio. It is the local “Claridge,” and is largely patronized by Americans to whom the high charges are attractive. The Métropole, though less centrally situated, we found to be extremely comfortable, and the table reflected the greatest credit on the French <i>chef.</i> This hotel is under European management, and the charge of five dollars a day per head is moderate enough.

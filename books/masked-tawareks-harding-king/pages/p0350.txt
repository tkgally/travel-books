Very few of the defeated Tawareks succeeded in reaching their tents, for before they could do so they were met by a body of Shaambah who were wandering knight-errant-like in the Sahara in quest of adventure. These promptly attacked them, and, being considerably the larger party, killed several of their number, and took most of the remainder prisoners. But, as soon afterwards they found these rather an encumbrance to their movements, they disposed of them by the simple but effective method of putting them to death.

In the meantime events were proceeding rapidly at Hassi Inifel. It so happened that the second body of Tawareks, returning from an unsuccessful foray, had almost reached the well by the time that the Tawarek attack took place. Hearing the shots they realised at once what had happened, and immediately fell back without disclosing their presence. They passed the night in the desert, and when morning broke, hoping that the Shaambah would have left the well, marched down to it to refill their <i>gurbahs</i>.

The Shaambah, however, were waiting for them, and on seeing them approach immediately advanced to the attack.

The trees and shrubs all grew alike, and there was no telling one from the other. The fitful flashes of the moon, as they shot through the thick clouds overhead, seemed to cast strange shadows across my path. The wind too was rising, and the boughs of the trees groaned weirdly. Presently a swimming seemed to fill my head; my limbs refused to move another inch, and again I sank down exhausted—this time by the side of a small creek whose green mossy bank looked restful.

On all sides the tall trees stared down at me. Queer noises made me start, and every now and then I felt as if some unknown horror were upon me. The shadows cast by the moon danced like demons in this wild unearthly grotto, and my brain began to mix the real and the unreal. A sort of haziness came over my vision, and the shadows took shape and danced round me, as if mocking me in my sad plight. The wind gained strength, and howled piteously through the trees.

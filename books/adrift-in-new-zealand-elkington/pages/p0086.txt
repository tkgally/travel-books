For a moment you think you are on an island; for as you follow the harbour round it joins a river, and, from your eminence, seems as if it ran right into Manukau Harbour on the other side of the island. Then the eye travels to a wide expanse of sea, and then back to the land, which is all cut up into small paddocks divided from one another by stone walls. There are scoriæ everywhere, and boulders of immense size, which remind you that at one time Mount Eden was a volcano; instinctively you look for the crater. It is like a great brown basin, weird and uncanny, and you crane your neck to see into its depths. Your eye follows the windings of the lava till it terminates at the bottom. You become giddy, the even markings of its sides fascinate your eyes, and you wonder what the crust at the bottom hides.

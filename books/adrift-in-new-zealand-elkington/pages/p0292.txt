“But you’ll come back all right,” she added; “you won’t be like a lot of them. Ugh! I hate the gum-fields; they have swallowed up dozens and dozens of good men, and they will go on doing it until the end of time.”

“Or the end of the gum,” I put in; but she would not smile.

“Good-bye,” she said presently. “You will come back.”

Now if I had believed in Fate, I should certainly have said that he had fixed things up with a deep-set purpose to drive me on to those very gum-fields; but I don’t believe either in Fate or Luck—nevertheless, I went to the gum-fields, and went there of my own free will; for, on arriving at my friends’, where I had intended to stay for a couple of weeks to help get in their cattle, I found there the one person in Auckland whom I thoroughly disliked, and so I decided to feign a most tremendous interest in the diggings, and spent most of my days looking round them.

[[cont]]needs a very big sense of humour to be philosophic under such circumstances. Honestly, I don’t think it would worry me much if I got lost now, simply because I know the bush, and if I couldn’t get out of it in a reasonable time, I wouldn’t mind stopping there, provided I had some matches and a “billy,” for there’s plenty of food about if you know where to look for it and how to cook it; but at the time when I got lost I knew neither, and I remember very vividly the peculiar sensations I suffered. It was just midday, and I had been tramping all the morning, when I came to a small river. The day was a dull and sultry one, and the river looked cool; so, seeking shelter behind a clump of ti-tree, I threw off my swag and hunted for some dry sticks to light a fire Having found them I next got a long pole, which I drove into the ground at an angle of about 75°, hung the billy of water on it, and set fire to the sticks underneath. In my tucker-bag I had some cold meat, a crust of bread, and a small packet of tea, so, for the time being, the world was very bright, and I lay back with my head on my swag, enjoying the rest and looking forward to my lunch and billy of tea.

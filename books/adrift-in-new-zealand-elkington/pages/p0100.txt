The next important daily in Auckland is <i>The Evening Star</i>, from which office many weekly journals are issued. <i>The Herald</i> is the morning paper, and the other is, of course, the evening one. I know of no morning star, but why there should not be I cannot understand. Besides these two papers there are several other weeklies: one, <i>The Observer</i>, though very amusing, is decidedly rude, and secures its readers by gossiping about everybody, caricaturing the respectable residents of the town and country, and ridiculing everything. It is run on the same lines as <i>The Sydney Bulletin</i>, though it lacks that journal’s humour and brilliancy. <i>The Bulletin</i> is the <i>Punch</i> of the Southern Seas, but many English people take it seriously and quote weird paragraphs from it, thinking that they are then expressing the views of Australia. <i>The Sydney Bulletin</i> is unique: it is against the Church and against the State; it will ridicule the Opposition, has no respect for Nonconformists, abominates earnestness, scorns quacks, loathes humbugs, and, in fact, is against everybody and everything except

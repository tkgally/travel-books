“Sit down, Bob,” I said, “and don’t be an ass.”

He sat down.

“Now light your pipe again,” I suggested.

He knocked the ashes out on his gaiters and slowly refilled his briar. “You know,” he began, “there’s a horse show at Hastings. Well, last year I was there, and I met her. I was stopping at the hotel, and she was staying with friends, and—well, hang it! I fell in love with her the first day I saw her. She rides like a——”

“Duchess,” I suggested.

“Yes, and she’s——”

“Never mind the description,” I put in; “the thing is, does she return your love?”

“Yes, by Heaven, and that’s where the trouble is; if it wasn’t that she loves me I wouldn’t care a hang: I could bear all the misery of it myself, but I hate to see her suffering.”

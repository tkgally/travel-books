Of the town itself I can say very little, for, beyond driving through it from the station and back again, I saw nothing of it, and for the first three days I think I only wandered about in the Sanatorium grounds. I was in excellent company, and there is enough to keep you busy, if you are keen on sight-seeing, within a stone’s-throw of your <i>pension</i> or hotel. I really believe if I had had my own way I would have remained in those grounds watching the geysers shooting their great streams of water into the air and causing innumerable rainbows to form, and listening to my companions’ interesting, idle chatter, till to-day; but, being in Rotorua, one must do the sights. They are, as I have already said, remarkable, and if seen as I saw many of them—by moonlight—they assume a virtue they do not possess at any other time.

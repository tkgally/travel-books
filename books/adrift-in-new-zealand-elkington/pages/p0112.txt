Some one in the house was playing the piano, and, as the soft tones floated out to us, the scent of the ground and the calm of the evening soothed her last fears away, and when we went in later on I knew she was no longer afraid.

So it is with all the strange sights of New Zealand: they repel one at first, as the bush does, but when once this first sensation has gone it never returns; its place is filled with a love and longing for them.

The baths at Rotorua, as every one knows, or should know, are famous for their healing properties, and, in the words of the guide-book, “they are divided into five classes—Sulphurous, Alkaline, Acidic, Saline, and Alkaline-silicious”; but, as I shouldn’t know one from the other except the sulphurous one, I will say nothing about them, beyond testifying that I have seen many people from various parts of the world soaking in them and looking very much like boiled lobsters.

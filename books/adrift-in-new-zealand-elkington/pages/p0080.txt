“Spare me!” I pleaded. “I see tea is ready. Can I get you some?”

“If you are thirsty, yes.”

“And whilst we are drinking it, you can go on and tell me who the men are,” I said.

“The men are chiefly in business. There are practically no idlers here. One is a stockbroker, one an architect, one an actor, and one is of your fraternity, an insurance agent, and that little man over there with the dark moustache is the editor of <i>The Star.</i> That stout, jolly-looking man is on a mining journal; and has been twice in prison for criminal libel; he came here from <i>The Sydney Bulletin</i>, and prior to that has been on the staff of half the papers in London, Paris, and New York. For variety,” added Miss Frazer, “you can’t beat New Zealand tea-fights.”

After a few more adventures we were landed at our destination, where we were met by the storekeeper’s man in a bullock-dray.

The duke, three other diggers, and myself were all making for the same place, so we threw our swag and tools on the dray and walked alongside it for about a mile to the store, where we purchased what things we wanted, and then “stuck up” a month’s provisions; next morning, accompanied by the bullock-dray, we started for the camp, which lay about fourteen miles off.

That walk was not an interesting one, for the country in this part of Auckland is only good in patches, and, as this land is always being dug, where it is not bare it is covered by a short bracken and poor ti-tree scrub. The road we followed was as rough as it could be, and seemed to wander all over the place.

“Ah, yes,” he sighed. “I have been tempted, but not by work.” He began to roll the shavings of tobacco in the palm of his hand. “Shall I tell you,” he asked, “my greatest temptation?”

“Pray don’t!” I cried, in as sympathetic a voice as I could.

“No, brother, it is not a sad story, but I really would like to set fire to some of these surly squatters’ haystacks—that’s my temptation. Do you believe me, only last week one of the brutes refused to give me a night’s lodging unless I chopped firewood for half an hour. Did I do it? No, I didn’t, but I left all his bloomin’ gates open, and the next morning I bet he wished he’d given me a bed.”

“Tell me,” I asked, “how far is it to the next station?”

“About a mile; it’s one of the biggest near here, and they do you well, too; plenty to eat and a nice, comfortable hut to sleep in, and they never asks you to work. But come along; we’ll get up there, as it’s nearing sundown.”

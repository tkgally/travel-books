It was the same in 1787. Franklin says: ‘Opposite to the citadel in a large handsome square is a gallery where the Khan’s music, consisting of trumpets, kettle drums, and other instruments, plays regularly at sunrise and sunset.’ There they are,—the three kettle-drums; the trumpet, which has two notes and resembles a coach-horn in appearance; and the ‘other instrument,’ a whistle, from which the performer extracts a series of intermittent scales;—banging and blowing just as they did a hundred and sixteen years ago, when the shrill runs, punctuated by the hoarse blasts from the trumpet and accompanied by the incessant roll of the kettle-drums, beat upon the ears of that traveller who leaves an account of his ‘tour’ from Bushire, which might serve as a guide to the traveller of to-day—such is the rate of progress in this Eastern land.

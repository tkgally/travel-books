I was specially moved to pity by a little girl about nine years old, evidently a half-breed, a truly pretty child, with beautiful eyes and fine features, a little gypsy creature, who sat in a filthy calico dress, her only garment, and a bright red handkerchief across her black matted hair; the industry of her little fingers told the story of the lack of care of which this neglected bit of humanity was the victim; and in all her squalid loneliness my mother instincts went out to her, and I wished with all my heart that I could have saved her, body and soul. The printed “Programme” of the dances announced the following:

<blockquote>
“<i>Tash-Noosh</i>—An ancient dance participated in by the Indians more than two hundred years ago.
<i>Ya-Koo-tee</i>—An ancient dance in honor of visiting chieftains from afar.
<i>Ta-Heen-nah-kla-kee</i>—A wild and weird dance of the interior tribes—very ancient.
<i>On-de-koo-sot</i>—A representation of the Shaman, or Indian doctor’s dance when expelling an evil spirit from a patient.
<i>Kow-whi-ka-klanik</i>—A dance making friends among the tribes—never performed since the days of Baranoff.
<i>Salmon dance</i>—This dance takes place on the occasion of the spring run of salmon.
<i>Love dance</i>—Chaste and pure, with its beautiful and soul-inspiring music.
<i>Chichigoff waltz</i>—By Hoonyiah Indians.”
</blockquote>
